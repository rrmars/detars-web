You came to the UK on BN(O) visas in March 2022, and on 2 November 2026 the government announced a tougher settlement system. You want to know whether your 5-year route is safe, when you can apply, and what it will cost. **Short answer: aim to submit one ILR application for each of the four of you as early as the rules allow, which is 28 days before your 5-year anniversary in the UK. With a March 2022 arrival, that is between 1 February 2027 and 3 March 2027. The 2 November 2026 announcements say BN(O) holders keep the 5-year path, but the English level (B1 or B2) is contradicted between official statements and no start date has been published.**

This guide follows Wing (44), Mei (42) and their children (15 and 11), who arrived on BN(O) visas in March 2022. It was written on 8 November 2026 from official GOV.UK pages, the Immigration Rules, the Home Office fee table, Hansard (the record of Parliament) and UKCISA (the UK's advice body for international students). Nothing here has been checked against your own documents, and no Home Office, lawyer or school was contacted. "Unconfirmed" marks anything that did not come from an official page, and "assumed" marks our own planning figure. **It is general information, not legal or immigration advice.** The source materials are not published.

## The recommendation and why

**Apply for ILR (indefinite leave to remain) for all 4 of you as early as the rules allow.** The earliest day is 28 days before your 5-year anniversary; the Home Office refuses an application made earlier. This assumes you entered in March 2022 and have kept continuous permission; confirm your own dates first.

- **Why the BN(O) route matters:** on 2 November 2026 the Home Secretary said Hong Kong BN(O) holders keep their current settlement path of 5 years, outside the new 10-year baseline and the new income tests (GOV.UK and Hansard, 2 November 2026). Nothing we found says the 5 years for BN(O) will lengthen.
- **The catch:** the same publications say BN(O) applicants must meet new minimum requirements: a stricter criminality test and, in the written government response, an English standard of B2. The Commons answer on the same day says only "minimum requirements for English". A ministerial statement reported by the Foreign Office says B2 would not apply to BN(O) and B1 stays. **These conflict, and no start date has been published. The rule in force today is B1.**
- **Why early:** Home Office policy is that settlement rules apply at the time of the decision, not when you arrived. Applying at the earliest date gives the least exposure to a change. This is our judgement, not a guarantee.
- **Your exact day:** it depends on your real entry date (the exact day is your arrival day in March 2027 minus 28 days). **Next step:** find the entry stamp or eVisa record for each of you before you plan anything else.
- **If your visa expires before your earliest ILR date,** GOV.UK says you must renew first rather than wait.

## How continuous residence and absences are counted

- Only time with permission on a route that leads to settlement counts, and your most recent permission must be BN(O) (Appendix HK 62.1).
- The Home Office counts back from the most favourable of: your application date, any date up to 28 days after it, or the decision date (Appendix Continuous Residence CR 1.1).
- You must not be outside the UK for more than 180 days in any 12-month period. Days abroad because of travel disruption or compelling compassionate reasons may be disregarded (CR 3.1 and 3.4). **Next step:** list every Hong Kong trip since March 2022 with dates.
- Citizenship has its own rules: you must have been in the UK exactly 5 years before the day the Home Office receives the application, with no more than 450 days abroad in those 5 years and no more than 90 in the last 12 months.
- Time under "leave outside the rules" at the border does not count.
- Do not plan trips outside the UK, Ireland, the Channel Islands or the Isle of Man between submitting and the decision: the application is withdrawn if you leave.

## Later: citizenship

- **Adults:** apply 12 months after ILR. You need the Life in the UK Test (the settlement pass can be reused), English, good character and an intention to live in the UK, within the absence limits above.
- **Children born outside the UK:** register on form MN1 while under 18. A child aged 10 or over must be of good character, and both parents' consent is expected. Whether your children would use the discretionary route for children whose parents are applying (section 3(1)) is our reading of the guide, not stated for BN(O) children on GOV.UK. **Next step:** confirm it in the form guide before you apply.
- We found no announced change to naturalisation or child registration in what we read, but we did not search every citizenship publication.

## The 15-year-old's university fees (start September 2029)

**Bottom line:** if the family gets ILR in 2027 as planned, the 15-year-old should qualify for home fee status and student finance in England for a September 2029 start. If the family is still on BN(O) limited leave on 1 September 2029, the child would very likely be assessed as international and miss student finance. This is our inference from UKCISA and GOV.UK; the regulations themselves were not read.

- **Main category, "3 years in UK and Islands, settled in UK"** (UKCISA, England, courses from 1 August 2024): the student must be settled on the first day of the academic year (ILR or British citizen), ordinarily resident in the UK and Islands for the full 3 years before it, and not resident mainly for education. A BN(O) passport without ILR, or limited leave, is not "settled". Settled status can be obtained as late as that first day (UKCISA document dated 13 December 2024; list updated 1 October 2026).
- **Back-up category, "Family member of a settled person":** a child under 21 of a parent who is settled on the first day of the first academic year, with 3 years of ordinary residence, can qualify even if the child's own status is not yet settled.
- **Long residence** (7 years in the UK if under 18 on day one; otherwise half a life or 20 years) is unlikely to help, because the child will normally be 18 in September 2029.
- **No BN(O) category:** UKCISA's England list (updated 1 October 2026) shows no category specific to BN(O) holders. **Next step:** do not rely on home fee status as a BN(O) holder without ILR; ask the universities you choose.
- **Student finance:** UK nationals or people with "settled status" can get a Tuition Fee Loan and a Maintenance Loan if their home is in England and they have lived continuously in the UK for 3 years before the first day of the month the course starts (GOV.UK, courses from 1 January 2027).

| | Home fee status | International fee status |
|---|---|---|
| Tuition fee | Regulated; Tuition Fee Loan up to £9,790 per 120 credits in the current GOV.UK figures for courses of the 2026/27 era. The amount for 2029/30 is not published | Set by each university and not capped. No figure was collected; check your chosen universities |
| Student Finance England loans | Eligible (tuition and maintenance loans; maintenance is means-tested, and a GOV.UK example shows up to £15,415) | Not eligible (inference) |

**Timing for your two children:** if you apply for ILR between 1 February 2027 and 3 March 2027 and it is granted in 2027, there are roughly 2 years of buffer, but ILR must be granted by 1 September 2029. Becoming a British citizen by then (by registration) also satisfies "settled". The 11-year-old would start around 2033 to 2034, by which time settlement and probably citizenship should be in place. The 3 years of residence before September 2029 are covered by your March 2022 arrival if you keep living in the UK and avoid long stays abroad.

## What to do now (November 2026 to February 2027)

- **Pin down your exact dates:** entry date in March 2022, current eVisa expiry date and each person's permission history.
- **Count your days abroad:** no more than 180 days outside the UK in any rolling 12-month period.
- **Wing and Mei:** book the Life in the UK Test (£50 each) and keep the pass; it also counts for citizenship. The children do not sit it.
- **Gather English evidence at B1 speaking and listening** (an approved test, or a degree taught in English). Because the B2 question is unresolved, check whether a degree route is open to you before paying for a test, and do not buy a B2 test until the detailed rules appear.
- **Check character issues:** any conviction, caution or serious driving offence. The announced criminality threshold is stricter than today's.
- **Set aside about £13,000 for the ILR stage and a further £5,700 or so for citizenship in 2028,** and decide whether to pay the optional £500 priority fee per application.
- **Check GOV.UK monthly** for the technical guidance, the impact assessment and a Statement of Changes on "earned settlement".

## What is still unconfirmed

- **Start date of the earned-settlement rules:** not announced. A law-firm search snippet claimed April 2026; the 2 November 2026 publications, which say detailed rules are still to come, contradict it, so we treat the date as not announced.
- **B1 versus B2 for BN(O) ILR:** conflicting official statements (16 March 2026 versus 2 November 2026). The original 16 March Hansard text was not retrieved; the claim rests on the Foreign Office report. **Next step:** wait for the detailed rules before paying for any English test above B1.
- **National Insurance and debt conditions for BN(O):** whether BN(O) applicants face them is open; the Commons answer names English and criminality only, and the written response does not say otherwise.
- **Priority fee:** whether the £500 is charged per person is our assumption, and whether MN1 child registration qualifies for priority is unconfirmed (it is not on the list we read).
- **Children's registration route:** the section 3(1) route is our reading of the MN1 guide.
- **University fees:** that a BN(O) child without ILR would pay international fees is an inference; no BN(O) category appears on the UKCISA page, but the underlying regulations were not read. International tuition fee levels, English-test and degree-statement prices, and the 2029/30 home fee cap and loan amounts were not collected.
- **A change to the rules in March 2026:** a Statement of Changes from 5 March 2026 appeared in searches and was not read, so we make no claim about what it changes for BN(O).
- **Third-party commentary** (law firms, campaign groups, news) appeared in searches and was not used.
