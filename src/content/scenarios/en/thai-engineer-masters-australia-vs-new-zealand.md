You want a master's that helps your engineering career without spending more than your family can safely give you. **The lowest-cash option in this comparison is the University of Canterbury's on-campus Master of Engineering Management in Christchurch. It still sits THB 51,711 above your family's THB 2,500,000 limit, so earn a buffer first.**

This guide is for **Nattaporn, 24, in Bangkok**: BEng civil engineering, GPA 3.1, two years on site, THB 32,000 a month, and THB 2,500,000 of family support. She is aiming at a **February 2028** start. The figures were read on **7 November 2026**. They are dated examples and planning allowances, not offers, quotes or visa decisions.

The source materials are not published.

## Recommendation: Canterbury, once the money gate is met

**First choice: the University of Canterbury's on-campus, 1.5-year Master of Engineering Management in Christchurch.** It matches an engineering background and your preferred length, and its published 2028 programme tuition is NZD 62,400 [P5]. The shorter course saves another half-year of living costs and Thai earnings. An eligible New Zealand level-9 master's currently carries the same three-year post-study work window whether you study for 1.5 or two years [N3–N4]. That is today's rule, not a promise for a 2029 graduation.

**Do not commit on the family budget alone.** The cautious all-in planning envelope is **THB 2,551,711**, including a three-month job-search runway and a 10% reserve. That is **THB 51,711 above the family cap.** Build an extra **THB 200,000** of personal savings by November 2027 (a target, not an asset you have), then reprice. Family support plus that target leaves about THB 148,289 above the base envelope. A further 10% adverse exchange-rate move still leaves **THB 106,882 unfunded**. If the funding gate cannot be met, defer rather than fund the gap with hoped-for student work.

- Canterbury study-period cash: THB 2,146,817, before the transition runway and reserve.
- Family support: THB 2,500,000, the ceiling you gave. It is not disposable income.
- Current eligible NZ master's work window: 3 years. Not guaranteed for a 2029 graduation.

**Australia is not the affordable default.** All three two-year Australian scenarios exceed your budget by a wide margin. Adelaide's possible extra graduate-visa year is conditional, and it is not cash for tuition. Auckland is the second-choice city only if its final offer and budget beat Christchurch. No university rankings or assumed scholarships drive this recommendation.

**Before you decide:** keep emergency and return money aside, check how each university converts a Thai GPA, confirm the campus mode, and get the actual February 2028 fee and offer terms. A management master's does not by itself give professional-engineer registration or an immediate project-manager role.

## Total cost and payback, with the arithmetic

Exchange-rate reference: RBA, 6 November 2026, AUD 1 = THB 23.12 and AUD 1 = NZD 1.2401, so NZD 1 = 23.12 ÷ 1.2401 = THB 18.64365777 [F1]. This is a historical cross-rate, not a bank-transfer quote or a 2028 forecast. No student earnings, scholarship or future wage rise is counted.

- **Tuition:** latest dated annual fee × 1.05 for each year from the fee year to 2028 × study years, then held constant for the course. The 5% is an assumption and actual 2029 continuing fees may differ. Canterbury's published 2028 whole-programme fee of 62,400 is used directly, without inflation.
- **Annual allowances:** insurance and levy Australia 1,500 and 500; NZ 1,100 and 1,500. Levies are planning allowances, not verified university invoices. Flights 2,000, set-up and bond 3,000, documents, tests and medical 1,000, in destination currency for either country. Student visa allowance AUD 2,500; NZD 850.
- **Study cash** = tuition + living for the course + insurance + levies + one-offs. **Transition** adds three months of city living and a health-cover allowance, plus the graduate visa (AUD 5,750 or an assumed NZD 2,000). The decision envelope adds 10% to both. That reserve covers exchange-rate and price surprises but is not a guarantee against them.
- Everything is a cash requirement. A bond may come back and unspent reserve is not an actual cost. No loan interest or extra second-485 fee is included. If a one-year student visa needs renewing, add the charge; the model assumes one student-visa charge.

**Earnings you give up:** THB 32,000 × 12 = THB 384,000 a year, so 18 months is THB 576,000 and 24 months THB 768,000 of gross earnings not received during study. That is not all lost savings, because your Bangkok living costs would also stop or change. For the stay-home comparison below, assume Thai savings of THB 10,000 a month (THB 120,000 a year), so forgone savings are THB 180,000 or THB 240,000. Replace this with your real savings history.

**Payback depends on getting and keeping a full-time job.** Take off an assumed 25% of gross pay as a tax and payroll reserve, then the city living budget and an annual post-graduation health-cover allowance (AUD 1,500 or NZD 1,100, not an actual policy quote). This is not an individual tax calculation. Salary and living costs are frozen, with no unemployment, promotions or remittance spread. NZ results rely on the unconfirmed Payscale figure. Cash recovery conservatively treats the full reserve and bond as spent. The stay-home catch-up is (envelope + forgone Thai savings during study) ÷ (overseas annual saving − 120,000). Both are years after paid work starts, so add study and job-search time to measure from departure. Three months of job-search living is funded in the envelope but produces no savings. Staying beyond a temporary graduate visa needs another lawful work route; no residence is assumed.

**Canterbury sensitivity:** at the unconfirmed NZD 58,000–72,000 pay band, annual savings are THB 268,469–464,227 after the same 25% reserve and living budget, and envelope cash recovery is 5.5–9.5 years. A 10% adverse exchange-rate move raises the envelope to THB 2,806,882. Do not treat today's conversion as secured.

**What this means:** it is an overseas-career investment, not a quick three-year money-back trade. Do not justify the degree with experienced managers' salaries, and do not assume you can repay it within the first graduate-visa window. On this family budget, a cheaper, shorter qualifying NZ route is financially less risky than an Australian two-year route, but only after the cash and admission gates are closed.

## Timeline from today

These are personal planning milestones, not invented university deadlines. Read the February 2028 opening and closing dates on your chosen university's page when they are published.

- **November–December 2026:** choose between engineering-management and construction-management career targets. Collect your degree, full transcript, grading scale and certified translations, and document your site projects and employment. Audit your savings and the family's source of funds. Start IELTS preparation. Keep working.
- **January–March 2027:** sit IELTS for the first time. Aim for 6.5 overall with no band below 6.0 as the common shortlist level, or higher for job communication, and allow for a retake. Check whether 2028 intake information has been published and each university's overseas-degree and GPA equivalence requirements. Do not assume 3.1 passes every scale.
- **April–June 2027:** as applications open, prepare a focused shortlist: Canterbury first, Auckland as the alternative, Australia only with genuinely larger funding. Recheck fees, campus delivery, credit consequences and English validity. Build your CV, purpose statement and employment evidence.
- **July–September 2027:** compare actual offers, non-tuition charges, insurance and refundable or non-refundable deposit terms. Recalculate with real transfer quotes and updated rent. Confirm on-campus NZ weeks or Australian CRICOS study eligibility before accepting any shortened offer. Check enacted immigration changes, not consultation proposals.
- **November 2027:** funding decision: family THB 2,500,000 plus the THB 200,000 personal-savings target. Hold the contingency and runway. Saving THB 200,000 ÷ 12 ≈ THB 16,667 a month through twelve deposits from December 2026 to November 2027 is demanding against a THB 32,000 salary, so check feasibility and do not assume it. If it is not funded, defer. Prepare the current visa checklist, health and character evidence and your source-of-funds trail.
- **November 2027 – January 2028:** after an acceptable offer and a passed funding gate, lodge under the process and processing advice in force then. Leave substantial lead time; no number of weeks is guaranteed. Take out required insurance and book accommodation only after checking cancellation and refund exposure. Do not rely on part-time income for approval.
- **February 2028 onward:** arrive for confirmed orientation and course dates. Track your actual granted work limits and look for locally relevant project experience without exceeding them. Six months before completion, recheck graduate eligibility, English, insurance and police requirements and the filing window. NZ currently normally requires application within 3 months after the student visa expires, with qualifying study and maintenance funds [N3].
