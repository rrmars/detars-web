You have an offer of SGD 8,200 a month plus a one-month bonus, starting in April 2027, and two worries: will the pay actually clear the visa bar, and will the move leave you better off than staying in Bangkok? **Short answer: accept in principle, but negotiate before you sign. The money works; the risk is the pass, not the pay. SGD 8,200 clears the general Employment Pass minimum, but it falls SGD 64 short of the financial-services minimum that applies to new applications from 1 January 2027.**

This guide follows Krit (29, single, a full-stack developer with six years' experience and a Chulalongkorn University bachelor's degree), who earns THB 115,000 a month in Bangkok. It was prepared on 8 November 2026 from public Singapore government and other pages read on 7 November 2026. Nothing was applied for, no employer, agent, landlord or official was contacted, and the Ministry of Manpower (MOM) Self-Assessment Tool was not used. "Unconfirmed" marks anything that does not come from an official Singapore government or central-bank page, and "assumed" marks our own planning figure. **It is general information, not legal, tax or immigration advice.** The source materials are not published.

## The recommendation and why

**Accept in principle, but negotiate before you sign.** Your fixed monthly salary of SGD 8,200 beats the general Employment Pass (EP) minimum of SGD 7,500 at age 29 from 1 January 2027. It is SGD 64 below the financial-services minimum of SGD 8,264 for the same age and date. The bonus does not count toward either figure.

- **Find out how MOM classes the employer.** A fintech that is a bank, payments, fund-management or other regulated financial business is likely treated as financial services; a pure software or platform company would more likely fall under infocomm technology or "all sectors", but only the employer's MOM record settles it. The employer can see this in its own MOM account; you cannot check it yourself. **Next step:** ask HR to confirm the classification in writing.
- **If it is financial services, ask for one of two fixes.** (a) The employer files the EP application on or before 31 December 2026, when the age-29 minimum is SGD 7,727 (SGD 7,982 at age 30) and SGD 8,200 passes. Or (b) the fixed monthly pay is raised to at least SGD 8,700, which clears the 2027 age-30 figure of SGD 8,541 with margin, for example by moving part of the bonus into a fixed monthly allowance, which MOM does count.
- **Ask for relocation and setup support.** The month-one cash need is about SGD 5,770 to SGD 12,970 (THB 151,059 to THB 339,555) depending on housing (assumed; see the setup table below).
- **Ask whether medical insurance is provided and who pays the EP fees.** MOM says the employer chooses on insurance. The fees are SGD 105 for the application and SGD 225 for issuance.
- **Keep the April start.** A start by about 1 July 2027 still gives 183 or more days in Singapore in 2027 (1 July to 31 December is 184 days), so you would be a tax resident in your first year. A start in autumn could put you in the non-resident 15% flat-rate regime for the first year (see the tax section).

**If the employer will do fix (a) or (b) in writing, accept. If it cannot, do not resign from your Bangkok job until the in-principle approval (IPA) letter is issued.**

The headline numbers, all on the original SGD 8,200 offer:

- **Take-home:** about SGD 8,359 a month on average in a full year (13 months, resident tax, no CPF), against THB 100,177 a month in Bangkok today (about SGD 3,826 at SGD 1 = THB 26.18).
- **Savings:** about SGD 5,589, SGD 4,589 or SGD 3,189 a month for a room, a condo room or a studio (illustrative; the spending figures are assumed).
- **First-year tax:** SGD 3,465 on SGD 82,000 of 2027 income as a resident, against SGD 12,300 if the same income were taxed as non-resident.

## Stage 1: the salary floor

MOM runs a two-stage test: first an age-based qualifying salary, which is a hard floor, then COMPASS points. A candidate who fails stage 1 is ineligible "regardless of the points they would have scored" (MOM eligibility page, read 7 November 2026). The salary that counts is the fixed monthly salary: basic pay plus fixed monthly allowances. It excludes bonus, commission, annual wage supplements, overtime and reimbursements.

| Your age at application (assumed) | Sector | New applications before 1 January 2027 | From 1 January 2027 | Your SGD 8,200 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 29 | All sectors (not financial services) | 6,991 | 7,500 | passes (+700 margin) |
| 29 | Financial services | 7,727 | 8,264 | passes before 2027; fails from 2027 (−64) |
| 30 | All sectors | 7,223 | 7,750 | passes (+450) |
| 30 | Financial services | 7,982 | 8,541 | passes before 2027; fails from 2027 (−341) |

**Counterintuitive finding:** the usual advice is "your pay is well above the EP minimum, so you are fine". That is true only if the employer is not classed as financial services. MOM's own list of financial-services activities includes banking, fund management, payment and transaction processing, credit granting, remittance and activities auxiliary to financial services, and a fintech doing any of these may fall inside it. The 1 January 2027 step-up (SGD 537 at age 29 for financial services) lands between now and your April start.

- **Which date counts (unconfirmed):** the MOM table is headed "new applications before" and "from" 1 January 2027, so the date of submission is the likely test. **Next step:** ask the employer which date it will use.
- **Which age counts (unconfirmed):** measuring your age at submission is our reading of the table, not a stated MOM rule. If you turn 30 before the application is filed, use the age-30 rows.
- **The 5-year "tech EP"** for shortage-list tech roles needs a fixed monthly salary of at least SGD 11,500 (age 36 and under) for new applications from 1 January 2027, so it is out of reach at SGD 8,200. A first EP for other applicants runs up to 2 years, and renewals up to 3 years.

## Stage 2: COMPASS points

COMPASS needs 40 points to pass. There are five scored criteria (C1 to C5); C6 is an organisation bonus that matters only if the employer is in a listed programme. The official Self-Assessment Tool needs an employer EP eService login, so only the employer can run it. MOM says an "eligible" result in the tool means around a 90% chance of approval, and that the actual score may differ.

The takeaway: C2 (20 points) and the SOL bonus carry your application, and the SGD 8,200 salary earns nothing under C1. If the SOL points are what get you over 40, MOM says you may only be employed in that shortage occupation and must notify MOM before any redeployment. **Next step:** ask HR whether the employer has run the Self-Assessment Tool for your role, and what job title it will use.

## Must the employer advertise on MyCareersFuture first?

Yes, normally. Under the Fair Consideration Framework the job must be advertised on MyCareersFuture for at least 14 consecutive days before the EP application. The ad must show a salary range (the maximum no more than 2× the minimum) that includes your offered salary. The job and employer in the ad must match the EP application, an ad that closed or expired more than 3 months ago cannot be used, and the employer should not make a job offer during the 14-day window. Exemptions: fewer than 10 employees, a fixed monthly salary of SGD 22,500 or more, a role of one month or less, and local or overseas intra-corporate transfers.

Why this matters to you: you already hold an offer. **Next step:** ask HR whether the ad has run and when. Filing by 31 December 2026 needs the ad to have started by roughly mid-December at the latest, plus preparation time. The employer decides whether that is feasible.

The pass is tied to this employer and job; changing employer needs a new application. Medical insurance is the employer's choice, and there is no levy or quota (MOM key facts and application pages, read 7 November 2026).

## CPF: none for EP holders

EP holders do not contribute to CPF, and the employer does not contribute for you. The CPF Board lists foreigners (persons who are not Singapore Citizens or Permanent Residents) among employees exempted from receiving CPF contributions. The upside is higher take-home pay, with no deduction of roughly 20% for the employee share. The downside is no employer CPF top-up, so retirement saving is entirely yours. The take-home figures below have no CPF deduction. Becoming a Permanent Resident later would change this.

## Singapore income tax: resident or non-resident in year one

The rule (IRAS income tax rates page, read 7 November 2026): a foreigner is a tax resident for a Year of Assessment if they stayed or worked in Singapore for at least 183 days in the previous calendar year, or continuously for 3 consecutive years, or worked a continuous period straddling two calendar years with a total stay of at least 183 days (for employees; not directors, entertainers or professionals). Non-residents pay on employment income the higher of a flat 15% or the resident progressive rates, with no personal reliefs. Residents pay progressive rates after reliefs: 0% on the first SGD 20,000, rising to 24% above SGD 1 million.

Assumptions for the example: start on 1 April 2027; SGD 8,200 × 9 months (April to December 2027) = SGD 73,800, plus the one-month bonus of SGD 8,200 paid in December 2027 = SGD 82,000. Only Earned Income Relief of SGD 1,000 (under age 55) is claimed. Rates are assumed to stay as published for Years of Assessment 2024 onward, because no 2028 rebate or rate change was found (unconfirmed).

| Step | Resident (183 days or more in 2027) | Non-resident (hypothetical, same pay) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2027 employment income | 82,000 | 82,000 |
| Less Earned Income Relief | −1,000 | not available |
| Chargeable income | 81,000 | 82,000 |
| Tax calculation | 3,350 on the first 80,000 + 11.5% × 1,000 = 3,465 | Flat: 15% × 82,000 = 12,300. Progressive: 3,350 + 11.5% × 2,000 = 3,580. Higher = 12,300 |
| Effective rate | 4.2% | 15.0% |

The non-resident column is hypothetical. With a 1 April start you pass 183 days (1 April to 31 December is 275 days), so you would be a resident for Year of Assessment 2028. The gap it shows is why the start date matters.

When the first year would be non-resident: if the start slipped to 1 October 2027, you would have only 92 days in 2027. On 3 × SGD 8,200 = SGD 24,600, non-resident tax would be the higher of 15% (SGD 3,690) and progressive (SGD 92), so SGD 3,690. If the straddle rule applies (1 October 2027 to 31 March 2028 is exactly 183 days if you stay continuously and count presence), resident tax would be on SGD 23,600, which is SGD 72. Whether the straddle rule applies in your case is for IRAS or a tax adviser to confirm; that is our reading of the IRAS wording.

A full year (2028, 13 × SGD 8,200 = SGD 106,600): chargeable income SGD 105,600; tax 3,350 + 11.5% × 25,600 = SGD 6,294 (5.9%). Singapore has no pay-as-you-earn: the employer reports your income and you receive a bill the following year, so set aside about SGD 524 a month. Thai tax on any Thai-side or foreign income in 2027 is outside this guide; take Thai advice.

## Before you sign

- Get the sector classification, the filing date it will use, and your job title in writing.
- Ask for fix (a) or fix (b) if it is financial services.
- Ask whether the MyCareersFuture ad has run, and whether the employer has run the Self-Assessment Tool.
- Ask about relocation support, medical insurance and who pays the EP fees.
- Do not resign in Bangkok until the IPA letter is issued, and recheck the figures if rates, rules or your start date change.
