You are a freelance UX designer in Florianópolis with two US clients, about USD 4,500 a month now and about USD 6,500 a month during 2027. You want to bill in dollars without a tax surprise, and you want to know how much reaches your account. **Short answer: MEI does not fit; open an ME (microempresa) under Simples Nacional; and receive the dollars through Wise Business at about 1% all-in, if a Brazilian CNPJ can get USD account details (not confirmed).**

This is general information from public pages read on 8 November 2026, not tax or legal advice. An accountant has to confirm every point before you register anything. Nothing was applied for and nobody was contacted. The source materials are not published.

## Why MEI does not fit and an ME under Simples Nacional does

**MEI does not fit.** The MEI revenue cap is **R$ 81,000** a year, which is R$ 6,750 a month. USD 4,500 is about R$ 24,755 a month at the Central Bank (BCB) mid rate of 5.5010, roughly 3.7× the cap. MEI also has no extra allowance for exports. On top of that, no UX, design or software-developer occupation turned up in the Simples Nacional portal's list of MEI-permitted occupations (Annex XI). That was a keyword search of the document, not a page-by-page read, so ask the accountant to confirm.

**An ME under Simples Nacional fits.** The next step for you is to ask an accountant whether the company can be opened with Simples Nacional applying from day one. For a new company, the Simples roteiro says the opt-in must be requested at CNPJ registration; a new company is not offered a later 30-day window. That item was reported by the research and not re-opened, so treat it as something to confirm.

| Question | MEI | ME · Simples Nacional | Individual (PF, carnê-leão) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Annual revenue cap | R$ 81,000; MEI has no extra export allowance (Simples portal Q&A) | Simples limit R$ 4.8 million; an additional equal limit for export revenue (same Q&A). The ME label up to R$ 360,000 comes from LC 123 (secondary source, unconfirmed here) | No cap |
| Does it fit you? | No: cap exceeded; design and UX not found in Annex XI | Yes | Possible but the highest tax |
| Tax regime | Fixed DAS of about R$ 86.05 (R$ 5 ISS + 5% × R$ 1,621 minimum wage), our own sum | DAS on Annex III or V by fator R; export portions of ISS, PIS and Cofins not due | Monthly IRPF up to 27.5% plus INSS; exports not subject to ISS |
| Admin burden | Light | Accountant, NFS-e, pro-labore, monthly DAS | Monthly carnê-leão filing |
| Accountant (published prices) | Not applicable | R$ 137–450 a month (see the accountant section) | Low; IRPF form extra (R$ 279 on one page) |

In plain words: DAS is the single monthly Simples tax payment, NFS-e is the electronic service invoice, pro-labore is the salary you pay yourself from the company, ISS is the municipal service tax, and IRPF is personal income tax.

## Fator R: Annex III or Annex V

Design services are in the **fator R** group. Fator R is your payroll (including your own pro-labore) divided by revenue over the prior 12 months. At **0.28** or more the company pays under Annex III; below 0.28 it pays under Annex V. Annex V is the more expensive table. "Design, desenho" appears in the portal's list of fator R services.

**Two limits sit next to this.** First, the portal Q&A was only partly updated on 9 October 2026. It says that from 2027 the 12-month window moves to the months before the prior month, and that this item will be completed once the CGSN 140 changes for the tax reform are published. Second, the estimate below uses 2026 tables, so the 2027 reform (IBS and CBS entering Simples) may change the DAS and the export treatment. Ask the accountant how the 2027 change affects you.

**In this estimate the two annexes come out within about R$ 360 a month of each other**, so the pro-labore level is an accountant's decision, not a given. Annex III with a pro-labore of 28% of revenue is the more cautious case; Annex V with a pro-labore of one minimum wage is the alternative. Both are in the take-home table below.

## How service exports are taxed, and the IOF caveat

If your clients are abroad and the result of your work is used abroad, the revenue is an export of services. Here is what the sources say, with how firm each point is.

| Item | What the sources say | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ISS | Not due on export revenue (CF art. 156 §3 II; LC 116 art. 2), per the Simples portal Q&A list of export immunities. The service must be for a client resident abroad and the result must occur abroad; services developed in Brazil whose result is verified in Brazil are not exports. | Primary |
| PIS / Cofins | The same list: PIS/Pasep and Cofins are immune on export revenue (CF art. 149 §2 I). CSLL is still due. | Primary |
| Inside Simples | In the estimate, the ISS, PIS and Cofins slices of the DAS are dropped for export revenue, and IRPJ, CSLL and CPP are kept. The slice percentages come from a third-party site (unconfirmed). A conservative case keeps the full DAS. | Mixed |
| IOF on FX | PGFN Parecer 83/2019, quoting Cosit: foreign-exchange operations for the inflow of export revenue of goods and services bear IOF at a zero rate (Decreto 6306 art. 15-B). A search snippet suggests art. 15-B was re-worded in 2025, and the current text was not verified, so confirm the rule that applies today. Proceeds kept abroad and later remitted: 0.38% per the same opinion. | Primary (2019 opinion) |
| Payment proof | An export needs actual inflow of foreign currency (or funds kept abroad under Lei 11371 art. 1). | Primary |

**What stays due:** income tax, the social contributions inside the rest of the DAS, and INSS. Exempt does not mean untaxed. IOF is the tax on foreign-exchange operations; the zero rate rests on a 2019 opinion, so ask your Wise, Remessa or bank route whether it charges any IOF in practice and which contract documents it wants for export-service inflows.

**Wise Business is the lowest-cost option on the published and proxy numbers, at about 1% all-in:** about R$ 24,503 received on USD 4,500. Two conditions apply. It only works if a Brazilian CNPJ can get USD account details from Wise, and that is not confirmed. And the 1% rests on a proxy, because Wise does not publish a flat USD to BRL percentage for Business. The USD 45.65 and USD 62.91 figures (about 1.0%) come from Wise's public comparison tool for a send quote, collected on 7 November 2026. Wise also notes that IOF may be imposed by its banking partner on BRL transactions.

**If Wise Business turns out not to be open to you, the fallback is Remessa Online** (from 1.3%). **Avoid PayPal** unless a client insists: it lands about 9.7% below the mid-market gross.

Worked example, Wise ACH, USD 4,500: (4,500 − 45.65) × 5.5010 = 4,454.35 × 5.5010 = R$ 24,503.38. Payoneer at 2%: 4,500 × 0.99 = 4,455, then × 0.98 = 4,365.90, then × 5.5010 = R$ 24,016.82.

**What is missing here:**

- Wise publishes no flat USD to BRL percentage for Business, so the 1% is a proxy.
- Payoneer's Brazil withdrawal fee is shown only after sign-in, which was not done. The table uses the published global range of 1.2% to 4%.
- No Brazilian bank's tariff for receiving international transfers, or its spread, was found. The three bank rows are illustrative assumptions, not facts. Ask your bank for the all-in rate and tariffs in writing.

## Monthly take-home in BRL, with the assumptions

- Revenue is the USD amount × the PTAX mid of 5.5010, and the receipt is converted at the same rate.
- The receipt route is Wise ACH with the proxy fee (unconfirmed).
- RBT12 (the prior 12 months of revenue) is taken as 12 × the monthly revenue; a first-year company is annualised differently.
- The 2026 Simples tables, the minimum wage of R$ 1,621.00, the INSS ceiling of R$ 8,475.55 and the 2026 IRPF table are used as proxies for 2027.
- The accountant costs R$ 259, and there are no other business costs.
- Profit distributions are assumed income-tax-exempt (see the accountant questions below).
- The Lei 15270 monthly reduction is applied as read from the Receita page.
- The Annex III and V tables and their slice percentages come from a third-party site (unconfirmed).

**Reading the table.** In case B the export-adjusted take-home is R$ 21,735.82 a month at USD 4,500 and R$ 30,922.20 at USD 6,500, against R$ 17,444.73 at USD 4,500 as an individual (case D). The ME beats the individual route by about R$ 4,291 a month at USD 4,500 and R$ 5,570 at USD 6,500 (export-adjusted case B against case D). Annex III against Annex V is close. In the conservative case Annex III is ahead by R$ 772 at USD 4,500 and R$ 448 at USD 6,500. The export-adjusted figures depend on the third-party slice percentages and on our reading of how the export slice is removed, which is why the conservative column sits beside them. ## Questions to take to your accountant

- Can all your activities (CNAE for UX/UI design, plus any software or consulting) sit in the fator R group, and which CNAE should be the primary one?
- For a company opened in January 2027, who files the Simples opt-in at CNPJ registration, and can the opening date be set so Simples applies from day one?
- How exactly will the 2027 Simples change (CBS replacing PIS/Cofins, IBS, the new fator R window) alter the DAS and the export exclusion? The estimate uses 2026 tables.
- How is the export slice removed in PGDAS-D, and what proof is needed (contract, invoices, FX contract)? How does the national NFS-e emitter record a service export to a US client, and does Florianópolis ISS treat "result abroad" as met for UX work used in your clients' US products?
- Profit distribution: with full bookkeeping, distributions are generally exempt; without it, the exempt amount is limited to a presumed profit. In case B, distributions of roughly R$ 16,299 a month exceed 32% of revenue (about R$ 7,921). Do you need full accounting (contabilidade completa)? Does the 10% dividend withholding in Lei 15270 (above R$ 50,000 a month, per the source) or any minimum tax apply?
- What pro-labore is best, 28% of revenue or the minimum wage, given the effect on INSS, retirement and IRPF, and the Lei 15270 monthly reduction?
- What happens at the R$ 360,000 ME line (crossed in 2027 at USD 6,500), and does the extra export sublimit matter?
- Is IOF zero in practice on your chosen Wise, Remessa or bank route, and which contract documents do they require for export-service inflows (Banco Central FX reporting thresholds)?
- Is the informal period before 2027, with foreign income, a compliance exposure (carnê-leão, ISS) that should be regularised?
- Does the accountant charge extra for export and FX bookkeeping, and what does the monthly fee include?

- Ask your bank for its international-receipt tariff and spread in writing before you compare it with the illustrative rows.
- Check IOF and the contract documents with whichever route you choose, before the first invoice is paid.
