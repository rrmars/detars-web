You are a French nurse (IDE) in Annecy, single, taking home about €2,450 net a month, and a Geneva hospital job from mid-2027 looks far better on paper. You are right to ask what could go wrong before you resign. **Short answer: a conditional go. On the figures that could be sourced, commuting to Geneva leaves you about €2,410 to €2,907 a month better off than staying, but the gain depends on four conditions: start diploma recognition early, get a signed contract before the G permit, get your pay class in writing, and choose your health insurance within 3 months.**

This guide was written on 8 November 2026. It is general information from public pages, not tax, legal or immigration advice. Nothing was submitted and nobody was contacted. The source materials are not published.

## The conditional go: what has to be true

On the numbers that could be sourced, commuting to Geneva looks roughly €2,400–€2,900 a month better than staying: about €4,860–€5,360 a month after health insurance and a car commute, against €2,450 today. The gap is large enough that the decision turns on conditions rather than money. **Read the net figures with care: they use two deduction rates (9% and 10%) that were assumed, not sourced.**

- **Start diploma recognition now.** The Swiss Red Cross (SRC) PreCheck is free and takes up to 4 weeks, then the decision takes up to 4 months after a complete file. For a mid-2027 start, begin between December 2026 and January 2027.
- **Get a signed contract before the G permit.** The G permit is requested for a specific employer, not in advance. The canton quotes about 4 months of processing but says work may start once the complete file is sent.
- **Get four things in writing from the employer:** the function class, the step (credit for your French years), how the 13th month is treated, and what happens to your pay while recognition is pending. The Geneva university hospitals (HUG) say pay is one class lower until a Swiss-recognised diploma is shown.
- **Decide on health insurance within 3 months of starting.** The choice is irrevocable, and if you do nothing you are enrolled in LAMal.
- **Test the commute before you resign.** About 1 h 30 each way by train comes from one unconfirmed source; by car it is 90 km a day on an assumed route. Check that you can accept a Geneva shift schedule.
- **Take a cross-border tax adviser's view before you sign.** The net-pay figures use placeholder deduction rates.

**Do not go if** recognition is refused or needs compensation measures you cannot do, if the contract class is well below class 14, or if the daily commute would make your shifts unworkable. This is a research summary, not advice.

## Permit G and diploma recognition

### The G permit (cross-border worker from the EU or EFTA)

This section follows the official pages of the Geneva population and migration office (OCPM) and the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM).

- **Three conditions:** you live in an EU or EFTA state (Annecy qualifies), your employer is based in Switzerland, and you return home at least one day a week. You do not need to live in Switzerland.
- **How it works:** the application is filed online for the employee, the permit is sent to the employer, and it must be filed before work starts. There is no permit for job hunting.
- **Documents:** a colour copy of your ID, one portrait photo and an employer attestation or contract.
- **Delay:** an average of 12 weeks, and "currently 4 months" (page updated 19 October 2026). Work may start once the complete file is sent; keep the employer's acknowledgement e-mail as proof.
- **Validity:** 5 years if the contract is open-ended or longer than 1 year. Report a change of employer or the end of the job within 14 days. The cap is 50 working hours a week.
- **Permit fee: not found.** The canton's fee table was not read.

### Diploma recognition by the Swiss Red Cross

- **Who decides:** for nurses the body is the Swiss Red Cross (SRC), not MEBEKO. MEBEKO covers university medical professions; Geneva's page lists nurses under the federal NAREG/GesReg register and MEBEKO only for pathology. **That conclusion rests on Geneva's page alone.**
- **Steps:** (1) PreCheck, mandatory, free, up to 4 weeks; (2) the recognition request, with a first decision up to 4 months after a complete file; (3) entry in the federal register.
- **Fee:** CHF 550 if the nursing diploma is listed in Annex V of EU Directive 2005/36/EC (the alternatives are CHF 930 and CHF 1,000), plus a CHF 130 registry fee. That is CHF 680, about €744 at 1.0937. **Whether the French diplôme d'État infirmier is in Annex V was not verified;** the PreCheck will tell you.
- **Papers:** a certificate of good standing less than 3 months old (the issuer for France was not found). The language requirement was not found either.
- **Timeline:** about 1 + 4 months, so about 5 months from a complete file. That is why the advice is to start now.
- **Geneva practice authorisation (CHF 500):** for nurses only in listed cases (self-employed, leadership, working alone, training peers, a billing code). An ordinary staff nurse in a supervised hospital team is probably not in those cases; ask the employer. French mother-tongue speakers need no B2 certificate.

## Health insurance: LAMal or the French system

- **Deadline:** 3 months from the start of Swiss work. If you do nothing, you are enrolled in LAMal automatically. The choice can be made once and is irrevocable; it re-opens only on events such as resuming work after unemployment or moving residence, and a change of employer does not re-open it.
- **Form:** the Geneva "choice of health-insurance system" form must be stamped by the nearest CPAM whichever system you choose. Without the stamp, LAMal becomes final.
- **LAMal cost (2026, France residents, age 26 and over, CHF 300 deductible):** 13 insurers; no-accident premiums range from CHF 200.00 to CHF 823.10 and the median is CHF 489.30, about €535 a month (€6,422 a year). The range is about €219–€900. The table does not state the insurance model.
- **2027 is missing:** no 2027 table for France residents was found. The Swiss-wide 2027 adult average of CHF 487.60 is a different measure.
- **Accident cover:** employees working 8 hours a week or more are covered for non-occupational accidents through the employer's insurer, hence the "no accident" column. Confirm this.
- **French option (URSSAF):** 8% × (income from two years earlier − 25% of the Social Security ceiling), prorated by days in the first year. For 2027 the base is your 2025 income.

## Tax: questions for an adviser

This is general information, not tax advice. Geneva withholds tax at source from the salary. Quasi-resident status lets non-residents request ordinary Geneva taxation, to claim deductions, if at least 90% of worldwide gross income is taxable in Switzerland. The request must be made each year by 31 March of the following year, or the source tax becomes final.

In France, Geneva is not in the 1983 cross-border regime (8 cantons). You would be in "Cas 2A": taxed at source in Switzerland, required to declare the salary in France (form 2047-SUISSE), and given a credit equal to the French tax on that salary. The "3.5% retrocession" to French communes appears only in unconfirmed snippets and does not change your bill.

Questions to put to a cross-border tax adviser:

- What is my total Swiss + French tax at CHF 88,000–95,000 compared with staying at €2,450 net? Please show a worked 2027 example, including the exchange-rate treatment on form 2047.
- Should I request ordinary taxation as a quasi-resident each year, and does it lower my bill after the French credit?
- Which source-tax scale will HUG apply to a single person living in France?
- Does any French income (rental, savings) risk the 90% test?
- Is the credit really equal to the French tax in my case (Cas 2A rather than 2B)?
- What is the employee pension-fund rate, and what happens to my pension capital if I leave Switzerland? What are the AVS and AC lines on my payslip?
- How does the health-insurance choice interact with my French tax return?
