**Moving from Nigeria for a diploma at George Brown and wondering whether CAD 1,300 is enough? Start with a private bedroom in a shared home, not a whole downtown apartment. Aim for CAD 950–1,150 rent near a subway or direct streetcar route; keep CAD 1,300 as your ceiling, not your target. At a CAD 1,100 target rent, the modest monthly living plan below comes to CAD 2,003.15.**

**Protect the money before chasing the room. For an Ontario Residential Tenancies Act (RTA)-covered monthly tenancy, the last-month rent deposit is at most one month's rent, not a damage deposit. At CAD 1,100 rent, first plus last month totals CAD 2,200—not that amount plus another “security” payment. Verify who can rent the room, who receives your payment and whether the RTA covers your arrangement before sending anything.**

## Put the deposit and scam checks first

### Ask: who lives here, and who am I renting from?

A room rented directly from a landlord and shared with other tenants is usually RTA-covered. **Required kitchen or bathroom sharing with the owner or certain close family living in the building can exclude the arrangement.** Paying an existing resident tenant as their roommate can also leave you outside the usual Landlord and Tenant Board (LTB) protection. College residence agreements can have different rules. Do not apply ordinary deposit protections blindly to every advertised “room.” See [RTA section 5](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/06r17) and [LTB tenancy/roommate guidance](https://tribunalsontario.ca/ltb/application-and-hearing-process/).

| Before signing an ordinary RTA-covered tenancy | What to check |
|---|---|
| Standard Lease | Use Ontario's Standard Lease for most ordinary private tenancies. Specify the exact room, term, rent, included utilities and any shared-bill formula; get the signed copy within 21 days. A joint lease can make you responsible for the full rent when another tenant does not pay. |
| Last-month rent deposit | At most one month's rent for monthly rent, or one rental period if shorter. It pays the final rental period, not damage. Annual interest is owed; ask for free receipts. First-period rent is separate from the deposit. |
| Damage, pet or cleaning deposit | Not allowed for an RTA-covered tenancy. A CAD 500 “damage deposit” does not become lawful because someone renames it. |
| Refundable key/fob/card deposit | Only up to the expected direct replacement cost. |
| “International students must pay a year upfront” | Not a special legal deposit rule. Do not offer large prepayments to win a room. A genuinely voluntary advance-payment arrangement is a different legal question; get independent advice first. |

The dated sources are [Ontario's Standard Lease guide, including sections 8–9 and 15](https://www.ontario.ca/page/guide-ontarios-standard-lease), the [LTB rent-deposit guide](https://tribunalsontario.ca/documents/ltb/Brochures/Guide%20to%20RTA%20%28English%29.html) and [Regulation 516/06, section 17](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/060516). The advance-rent case law was not verified; this guide does not claim all genuinely voluntary prepaid rent is illegal.

**No Canadian credit history is not the same as bad credit.** A guarantor should not be demanded merely because you are a newcomer. The [Human Rights Legal Support Centre](https://hrlsc.on.ca/faq/housing/) and [Ontario Human Rights Commission's screening guidance](https://www.ohrc.on.ca/en/policy-human-rights-and-rental-housing/v-identifying-discrimination-rental-housing) explain the distinction. Owner-shared accommodation has human-rights exemptions too; ask for help rather than assuming every arrangement has identical protection.

### Do not let “reserve it today” empty your account

Copied or already-let ads, phantom rooms, fake agents, missing amenities and “I'm abroad—send money and I'll courier the keys” are scam patterns. A familiar accent, a Nigerian community group, a student logo or a polished lease is not proof of authority.

1. **View the real room.** While you are in Nigeria, ask a trusted person in Toronto to inspect it. A live video tour helps screen an offer; it does not prove ownership or authority to rent. If verification fails, use a short temporary stay instead of gambling on a long lease.
2. **Match the person, address and payee.** Search the address, reverse-search photographs and compare duplicate ads. Get the owner or management company's full name and service address. Find the company's official number independently, or speak with building management and current tenants. Verify an agent's identity and written authority through a separate channel. For a sublet, inspect the head lease and landlord's permission. A property record alone does not identify the person messaging you.
3. **Read the complete agreement before payment.** Match the exact room, dates, included bills, occupants and payee. Ask what happens if your visa or travel is delayed. Do not follow an unfamiliar background-check payment or holding-fee link before verifying who is asking.
4. **Limit identity exposure.** Prepare admission/enrolment evidence and proof you can afford the rent, but share only necessary documents after verification. Watermark copies for that application. Do not casually send a SIN, bank-login details or a full identity bundle. Avoid cash, crypto, gift cards or money transfers to a stranger for keys. Even an e-transfer can be hard to recover. Get receipts; save the ad and messages.
5. **Act quickly if money goes missing.** Contact your bank/payment provider, then local police and the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre; ask the college housing team for support. No report or contact has been made for you.

These precautions draw on [University of Toronto housing-scam guidance](https://www.studentlife.utoronto.ca/task/housing-scams-and-how-to-spot-them/), its [Community Safety Office advice](https://www.communitysafety.utoronto.ca/fraud-prevention/types-of-frauds-and-scams/rental-and-landlord-scams/), [Toronto Police fraud guidance](https://www.tps.ca/fraud/) and [George Brown housing support](https://www.georgebrown.ca/current-students/services/housing). The verification sequence is practical advice, not authentication or an endorsement of any listing.

For shared homes, ask for a separate room lease if you do not want responsibility for the whole apartment's rent. Confirm whether the owner or an existing tenant is renting to you. Long-term does not automatically mean a school-year lease. Ask how many people share the kitchen/bathroom, cooking and guest rules, quiet hours, laundry fees, cleaning, pests and who holds the lease.

The ordinary adult monthly pass ended. Since **September 1, 2026**, eligible pay-as-you-go cards cap after **47 charged trips per calendar month**: 47 × CAD 3.30 = **CAD 155.10** at the dated adult fare. ## Think past the first study year before signing

Ontario's published **2027 guideline was 1.9%** for guideline-covered increases taking effect that year. It does not cap a new tenant's starting rent. For a September 2027 move-in, your first ordinary increase would normally be no earlier than **September 2028**, under that year's rules—not the 2027 figure. The 2028 guideline is not supplied here. Ordinary increases generally require **12 months** and **90 days' proper written notice**.

Units first occupied residentially after **November 15, 2018** may be exempt from the amount limit; new units in existing houses have extra conditions. Ask for the exemption basis. Special adjustments, including qualifying seasonal air-conditioning electricity charges under distinct rules from July 2026, need separate checking. See [Ontario's rent-increase rules](https://www.ontario.ca/page/residential-rent-increases).

An ordinary fixed-term RTA lease usually continues month-to-month rather than simply expiring into eviction. If leaving a monthly/fixed-term tenancy, the usual notice is **at least 60 days with the correct termination date**; exceptions and your agreement matter. An eight-month school year does not cancel a twelve-month lease. Read the [LTB continuation and ending-a-tenancy guidance](https://tribunalsontario.ca/documents/ltb/Brochures/Guide%20to%20RTA%20%28English%29.html) and get help before committing to an end date.

## A calm route to move-in

| When | Useful next step |
|---|---|
| Now to early 2027 | Confirm your campus, private versus shared bedroom and total funding. Watch official residence information for 2027–28 fees/dates; do not recycle a 2026 deadline. |
| Spring 2027 | Compare residence terms with a twelve-month private lease and plan summer housing. Assemble a small, safe application pack and identify a trusted Toronto viewer. |
| June–August 2027 | Suggested search window, not an official letting calendar. Refresh listings, compare at least three real all-in offers, and check commute, coverage and lease before payment. |
| On arrival | Photograph the room's condition, test keys and alarms, save receipts, arrange insurance and check student-ID requirements. If no room is verified, use temporary accommodation instead of signing under airport-day pressure. |

A modest private bedroom with shared facilities is the best starting plan for this budget. A cheap dorm bed is a different compromise; residence buys convenience but the published fee already exceeds the rent ceiling. Keep the commute manageable, the bills explicit and the payee verified.
