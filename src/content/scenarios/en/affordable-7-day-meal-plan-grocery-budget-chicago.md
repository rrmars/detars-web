Each day's figures below are **per adult**, including breakfast, lunch, dinner and **both snacks**. Every day includes S1, a cup made with **100 g plain nonfat Greek yogurt + 20 g oats** per person. Snack 2 is **S2: 120 g edible apple + 16 g peanut butter** on Monday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday and Sunday; **S3: 100 g peeled banana + 16 g peanut butter** on Tuesday and Saturday.

Breakfast B1 is **60 g dry oats + 150 g milk + 16 g peanut butter + 100 g peeled banana** per adult. B2 is **2 large eggs (100 g without shells), 60 g bread, 50 g spinach, 5 g oil, 100 g milk, 0.1 g pepper and 0.5 g salt** per adult. ## Sunday prep: freeze the late-week components now

**Prep for the week, but do not refrigerate cooked rice and lentils for the whole week.** Freeze the late-week portions promptly. Without that step, the storage schedule below is not safe to rely on.

Allow roughly **100–120 minutes elapsed, 50–60 minutes hands-on**, using parallel cooking and enough pan/container capacity. These are planning estimates, not measured kitchen results. Cook **1,200 g dry brown rice** and **820 g dry lentils** separately following package water/cooking directions. Weigh the resulting whole cooked rice batch as **W** and the drained cooked lentils as **L**. Do not assume either becomes exactly three times its dry weight.

Make only the next **2–3 days** of yogurt-oat cups at once.

Keep the two **600 g raw chicken** allocations frozen until their thaw schedule; about 161 g remains frozen for another week. Move Tuesday's allocation into the refrigerator **Sunday night**, Friday's **Wednesday night**. Allow 1–2 days or longer if needed; cook within 1–2 days after thawing. If separating a frozen block, thaw in the refrigerator just enough to separate, then promptly refreeze the later portion. Never thaw on the counter. The USDA's [chicken handling guidance](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/poultry/chicken-farm-table), checked November 5, supports refrigerator thawing and refreezing chicken thawed that way; it does not guarantee this bag thaws to your timetable.

Night-before moves into the refrigerator: **Tuesday** D3 rice; **Wednesday** D4 lentils and Friday chicken; **Friday** D6 rice/lentils; **Saturday** D7 lentils. At each Monday–Saturday dinner, promptly chill the two next-day lunch portions and keep them cold in transport.

**Food safety is part of the plan:** use shallow containers; refrigerate/freeze perishables within **2 hours**, or **1 hour above 90°F**; keep the refrigerator at **40°F or colder**. Refrigerated cooked leftovers are a **3–4-day maximum**, not permission to leave Sunday rice until Saturday. Cook chicken to **165°F**; reheat leftovers throughout to **165°F**, and bring reheated soups/sauces to a rolling boil. Do not leave a large pot cooling on the counter for hours. For the guidance checked November 5, see [USDA FSIS Leftovers and Food Safety](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety); the page itself carries a July 31, 2020 update date. Check your own fridge and food handling; your kitchen has not been inspected here.

## Use the nutrition estimates as a starting point

The workbook gives approximately **1,920–2,150 kcal and 84–117 g protein per adult daily**, averaging **2,010 kcal and 100 g protein**. To check the sums, multiply edible grams by the USDA per-100-g value, divide by 100, then add by portion and day. All measured oil is counted as eaten. Keep dry grains/lentils and raw chicken on those weight bases: cooking water adds weight, not ingredient calories. Daily figures round to the nearest 10 kcal and 1 g protein; recipes to one decimal.

The figures use [USDA FoodData Central's SR Legacy download](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/download-datasets/), obtained November 5, 2026. Its generic entries are **not the exact Great Value/Eggland's/Wesson labels**. For example, its frozen broccoli spears stand in for the florets on the list. Use your actual food labels when adapting the ingredients, then recalculate nutrition per portion. Protein and calories alone do not tell you whether fiber, micronutrients, sodium or energy suit you. Extras and drinks are excluded. Adjust portions to your needs, recalculate the budget and seek personal guidance where needed; this is not a weight-loss prescription.

## Questions you may have before starting

Before shopping, use these **planning questions** to check the price, portions and changes you need.

**Can two adults really eat for $90 for the week in Chicago?** The $80.90 full-package example uses public Sacramento offers, not a Chicago checkout. You need the fixed portions, no discretionary extras and freezer space. Check local prices, stock and usable produce weights first: differences and fees could use up the $9.10 left over.

**Does four portions at dinner mean four people are covered?** No. Monday–Saturday's four portions cover **two adults at dinner and the same two at next-day lunch**. Sunday's two-portion soup ends the plan. Adding two dinner guests without changing purchases would remove the planned lunches.

**Can I change the shopping quantities and keep the nutrition numbers?** Only if the food actually eaten stays unchanged. The workbook changes grocery cash totals, not the recipe scale. Changing foods or portions requires recalculating demand and nutrition; simply reducing the amount bought can make the basket insufficient.
