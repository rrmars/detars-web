If your mother can no longer live safely alone, you do not have to solve the house sale before finding care. **Start urgent care and financial assessments in parallel, keep the home for now, and get specialist aged-care financial advice before selling or paying a large refundable accommodation deposit (RAD).** Daily accommodation payments (DAP) can buy time, but they are a costly bridge—not a promise that keeping the house indefinitely is best.

This example follows Daniel and his 82-year-old widowed mother, an Australian citizen receiving a full pension, with a home worth approximately $1,300,000 and savings of $180,000. She speaks Mandarin. The family wants a suitable place near Glen Waverley or Box Hill by **February 7, 2027**. That is a planning target, not a guaranteed placement date. All money below is AUD.

**Safety cannot wait for that target.** With her consent, work with her GP, hospital discharge team and family on a safe interim plan now. Ask about urgent assessment, residential respite and short-term support; do not leave her alone in an unsafe situation while waiting. Involve her in decisions as far as possible, arrange Mandarin interpreting when wanted, and check who has legal authority rather than assuming an adult child can sign everything.

Start with Uniting AgeWell Box Hill for its advertised English/Mandarin services and Campbell Place for Glen Waverley proximity. These are candidates, not endorsements. **Confirm care suitability, current fees, vacancies and actual Mandarin-speaking staff coverage with each home.** Translated brochures do not establish bilingual nursing on every shift.

## A practical route to a suitable place

The [My Aged Care assessment entry point](https://www.myagedcare.gov.au/how-apply-assessment) is separate from the financial means assessment. Its usual assessment range is **5–40 days depending on urgency**, not a local appointment or bed guarantee. The application itself takes approximately 15–20 minutes; assessment and finding a room take longer.

| Planning window | What your family can do | If it does not go to plan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| November 7, 2026 to November 9, 2026 | Help her call My Aged Care on **1800 200 422**; explain the fall and why living alone is unsafe. Ask for urgent residential-care assessment and respite consideration. | A dated website notice says online applications/referrals are unavailable from November 6, 2026 at 5:30pm until November 9, 2026 at 9am AEDT. Phone hours are Monday–Friday 8am–8pm and Saturday 10am–2pm. Do not wait for an online form to arrange safety with the treating team. |
| November 9, 2026 to November 20, 2026 | Prepare care and financial information in parallel. Ask to register Daniel as a supporter and request an interpreter. | Supporter status is not financial decision-making authority. Check existing powers of attorney and nominee arrangements. |
| November–December 2026 | Seek the care decision, support plan, residential/respite approval and referral details. Start the home shortlist and financial assessment. | Follow up with the assessment organisation; report deterioration promptly. Approval does not allocate a particular room. |
| December 2026–January 2027 | Compare written offers, actual room categories, language coverage and all extras. Arrange visits and multiple suitable waiting lists. | Keep a safe interim/respite alternative. January–February 2027 agreements may use a different MPIR; do not reuse this guide's rate blindly. |
| By February 7, 2027 | If a suitable place is ready, transfer medicines, care plan, falls information and communication preferences; check the first invoice. | A delayed vacancy means revising the care plan, not forcing an unsuitable placement or distressed sale. |

The [Single Assessment System](https://www.health.gov.au/our-work/single-assessment-system) brought together the former RAS/ACAT and independent AN-ACC assessment workforces from **December 9, 2024**. My Aged Care remains the entry point. Read its [assessment preparation guidance](https://www.myagedcare.gov.au/assessment/prepare-your-assessment) and [outcome guidance](https://www.myagedcare.gov.au/receiving-your-assessment-outcome) with your mother.

Have Medicare and another ID, doctor referrals, GP/health contacts and existing support details ready. For a useful care conversation, also organise medicines, discharge information, falls history and what help she needs with walking, bathing, toileting, meals, medication and overnight safety. Add her wishes, Chinese meals and activities she values, current support and family availability. These organising suggestions are not a claim that every item is a compulsory application attachment.

For the financial track, gather pension details, balances, property ownership/value/debt, other assets and contents, income, gifts, rental arrangements and any protected occupant. Include prior care approvals/contributions and existing authority documents. As a pensioner who owns her home, she normally needs **SA485** or the current Services Australia process; **SA457** is the route described for people without means-tested income support. [Pre-entry fee advice](https://www.myagedcare.gov.au/means-assessments-residential-aged-care) is valid for **120 days**. Disclose assets and changes accurately: non-disclosure can lead to maximum contributions. Use authorised official channels for personal information.

## Understand the fees before comparing deposits

**First confirm which regime applies.** This example assumes a first permanent entrant under the **November 1, 2025** care-fee arrangements, with no grandfathering or prior cap credits. A person approved for or receiving a Home Care Package on **September 12, 2024** may retain the earlier care-fee regime unless opting in. Prior residential history, opt-in decisions and breaks matter; care-fee and accommodation rules can differ. New retention/indexation can still apply. Do not stack the old means-tested care fee on top of hotelling and NCCC. See the [department's arrangements guidance](https://www.health.gov.au/our-work/residential-aged-care/charging/fee-and-accommodation-arrangements).

The fee-rate basis is **September 20, 2026**, read on **November 7, 2026**. These are dated rules and examples, not a future room quote. Recalculate for entry or agreement in 2027 using the [fee schedule](https://www.health.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-09/schedule_of_fees_and_charges_for_residential_care_20_september_2026.pdf).

| Charge | Dated rate or rule | What to budget/check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic daily fee | Maximum **$68.93/day**, based on 85% of the maximum single basic Age Pension, not the total including supplements | Food, cleaning and laundry. A full pension does not mean free care. |
| Hotelling contribution | Means-tested, up to **$22.15/day**; no annual or lifetime cap | Additional everyday-living contribution; the keep-home model reaches this maximum. |
| Non-clinical care contribution (NCCC) | Means-tested, up to **$109.45/day**; current indexed lifetime cap **$140,652.80**, or four years of NCCC payments, whichever comes first | Personal support such as bathing/mobility. Eligible earlier home-care contributions can count toward the dollar cap, not the four-year residential clock. Basic, hotelling and accommodation costs continue after the cap. |
| Accommodation | RAD, non-refundable DAP or a combination | This example is not low-means: it pays an agreed room price. Low-means residents may instead pay RAC/DAC with government assistance. A full RAD removes DAP once received, not other fees. |
| Optional services | Agreed higher everyday living fees | Get itemised inclusions, price and cancellation terms. No mandatory extras package is assumed, and none is in the budget. |

The [basic daily fee guidance](https://www.health.gov.au/our-work/residential-aged-care/charging/basic-daily-fee) explains its pension basis. The [means-tested fee guidance](https://www.health.gov.au/our-work/residential-aged-care/charging/means-tested-fees) explains caps: the monetary cap is the indexed amount when reached, not necessarily the entry-day amount. Clinical care is government-funded; the department's [care-contribution explanation](https://www.health.gov.au/resources/publications/guide-to-aged-care-law/chapter-4-funding-aged-care-services/contributions-in-residential-care) is useful general guidance, not a complete legal interpretation.

### A RAD is no longer completely refundable

For this new-entry cohort, [RAD retention](https://www.health.gov.au/our-work/residential-aged-care/charging/rad-and-rac-retention) is **2% per year on the daily remaining balance**, for at most five years from first deposit payment. It is non-refundable, stops after permanent exit, and is deducted no more often than monthly and at least every three months. Retention alone cannot trigger a forced top-up or extra DAP. Other agreed fee drawdowns are different: they can reduce the refund and increase DAP.

For a room-price agreement from **October 1, 2026 to December 31, 2026**, MPIR is **8.51%**, not the previous quarter's 8.43%. Initial DAP = `(room price − RAD paid) × 0.0851 ÷ 365`. The agreement's MPIR is not reset every quarter. Applicable [DAP indexation](https://www.health.gov.au/our-work/residential-aged-care/charging/dap-indexation?language=en) occurs on **March 20 and September 20**; future indices are unknown. The published index values are 1.00 for September 20, 2025, 1.02 for March 20, 2026 and 1.04 for September 20, 2026. Do not multiply a newly calculated November initial DAP by the existing September index again. DAC is not indexed this way.

You do not have to choose the payment method before moving in or pay the full RAD upfront. Daily charges apply until the lump sum arrives. A provider cannot charge above the published room maximum, though a lower price may be negotiated. Read the [accommodation-cost guidance](https://www.myagedcare.gov.au/understanding-aged-care-home-accommodation-costs) before signing.

### The house has different rules in two means tests

- **Aged-care fees:** a retained home counts at net value or **$219,858.40**, whichever is lower, unless a protected person occupies it. This can be a partner/dependent child, an eligible income-support carer resident for two years, or an eligible income-support close relative resident for five years. Being her son does not automatically protect Daniel. RAD remains an aged-care asset even if family pays it; it is not deemed for aged-care income. See the [department's means-assessment guidance](https://www.health.gov.au/our-work/residential-aged-care/charging/means-assessment).
- **Age Pension:** RAD is exempt from the pension means test. The retained former home normally has a two-year care-related exemption, then full value can enter the assets test with non-homeowner treatment. This is not the aged-care home cap. Rent can affect pension and care fees immediately. Read [Services Australia's real-estate guidance](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/real-estate-assets?context=22526) alongside the [DSS residential-care detail](https://guides.dss.gov.au/social-security-guide/4/6/3/70). Her precise exemption dates still need Services Australia confirmation; do not treat them as settled here.

## Compare three payment choices on the same assumptions

The **$600,000 room is illustrative, not a quote for a shortlisted single room**. Assume sole debt-free ownership, no protected occupant, other assets/income, prior cap credits or grandfathering, and a vacant home if retained. Declare actual contents and other assets. A sale at $1,300,000 excludes transaction costs and tax. Savings start at $180,000. Each budgeting month is **30 days**, not an average calendar month or a guaranteed invoice.

### Why keeping the home gives a different care-fee result

Retained aged-care assets: `$219,858.40 + $180,000 = $399,858.40`. Paying a $100,000 partial RAD leaves $80,000 cash but still counts the RAD, so total assessed assets do not fall.

The [department's fee scenarios](https://www.health.gov.au/our-work/residential-aged-care/charging/fees/scenarios2025?language=en) give the asset calculation:

`[17.5% × ($219,858.40 − $66,500) + 7.8% × ($369,366.66 − $266,000)] ÷ 364 = $95.88/day`.

Assets between $369,366.66 and $552,858.40 have a zero taper. The model uses the schedule's current MPIR rather than the older example's rate.

[Deeming](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/deeming?context=22526) uses a single-person threshold of $66,800 effective July 1, 2026, with rates of 1.75% and 3.75% effective September 20, 2026. For $180,000: `$66,800 × 1.75% + $113,200 × 3.75% = $5,414/year`. Cash of $80,000 deems to $1,664; $880,000 deems to $31,664. Deeming is not actual cash earnings; do not add actual interest to deemed income for the same asset.

The normal [single Age Pension](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/how-much-age-pension-you-can-get?context=22526) is $1,135.40 basic + $88.20 pension supplement + $14.10 energy supplement = **$1,237.70/fortnight**. The [minimum pension supplement](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/how-much-pension-supplement-you-can-get?context=22321) is $47.50.

**The pension-income exclusion remains conditional.** [Official DVA guidance](https://clik.dva.gov.au/compensation-and-support-reference-library/aged-care-means-testing/4-aged-care-means-testing/43-what-total-assessable-income), amended February 14, 2025, excludes the minimum pension supplement and energy supplement. Services Australia still needs to confirm this exclusion for your mother. Until then, do not treat the resulting $0 NCCC as final fee advice.

With those exclusions, `($1,237.70 − $47.50 − $14.10) × 26 = $30,578.60/year`. Add deeming to get $35,992.60 with all DAP or $32,242.60 with partial RAD, both below the $36,454.60 income-free area. The income-tested amount is $0. Daily means amount M is **income-tested plus asset-tested**, not whichever is larger:

`Hotelling = min($22.15, max(0, M − $73.73))`

`NCCC = min($109.45, max(0, M − $73.73 − $22.15))`

This gives hotelling $22.15 and provisional NCCC $0. The $73.73 is an accommodation-supplement formula threshold, not another bill. If the whole pension is counted instead, annual pension is $32,180.20, all-DAP income is $37,594.20 and NCCC rises by approximately **$1.57/day, or $47.10 per 30 days**. That sensitivity is not a ruling that the exclusions are wrong. Get Services Australia's actual calculation before committing.

After selling and paying full RAD: `$1,300,000 + $180,000 = $1,480,000`; cash becomes `$1,480,000 − $600,000 = $880,000`. Aged-care assets still total **$1,480,000**, including RAD. The asset-tested amount alone is `[$26,837.72 + $8,062.59948 + 7.8% × ($1,480,000 − $552,858.40)] ÷ 364 = $294.55/day`. It exceeds the $205.33 threshold for maximum hotelling/NCCC, regardless of the conditional pension exclusion.

### Cash bills and loss of refundable capital are different

| Initial cost or balance | Keep home, all DAP | Keep home, $100,000 RAD + DAP | Sell home, full $600,000 RAD |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Upfront RAD capital, not a fee | $0 | $100,000 | $600,000 |
| Cash before bills and sale costs | $180,000 | $80,000 | $880,000 |
| Basic fee × 30 | $2,067.90 | $2,067.90 | $2,067.90 |
| Hotelling $22.15 × 30 | $664.50 | $664.50 | $664.50 |
| NCCC × 30 | $0 provisional | $0 provisional | $3,283.50 |
| Initial DAP/day | $600,000 × 8.51% ÷ 365 = **$139.89** | $500,000 × 8.51% ÷ 365 = **$116.58** | $0 after RAD receipt |
| DAP × 30 | $4,196.70 | $3,497.40 | $0 |
| **Provider cash bill / 30 days** | **$6,929.10** | **$6,229.80** | **$6,015.90** |
| First 30-day retention, unchanged balance until deduction | $0 | ($100,000 × 2% ÷ 365 → $5.48/day) × 30 = **$164.40** | ($600,000 × 2% ÷ 365 → $32.88/day) × 30 = **$986.40** |
| **Cash fees + lost refundable principal, initial 30 days** | **$6,929.10** | **$6,394.20** | **$7,002.30** |

Daily accommodation/retention amounts are rounded to cents before multiplying by days. Means-test annual amounts divide by 364; accommodation/retention divide by 365. Later retention falls as the balance is deducted. This first period is not a fixed monthly charge or exact annual forecast, and permitted deduction timing can differ. No RAD top-up is assumed.

At the stated full pension, a 30-day accrued benchmark is `$1,237.70 ÷ 14 × 30 = $2,652.21`; actual bank deposits depend on pay dates. Cash-fee shortfalls are therefore **$4,276.89** with all DAP and **$3,577.59** with partial RAD, before house costs and personal spending. Actual investment cash earnings are excluded. Retention reduces RAD separately. Pension alone does not cover this bridge.

**Do not carry the full pension into the sale budget.** Residual cash of $880,000 exceeds the [single non-homeowner full-pension asset limit](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/assets-test-for-age-pension?context=22526) of $600,000, though it is below the $1,012,750 part-pension cut-off. Income testing also applies. Budget conservatively for the full **$6,015.90 per 30 days** before any recalculated pension or investment cash income; no exact post-sale pension is asserted.

Every option excludes extras, medicines/personal spending, home rates/insurance/maintenance, selling/letting expenses, tax, loan costs, interim DAP before RAD receipt, future indexation and changes in wealth or cap status. Pension rates are reviewed on March 20 and September 20; pension asset limits in March, July and September. Deeming thresholds/rates have their own effective dates. Do not assume all figures rise on the same schedule.

## Keep, rent or sell: choose after care is secure

| Choice | What it helps with | Cost or trigger to review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keep vacant temporarily | Preserves choice and capped aged-care home assessment; avoids a distressed sale | DAP cash burn, property costs/security and the two-year pension exemption need review. This is the preferred bridge, not a permanent verdict. |
| Rent | Can help fund fees while retaining the property and aged-care home cap | Net rent can reduce pension and raise contributions. Model vacancy, repairs, management, insurance, tax and family workload. Obtain an appraisal; no rent or return is assumed here. |
| Sell and pay RAD | Releases liquidity, removes property management and reduces/eliminates DAP | Loss of capped-home treatment can increase care contributions; cash can reduce pension and retention erodes RAD. Include sale costs/tax and estate preferences. A RAD is not compulsory. |

**Counterintuitive finding:** a RAD can help pension treatment without reducing aged-care assessed wealth. In this example selling lowers the provider cash bill but increases care contributions and adds retention loss. The smallest DAP is not necessarily the lowest total cost.

Before selling, renting, borrowing or paying a large RAD, ask an **appropriately licensed specialist aged-care financial adviser** to compare the actual offer, housing choices, pension reassessment, liquidity reserve, cap timing and future indexation. Ask a tax adviser about sale/rental tax and a solicitor about capacity, authority, contracts and family loans. Services Australia's free Financial Information Service explains rules; it is not a substitute for personalised strategy/product advice.

Use the [Star Ratings explanation](https://www.health.gov.au/our-work/star-ratings-for-residential-aged-care/how-star-ratings-works) to read components and update dates: residents' experience 33%, compliance 30%, staffing 22%, quality measures 15%. A rating screens candidates; it does not promise individual care. These are government ratings, not new consumer-review scores.

For the final two or three homes, ask about falls prevention, transfers/hoists, overnight help, registered-nurse access, GP arrangements, dementia support if needed, hospital transfers and escalating care. Ask how they will use her care and medicine plans. Discuss Chinese meals, festivals, activities and family visits—not just brochure language. Ask how professional interpreters work for consent and medication discussions.

Compare the same room product: single/shared, bathroom, floor/size, maximum RAD, initial DAP, agreement-date MPIR, indexation, retention and all extras in writing. Ask what each waiting list covers, required documents, offer expiry and whether respite can become permanent care. Join multiple suitable lists when ready; a published price does not mean a vacancy.

## What still needs your family's answer

The budget stays conditional until you know her **fee-regime protection, care approval, ownership/debt/protected-occupant facts, Services Australia fee and pension calculation, and a suitable written room offer**. Confirm the pension exclusions and exact home-exemption dates; do not treat the provisional NCCC as settled. Confirm current fees, vacancy and shift-level Mandarin with every home. No home or agency has been contacted and no application/enquiry has been submitted for this guide; the next steps are for your family to authorise and take.

Your most useful next move is a safe interim care plan plus both assessments—not a sale listing. Keep the property decision reversible until the actual care and financial picture is clear.
