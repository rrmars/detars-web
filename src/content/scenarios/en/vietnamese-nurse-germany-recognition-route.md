You have a 4-year bachelor’s degree, 3 years on a ward and German at A2. You want to know which route is real, what an agent may ask you to pay, and whether the money works once you arrive. **Short answer: apply through an employer-pays route, put your effort into German, and treat any request for money before a signed German employment contract as a warning sign.**

This guide was written on 8 November 2026 for a 27-year-old registered nurse in Da Nang. It is general information from public pages, not legal or immigration advice. Nothing was applied for and nobody was contacted. The source materials are not published.

## Don’t plan around Triple Win; use the employer-pays route

**Do not plan around Triple Win.** The Federal Employment Agency (BA) lists only India (Kerala, Telangana), Indonesia, the Philippines and Tunisia as current Triple Win partner countries. Vietnam is not on that list. The Vietnam strand that turned up was a 2021 trainee (Ausbildung) programme, not recruitment of qualified nurses. If an agency tells you it is “Triple Win” or “BA-official” for Vietnam, treat that as a red flag until the BA confirms it. No 2026 BA or GIZ programme for qualified Vietnamese nurses was found. That is not proof that none exists, so ask the BA (see the next steps below).

**The best-fit route is employer-pays.** Apply to German hospitals and care employers directly, or through an agency that the employer pays and that carries the state seal “Faire Anwerbung Pflege Deutschland”. The seal’s operator says the employer, not the nurse, carries the costs; that comes from the operator’s own site and is not confirmed elsewhere. Vietnam is not on the WHO “safeguards” list, so private recruitment is not barred. German law caps what any agent may charge you at **€2,000 including VAT, with no advance payment**. An agency with the seal should charge you nothing.

**Language is the real bottleneck.** Aim for a B2 certificate (Goethe, telc or ÖSD) before you finish. German authorities ask for B2 to license a nurse (the example found is Bavaria). The visa laws only say “hinreichend” (sufficient) German and do not define it. Going from A2 in November 2026 to B2 by autumn 2027 is possible but tight; starting work in early 2028 is the safer plan. These dates are planning assumptions, not official timelines.

**Money outlook (an estimate):** about €10,600 (about VND 298 million) left after year-one costs in Stuttgart and about €11,900 (about VND 333 million) in Leipzig, if you work 6 months as a care assistant (Pflegehelfer, pay group P5) and then 6 months as a recognised nurse (P7). The detail and the assumptions are in the budget section below.

**Which list is the “WHO list”?** Germany’s rule (BeschV §38) lets only the BA recruit health and care staff from the states in the list annexed to that regulation. The research found Vietnam is not among the 55 states of the WHO 2023 list. The regulation’s annex heading was not seen directly, so linking the two lists is an inference and **unconfirmed**. A 2026 WHO update was only provisional, and the final list is not confirmed.

## Recognition (Anerkennung) of your Vietnamese nursing degree

Nursing is a regulated profession. You need recognition and a licence to practise (Berufsausübungserlaubnis) to work as a nurse (Pflegefachfrau). Until then you can only work in lower roles.

The authority compares your training with the German one. Under §40 PflBG, training is equivalent if there are no “substantial differences”. **No official statement was found saying whether a Vietnamese Bachelor of Nursing is or is not equivalent.** The Bavarian sheet for Vietnam lists the “Bachelor of Nursing Science (BSN)” as a qualification you can submit, so it can be applied with. The outcome is decided case by case.

The steps on the Recognition Portal: advice, then an application with documents, then the authority confirms receipt within 1 month, then a decision “within 3 to 4 months at the latest” once the documents are complete. Bavaria’s nursing office (LfP) charges €40–70 plus €40 for the certificate, with translations extra. Other states differ; Bavaria is only an example.

You may be able to apply from Vietnam; Bavaria asks for a power of attorney and a cost declaration. You can also waive the in-depth equivalence check and go straight to the knowledge test (§40(3a) PflBG). That can shorten processing, but it means you must pass the test.

Financial help (a recognition grant of up to €600 and up to €3,000 for training) generally needs you to live in Germany and to be approved before you apply.

### Recognition notice or deficit notice

| | Full recognition notice | Deficit notice (partial, not yet equivalent) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meaning | Training is equivalent. The licence follows once the other conditions (language, health, conduct) are met. | “Substantial differences” found. You prove equivalent knowledge by a knowledge test (Kenntnisprüfung) or an adaptation course (Anpassungslehrgang, up to 3 years). You choose. |
| Visa (embassy pages) | Skilled-worker visa, about 3 months processing (about 3 weeks in the employer’s accelerated procedure) | Visa for a qualification measure (§16d(1)), about 4–6 weeks processing; by statute up to 24 months, extendable to 3 years; work limited to 20 h/week unless a separate permission applies |
| Job on arrival | Nurse (for example pay group P7 at a municipal employer) | Usually assistant work (for example Pflegehelfer) until you pass; one German hospital states P5 before and P7 or P8 after recognition (single employer page) |
| Time and cost risk | Lowest | Extra exam or course time; test-preparation costs were not found |

**A third path is the recognition partnership (§16d(3) AufenthG).** You arrive on a job offer before recognition, and you and the employer agree that you start recognition immediately after arrival and that the employer lets you attend the required training. The statute requires “sufficient” German, a recognised degree or a vocational qualification of at least 2 years, and the Federal Employment Agency’s approval. For approved care facilities the job need not be “qualified employment” if the grade and pay lead toward the target profession. The embassy page gives about 3 months processing, through VFS only. The permit is for 1 year, extendable to 3.

**On “§18a”:** §18a AufenthG is the short rule for skilled workers with vocational training, and §18b is the equivalent for those with an academic qualification. Both texts are one sentence (“residence permit for any qualified employment”). §18(2) requires a job offer, BA consent where needed, the licence to practise where required, and equivalence where required. The Hanoi embassy page for nurses is headed “skilled worker with vocational training” and notes that some foreign degrees are treated as vocational training. Which paragraph applies to you is the authorities’ decision.

## The visa route in plain steps

- Start with a job offer or contract from a German employer. Both the skilled-worker permit and the §16d(3) permit need one.
- The employer may start the accelerated skilled-worker procedure (§81a AufenthG) at the local immigration office on your behalf. The embassy then needs about 3 weeks after your application. **The §81a fee was not found**; a figure of €411 is quoted online and is unconfirmed.
- Apply through the German mission’s online portal and the VFS centre (biometrics, originals). The fee is €75, payable in VND; the VFS service fee is not stated on the page. **Which mission covers Da Nang was not confirmed from an official page**; a secondary site implies the consulate in Ho Chi Minh City.
- The visa pages say a national visa takes “several months”; the embassy gives about 3 months for the skilled-worker and partnership routes.
- Age 45 or over needs a pension or salary proof. That is not your case.

## Warning signs, each tied to a source above

- Any payment requested before a signed German employment contract, or any “advance” (§296(2) SGB III; Vietnam Art. 23(2)).
- A total placement fee above €2,000 including VAT (§296(3)).
- No written placement contract, or no written information in your language about employer, pay including deductions, hours, leave and notice before you sign (§296, §299).
- “Brokerage” money, a deposit paid to the agency instead of a blocked bank account, or a fee above the Vietnamese cap (Law 69/2020 Art. 7, 23, 25).
- No Vietnamese licence for the company that sends you. Check with DOLAB; the list was not found.
- Claims of being “BA/Triple Win official” for Vietnamese nurses. The BA lists no such partner.

**Other things people warn about**, such as salary deductions, training-cost repayment clauses and passport retention, are not backed by a source that was read, so they are not listed as established facts. Still read every clause before you sign.

### Deductions (an estimate, not a payslip)

Employee share of social insurance in 2026: pension 18.6% total, so 9.3%; unemployment 2.6%, so 1.3%; health 14.6% general plus an average supplement of 2.9%, split equally, so 7.3% + 1.45% = 8.75% (your insurer’s real supplement may differ); long-term care 3.6% base, with childless people over 23 paying 4.2% in total. In most states the employee pays 2.4%; in Saxony (Leipzig) 2.9%. The total is about 21.75% in Stuttgart and 22.25% in Leipzig. The rates come from BA, BMG and Federal Government pages from an earlier stage of the research; the 50/50 split and the childless-over-23 conditions should be checked for yourself.

Income tax (tax class I, no children, no church tax) is computed with the official 2026 formula in §32a EStG (allowance €12,348) on an approximate taxable income of gross − €1,230 work-expense lump sum − €36 − social contributions. **The €1,230 and €36 lump sums are unconfirmed.** Real payroll tax depends on tax class, insurance details and more; the official BMF calculator would give the exact figure but was not used.

The occupational pension (VBL) employee share of 1.81% is deducted for illustration (a secondary figure). The 2027 tariff, 2027 contribution rates and the tax tariff will differ. The annual special payment in November (90% of monthly pay in P5–P8, secondary) is not included; it would be an upside.

## What is still open

- **Tariff source:** the salary rests on two agreeing secondary sites; the primary VKA tariff PDF was not re-opened.
- **Blocked or missing official pages:** Make it in Germany (blocked by a bot wall, not bypassed), the Auswärtiges Amt consular-district page, thuvienphapluat.vn and the DOLAB site could not be read. The official Da Nang consular district, the VFS fee, the §81a fee, Decree 112/2021, the DOLAB licence list and the telc/ÖSD acceptance list were not obtained.
- **Degree equivalence:** no official statement was found on whether a Vietnamese Bachelor of Nursing is equivalent. The timeline and B2 by autumn 2027 are assumptions.
- **Vietnamese fee-cap law:** Law 69/2020 was read only on a mirror site. Whether Vietnam’s cap applies to a German agency is not determined.
- **Estimates:** the tax lump sums, several living-cost and one-off lines, the broadcast fee and the Deutschlandticket are unconfirmed or assumptions. Real payroll tax may differ by tens of euros a year, and the 2027 tariff will differ from the table above.
- **Programmes:** the Triple Win finding is worded as “found”, not as proof of absence. Prices change, and listings are examples, not availability.
