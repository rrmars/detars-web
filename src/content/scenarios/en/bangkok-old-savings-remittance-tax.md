A condo down payment is already a big commitment. You do not want an avoidable tax surprise on top. **Prefer a transfer from genuinely traceable pre-2024 Singapore earnings.** If you can establish that history, the transfer itself adds **THB 0** to Thai income tax under the rules considered here. That does not mean your salary is tax-free, or that writing “old savings” on a transfer proves its origin.

Meet Pim, a **hypothetical** Thai national, 36, living and earning in Bangkok. She plans a THB 1,500,000 condo down payment in 2027, has SGD 60,000 described as savings from Singapore work in 2019–2023, and a USD 40,000 US brokerage portfolio. For 2026 she assumes USD 900 of gross dividends and USD 3,000 of realized gains. None of her bank, broker, payroll or residence records has been supplied or authenticated. The approximate amounts are treated as exact only for the illustrations.

**Research snapshot: November 8, 2026. All 2027 results below apply the rules considered at that date, not a guaranteed future assessment.** Before sending money, obtain the December 31, 2023 account statement and every later movement. Establish how much old money remains after withdrawals and later interest. If that trail is missing, pause the tax-free characterization, not necessarily the condo plan.

## Check what the money actually is

Thai residence is generally **180 days or more** in Thailand during a calendar year, cumulatively—not a passport test. For the foreign income considered here, residence in the **year the income arose** matters. Check Pim's actual 2026 days. Income earned in a nonresident year is not caught merely because it is remitted later; leaving Thailand in the transfer year does not erase exposure on income earned in a resident year.

P.161/2566, dated September 15, 2023, applies the remittance interpretation from January 1, 2024. P.162/2566, dated November 20, 2023, excludes income arising **before January 1, 2024**. These interpret section 41; there is no separate flat tax merely for transferring money.

| Money bucket | What happens on a 2027 transfer | What to establish |
|---|---|---|
| Singapore earnings from 2019–2023 | Outside this new remittance charge if genuinely traced | Original earnings, opening balance and all later movements |
| 2026 foreign dividends | Remitted taxable portion enters 2027 income if earned in a Thai-resident year | Issuer, gross dividend, income year and remitted amount |
| 2026 realized share gains | Relevant gains enter 2027 income on remittance under those conditions | Sale proceeds less acquisition cost, transaction by transaction |
| Unsold appreciation or returned investment principal | Not itself a realized gain | Distinguish principal, actual gains and later returns |
| Income kept offshore | No remittance charge yet on that portion under this framework | Later remittance may trigger tax; treaty relief has its own timing |

The USD 40,000 portfolio value is not income. Old purchase money also does not make a gain realized in 2026 into old income. Do not assume a US broker's net profit after loss offsets equals the Thai-assessable gain. Routing US returns through Singapore does not change their source or nature.

**No enacted general exemption or effective date for the reported same-year/next-year remittance proposal was verified as of November 8, 2026.** The inspected official registers do not prove Gazette-wide absence. Keep it as an unconfirmed proposal and recheck before transfer. Do not confuse it with the US treaty's timing clause below.

Special regimes do not solve the example by default. Decree 743's foreign-national LTR relief does not apply to a Thai national. Decree 793's returning-Thai talent provisions concern qualifying employment, with employer, education, experience and return/work-date conditions still unknown here. No special salary rate or portfolio exemption is assumed.

## See the tax before choosing the transfer

Assume 2026 Thai residence, unchanged ordinary 2027 salary of **THB 95,000 × 12 = THB 1,140,000**, only the **THB 100,000** salary expense deduction and **THB 60,000** personal allowance, and no quantified other deductions. Social security and other reliefs are not assumed unavailable; their amounts simply remain unknown. No foreign tax credit or Thai payroll withholding is deducted.

Salary-only taxable net is THB 1,140,000 − THB 100,000 − THB 60,000 = **THB 980,000**. Annual salary-only personal income tax (PIT) is **THB 111,000**.

**Planning FX, not a quote:** the Bank of Thailand transfer-buying observations for November 6, 2026 are **SGD 1 = THB 25.9034** and **USD 1 = THB 33.3142**. They are not the tax-conversion rates or bank quotes for a 2027 transfer.

## Make sure the savings really cover the payment

At the dated planning rate, **SGD 60,000 × 25.9034 = THB 1,554,204**. Reaching THB 1,500,000 takes about **SGD 57,907.46**, leaving **THB 54,204 before fees and exchange-rate changes**. That is a buffer, not guaranteed headroom in 2027. Obtain an actual transfer quote when the payment date is known.

Withholding can reduce the cash that arrives without changing the need to reconcile gross taxable income. If treaty-eligible US withholding were 15% of USD 900, it would be USD 135, or THB 4,497.42 at this planning rate. **That is not an approved Thai foreign tax credit.** B's resulting cash would be THB 125,427.96 before fees. In C, illustrative withholding of USD 67.50 / THB 2,248.71 would leave THB 1,497,751.29 of cash. A full down payment would need an additional documented old-savings amount.

Whether the dividend is gross or net and what was actually withheld remain unknown. Do not count withheld money as a second physical remittance. Keep the gross-income tax budget, net transfer cash and any allowed credit separate.

## Treat treaty relief as a separate check

For an eligible individual beneficial owner, US treaty Article 10 caps source-country tax on ordinary US-company dividends at **15% of gross dividends**, while permitting Thai residence taxation. That is not a final Thai tax rate. A US broker can hold non-US companies, funds or special securities. Verify the issuer, instrument, US status and W-8BEN process; Thai nationality does not rule out US citizenship or a green card. The Thai domestic 10% final-dividend option and domestic corporate dividend credit do not automatically cover foreign dividends.

**US Article 18(6) matters:** where residence taxation is remittance-based, source-state treaty relief is restricted to income received/remitted in its accrual year or the following year. Income arising in 2026 and remitted in 2027 meets that timing window, subject to the other conditions. Keeping dividends offshore longer can affect US treaty relief even before Thai remittance tax arises. This is not a Thai exemption.

US Article 13 generally preserves domestic taxing rights, not a blanket Thailand-only exemption for share gains. A nonresident's ordinary stock gain may have no US tax under domestic rules, but citizenship/green-card status, tax home, presence, business connection and security type remain open. Do not invent a US gain tax or credit. The US **183-day** gains rule is not Thailand's **180-day** residence test.

For any foreign tax credit (FTC), prove actual qualifying tax, allocate it to the remitted portion and respect treaty limits. The RD manual's Thai limit uses relevant foreign income remitted divided by total assessable income **before expenses and allowances**, multiplied by Thai tax before credit. Keep country and income type separate. **US Article 25 also caps relief at Thai tax attributable to the same item: do not use gains to enlarge a dividend credit.** Excess credit is not simply carried forward. If 30% was withheld, investigate source-country correction rather than automatically crediting treaty-excess tax in Thailand. No FTC is subtracted from the scenario table.

For Singapore, the current DTA was signed in 2015, entered force on February 15, 2016 and began relevant Thai application in 2017. Article 15 addresses employment exercised there, subject to its conditional short-stay exception; Article 4 addresses dual residence. **Proven pre-2024 savings need no treaty rescue under P.162.** Prior Singapore tax does not create a refund against Thai tax that is not due. Article 22(b) allows capped credit if Thai tax is due on the same Singapore income. Later bank interest belongs in a new bucket; Article 11 gives source-country limits, not a flat Thai rate. No interest amount or special MLI benefit is assumed.

## Keep a record trail that survives the transfer

Account separation can help explain the facts. **It is not a statutory safe harbor, and there is no automatic FIFO guarantee here.** A balance at year-end alone does not prove that later withdrawals are still old money.

- Establish the opening pool with the year-end 2023 statement, Singapore payslips and employment records from 2019–2023, tax assessments and matching deposits.
- Roll the pool forward through all withdrawals, transfers, additions, interest and reinvestments from 2024 onward. Identify the old amount still present.
- Where practical, separate later returns into another account or subaccount. Separation now cannot repair missing historical proof.
- Maintain a remittance schedule: date, source account, earning year, country, income type, principal versus income, currency, FX basis, baht amount and record references. Keep bank confirmations and condo payment evidence.
- Keep broker purchase/sale confirmations, transaction-level basis and gains, gross dividends, withholding statements and 1042-S where issued, treaty paperwork and the actual cash path. Retain records and useful translations while the old pool may still be remitted or questioned.

## File for the remittance year, not the wrong March

| Situation | Return choice under these assumptions | Ordinary deadline |
|---|---|---|
| 2026 salary; foreign returns stay offshore throughout 2026 | PND 91 if employment is the only assessable category | March 31, 2027 |
| Taxable investment returns remitted in 2026 | PND 90 with the relevant foreign income | March 31, 2027 |
| 2027 scenario A; salary remains the only assessable income | PND 91; excluded old savings alone do not force PND 90 | March 31, 2028 |
| 2027 scenario B or C | PND 90; include remitted taxable income in 2027 and record 2026 as earning year | March 31, 2028 |

The March dates are ordinary deadlines, subject to applicable holiday or extension notices. **No future e-filing extension or final 2027 form is promised.** Use the actual form when released. Salary is section 40(1), foreign-company dividends 40(4)(b), and share gains 40(4)(g). Historical line numbers are not a substitute for checking the future form. These salary and investment categories alone do not create a PND 94 half-year obligation.

For conversion, follow the applicable commercial-bank buying rate on remittance day, or the BOT previous-business-day rate specified in the RD manual. Then apply actual deductions and substantiated capped FTC and subtract actual Thai payroll withholding from the employer certificate. **Annual liability is not the balance due with the return.** Do not report the same income twice.

Before sending, resolve the income-year day count, provable old pool, dividend gross/net status, actual withholding, issuer/security type, transaction-level gain, 2027 salary and deductions, actual FX/fees and any enacted legal change. You can prepare the records now without pretending those inputs are settled.
