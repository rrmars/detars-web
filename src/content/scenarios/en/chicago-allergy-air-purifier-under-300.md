## Size it for your room, not the biggest number on the box

CADR means clean air delivery rate: cubic feet of filtered air each minute. AHAM reports separate smoke, dust and pollen scores. Its [two-thirds rule](https://ahamverifide.org/ahams-air-filtration-standards/) gives a 300 sq ft room a starting smoke CADR of **200 CFM**. The [EPA sizing chart](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/guide-air-cleaners-home) is similar, at 195 CFM for 300 sq ft.

Air changes per hour, or ACH, describes room-sized volumes of cleaned air. These are equivalent clean-air changes, not fresh outdoor air. [AHAM's room ratings use 4.8 ACH](https://ahamverifide.org/ach-faq/). That is a comparison benchmark, not a guarantee of allergy relief.

With an assumed 8 ft ceiling:

- Room volume: 300 × 8 = 2,400 cubic feet.
- Equivalent ACH = CADR × 60 ÷ 2,400.
- 4.8 ACH needs 192 CFM; 5 ACH needs 200 CFM.
- A 10 ft ceiling raises the 4.8-ACH requirement to 240 CFM. An open door adds space to clean.

Aim for at least 200 smoke CFM, ideally with headroom. Published rated figures suggest all six meet that starting target. Coway and Winix tables do not explicitly label the CADR unit, however, and their ionization-off performance is not established here. These are sizing calculations, not bedroom tests.

**The catch: Sleep Mode and rated CADR are different settings.** EPA says CADR is typically measured at the highest speed. Turning the fan down reduces cleaning. A 23 dB minimum and a 250 CFM maximum do not describe the same operating point. The bedtime settings below are trials, not verified promises of 4.8 ACH overnight.

### Choose the Core 600S-P only if the budget and sound work

If you need both very quiet sleep and demonstrated 4.8 ACH, neither pick proves that combination. Check per-speed evidence before spending more. The benchmark is not a cliff where a purifier stops helping; it is a way to compare capacity honestly.

## Skip ozone shortcuts and misleading coverage claims

[EPA guidance](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/guide-air-cleaners-home) warns against devices that intentionally produce ozone, which irritates lungs. It also flags possible ozone emissions from ionizers, plasma devices and some UV/electrostatic systems. Prefer mechanical filtration without an active ionizer for this shopping brief.

[California CARB](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/resources/fact-sheets/indoor-air-cleaners-and-wildfire-smoke-faq-0) recommends mechanical HEPA filtration. CARB certification checks electrical safety and an ozone limit of **0.050 ppm**. It is not an effectiveness rating or a promise of zero ozone.

Coway's ionizer and Winix's PlasmaWave can be switched off while the fan and filter keep working. Keep the indicator off and recheck after mode changes or restarts. Their published CADR should not be assumed unchanged with ionization disabled. That extra check is why they remain alternatives rather than the simplest picks.

Also skip undersized desktop units, huge coverage claims based on one ACH and offers you cannot actually buy. Honeywell Store marked HPA200/HPA204 discontinued. A live direct Winix 5510 offer could not be verified, so this list uses the 5520. Those are availability exclusions, not evidence that the machines are ineffective or unavailable at every US seller.

## Before buying in March 2027

- **Recheck unit and genuine-filter prices together.** Match the exact US revision to its CADR entry. Check stock, tax, delivery and return-shipping/restocking terms. The Core 600S-P needs a discount if $300 is all-in.
- **Measure ceiling height and the space open to the fan.** This plan assumes a closed 300 sq ft bedroom with an 8 ft ceiling.
- **Use the return window for a real bedroom trial.** Start at the suggested manual setting. If you turn it down further, rated airflow no longer describes overnight use.
- **Unwrap the filter before switching on.** Keep curtains and furniture clear. Run higher before bedtime if useful, but keep filtering overnight; a pre-bed blast is not a substitute for ongoing cleaning.
- **Keep the job realistic.** A purifier catches airborne particles, not all settled dust on bedding and floors. Keep up ordinary dust removal. It will not fix dampness or a mold source; tell the landlord about moisture problems.

## Questions you may still have

### Will a purifier cure my allergies?

No symptom-relief guarantee is established here. The 4.8 ACH figure is a sizing benchmark, not a medical outcome. Filtration is one part of managing airborne particles, alongside dust removal and fixing moisture sources.

### Is Sleep Mode the best setting overnight?

Not automatically. It trades airflow for sound. The low manufacturer dB number does not come with maximum CADR. Try a tolerable manual setting and keep the fan running; lower it only knowing that cleaning falls too.

### Is the more expensive Core 600S-P worth it?

Possibly, if you want more capacity and can tolerate the sound. Its older-sample Medium result is closer to the room benchmark, but still below it. It also exceeds a tax-inclusive $300 cap at the listed price. Do not pay more assuming silent full-capacity cleaning.

### Can I use Auto or Eco instead of choosing a speed?

You can, but this comparison does not establish a maintained airflow in those modes. An air-quality colour is not a pollen counter. A manual-speed trial makes the sound/airflow compromise easier to judge.
