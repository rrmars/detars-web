Andrés (30) works as a contractor for a US company and wants to move to Spain with Laura (29), his partner of four years, in spring 2027. Can they go on the teleworker visa, will his income be enough for two, and is Laura covered if they are not married? **Short answer: the route that the official pages support is the teleworker (digital nomad) visa filed at the Spanish consulate in Bogotá, with Andrés as main applicant and Laura as his pareja de hecho; his income clears the test on both possible readings of the rule; but which reading the authorities use is unresolved, and Laura must show the partner proof the consulate lists (the “unión marital de hecho” note on her birth certificate); how to obtain it is an adviser question.**

Written on 8 November 2026 from public pages only; nobody was contacted and nothing was filed. “Unconfirmed” marks anything that did not come from a primary source (a broker, a listing, a news piece or our own assumption). **This is general information, not legal, tax or immigration advice.** The source materials are not published.

## The recommended route: a consulate visa in Bogotá, Laura as pareja de hecho

Apply for the Spanish “visado de residencia para teletrabajo (nómada digital)” (Ley 14/2013 as amended by Ley 28/2022) at the Spanish Consulate General in Bogotá, filed through BLS. Andrés applies as the main applicant; Laura applies at the same time as his pareja de hecho family member. Later, switch to the residence authorisation of up to 3 years, applying in the 60 days before the visa expires.

- **Income.** Andrés earns USD 5,800 a month, which is €5,194.81 at 1 EUR = 1.1165 USD. That is above the couple total of €3,917.38 on the higher reading and above €3,357.75 on the lower one (section 3). The recommendation holds on both readings.
- **Fee.** The Bogotá consulate page says Colombian citizens pay no visa fee. The fee for the later residence authorisation was not found.
- **Partner proof.** The consulate page names the proof for a de-facto partnership: the Colombian birth certificate showing the “unión marital de hecho” as a marginal note.
- **Laura goes as a dependant, not as a second main applicant.** Her income of USD 1,500 a month (about €1,343.48) would not meet the €2,849.00 single-applicant threshold on the higher reading.
- **Not settled by the official pages: ask an adviser.** Which SMI base is used (1,221 or 1,424.50), whether a contractor can use the Beckham regime, and whether applying from inside Spain is possible after a tourist entry.
- **Start now, because these hold up the file:** a Spanish-authorised health policy with no co-payment, the apostilled Colombian criminal record, the ES-CO 3 social-security form (or RETA proof), the US company’s registry certificate (at least 1 year old) and a letter on the contractor relationship (at least 3 months).

**City: Málaga, as a low-confidence lean on cost only.** In a small sample of listings, 1-bedrooms were €850–1,225 in Málaga against €1,200–1,325 in Valencia, and a coworking desk was €99 against €155 + IVA. But city-average rents are almost equal (16.7 against 16.5 €/m², from a news report), the coworking operators differ, and neither city has a direct flight from Bogotá. The budget gap is about €267.76 a month against Valencia, using mid-examples. Choose by neighbourhood and a 12-month contract, not by this sample.

## 1. Which route? Consulate in Colombia or UGE-CE from inside Spain

| | A. Visa at the Spanish consulate, Bogotá (BLS) | B. Residence via UGE-CE from inside Spain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basis | Visa of up to 1 year; renew into residence in the 60 days before expiry (law) | Residence authorisation directly, up to 3 years; renewals of 2 years (law) |
| Who may file | Residents of the Bogotá demarcation, in person or through an accredited representative | Someone already legally in Spain. A Moscow consulate news item from 2023 says so, and Colombians may stay 90 days visa-free. **Not confirmed for 2026**: the UGE how-to page returned nothing. |
| Fee | Colombians exempt (consulate page); the BLS service fee is not confirmed | Not found |
| Time | Legal limit of 10 days, extendable; collect the visa within 1 month. The BLS appointment wait is unknown | “20 días” according to the 2023 Moscow item; unconfirmed |
| Main risk | Document timing: apostilles, ES-CO 3, insurance and US company papers | You must prove the same documents while in Spain on a 90-day clock; the page could not be read |

**Counterintuitive finding:** the common advice “apply from inside Spain to get 3 years at once” relies on tourist entry always being usable. The only official page for Colombians that could be read in 2026 (the Bogotá consulate) describes route A completely, and the UGE application page could not be read, so route A is the one this guide can support with documents. Route B may still be better; **ask an adviser** before you drop it.

## 2. Requirements checklist (Bogotá consulate page and BOE)

- **Passports** valid for at least 1 year, with 2 blank pages, issued no more than 10 years ago (both of you). NIE requested before the visa (both). Official.
- **National visa form and photo.** Official.
- **Colombian criminal record** from the Policía Nacional (“NO REGISTRA ANTECEDENTES”), apostilled for migration purposes, plus a sworn declaration of no record in the last 5 years (both). Official.
- **Health insurance** with an insurer authorised in Spain, covering all risks of the public system (both). Official; the prices in section 8 are unconfirmed.
- **Certificate from the US client:** a relationship of at least 3 months and the terms for remote work from Spain (contractor path). Official.
- **US company registry certificate:** incorporation date (at least 1 year) and activity. Official. Apostille and translation are our expectation, not a stated rule; ask the adviser.
- **Social Security:** a sworn declaration plus RETA proof, or form ES-CO 3 apostilled (Colombia convention). Official. Whether Andrés can obtain ES-CO 3 as a contractor is an adviser question.
- **Degree or at least 3 years’ professional experience** (Andrés). Official.
- **Income evidence:** contracts, invoices and bank statements that show ownership, lawfulness and availability of the funds. Official.
- **Spanish translations** of foreign-language documents. Official.
- **Originals and copies,** complete file at the BLS appointment. Official.
- **Apostille fee and timing** at Colombia’s Cancillería: not verified, because the site blocked automated access.

## 3. Income thresholds: the arithmetic and the ambiguity

SMI 2026 is €1,221 a month in 14 payments, so €17,094 a year (Real Decreto 126/2026). The consulate page requires 200% of the SMI for the teleworker and 75% for the first relative, plus 25% for each further one. It gives percentages only, no euro amounts. Exchange rate: 1 EUR = 1.1165 USD (ECB, 6 November 2026).

| Reading | Teleworker 200% | Partner 75% | Couple total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A: annual SMI ÷ 12 = 17,094 ÷ 12 = 1,424.50 | 2 × 1,424.50 = 2,849.00 | 0.75 × 1,424.50 = 1,068.38 | 3,917.38 |
| B: headline 1,221 per payment | 2,442.00 | 915.75 | 3,357.75 |

**The 12-payment against 14-payment base is unresolved.** Reading A is what third-party sites quote (€2,849.00), which is unconfirmed; the official pages do not say which basis applies. **Next step:** ask the adviser or the consulate which monthly figure they use, and prepare the file to pass on both.

- **Andrés:** 5,800 USD ÷ 1.1165 = €5,194.81 a month, above the couple total on both readings (margin €1,277.43 on reading A).
- **Laura:** 1,500 ÷ 1.1165 = €1,343.48, below the €2,849.00 single threshold, so she depends on going as a family member.
- **Not stated anywhere read:** whether Laura’s own income can be added to the family threshold.

## 4. Can an unmarried partner be included? Yes, with proof

- **The law allows it.** Ley 14/2013 art. 62.4 lets the “cónyuge o persona con análoga relación de afectividad” apply jointly or later, and the consulate page lists “cónyuge o pareja de hecho”. Official.
- **The proof is specific.** The consulate lists a certificate of registered partnership or other proof of an unregistered partnership, apostilled. For a de-facto partnership, it requires the Colombian birth certificate with the “unión marital de hecho” entered as a marginal note.
- **What that probably means in practice.** Formalising the unión marital de hecho in Colombia before applying; ask how, and how long it takes (notary or conciliation route). This is our reading, not a stated rule: **ask an adviser.**
- **Four years living together helps as background,** but the consulate page mentions no minimum period and does not accept a simple declaration.
- **Laura needs her own** NIE, passport, clean record and insurance; the family income share is 75% of the SMI.
- **Open question:** whether Laura may work for her own clients from Spain on the dependant permit, or needs her own authorisation. Adviser.

## 6. Tax and Social Security: questions for an adviser

Not advice. The facts below come from BOE and Seguridad Social; the questions are what to put to a Spanish tax adviser (gestor or asesor fiscal) and a Colombian one.

- **Residence.** Staying more than 183 days or having the centre of economic interests in Spain makes you tax-resident. **This rule was not re-read from a primary source and is unsourced here: ask.** When does Colombian tax residence end for each of you?
- **“Beckham law” (art. 93 LIRPF).** An optional special regime: flat 24% up to €600,000 and 47% above on employment or professional income, with 5 years of non-residence required. The BOE text expressly names employees with the teleworker visa. Andrés is a contractor to a US company: can he use it as an autónomo? What is the deadline to opt in? Does it include Laura? Compare it against ordinary progressive IRPF with a worked example on €62,337.66 a year. Contractor eligibility is **not settled** by the text.
- **Autónomo social security (RETA 2026).** At net income of €4,050–6,000 a month the minimum base is €1,732.03; with an assumed rate of about 31.5% (unconfirmed) that is about €545.59 a month. Confirm the rate, any first-year reduction, and whether ES-CO 3 can replace RETA for Andrés.
- **Laura and RETA.** Does she also need it for her freelance design work for foreign clients? If so, her estimated cost is 950.98 × 31.5% ≈ €299.56 a month, not in the budget, assuming her net income falls in the €1,166.70–1,300 band; a higher band would cost more.
- **VAT and filings.** IVA on invoices to a US client and to Spanish clients (the limit for self-employed teleworkers is 20% of activity); quarterly filings; whether a Colombia–Spain double-tax treaty applies; how US-source income is declared; Colombian reporting of assets abroad.
- **Company or person.** When is incorporating better than invoicing as a person?

## What is still open

- **The SMI base:** the euro amount the authorities use as “monthly SMI” (1,221 or 1,424.50) is unresolved; the recommendation holds on both.
- **UGE-CE pages were blocked:** its teleworker page, application page and guidance PDFs could not be read, so the in-Spain route, UGE processing time and the fee for the residence authorisation are unverified.
- **Apostilles:** Cancillería pages were blocked; apostille cost and timing are not verified. BLS appointment waits are unknown.
- **Beckham law for contractors:** the BOE text names employees with the teleworker visa; Andrés’s eligibility as a contractor is unresolved.
- **The 183-day residence rule** has no source here, and Colombian-side tax is not sourced.
- **Insurance, flights and city averages** rest on one broker, search snippets and news pieces; dated fares and insurer-published prices were not found. The Málaga average conflicts between two articles (16.7 and 18.8).
- **The 31.5% autónomo rate** and any first-year reduction are not confirmed; whether Laura’s income counts towards the family threshold is not stated.
- **Some government pages could not be read automatically;** nothing blocked was bypassed.
