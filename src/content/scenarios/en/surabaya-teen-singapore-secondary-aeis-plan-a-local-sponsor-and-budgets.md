Nadia is 13, your family lives in Surabaya, and the budget is IDR 1.4 billion a year, all in. You want to know whether a Singapore secondary school from January 2028 is realistic, and which route to plan for. **Short answer: Plan A, a Singapore government school through the AEIS entrance test with Nadia in a homestay or guardian arrangement, at about IDR 0.70–0.87 billion in a steady year. It depends on one condition before money does: the family must have a Singapore Citizen or Permanent Resident aged 21 or older who can be the required local sponsor. Without that person, Plan A is not available and Plan B, a private school, becomes the Singapore route, though it is over budget in year one.**

This guide was prepared on 8 November 2026 for a Surabaya family in this situation, from public pages only. Nothing was applied for, requested or sent to any school, agent or official. "Unconfirmed" marks anything that did not come from an official page, and "assumed" marks our own planning figure. **It is general information, not legal or immigration advice.** The source materials are not published.

## The recommendation and why

**Plan A: a government school through AEIS, with Nadia in a homestay or guardian arrangement, not with a parent in Singapore. About IDR 0.70–0.87 billion in a steady year.** Fees are the lowest of the Singapore options (S$1,111.80 a month at the ASEAN rate), the AEIS fee is small, and the total sits well inside IDR 1.4 billion. Moving a parent in with her (Plan A with a parent) comes to about IDR 1.42–1.55 billion in a steady year, which is at or over budget.

- **Plan B: a private or international school in Singapore** (for example Stamford American International School, S$53,210 tuition for Grade 6–8), with a homestay. It avoids the AEIS test, and the ICA page we read lists no local sponsor for private schools (confirm with ICA and the school), but it costs about IDR 1.30–1.47 billion from year two and IDR 1.49–1.66 billion in year one, so it uses the whole budget or exceeds it.
- **Plan C (fallback): stay at an international school in Surabaya**, about IDR 0.35 billion on unconfirmed figures, and, as our suggestion, revisit Singapore at Secondary 3 or for pre-university.

**Two things decide Plan A before money does.**

- **A local sponsor is required (the deciding condition).** ICA says a Student's Pass for a government school needs a sponsor who is a Singapore Citizen or PR aged 21 or older, and companies cannot sponsor. If the family has no such contact, Plan A is not available and Plan B becomes the Singapore route (over budget in year one; confirm with ICA and the school). We found no ICA or MOE page saying whether a non-relative Citizen or PR, such as a guardian or homestay host, can serve, or whether any further conditions apply. **Next step:** before anything else, ask the family's own circle whether someone qualifies, and put that question to ICA.
- **AEIS has no published pass rate and no school choice.** MOE does not state a pass rate (we did not find one), and placement follows vacancies, residential area and test performance. **Next step:** keep Plan B and Plan C funded in case she is not placed.

**Why a homestay beats an accompanying parent in this recommendation:** it is the budget arithmetic, not a view on what suits Nadia. A parent who comes cannot work for the first year (MOM), and the parent route also needs a Singapore Citizen or PR sponsor. Whether a 13- or 14-year-old should live with a host family is the family's call.

## AEIS timeline and rules (MOE pages dated 2026)

S-AEIS is the Supplementary round. MOE has not published the AEIS exercise for January 2028 entry, so the 2027 dates below are **inferred** from the 2026 pattern and are unconfirmed.

| --- | --- | --- |

| Early January 2027 | S-AEIS applications open; tests end February to early March 2027 (tentative). It covers Secondary 1–2, starting April or May 2027. At age 13 MOE's AEIS eligibility checker offers Primary 5 or Secondary 1; S-AEIS itself covers Secondary 1–2. | MOE, 1 July 2026 |
| About July 2027 | AEIS 2027 (for January 2028) applications, if MOE follows 2026. Not published yet. | Inferred, unconfirmed |

| About December 2027 | Results, then a registration letter from the school, then the Student's Pass application to ICA. | Early January 2028 | School starts. | MOE pattern (January start); 2028 date not published |
| February–March 2028 | Fallback: S-AEIS for Secondary 1–2, starting April or May 2028. | Pattern inferred, unconfirmed |

- **Level:** MOE's checker offers age 14 either Secondary 1 or Secondary 2 (age 15 adds Secondary 3). Nadia, born about 2013, is 14 on 1 January 2028 if the checker uses her age at 1 January (this depends on her exact birth date). That is **our inference from the admission page; confirm with MOE.** Secondary 2 matches an Indonesian grade-8 finisher more closely, but it tests Secondary 1 content; Secondary 1 tests Primary 6 content and is the safer pass (our reading of "the level below", an inference). Secondary 3 is not offered at 14.
- **Fee:** S$630 for Secondary, plus 9% GST = S$686.70, non-refundable. The 2027 fee is not yet published.
- **Subjects:** English (with a composition) and Mathematics only; no calculators or dictionaries. No Mother Tongue paper is mentioned, and secondary applicants take no separate Cambridge English test.
- **Placement:** no choice of school; allocation by vacancy, declared residential area and test performance; results are final, with no marking checks and no transfer. We found no priority for ASEAN or Indonesian applicants.
- **Pass rate:** MOE does not publish one in anything we could find. A third-party blog says MOE never has; we did not open it, so treat that as unconfirmed.
- **If she is not placed:** she may re-apply to AEIS or S-AEIS at the same or another level within the age range. S-AEIS runs about six months after AEIS (an inference from the dates, not stated by MOE). MOE also points to private schools.
- **A place through AEIS rules out later DSA-Sec.** A Dependant's Pass holder must still sit AEIS.
- **Not yet published:** AEIS 2027 dates and fee. **Next step:** check the MOE international students AEIS page from about June 2027.

## Student's Pass, guardian and accompanying parent

**Student's Pass for Nadia (ICA pages dated 29 October to 6 November 2026)**

- **Government school:** S$45 application (non-refundable), about 1 week to process (2 weeks if a visa is required), applied for 2–3 months before the course starts. Nadia need not be in Singapore while it is processed.
- **Local sponsor required:** a Singapore Citizen or PR aged 21 or older; companies cannot sponsor. The sponsor completes forms V39s and V39Q. The rule is listed for government, government-aided and independent schools and ITE, and not listed for private schools.
- **After in-principle approval:** S$60 issuance, S$30 Multiple Journey Visa "if applicable", a medical exam "if required", biometrics in person. Whether Indonesians need that visa is **not confirmed.** ICA's visa page could not be read, and general knowledge that Indonesian passports are visa-exempt for short visits is not a confirmation for a student stay.
- **Private or foreign-system school:** S$45; the school must be EduTrust-certified; no local sponsor is listed on the page. Acceptance by a school does not guarantee the pass.
- **Documents** include birth certificate, passport, education and financial-support history, parents' details, a Singapore address and a recent photo. The vaccination requirement covers foreign-born applicants aged 12 or under, so not Nadia.
- **Health insurance:** whether it is required is unresolved. One comparison site says no separate proof is needed; other snippets say it is compulsory. No MOE or ICA page settled it.

**Option 1: a parent on a Long-Term Visit Pass (LTVP)**

- **Eligibility (ICA, 29 October 2026):** the parent of an international student holding a valid Student's Pass. "You need a local sponsor who is a Singapore Citizen or PR aged 21 or older." Only one person, a parent or a grandparent, can accompany each student. The sponsor applies through Singpass.
- **Fees:** S$45 application, S$60 issuance, S$30 Multiple Journey Visa if applicable; processing about 6 weeks. Our inference: on the 2026 pattern AEIS results arrive in December, and a parent's pass is applied for after Nadia's pass, so a parent may not be able to arrive with her at the January start. The ICA page does not state how long the pass lasts (not found).
- **Can the parent work? Not in the first year.** MOM says an LTVP holder accompanying a child is not allowed to work during the first year; after a year the parent can find a job and the employer can apply for a Work Permit. If a parent runs a business in Surabaya, that means the Surabaya business must be run remotely or by the other parent. The pages do not cover running one's own business.
- The ICA LTVP page does not say whether it also applies to students at private schools. **Check before assuming.**

**Option 2: a guardian or homestay**

- No ICA or MOE page we found says a homestay host or a non-relative can act as "guardian" or as the required sponsor. The ICA pages describe an application sponsor only. **Treat this as an open question for MOE and ICA, not a given.**
- Agencies advertise homestay with guardianship: a single room at S$2,500–3,500 a month (MG Student Homestay; undated agent site, unconfirmed).
- We did not contact any agency. Verify any host or agent claim directly.
