You rent for THB 14,000 a month, you have THB 650,000 saved, and you are not sure whether a first condo is a smart move or a way to run out of cash. **Short answer: yes, buy, but a resale condo rather than a new launch, with a price ceiling of THB 4.0 million (aim for THB 3.5–3.8 million). The Bank of Thailand already allows 100% financing on a first contract under THB 10M, so what limits you is the monthly instalment and your reserve, not a down payment.**

This guide follows Ploy (30, THB 52,000 a month) and Ton (32, THB 41,000 a month), who rent in Bangkok. It was written on 8 November 2026 from public pages only; no bank, developer or agent was contacted, and no personal quote was obtained. "Unconfirmed" marks anything that did not come from an official page, and "assumed" marks our own planning figure. **It is general information, not financial, tax or legal advice.** The source materials are not published.

## The recommendation and why

**Buy a resale condo. Price ceiling THB 4.0 million; aim for THB 3.5–3.8 million.** Stretch to THB 4.5 million only if a bank confirms in writing about 100% financing on its own appraisal, and you accept an instalment of about THB 24,157 a month at 5% (about 26% of your gross income, and 1.7 times today's rent).

- **The rule that helps you:** the Bank of Thailand (BOT) already allows 100% financing of a first contract under THB 10M (BOT Notification FPG 1/2563, official page). A down payment is not a legal requirement. The real limits are the bank's appraisal, its debt-service test and your own comfort.
- **Upfront cash at THB 4.0 million, resale:** about THB 120,950 with 100% financing, leaving THB 529,050 of your THB 650,000. With a cautious 10% down payment it is THB 520,910, leaving THB 129,090. Both cases are shown below.
- **Why resale:** you choose the transfer date, you do not pay instalments on top of rent while a project is built, you can see the real common-area fee, and you skip the sinking fund (THB 26,250 on a 35 sqm new unit, an assumption).
- **The deadline that matters (unconfirmed):** the 0.01% transfer and mortgage registration fees are reported to run to 30 June 2027, but that date comes from a news and company page, not from the Department of Lands. At THB 4.0 million with a THB 3.6M loan, normal rates (2% plus 1%) would cost THB 80,000 + THB 36,000 = THB 116,000 instead of about THB 760. A new launch bought in 2027 may not transfer until after the cut ends. **Next step:** pick a transfer date well before 30 June 2027, and check the Gazette notice before you sign.
- **What binds you now:** not the loan size (even at a 40% debt-service ratio and 6.5% interest the maximum loan is about THB 5.9 million) and, if 100% financing is granted, not upfront cash. It is the monthly instalment and a reserve. **Keep at least six months of housing cost untouched: about THB 125,403 (THB 3.6M loan) to THB 138,287 (THB 4.0M loan).** Borrowing 100% also adds interest: about THB 2,147 a month at 5% on the THB 400,000 you did not put down.
- **Versus renting (THB 4.0 million resale):** monthly cash out is THB 17,771–21,567 with 10% down and THB 19,497–23,715 with 100% financing (3.18%–5.00% interest), against THB 14,000 rent. Counting equity built and the cost of tied-up cash, owning is roughly THB 1,438 a month cheaper than renting at 3.18% with 10% down, but THB 4,009–4,998 a month dearer at 5.00%. Price growth is not counted. **If either of you may change jobs within a year, wait.**

## Bank of Thailand loan-to-value rules and the temporary relaxation

| Item | What the BOT pages say |
| --- | --- |
| **Standing rule for you** (first contract, collateral under THB 10M) | Loan-to-value (LTV) limit **100%**. Second contract: 90% if the first loan has been paid for at least 2 years, 80% if less; third and later: 70%. For collateral of THB 10M or more the standing limits are 90%, 80% and 70%. A top-up loan on a first contract under THB 10M may not exceed 10% of the collateral value (decoration, insurance and similar; a separate loan, not counted in the budgets below). Collateral value is the actual buying-selling price, excluding renovation costs. Source: BOT Notification FPG 1/2563, Art. 5.2.2 (English unofficial translation; official page). **This is the 2020 text. Later amending notices were not read, so confirm the current wording with the bank.** |
| **Temporary relaxation** | A 100% cap also applies to (1) collateral under THB 10M from the 2nd contract onward and (2) collateral of THB 10M or more from the 1st contract. It covers loan contracts signed 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027 (BOT Notification 30/2569 dated 26 June 2026, Gazette 30 June 2026, effective 1 July 2026; extension announced 14 May 2026; first relaxation 1 May 2025 to 30 June 2026). Official page. |
| **What it means for you** | Your first contract under THB 10M already had the 100% ceiling. The relaxation matters to you only if either of you already has a housing loan, because then this would be a second contract. The BOT cap is a ceiling, not an entitlement: each bank still decides using its own appraisal, credit check and debt-service ratio. KBank and Krungthai advertise up to 100% (of appraisal or price). |
| **Watch-outs** | Counting is by loan contract, so confirm with the bank whether either of you has an existing housing loan. A bank appraisal below the price (common on new launches) leaves a cash gap even when the BOT ceiling is 100%. No extension beyond 30 June 2027 had been announced as of 8 November 2026. |

## How banks assess debt service, with a worked maximum loan

The BOT pages above set LTV, not a mortgage debt-service ratio (DSR). Bank DSR limits are set by each bank. A May 2026 comparison blog reports caps of roughly 60–70% of income across 12 banks, stricter for borrowers with other debts (**unconfirmed**; blog). Banks also review credit-bureau record, job tenure (Krungthai: at least 6 months for employees) and age (borrower age plus loan term of at most 70 at both KBank and Krungthai; bank pages).

**Assumptions:** joint borrowers, combined income THB 52,000 + THB 41,000 = THB 93,000 a month treated as gross or assessable income, no other debts (assumed), 30-year term. Maximum instalment = DSR × 93,000; maximum loan = instalment divided by the monthly payment per baht at the rate.

| Maximum instalment (DSR × 93,000) | Max loan at 3.18% (KBank 3-yr average) | at 5.00% (planning) | at 6.50% (stress) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 40% × 93,000 = 37,200 | 8,623,613 | 6,929,676 | 5,885,442 |
| 50% × 93,000 = 46,500 | 10,779,516 | 8,662,095 | 7,356,803 |
| 60% × 93,000 = 55,800 | 12,935,419 | 10,394,514 | 8,828,164 |

All figures are THB. 40% is a conservative planning level, and 50–60% brackets the range banks may accept; these rows are scenarios, not a bank's answer. Even the lowest figure (THB 5,885,442) is more than the THB 4.5M ceiling, so DSR will not stop this purchase. The monthly instalment you are comfortable with, and a cash reserve, will. Banks may also stress the rate or apply tighter ratios to the lower earner. **Next step:** ask each bank for its ratio in writing.

## Common-area fee and sinking fund

- **Common-area fee** is charged per sqm per month: roughly THB 25–40 (low-rise), THB 40–70 (mid-market high-rise) and THB 70–150 (luxury). Example: 35 sqm × THB 45 = THB 1,575 a month. **Unconfirmed**; property-site guide.
- **Sinking fund** is a one-off contribution paid at transfer by the first buyer of a new unit, typically "hundreds of baht per sqm" (**unconfirmed**; search snippet only). We assumed THB 750 per sqm, which is THB 26,250 for 35 sqm. Resale buyers normally do not pay it again, but ask the juristic person (the building's management body) for arrears and the fund balance.
- **Prepaid fees:** developers often ask for 1 year of common fees in advance. We assumed 12 months for a new unit and 2 months for a resale unit.

## Before you commit

- Ask each bank in writing (without sharing personal data until you decide) for its appraisal ratio, MRR, fees and rate step-ups. **No bank was contacted for this guide, so every rate here is a published example, not an offer.**
- Check the Gazette notice for the fee measure and its end date, and the building's juristic-person records.
- Re-run the numbers if rates move; the BOT policy rate and bank promotions change often.

## What is still unconfirmed

- **The fee cut and its 30 June 2027 deadline:** the 0.01% rates, the THB 7M limit and the end date come from a news and company page. The Gazette text, the Cabinet page and a Department of Lands source were not obtained. **Next step:** confirm with the Department of Lands or the Gazette before relying on the saving.
- **Seller-side taxes:** a current official source for the 3.3% specific business tax was not found, and the Revenue Department pages show 0.1% (dated 2020). Stamp duty and withholding come from one agency blog.
- **SCB rates:** the SCB page could not be read, so no SCB rate is shown. GHB's 2.60% is a news report and applies only up to THB 3M. The KTB rate is from the bank's own page, which shows no promotion end date.
- **Later amendments to the BOT LTV notification:** the standing rule was read from the 2020 text. **Next step:** ask the bank to confirm in writing the 100% ceiling that applies to your first contract.
- **Debt-service ratios:** the 60–70% figure comes from a blog; the 40%, 50% and 60% rows are scenarios.
- **Assumed costs:** appraisal fee, fire insurance, fit-out, repairs reserve, the THB 750 per sqm sinking fund, prepaid common fees and the 2% return on cash are planning assumptions, not sourced figures.
- **Listings:** no verified THB 3.5–4.5M listing was found.
- **Arithmetic check:** the loan, instalment, budget and rent figures were recomputed by a second method, and the figures matched. That check was run by the same author as the guide, not by an outside reviewer.
