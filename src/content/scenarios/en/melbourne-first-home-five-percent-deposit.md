You want the keys, but you also want money left when something breaks. For this couple, I would start with established two-bedroom homes around $550,000, searching within $500,000–$560,000. Explore the Australian Government 5% Deposit Scheme, subject to lender and property approval. Do not turn its $950,000 Melbourne cap into your shopping budget.

With $48,000 saved, the $550,000 example needs $35,021.20 upfront and leaves $12,978.80. At the assumed 7.25% planning rate over 30 years, mortgage payments are about $3,564/month, or $4,364 with an $800 ownership-cost allowance. That is $1,664 more than the couple's $2,700 rent. Every property's owners-corporation fees below are UNCONFIRMED; the allowance is not a quote.

This is a dated November 6, 2026 planning example for an adult Australian-citizen first-buyer couple intending to live in Melbourne, with $165,000 combined salary excluding employer super and a June 2027 purchase goal. It is not a loan approval or personal financial advice. Rules, fees and rates have not been refreshed for this guide.

## Set a cash limit before a borrowing limit

Keep $12,000 aside as an emergency reserve. That is a planning judgment, not a scheme rule. About $570,000 is the example's practical ceiling with today's savings if actual costs match the allowances; the lower search range leaves more room for surprises.

A $600,000 purchase needs $37,638.20 upfront, leaving only $10,361.80 now. Saving another $2,000/month from December 2026 through May 2027 adds $12,000 and would leave $22,361.80 after that purchase. Treat $600,000 as a conditional stretch after saving and checking the building, not today's default target. At $650,000, you need about $63,612 total savings to pay the modelled costs and retain $12,000.

A small deposit does not make a home inexpensive. Fees sit outside the deposit, first-home duty relief tapers above $600,000, and a guarantee protects the lender—not your repayments or the home's value.

Use its [indexed thresholds](https://firsthomebuyers.gov.au/HTB-thresholds), [scheme terms](https://firsthomebuyers.gov.au/australian-government-help-buy-scheme) and [customer guide](https://firsthomebuyers.gov.au/helptobuycustomerguide), rather than older launch income limits. State details are in the [SRO grant guidance](https://www.sro.vic.gov.au/buying-property/first-home-owner-grant/understanding-first-home-owner-grant) and [first-home duty guidance](https://www.sro.vic.gov.au/buying-property/land-transfer-stamp-duty/concessions-exemptions-and-waivers/first-home-buyers/first-home-buyer-duty-exemption-or-concession). The state benefits generally require 12 continuous months of occupation starting within 12 months; have your conveyancer confirm the applicable conditions.

### Help to Buy lowers the loan, but gives up an equity share

Exactly $165,000 combined taxable income is within the retained indexed limit. It does not establish eligibility: the financial-capacity test can exclude buyers able to purchase a suitable home without shared equity. No numeric asset ceiling was established here; that does not mean assets are ignored.

For illustration only, a $550,000 established home with a 2% deposit ($11,000) and the maximum 30% government contribution ($165,000) leaves a $374,000 bank loan. At 7.25% over 30 years, that is about $2,551/month before ownership costs. The maximum contribution is not automatic, and a larger deposit may be required. A 30% share would cost $195,000 to buy back if the home were worth $650,000; gains, losses and capacity reviews affect the long-term trade-off.

You cannot combine Help to Buy with the 5% guarantee. Eligible state grant and duty relief can coexist, subject to their rules. The settlement table below models the guarantee route only, with one mortgage registration; Help to Buy's second mortgage and transaction costs are not silently included.

## Can you live comfortably with the repayments?

Use 7.25% as a planning assumption, not an offered rate or a forecast. The model has 30 years of monthly principal-and-interest payments at a constant rate, no offset, extra repayments, fees or capitalised LMI.

| Purchase price | Loan at 95% | Mortgage/month | With $800 owner costs/month | Extra over $2,700 rent/month | Buffered payment at 10.25% |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $550,000 | $522,500 | $3,564 | $4,364 | $1,664 | $4,682 |
| $650,000 | $617,500 | $4,212 | $5,012 | $2,312 | $5,533 |
| $750,000 | $712,500 | $4,861 | $5,661 | $2,961 | $6,385 |

The $800 monthly allowance is OC $333, council $150, water service charges $67, contents insurance $25 and maintenance $225. These are assumptions, not property costs. The rough $4,000/year OC budget is not an amount for any listed home; the rounded monthly components deliberately total $800. Check whether building insurance is included in OC charges to avoid double counting. Special levies and ongoing lender fees are extra.

For the income model, each partner earns $82,500 taxable salary, excluding super. With the retained 2026/27 [resident tax rates](https://www.ato.gov.au/tax-rates-and-codes/tax-rates-australian-residents) and [Medicare levy](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/medicare-and-private-health-insurance/medicare-levy/what-is-the-medicare-levy), each pays $15,270 tax and $1,650 Medicare, giving $10,930/month combined take-home. No HELP, cards, car loan, other commitments, deductions, offsets, levy exemption or additional Medicare levy surcharge is assumed. If one partner earns the whole $165,000, estimated take-home falls to $9,965/month. Replace the assumed split and $3,600/month non-housing living costs with your own figures.

The retained [APRA buffer explanation](https://www.apra.gov.au/news-and-publications/apras-system-risk-outlook-may-2026) uses 3 percentage points above the loan rate: 7.25% becomes 10.25%, not 7.47%. After $3,600 living costs, $800 owner costs and $1,000 extra monthly headroom, the $5,530 stressed-payment budget supports about $617,118 borrowing. Without that headroom, the mathematical ceiling is $728,712—not a recommendation. Another $500/month commitment lowers this simplified capacity by about $55,797. Lenders use their own expenditure benchmarks, floors, credit and property rules; none of these figures is approval. The retained APRA research did not exhaustively check every later announcement.

## A calm route to June 2027

- November–December 2026: confirm income split, FY2026 notices, HELP, card limits and actual spending. Ring-fence $12,000. Practise the higher housing payment while renting; aim to save $2,000/month for December–May rather than assume it will happen.
- January–February 2027: when ready, seek participating-lender assessment and written scheme/property criteria. Ask about apartment size, postcode, building exposure and maximum LVR. Loan approval, guarantee approval and valuation are separate checks.
- February–April 2027: inspect within $500,000–$560,000. Only consider up to $600,000 after additional savings and verified ownership costs. Compare transport, area, light, noise, layout, parking and resale appeal—not just price.
- April–May 2027: obtain contract review before signing. Negotiate a 5% contract deposit if needed; the scheme does not force a seller to accept it, and a 10% deposit may exceed cash available. Seek suitable finance/inspection conditions. Auctions are generally unconditional: understand funding and valuation-shortfall risk before bidding. Allow roughly 30–60 days for settlement before June 30, 2027, not merely contract signing.
- May–June 2027: confirm unconditional finance and guarantee approval, duty relief, operative registration fees, adjustments and insurance responsibilities. Keep the emergency reserve after moving and diarise occupation deadlines.

Walk away if the home only works by removing the reserve, assuming unknown OC fees are cheap, relying on an unapproved benefit or stretching to the federal cap.

## FAQ

### Does a 5% deposit mean we can buy for twenty times our savings?

No. Settlement fees and any duty are additional, and you still need a reserve. The $650,000 example needs $51,611.87 before retaining emergency money—already more than $48,000 savings.

### Are the listed homes' owners-corporation costs low?

We do not know. Every fee is UNCONFIRMED. The $800/month owner budget is a generic allowance, not a checked cost for any address. Obtain the building's documents before deciding.

### Does earning exactly $165,000 rule out Help to Buy?

Not under the retained joint threshold, which is inclusive. But FY2026 taxable-income notices, assets, debts, lender approval and the ability-to-buy test still decide whether the couple qualifies. Current gross salary alone is not enough.

### Should we buy new to get the grant?

Not just for the grant. The $10,000 benefit applies only to an eligible new home up to $750,000, not this established-home baseline. Compare total cash, building quality and running costs without assuming approval or settlement-time payment.
