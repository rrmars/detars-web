**Worried that buying will use up your savings? With £38,000 saved, a 10% deposit and estimated buying costs of £5,399, focus on £220,000–£260,000 after an initial £220,000–£280,000 search. The cash-only ceiling is £266,010 if you keep £6,000 back. That is a cash calculation, not mortgage approval or proof the monthly payments will suit you.**

**Before relying on a Lifetime ISA, check the timing. A first payment on 2026-11-05 cannot meet the 12-month rule by September 2027. The earliest qualifying date is 2027-11-05. An existing eligible account may help, but the budget below includes no bonus or new savings.**

## Start with your household, not a lender's maximum

This example is for a working first-time-buyer couple earning £72,000 gross a year, with £38,000 saved, hoping to complete by September 2027. Aim to collect the keys in August 2027 and leave September 2027 for manageable delays. Your own budget needs both incomes separately, pensions, student loans, debts, dependants, ownership history, SDLT residency, credit records, ages and the lender's actual rules. Having a job does not establish SDLT residency.

The original supporting notes remain private; the approved workbook is listed under Downloads. Update your inputs, recalculate and check the totals before relying on them. The source information was accessed on 2026-11-05. All money stays in GBP, without conversion. Check rates and rules again before buying in 2027: these dated figures may no longer apply.

## Your cash budget: more than the deposit

| Purchase price | 10% deposit | Mortgage | Cash left after £5,399 costs | Above/below £6,000 reserve |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| £220,000 | £22,000 | £198,000 | £10,601 | +£4,601 |
| £260,000 | £26,000 | £234,000 | £6,601 | +£601 |
| £280,000 | £28,000 | £252,000 | £4,601 | −£1,399 |
| £300,000 | £30,000 | £270,000 | £2,601 | −£3,399 |

The deposit can make a home look more affordable than it is. At £300,000, you have £8,000 left after the deposit, but only £2,601 after costs. At £260,000, you are just £601 above the chosen reserve. The ceiling is (£38,000 − £5,399 − £6,000) / 10% = £266,010. Keeping £6,000 is a starting choice, not a reserve matched to your household's monthly spending. Repairs, major works, furniture, overlapping rent or a surcharge could quickly use it up.

Use [MoneyHelper's buying-cost guide](https://www.moneyhelper.org.uk/en/homes/buying-a-home/estimate-your-overall-buying-and-moving-costs) as a checklist, not a quote. Get actual legal, search, registration, survey, broker and removal prices. The lender's valuation does not replace an independent condition survey. The exchange deposit is part of your purchase deposit, not an extra fee; if you need a smaller exchange deposit, agree it in advance. To avoid relying on money you may not have, this budget excludes savings growth, interest, pay rises, new bonuses, fees added to the loan and cashback after completion.

Could stamp duty change the picture? The dated [HMRC SDLT rates](https://www.gov.uk/stamp-duty-land-tax/residential-property-rates) give qualifying first-time buyers £0 up to £300,000 and 5% on £300,001–£500,000. Above £500,000, first-time relief is lost entirely. Both buyers must qualify. Check SDLT residency and any non-resident surcharge. Additional-property, special-transaction and new-lease rent rules are not included here, so get a separate calculation if they apply. Recheck the law before exchange/completion in 2027 rather than relying on this snapshot.

## What the mortgage and monthly bills could look like

A lender's maximum is not a spending target. [NatWest's explanation](https://www.natwest.com/mortgages/mortgage-calculators/how-much-can-i-borrow.html) describes typical borrowing at 4–4.5 times income: a £288,000–£324,000 mortgage on this income, not a house price or approval. [Nationwide's criteria](https://www.nationwide-intermediary.co.uk/lending-criteria/affordability) give a standard maximum of 4.49 times, or £323,280. [Helping Hand](https://www.nationwide-intermediary.co.uk/products/helping-hand) can reach 6 times, or £432,000, subject to eligibility and affordability. That higher figure is not a recommended budget. Debts, spending, employment, age and underwriting can all reduce borrowing. In this example, available cash limits the purchase before those headline borrowing figures do.

For a £250,000 home, here is how different deposits affect cash and payments. The workbook uses the [Nationwide product guide](https://www.nationwide-intermediary.co.uk/-/media/nfi/documents/f6-product-guide.pdf), effective 2026-11-04 and accessed 2026-11-05, for 5-year fixed examples matched to each loan-to-value ratio (LTV). The assumed term is an editable 30-year period. These are initial rates, not rates for the whole term, best-buy recommendations or offers for 2027. Each example has a £999 cash fee and a £25,000–£299,999 loan range.

| Deposit | Loan | Initial rate | Initial mortgage/month | Cash needed including fees | Cash remaining |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5% / £12,500 | £237,500 | 5.64% | £1,369.43 | £17,899 | £20,101 |
| 10% / £25,000 | £225,000 | 5.29% | £1,248.04 | £30,399 | £7,601 |
| 15% / £37,500 | £212,500 | 5.21% | £1,168.17 | £42,899 | −£4,899 |

The 15% option uses more than your savings before you keep any reserve. A 5% deposit leaves more cash, but exposes you to higher interest and payments. You cannot use an 85% LTV product at 90% LTV. The dated guide capped new-build flats at 85% LTV. For the 5-year deal, early-repayment charges were 5%, 4%, 3%, 2%, 1% by year; the stated variable rate was 6.49%, not a forecast. Check how long you can reserve the product, when the offer expires and the full deal cost. The £500 cashback arrives after completion, so it cannot fund upfront bills.

For the 10% option, allow estimated monthly extras of council tax £200 + utilities/broadband £250 + insurance £35 + maintenance £150 = £635. That brings mortgage plus extras to £1,883.04 before food, commuting, childcare, debt payments, savings and actual service/estate charges. The £0 entered for those charges means unknown, not free. At a hypothetical 7.5% rate, the same loan and term would cost £1,573.23/month for the mortgage alone. That is a what-if calculation, not a published product or lender stress test. Work from take-home pay and test what happens if an income stops; gross-income multiples cannot tell you whether daily life still works.

## Work backwards, without rushing into exchange

| Planning month | What to do / when to pause |
|---|---|
| November 2026 | Agree cash and payment limits. Check credit files, income, spending and deposit-source evidence. Ask whether the initial AIP uses soft or hard checks. |
| December 2026 | Gather both buyers' ID, payslips and bank statements. Get itemised conveyancing quotes, agree must-haves and test journeys. Keep deposit, fees and retained cash separate. |
| January 2027 | Refresh the AIP when needed. Typical validity is 30–90 days, but use its actual expiry date. Start focused viewings in the priority districts. |
| February 2027 | Compare homes before offering; exclude shared ownership, auctions and retirement stock. Ask about vacant possession and the seller's onward purchase. Recalculate cash for every offer. |
| March 2027 | Aim for an accepted offer. Instruct a conveyancer, apply for the property-specific mortgage and book a survey. Request the leasehold management pack straight away if relevant. |
| April 2027 | Follow up searches, title and enquiries. Read the survey and price repairs. For flats, get charges, accounts, reserve balances, works and building-safety documents. |
| May 2027 | Resolve or renegotiate liabilities, missing replies, lease acceptance and external-wall evidence. Have the conveyancer record who pays for unresolved works in writing. |
| June 2027 | Review the written mortgage offer and expiry, survey, title, searches and important enquiries. If progress has stalled, rethink the deadline. Do not exchange just to save a date. |
| July 2027 | Exchange only with cleared funds, a satisfactory legal report, a written mortgage offer and an agreed completion date. Confirm insurance responsibilities and that the chain is ready. |
| August 2027 | Aim to complete. Verify the conveyancer's bank details using a trusted number. Coordinate lender funds and removals, confirm vacant possession, read meters and keep repair cash. |
| September 2027 | Allow for manageable delays, not unresolved fire safety or a rejected mortgage. Review rental or temporary options; a collapsed purchase or remediation may take longer. |

An [AIP](https://www.moneyhelper.org.uk/en/blog/buy-or-rent-a-home/what-happens-when-I-get-a-mortgage-in-principle) gives an indication, not a written mortgage offer for your chosen home. The [home-buying guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/how-to-buy-a-home/how-to-buy), updated 2019-09-06, helps explain the process and tenure; check current schemes and reform changes separately. [LEASE's process guidance](https://www.lease-advice.org/buying-and-selling/leasehold-property/process-and-timing/), updated 2025-05-11, explains management packs and why they can delay a purchase. Treat this monthly plan as a suggested route, not a promised timetable. Even a chain-free home can take longer than expected.

## Checks worth making before you commit

- Worried about hidden responsibilities? Freehold still means repairs, insurance, title restrictions, rights of way, boundaries, shared access and possible estate charges. With leasehold, check remaining years, ground rent and reviews, restrictions, lender acceptance and extension implications. Houses can be leasehold too. A share of freehold does not remove the flat's lease and management checks.
- Ask for current service-charge budgets, recent accounts, arrears, reserve/sinking funds, insurance, planned works and Section 20 notices. Agree who pays outstanding bills and convert annual charges into monthly costs. [LEASE's charges guidance](https://www.lease-advice.org/costs-and-charges/service-charges/about-service-charges/what-can-be-included/), updated 2025-12-19, does not mean your costs are capped or every buyer is protected. A future review date does not mean the guidance has already been updated.
- **An EWS1 is not a safety certificate.** Your lender decides whether it needs one. The [external-wall appraisal guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fire-risk-appraisals-of-external-walls-fraews/understanding-fire-risk-appraisals-of-external-walls-in-blocks-of-flats), published 2026-09-14, separates valuation information from safety assurance. Ask for relevant risk assessments and remediation, funding and liability documents. Have your conveyancer confirm whether you qualify for any protection.
- [Exchange](https://www.moneyhelper.org.uk/en/homes/buying-a-home/contract-exchange-completion-when-buying-a-home) makes the purchase legally binding. If major works are unpriced, the lease is unacceptable, safety evidence is missing or the chain is fragile, resolve the issue or reconsider. Do not exchange faster. A low asking price cannot make an unknown liability safe.

If you are counting on a Lifetime ISA, the [withdrawal rules](https://www.gov.uk/lifetime-isa/withdrawing-money-from-your-lifetime-isa) require at least 12 months from the first payment, a qualifying mortgaged home costing no more than £450,000 and conveyancer handling. The [overview](https://www.gov.uk/lifetime-isa) allows eligible annual contributions up to £4,000 with a 25% bonus, up to £1,000 per person. The first payment must be before 40; contributions and bonuses stop at 50. Check your own eligibility and residence conditions. Do not count existing balances or bonuses twice, and remember that a non-qualifying early withdrawal can incur a charge. Opening and funding an account now will not solve the September 2027 timing problem above.

**Before committing, replace these allowances with your actual fees, take-home pay, debts, living costs, charges, rate, term and property liabilities. You do not need every answer before you start looking, but you do need them before taking on the commitment.**
