**Your US$600–800 rent budget can start a useful shortlist, but plan $990–1,667 a month for the full routine, with roughly $1,300/month as a middle target. Start with a rear-facing furnished one-bedroom in Tây Hồ or a quiet lane in Ba Đình. For Sydney hours, you need a home desk ready at 05:00—not a café that opens after your first call.**

This plan is for one Australian freelance designer arriving February 1, 2027 and staying through April 30, 2027. Prices and policies below were checked November 6, 2026, using the Asia/Singapore and Hanoi calendar date; they are dated references, not confirmed February availability or future quotes. Paid-work permission is a separate decision from entry permission. No booking or contact is implied.

**My pick:** a rear-facing furnished one-bedroom in Quảng An/Tây Hồ, or a quiet lane in Ba Đình. Put the desk and a closed bedroom ahead of a lake view. For Sydney 09:00–17:00, that means **05:00–13:00 Hanoi time** until 3 April, then **06:00–14:00 from 4 April**. The exact clock change is explained below. A studio can be fine, but only if the chair, desk and overnight noise pass the test.

This is friendly planning information, not legal or immigration advice; for your paid-work situation, get the appropriate official guidance before committing.

## The bill that catches people out

**“Internet included” does not mean “utilities included”.** Serviced apartments often separate electricity and charge their own stated per-kWh rate. That is the landlord's tariff, not necessarily your direct household utility rate. Some pages do not publish it at all. Get the tariff, meter basis, water charge, tax and service fee written beside the rent before you pay.

**Planning example, not a quoted tariff:** 200 kWh × 4,000 VND = 800,000 VND (about US$31 at the planning rate used below). An air purifier, hot water, heating and later air-conditioning add up. Photograph the opening and closing meter readings. Ask whether power is individually metered and whether electricity has a markup or minimum charge.

- Ask for a written price for **1 February–30 April 2027**, with the checkout date explicit. Confirm the deposit in months and VND, refund deadline, deductions, payment schedule, cancellation and early-departure terms. A published annual price is not a short-let offer.
- Verify the flat exists and the payee is entitled to rent it. A live listing is not verification. Do not send a large deposit just to “hold” an unviewed bargain.
- During a viewing, close the windows and listen. Check nearby construction and karaoke. Ask for a live video if you cannot view in person. Test the desk, chair, hot water, kitchen, washer, safe exit and locks. Look behind curtains and wardrobes for damp.
- Ask whether cleaning, linen changes, drinking water, parking, VAT and building fees are included. Get the **temporary residence declaration** responsibility in the agreement. Get a working contact for the Tết week.

## Entry: 90 days is not always three months

At the dated check, the Vietnam Immigration Department published an **e-visa for a maximum of 90 days, single or multiple entry**. The official fee is **US$25 single entry / US$50 multiple entry**, non-refundable if refused. Apply from outside Vietnam through [the official portal](https://evisa.gov.vn/). Choose multiple entry only if you want to leave and return during the visa's validity; it does not reset the expiry date.

**For these dates:** 1 February–30 April 2027 is 89 calendar days including both dates. A 1 May departure is day 90. Do not book a casual “three months plus a few days” on a 90-day document. Check the granted valid-from / valid-until dates, entries and passport details. Arriving later does not move a fixed expiry date.

The [official FAQs](https://evisa.gov.vn/faq) say the e-visa must expire at least 30 days before your passport. They also say foreigners working in Vietnam need a work permit or confirmation of exemption. **An entry e-visa is not published proof that your Australian freelance work is authorised.** The remote-overseas-client case is not resolved by the page. Seek specific guidance from Vietnam Immigration/the Vietnamese mission before relying on this route for paid work.

The [older immigration portal](https://evisa.xuatnhapcanh.gov.vn/trang-chu-ttdt) points to the current portal; use the current official application route rather than an old unavailable form.

Noi Bai is on the portal's permitted airport list. Use the correct arrival address and carry your approval. Give yourself several weeks of application margin before the Tết rush; this is a planning buffer, not a promised processing time. Recheck the portal before applying and again before flying.

For your visa application, the [official FAQs](https://evisa.gov.vn/faq) say to match your full name—including middle names—to your passport's ICAO line, in uppercase, and answer the travel-history and past-violation questions fully and truthfully. They permit applications up to one year ahead. You need not leave through the airport where you entered, but both border gates must be permitted. At entry, present your e-visa and check the temporary residence stamp immediately; tell border officers if it is wrong. The FAQs do not settle whether your overseas-client freelancing is authorised.

### The landlord's job: declare your stay

The accommodation owner, legal representative or authorised manager must make the foreign guest's **temporary residence declaration**, including overnight stays in rented apartments. The Ministry of Public Security's current guidance says to do it on arrival through its [electronic declaration system](https://tbltkbtt.bocongan.gov.vn/), with the local police fallback if electronic submission is not possible. Show your passport and stay documents; ask for confirmation it was filed. A hotel first, then an apartment means another declaration when you move. This is separate from your visa—not a temporary residence card.

The [residence guidance](https://bocongan.gov.vn/chinh-sach-phap-luat/bai-viet/khai-bao-tam-tru-cho-nguoi-nuoc-ngoai-co-so-luu-tru-can-chu-dong-1792748300) cites Circular 87/2026/TT-BCA dated June 9, 2026. Confirm that the owner, legal representative or authorised manager—not just a booking intermediary—will file your declaration on arrival and again when you change accommodation.

## Your workday starts before the cafés

**Assuming Sydney 09:00–17:00:** work **05:00–13:00 Hanoi time** from arrival through 3 April. Sydney daylight saving ends **Sunday 4 April 2027** (03:00 goes back to 02:00); from then work **06:00–14:00**. First normal weekday on the new schedule: Monday 5 April. [NSW Government dates](https://www.nsw.gov.au/about-nsw/daylight-saving).

That is why I would pay for a quiet home desk first. At the viewing, ask for the ISP, plan, router location, Ethernet access and whether the connection is shared. Run an actual upload to your design cloud and a Sydney video call, ideally during your early shift. Aim for at least **50 Mbps down / 20 Mbps up as a personal screening target**, not a local service guarantee. Test your VPN and big-file workflow too. A fast domestic speed test alone is not enough.

## February: plan for bad-air days

[IQAir's 4 February 2026 report](https://www.iqair.com/newsroom/hanoi-among-top-10-most-polluted-cities-in-the-world-2-4-2026) recorded Hanoi's city AQI **above 180 (“unhealthy”)** at 02:00 Pacific Time. It describes winter stagnation and inversions. That is a named monitor's **historical city snapshot**—not a February monthly average, a Tây Hồ station reading or a forecast for February 2027. It is enough reason to choose your indoor setup carefully.

- Look for windows that close properly and a working, room-sized particle-filter purifier. Ask who buys replacement filters. Check for damp and mould; a purifier does not fix a wet wall.
- After arrival, check IQAir/AirVisual's nearby reporting station and timestamp each day. Follow its bad-air guidance: shift exercise indoors and use a well-fitting KN95/FFP2 mask outside when needed. Do not assume West Lake air is clean because the view is pretty.
- On polluted mornings, keep windows closed and use filtration. Air the room when readings improve. Pick an indoor backup for walks and workouts rather than promising yourself an outdoor routine every day.

February 2027 air quality is unknowable this far ahead. No evidence here establishes one of the five districts as reliably cleaner than the others.

## You land five days before Tết

**Tết / Lunar New Year's Day is Saturday 6 February 2027; New Year's Eve is Friday 5 February.** The [Vietnam Government policy portal](https://xaydungchinhsach.chinhphu.vn/de-xuat-2-phuong-an-nghi-tet-nguyen-dan-2027-tet-dinh-mui-11926080513033257.htm) already reports the approved **public-sector break: 4–10 February 2027**. The page retains earlier proposals below its update; the older ten-day proposal is not the adopted schedule. Private employers have separate five-day arrangements. That does not establish any particular shop's or coworking space's closure dates.

For Tết, the adopted public-sector dates are backed by Notice 11483/TB-BNV of October 9, 2026, following Government Office document 10065/VPCP-KGVX of October 2, 2026. Do not substitute the old ten-day proposal still visible lower down the government page. Private-employer five-day patterns may put one day before and four after New Year, two before and three after, or three before and two after; employers are told to notify staff at least 30 days beforehand. None of this confirms your building's holiday support.

1–3 February

### Get the basics working

Have accommodation and airport pickup arranged before flying. On arrival, test Wi-Fi, activate mobile backup, get keys and a holiday contact. Buy drinking water, breakfast food and essential supplies by 3 February. Keep some cash.

4–10 February

### Don't depend on a repair

Plan for reduced agent, cleaning, food and technical support. Some businesses stay open, others close; exact dates vary. Keep your normal Sydney work commitments separate from Vietnam's holiday. Expect busy travel and possible surcharges—ask the price first.

11–14 February

### Keep a little slack

The official break ending does not mean every service restarts immediately. Use this as a settling-in buffer, not a guaranteed reopening date. If you start in a hotel, book enough nights to avoid a forced move during the holiday and ensure each host declares your stay.

**My move:** secure a flexible first week before arrival. Inspect a long-stay flat early if possible, but don't let Tết pressure push you into paying for an unverified one. If you need a new fibre installation, have a tested mobile/workspace backup rather than assuming it will happen that week.

## The short version

Shortlist Tây Hồ and Ba Đình. Get a three-month all-in quote. Test internet in the actual room. Keep a mobile backup. Land with your first week sorted, because Tết starts almost immediately. And buy your quiet bedroom before you buy your lake view.
