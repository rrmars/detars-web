**If the shopping list feels overwhelming, start somewhere else: childcare, leave pay and health coverage. In this Columbus example, value gear, formula and center care add up to about $33,451 in baby spending and reserves. Add $13,800 of illustrative lost take-home pay and the funding need becomes $47,251. These are planning amounts, not your family's bill or a recommendation to spend that much.**

**Ohio paid-leave caveat: this budget credits $0 in state wage replacement. The official inventory inspected did not identify a statewide private-sector paid-family-leave program, but its vintage is September 2023; it does not settle November 2026 enactment status. Check current Ohio rules and each employer's benefits before deciding how much unpaid time you can afford. Public-employee benefits are separate. This is not medical, tax or legal advice.**

This example assumes one baby due May 15, 2027, with a separate prebirth purchase allowance and a monthly view from May 2027 through April 2028. It covers incremental baby costs, not your entire household budget or exact birth-to-birthday days. All amounts are USD. Source access dates are November 5, 2026; prices, benefits and availability have not been refreshed for this page or guaranteed for 2027.

## Choose care and leave together

Care starts in August 2027 in this example, with nine full monthly charges through April 2028. A planned August 9 start still reserves full August tuition. Confirm minimum infant age, a place, opening hours, closures and deposits before relying on that handoff.

| Care option | Monthly planning amount | Why it is only a starting point |
|---|---:|---|
| Center: JCC Columbus example | $1,980.30 | Published 2026–2027 infant tuition of $1,886 plus an estimated 5% increase; not a 2027–2028 quote |
| Licensed Type B family home | $1,072.50 | Historical $225/week benchmark × 52 / 12, plus an estimated cumulative 10% increase |
| Nanny share, per family | $3,494.40 | Estimated $18/hour × 40 hours/week × 52 / 12, plus estimated 12% employer overhead |

The [JCC fee schedule](https://columbusjcc.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/2026-2027-ECLC-FEES.pdf) lists five-day infant care from 7am to 5:30pm. Membership, registration and places were not confirmed. The [Ohio 2024 market-rate survey](https://dam.assets.ohio.gov/image/upload/v1744211799/childrenandyouth.ohio.gov/For%20Providers/CCDF/2024_Ohio_Child_Care_MRS_Report.pdf) puts Franklin County in cluster 3 and gives the Type B infant median in Table 16i. A Type B home is care in the provider's home, not a nanny in yours. Its historical center median of $325/week would become $1,549.17/month with the same estimated 10% uplift, but that is not the selected center plan or a live offer.

Do not halve a nanny rate just because two families share. The [Columbus Nanny Agency page](https://www.columbusnannies.com/nanny-share-program) gives an undated $16–19/hour range without clearly saying whether it is combined or per family. The budget deliberately uses $18/hour **per family**. It allocates $850 of the published $1,700 placement fee to each of two families. Confirm the split, guaranteed hours, holidays, overtime, payroll, taxes, insurance, host-home liability and what happens if the other family leaves. The 12% overhead is an allowance, not a payroll assessment.

Add $300 setup for center/home care, or the nanny placement allocation, plus $100/month of backup-care reserve during care months. These and the expense contingency are outside the monthly care rates above. A refundable deposit would need additional cash; none is assumed.

## Make the leave-to-care handoff realistic

The example assumes parent A loses 12 weeks at $1,000 net/week and parent B loses two weeks at $900: $12,000 + $1,800 = $13,800. Both replacement rates are set to zero only because personal benefits are unknown. Employer paid leave, PTO and disability may overlap rather than stack; enter the combined net effect once.

[DOL's FMLA FAQ](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fmla/faq) describes up to 12 workweeks of unpaid, job-protected leave for eligible employees, with continuing group health coverage on the same terms. Check each parent separately: covered employer, generally 12 months' employment, 1,250 hours in the preceding year and 50 employees within 75 miles. Private-employer coverage also has its own employee-count test. FMLA is not wage replacement, and your premium share still needs paying.

If spouses work for the same employer, a [combined birth/bonding limit](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/28l-fmla-spouse) can apply; medical recovery is different. The illustrated 12 + 2 weeks are not a determination of entitlement. Bonding must finish within a year, and intermittent bonding needs employer agreement. The [DOL state paid-leave inventory](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/wb/paid-leave/State-Paid-Family-Medical-Leave-Laws) is the dated basis for the Ohio caveat above, not a complete current legal audit.

| When | Practical next step |
|---|---|
| Now through February 2027 | Ask HR for written leave policies, premium costs and 2027 insurance/FSA options. Compare licensed care, waitlists, hours and all-in fees. |
| By April 2027 | Give 30 days' foreseeable FMLA notice where applicable. Agree paid/unpaid weeks, premium payments, return dates and backup care. |
| May 17–August 8, 2027 | Illustrative parent A leave; return August 9. Parent B's example is May 17–30, returning May 31. Revise for actual birth, recovery or prenatal leave. |
| Promptly after birth | Enroll the baby. [Job-based plans allow at least 30 days' special enrollment](https://www.healthcare.gov/glossary/special-enrollment-period/); confirm the actual deadline and effective coverage. |
| Before August 9, 2027 | Reconfirm the care place and return-to-work plan. If either moves, price the gap instead of assuming it will work out. |

## Replace the medical average with your plan

The default $3,218 combines $2,743 maternal pregnancy/birth/postpartum out-of-pocket costs and $475 for newborn care under three months. [KFF's report](https://www.kff.org/health-costs/health-costs-associated-with-pregnancy-childbirth-and-postpartum-care/) was published September 9, 2025, using employer-plan claims from 2021–2023. It is national, not Columbus-specific, not delivery-only and not a worst case or 2027 quote. No medical inflation is applied.

Ask the insurer for estimates for **both patients**, network status, remaining individual/family deductibles and out-of-pocket limits. The [SBC glossary](https://www.healthcare.gov/SBC-GLOSSARY/) helps with the terms: a deductible is the applicable amount before plan payment; coinsurance follows. An out-of-pocket maximum caps covered cost-sharing, not premiums, uncovered care or all balance bills. Embedded individual limits and copays can change the result.

Pregnancy costs paid in 2026 may not meet the 2027 deductible; January 2028 may reset it again. Subtract prenatal spending already funded or outside the window rather than counting it twice. The budget also estimates $200/month in extra premiums and $50/month for routine health reserves. Check payroll deductions to avoid duplicating premiums. An optional $3,782 stress add-on comes from invented insurance-mechanics inputs, not your policy's maximum; exceptional care can cost more and is not fully modeled.

## Treat tax benefits as a possible improvement, not money already available

The [IRS bulletin](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2025-45_IRB) confirms a $2,200 maximum Child Tax Credit per qualifying child for **2026**. An official indexed 2027 amount was not located in the inspected material; this is a labeled fallback, not a 2027 entitlement. [Eligibility](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/individuals/child-tax-credit), SSNs, income, dependency and tax liability affect the result. Cash-flow default stays zero; April 2028 is only an optional receipt-timing assumption, not a refund promise.

[IRS dependent-care information](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/individuals/child-and-dependent-care-credit-information) gives a $7,500 assistance exclusion, or $3,750 married filing separately, beginning in 2026. These are planning assumptions subject to the employer plan and eligibility, not a newly confirmed 2027 annual table. It is not $7,500 per spouse for joint filers or a refund of that amount. Estimate savings from the eligible election and your marginal tax rate; employer and earned-income caps can be lower. Keep 2027 and 2028 elections separate. Do not count reimbursements as free income on top of payroll deductions or use the same expenses for excluded benefits and the dependent-care credit. No unconfirmed tax saving is deducted here.

Before relying on a target, replace pay, leave coverage, premiums, insurer estimate, care quote, monthly surplus and savings. Housing or vehicle upgrades, financing costs, pre-May prenatal bills and exceptional care beyond the chosen medical reserve are excluded. Gifts are not assumed. A workable plan is the one that still covers your ordinary bills when the baby arrives—not the one with the longest shopping list.
