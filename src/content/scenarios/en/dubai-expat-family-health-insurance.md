You want to arrive knowing where to take a sick child—and what the visit will cost. For this January 2027 move, I would compare a sensible local enhanced plan for your wife and children, aged 4 and 8. Start with a nearby paediatric clinic and hospital that offer direct billing. Then compare limits and charges.

Your employer covers you. The figures here cover only the three dependants, with a sponsor salary of AED 28,000/month. **A reliable enhanced-family annual premium could not be established.** Your wife's age and underwriting details are missing, and the advertised enhanced prices do not state how many people they cover. Do not turn those starting prices into a family budget.

There is a conditional basic benchmark: **AED 3,467/year before PSP charges and VAT**, if your wife is aged 18–45 and all three are accepted. The historical GOLD illustration is **AED 14,412–15,478/year**, but it is not current pricing or a quote for this family. Keep those two limits in view as you compare.

These are November 6, 2026 findings, not refreshed prices or a guarantee of January 2027 terms. Before paying, get a written offer for each family member, the full benefit schedule and the exact provider network.

## Start with access, then ask what an upgrade earns

My first comparison target would be **Orient Health Plus – Plan 5**. Its published card shows AED 1 million cover and NEXTCARE RN3. That makes it worth investigating, not a selected policy: the card does not confirm your paediatrician, direct specialist access, co-pays, maternity terms or acceptance.

As a buying preference—not a legal requirement—compare UAE-focused plans with annual limits around AED 500,000–1 million. Give priority to:

- Your chosen clinic, hospital branch and pharmacy, with direct billing.
- Clear chronic-condition terms and enough cover for ongoing medicines.
- Manageable consultation and test charges, with caps you understand.
- Maternity, dental or optical benefits only where they fit your family's needs.
- Overseas cover only for countries and treatments you actually need.

A basic policy can still be sensible if its real network works for you and you accept the sublimits. A larger headline limit cannot rescue an inconvenient clinic or an excluded treatment.

## What you must arrange—and when

The retained [DHA FAQ](https://www.isahd.ae/Home/FAQ) and [Dubai health-insurance law](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2013/Law%20No.%20%2811%29%20of%202013.html) distinguish your employer's duty from yours. Your employer pays your employee premium. It is not generally required to pay your family's premiums. If it does not insure them, you—as sponsor—must enrol and pay for your wife and children and keep their cover valid throughout residence. Contractual patient co-pays are separate from premiums.

The Essential Benefits Plan, or EBP, is the minimum benefit floor, not the only policy you can buy. An enhanced compliant plan can meet or exceed it. Your salary does not remove the duty to insure. The special participating-insurer worker segment is for monthly earnings of AED 4,000 or less; its rates are not promised wife-and-child prices. Check the [DHA participating-insurer list](https://www.isahd.ae/Home/DHAPIResults), but ask the insurer to confirm this family's product eligibility rather than infer it from your salary.

**Have insurance in place for the residence-visa application and issuance, and for renewal—not sometime afterwards.** The [visa-enforcement circular](https://www.isahd.ae/content/docs/GC%2006-2015%20Enforcement%20of%20the%20Law%20through%20Visa%20applications.pdf), published June 16, 2015, says there is no compliance grace period. Its old implementation deadlines are not permission to wait now. Confirm the active start date and visa-system registration before submitting the application.

The exact legal start deadline relative to arrival or an entry permit was not established. As a precaution, align protection with arrival where possible, or arrange suitable entry/travel cover until resident cover starts. A bridge does not replace compliant residence insurance.

### Leaving it uninsured is not a saving

The [official fines schedule](https://www.isahd.ae/content/docs/Executive%20Council%20Resolution%20No.%20(7)%20of%202016%20Approving%20Health%20Insurance%20Fees%20and%20Fines%20in%20the%20Emirate%20of%20Dubai.pdf), issued March 7, 2016, lists AED 500 for each month of enrolment delay; part of a month counts as a full month. It separately lists AED 1,000 per incident for failing to provide the policy at permit issuance or renewal. Repeat violations within one year can double fines, subject to the statutory cap. The [government text](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2016/Executive%20Council%20Resolution%20No.%20%287%29%20of%202016.html) does not expressly state a per-person multiplier for that delay item, so do not multiply it into a supposed family fine. Visa processing may not proceed, and a sponsor who fails to insure can bear emergency treatment costs.

### If the visa is not issued in Dubai

Do not choose a regime only by where you will sleep. Confirm the visa-issuing emirate, employment arrangement and local provider access.

- **Sharjah:** the [MOHRE federal scheme](https://tasheel.mohre.gov.ae/en/media-center/awareness-and-guidance/health-insurance-scheme.aspx), effective January 1, 2025, concerns private-sector employees and domestic workers at residence issuance or renewal. It does not establish a Dubai-style dependant duty for this family's Sharjah arrangement.
- **Abu Dhabi:** [UAE government guidance](https://u.ae/information-and-services/health-and-fitness/getting-a-health-insurance) describes employer/sponsor responsibility for one spouse and three children under 18. Ask HR about the applicable contributions; do not assume Dubai's split or fully employer-paid dependant premiums.

## What the minimum cover actually gives you

The [DHA minimum-benefit schedule](https://www.isahd.ae/content/docs/GC%2003%202024.pdf), issued July 23, 2024, took effect on January 1, 2025. It includes limited routine dental and outpatient mental-health benefits. Older summaries saying EBP never covers dental are not a safe buying guide. An undated brochure may also show an old AED 7,000 normal-delivery limit; ask for the compliant schedule instead.

The table below includes the later emergency and telehealth changes. Use it to compare offers, then ask for the full policy and confirmation of the rules for your start date.

| Family need | Retained DHA minimum / EBP | What to check in your offer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Annual limit and admission | AED 150,000 per person/year, including patient co-insurance and deductibles. Inpatient share 20%, capped at AED 500 per encounter and AED 1,000/year; shared room. | Named hospitals, room type, treatment sublimits and any higher annual limit. |
| Visits, tests and medicines | In-person outpatient share 20%; qualifying follow-up within seven days exempt. GP referral normally required for specialists. Medicines AED 2,500/year including co-insurance, with 30% patient share and formulary restrictions. Telehealth has nil co-pay/deductible under the later circular. | Paediatric access, referrals, consultation caps, separate test charges and ongoing medicine availability. |
| Network and emergencies | Routine basic care in Dubai; emergencies across the UAE. Routine non-network treatment is not a free choice. | Exact network code, branches and direct billing. Obtain the emergency notification and post-stabilisation transfer process. |
| Maternity and newborn | Eight antenatal visits, three scans and 10% share. Normal delivery and medically necessary C-section/complications have AED 10,000 limits including share. Life-threatening mother/newborn cases reach the aggregate limit. Mother's benefits cover a newborn for 30 days; independent enrolment from day 30. | Existing pregnancy, waiting periods, chosen hospital, complications, NICU limits and newborn enrolment in writing. No universal maternity waiting period is assumed here. |
| Chronic and pre-existing conditions | No blanket exclusion. A permitted first-policy chronic-condition exclusion can last at most six months, once only; emergencies are immediately covered. | Disclose conditions and bring previous-cover evidence. Do not accept a fresh exclusion every year. |
| Routine dental | AED 500 benefit with 30% outpatient share; listed care includes fillings, extraction, scaling and root canal. Orthodontics and prostheses excluded. | Usable allowance, approved clinics and treatment exclusions. Children's orthodontics are a separate question. |
| Vision | Routine glasses and elective correction generally absent; hearing/vision aids and correction are emergency-only under the schedule's terms. | Eye-test and spectacle allowances, if useful; compare with paying separately. |
| Children's prevention | DHA-scheduled essential vaccinations included; the preventive/vaccine row shows an AED 100 limit. | How that limit applies, eligible clinics, direct billing, school requirements and non-scheduled vaccines. | Abroad and home country | No mandatory overseas benefit. AED 5,000 mortal-remains repatriation is not medical evacuation. | Named countries, trip duration, planned versus emergency care, reimbursement rates and evacuation. The [emergency policy dated October 27, 2025](https://www.isahd.ae/content/docs/PD-03-2025%20Emergency%20Coverage%20Regulation%20-%20v2.pdf) requires immediate emergency care through stabilisation without delay for network or pre-authorisation. Providers report the case within 24 hours. After stabilisation, policy terms, limits and authorisation matter. This is not a promise that every emergency patient charge is zero. Its chronic-condition rule permits only the eligible, documented and disclosed first-policy exclusion described above—not a blanket denial.

The [telehealth circular dated April 3, 2026](https://www.isahd.ae/content/docs/GC%2003-2026_Circular%20for%20Telehealth%20Services.pdf) specifies nil patient co-payment/deductible. Its effective date is three months after publication: July 3, 2026 if publication was on the issue date. The AED 50 minimum tariff is a payer/provider amount, not a patient fee.

The [October 7, 2025 circular](https://www.isahd.ae/content/docs/GC%2005-2025%20DHIC%20Issuance%20of%20Updated%20Regulations%20Superseding%20Previous%20regulatory%20documents.pdf) did not withdraw this minimum-benefit schedule. Ask the insurer to confirm any changes before January 2027. You can check the [DHA legislation index](https://www.isahd.ae/Home/LawAndRegulations), but still get the schedule that will apply to your issued policy.

Pay extra for usable access and protection too, not just small visit savings.

## A calm route to the move

**During November–December 2026:** ask HR for your own full benefits and whether dependants can join the group at your cost. Group cover is not automatically cheaper. Choose a neighbourhood, a paediatric clinic and a hospital before comparing exact network branches.

Prepare your wife's date of birth, nationalities, health disclosures, medicines, pregnancy status/plans and existing-cover dates. Use secure insurer channels when needed; you do not need to send sensitive documents just to use this guide.

Before paying, get written answers to these questions:

- Are all three dependants accepted under this exact product and sponsorship? What changes each member's price?
- What is the full annual amount, including all taxes, levies and fees? How is renewal priced?
- Are our named clinic, hospital branches, paediatricians and pharmacies direct billing? Are hospital outpatient departments included? Is a GP referral required?
- What would a consultation, tests, prescription and admission cost us? Which sublimits and deductibles apply?
- Are existing conditions and medicines covered from the start? What evidence preserves continuity?
- If relevant, what covers existing pregnancy, complications, NICU and newborn enrolment?
- What overseas treatments, trip lengths and evacuation are covered?
- What is the binding start date, pending-ID process and visa-system registration? What changes if we move emirates or leave the job?

**For the visa and arrival:** do not wait for the final physical Emirates ID to begin arranging insurance. DNI's dependant document permits e-visa and Emirates-ID application substitutes, but confirm this for your selected product. Get proof that cover has begun and registration has succeeded.

Have passports, visa/entry details, sponsor passport/residence visa, IDs or accepted applications, signed medical application, existing card and continuity certificate ready. Photos may be requested. The [DNI family document list](https://www.dni.ae/resources/downloads/products/DOCUMENTS_REQUIRED_UPON_CONFIRMATION.pdf) is an example, not a universal exhaustive list; ask whether marriage/birth certificates or sponsorship proof are needed.

Save certificates, e-cards, emergency contacts, pre-authorisation steps and claims contacts offline. Diarise renewal. You can revisit the saved family-cover guide (original supporting file is not published; use the information in this guide) and [source and calculation notes](#sources-and-access-dates) when comparing written offers.

## FAQ

### Does my employer's policy automatically cover my wife and children?

No. Check the actual employee benefits. If the employer does not insure them, Dubai's sponsor responsibility falls to you. Ask whether paid dependant membership in the employer group is available, then compare its terms.

### Can we budget AED 3,467 as the final basic premium?

No. It is a public starting subtotal conditional on your wife being aged 18–45 and the family being accepted. PSP charges and VAT are extra, and January 2027 rates are unconfirmed.

### Can we use the GOLD total as an enhanced-family estimate?

No. It is historical arithmetic for a hypothetical wife aged 30–35 and specified child-rate endpoints. A reliable current enhanced-family annual premium could not be established. You need a written three-member offer using your actual details.

### Is basic cover always missing dental, and are all consultations charged at 20%?

No. The retained minimum includes limited routine dental. The later telehealth circular specifies nil patient co-pay/deductible for telehealth. Neither means unlimited dental treatment or that every in-person visit is free.
