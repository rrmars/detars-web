You work as a caregiver in Bangkok on THB 16,500 a month, you have about THB 60,000 in savings, and you want to know whether care work in Japan by mid-2028 is realistic without paying a broker most of what you have. **Short answer: the Specified Skilled Worker (i) "kaigo" route (SSW (i)) is the one that fits your timeline. Plan to pass the Japanese and care tests in 2027 and arrive in roughly June to September 2028. Preparation costs about THB 19,920 (test fees from Prometric, the rest our estimate), and the main danger is placement fees, not the exams.**

This guide follows Nok, 26, who lives and works in Bangkok. It was written on 8 November 2026 from public pages only; nobody was contacted and nothing was applied for. "Unconfirmed" marks anything that did not come from an official page that was read in full, or that is an estimate. **It is general information, not legal or immigration advice.** The source materials are not published.

## The recommendation and why

**Take the SSW (i) kaigo route.** Use 2027 as the exam year: pass JFT-Basic (200 points or more, the A2 band) or JLPT N4, then the Care Skills test and the Care Japanese test. All are computer-based tests held in Bangkok, where Nok already works. Aim to arrive in roughly June to September 2028.

- **Why this route:** it is the only one that fits the 2028 timeline. The Japan EPA care-worker scheme (EPA) is open to Indonesia, the Philippines and Vietnam only; the official MHLW page names no Thai programme. **That point is well supported but not fully closed**, because the Japan–Thailand agreement's own annex could not be read, so treat "no Thai programme" as partly unconfirmed.
- **The backup:** the new Employment for Skill Development system (ESD) starts on 1 April 2027 and can take workers with no Japanese. It is a 3-year training status that still ends in the same SSW skills test and A2.2 language level, and it needs a Thai-approved sending organisation.
- **Money:** 2027 preparation costs about THB 19,920 (about ¥94,002), leaving about THB 40,080 of the THB 60,000 savings. Settling in Japan before the first payday needs about ¥186,628 (THB 39,548). That is almost all of what is left, so **do not pay a broker anything large.**
- **Pay:** Nok earns THB 16,500 a month now (about ¥77,864). Realistic SSW kaigo take-home is ¥159,830 to ¥182,774 a month after insurance, tax and dormitory rent (THB 33,870 to THB 38,732), roughly 2.1 to 2.3 times her current pay, before living costs.
- **Biggest risk:** placement fees. Anyone asking for a deposit, a penalty clause or several months' wages up front is a red flag (see the broker section).

Exchange rates used throughout: 1 JPY = 0.21191 THB, so 1 THB = 4.719 JPY (ECB reference rates via the Frankfurter API, 6 November 2026). These are non-primary and **not checked against the Bank of Thailand**. Bank and remittance rates are usually a few percent worse. **Next step:** before relying on any yen figure, check the rate you would really get at your bank.

**Long-term path:** after SSW, a Certified Care Worker (kaigo fukushishi) qualification leads to the "Care Worker" residence status with no renewal cap. Eligibility details (commonly 3 years' experience plus a training course) were **not confirmed**. Kaigo-specific ESD year-1 language level and the SSW rules on visiting-care services, family accompaniment and job changes were also not read: check them on the Immigration Services Agency (ISA) pages before relying on them.

## Study and test timeline, starting from N5

| When | What |
| --- | --- |
| November 2026 – January 2027 | Lock in N5: kana, about 100 kanji, basic grammar. Start a weekly class or tutor. Try the free JFT-Basic sample questions. |
| February – August 2027 | N4 grammar and vocabulary; add care words (bathing, meals, vital signs, handover). Estimate: 350–500 study hours, about 10 hours a week while working full time. |

| October – November 2027 | Care Skills and Care Japanese tests, ideally with a gap before a possible 45-day retake. |

| January – March 2028 | Find an employer through a channel that charges her little; contract and fee check (see the broker section). |

| June – September 2028 | Arrive in Japan. |

## Broker fees and warning signs

**What could be confirmed** (Thai Ministry of Labour page): a licensed agency must show its Department of Employment (DOE) licence, may not use unregistered agents, must give a receipt, and may not collect money more than 30 days before departure; it must refund if the worker is not sent. The state channel charges no service fee beyond necessary costs.

**Fee cap: unconfirmed.** A 2004 Ministry of Labour regulation, **read only on a mirror site**, says a service fee of at most 1× the first month's wage plus actual expenses of at most 3× the monthly wage. Whether it is still in force was not checked, and **no Japan- or caregiver-specific cap was found on any official page.** For illustration only: at a ¥250,000 wage, 1× is THB 52,978 and 3× expenses is THB 158,933, a ceiling of about THB 211,911, far above Nok's savings. A figure of "THB 15,000" seen on a recruiting website is not supported by any official page and is not used. **Next step:** ask the Department of Employment in writing what the current cap is before agreeing to anything.

**Red flags:**

- not on the DOE licensed-agency list;
- no receipt;
- a "security deposit" or a penalty-for-quitting clause (the Japan–Thailand SSW memorandum of 4 February 2020 names these as abuses);
- pressure to pay many months' wages before the Certificate of Eligibility;
- promises of guaranteed visas, or pay far above the ads;
- anyone keeping your passport;
- unregistered middlemen;
- fees for exams you can book yourself at Prometric for THB 450 to THB 1,260.

**Check:** the DOE licensed-agency list (153 entries when read) at https://toea.doe.go.th/LBANK-WEB/main.php?menu=viewer_agent&gmenu=2; the DOE overseas employment office on +66-2-245-6708. A hotline "1506" is widely quoted, but it was **not found on any official page opened (unconfirmed)**. No official scam-warning page for Japan caregiving was found.

| First month's living before the first payday (estimate) | 70,000 | 14,834 |
| **Cash needed on arrival** | **186,628** | **39,548** |

Her remaining savings of about THB 40,080 only just cover this, with no reserve. If the employer pays the flight, the need falls to ¥130,000 (THB 27,548).

## What is still unconfirmed

- Whether the 2004 fee-cap regulation is still current (it was read only on a mirror site); deposit and refund rules; the 1506 hotline.
- Which sessions in Bangkok are for the care tests; the JLPT fee and 2027 dates; kaigo-specific ESD entry rules.
- Official rules on dormitory deductions; the pension rate and withholding tax from primary sources.
- Whether the SSW (i) kaigo rules on visiting-care services, family accompaniment and job changes apply as expected; and the Certified Care Worker eligibility.
- The Japan–Thailand agreement's Annex 7, so "no Thai EPA care programme" is not fully closed.
- Certificate of Eligibility processing time; the exchange rate is an ECB reference rate, not a Bank of Thailand rate.
