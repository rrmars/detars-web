**Worried the bigger salary will disappear into rent? With the $125,000 Austin offer, you have about $3,512/month left after the budget below. With the $155,000 San Francisco offer, it is $1,724. That puts Austin ahead by about $1,788/month.** Your apartment and commute can change the answer; career opportunities matter too.

**Use this as a planning starting point, not tax, financial or legal advice; get help checking what applies to you.**

## Start with your own pay and household

This example is for a 2027-02-01 start, using price and rule information dated 2026-11-05. It assumes you are single, under 50, without children, earning wages only and using standard deductions, with no January earnings or other income. Both offers put 6% into a traditional 401(k) and assume you earn the full 4% employer match. The $120/month qualified pretax employee health premium is only an estimate: ask HR for your actual premium. All dollar amounts stay in USD, without conversion.

Prices are held unchanged into 2027, not forecast forward. Income tax and the employee 401(k) limit fall back to 2026 rules; FICA and California SDI use published 2027 figures. Check the final rules and your own deductions before relying on the take-home amount.

## What stays in your pocket each month

The table shows full-year monthly averages of annual tax liability, rounded separately. Your paycheck withholding can differ. Your retirement contribution is already deducted from take-home pay; the employer match is not money you can spend now.

| Monthly measure unless stated | Austin | San Francisco |
|---|---:|---:|
| Annual base salary | $125,000 | $155,000 |
| Gross pay | $10,417 | $12,917 |
| Employee traditional 401(k), 6% | $625 | $775 |
| Estimated pretax health premium | $120 | $120 |
| Federal income tax, 2026 fallback | $1,392 | $1,946 |
| Social Security + Medicare | $788 | $979 |
| State income tax, 2026 fallback | $0 | $752 |
| California SDI | $0 | $179 |
| **Take-home pay** | **$7,492** | **$8,165** |
| Spending + earmarked reserves | $3,980 | $6,441 |
| **Unallocated cash surplus** | **$3,512** | **$1,724** |
| Retirement saved, employee + match | $1,042 | $1,292 |

San Francisco puts $250/month more into retirement including the assumed match. Even adding that retirement saving to cash left over, Austin remains about $1,538/month ahead. Keep the match separate from spending money, and do not count your own retirement saving twice.

The [Zumper rent report](https://www.zumper.com/rent-research/national-rent-report) gives the 2026-10-27 rent benchmark, not a lease offer. Find an actual apartment and ask about fees and availability. Numbeo figures for [Austin](https://www.numbeo.com/cost-of-living/in/Austin) and [San Francisco](https://www.numbeo.com/cost-of-living/in/San-Francisco) come from rolling crowd contributions, not provider price lists. [MIT Travis County](https://livingwage.mit.edu/counties/48453) and [MIT San Francisco County](https://livingwage.mit.edu/counties/06075) help anchor food allowances, not price your shopping basket. Every nonrent amount is estimated or rounded. Remove utility costs already included in rent so you do not pay for them twice in your budget.

For Austin, the car allowance is $350 loan + $200 insurance + $120 fuel + $100 maintenance/tires + $30 fees + $50 parking. These are cash outgoings and reserves; do not add depreciation again. For San Francisco, allow the [SFMTA M pass](https://www.sfmta.com/fares/monthly-adult-ages-19-64) at $86 plus an estimated $100 for rideshare. The $104 A pass adds BART only within San Francisco, not a Peninsula commute. Check your actual office and journey before choosing either transport budget.

## The choices that could change your decision

- **Rent:** at about $2,642/month in San Francisco, the offered salaries would leave equal cash after this budget, with everything else unchanged. Each $500 rent change moves monthly cash left by $500. That does not mean a comparable apartment is available at that price: look for a real home before counting the saving.
- **Salary:** San Francisco would need about $194,866 annual base to leave the same annualized cash as Austin. This calculation covers $80,000–$250,000, not every salary or household. Recalculate rather than extending it to a different situation.
- **Car:** replace San Francisco's $186 transit allowance with the estimated $1,150 car budget and you lose another $964/month. Replace the allowance; do not add both in full. A different office or parking arrangement needs its own budget.
- **Lifestyle and benefits:** another $500/month of spending means $500 less left over. Ask for the actual health premium, deductible, provider network, match eligibility and vesting. Add your debts, dependants, equity, bonuses and relocation support separately; they are not included here.

## Leave enough cash for the move, not just a normal month

Starting on 2027-02-01 gives 11 employed months. With no January or other income, the average take-home after annual tax liability is $7,559 / $8,284, leaving $3,579 / $1,843 for Austin / San Francisco per employed month. Full-year deductions explain the difference from the table above. Actual withholding can be higher until filing settles the bill. January living costs, moving, deposits and furniture are not paid out of these figures. Set aside move-in cash instead of relying on a refund.

The income-tax estimates fall back to [IRS 2026 adjustments](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-releases-tax-inflation-adjustments-for-tax-year-2026-including-amendments-from-the-one-big-beautiful-bill), the [IRS publication index](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/inflation-adjusted-tax-items-by-tax-year), [California's 2026 schedule](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/about-ftb/newsroom/tax-news/2026/10.html) and the [2026 retirement limit](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/401k-limit-increases-to-24500-for-2026-ira-limit-increases-to-7500). Final 2027 equivalents were not confirmed for these estimates. Payroll figures use [SSA](https://www.ssa.gov/cola/factsheets/2027.html) and [EDD](https://edd.ca.gov/en/payroll_taxes/tax-rated-employers), including the latter's 1.4% SDI. Before making a commitment, check the applicable official schedules with payroll or a qualified adviser.

Check [traditional 401(k) treatment](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/401k-resource-guide-plan-participants-401k-plan-overview), [California benefit treatment](https://edd.ca.gov/siteassets/files/pdf_pub_ctr/de231eb.pdf) and [federal qualified health-benefit treatment](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15b) against your own benefits. [Texas's state publication](https://gov.texas.gov/uploads/files/business/txadvantage.pdf) supports the no-personal-income-tax allowance. Employer-provided insurance alone does not make your premium qualify for pretax treatment. These links were accessed on 2026-11-05, not freshly checked for your move; confirm the terms before relying on them.

## Before you say yes

1. Price a real apartment, including its fees, and test the exact commute. Keep recurring bills, refundable deposits and nonrefundable setup in separate pots.
2. Get payroll and benefits in writing. Refresh tax schedules, health deductions and retirement eligibility. If you have January income or contributions from a previous job, recalculate.
3. Put the team, manager, job stability, learning, future pay, social network and neighborhood beside the cash gap. Austin leaves more cash in this example. Choose San Francisco if its specific career or lifestyle benefits are worth the extra cost to you.
