You are 30, you have seven years of acute-care experience and ¥3,500,000 saved, and you want to work as a registered nurse (RN) in Australia from 2028. You do not know which registration route applies, whether you must fly to Australia to sit exams, whether your English is good enough, or whether the money lasts. **Short answer: plan for the outcomes-based assessment (Stream B) as a strong expectation, not a confirmed result, because only Ahpra's Self-check can assign your stream. The NCLEX-RN can be sat in Osaka, and only the OSCE needs a trip to Australia. English is the biggest risk to the 2028 date: the required scores are listed below. The budget comes to A$26,775 / ¥2,916,503, of which 59% is our own placeholder allowance, not quoted prices.**

## The recommendation and why

- **Registration stream: expect Stream B, but it is not confirmed.** Stream B means Orientation Part 1, a Portfolio, the NCLEX-RN, then the OSCE in Australia. Japan is not on the Nursing and Midwifery Board of Australia (NMBA) list of approved "comparable" jurisdictions (UK, Ireland, US, British Columbia and Ontario, Singapore, Spain), so the fast-track standard for internationally qualified registered nurses cannot be used on a Japanese qualification and Japanese practice alone. The NMBA's own page also lists Japan among the countries whose nursing qualifications are unlikely to meet its quality-assurance criterion, which is what sends a bachelor-level qualification to Stream B rather than the shortest route, Stream A. **The stream is assigned only when you complete the Self-check, and this reading is our inference from the published criteria. Next step: complete the Self-check on the Ahpra portal (about August 2027 in the timeline below) before you spend on anything that depends on the stream.**
- **Where the exams are sat:** the NCLEX-RN can be sat in your home city. Pearson VUE lists an Osaka test centre (Umeda). **Only the OSCE needs travel to Australia**, in person, in Adelaide or Melbourne.
- **English is your biggest risk to the 2028 date.** You estimate IELTS 6.5. The Boards need IELTS Academic 7 overall, 7 in listening, reading and speaking, and 6.5 in writing (or the OET, PTE, TOEFL or Cambridge equivalents in the table below). Results must be no more than two years old when you lodge the registration application. **Next step: start preparing now and book a first sitting in the first half of 2027, keeping a second sitting in reserve.**
- **Visa: the Skills in Demand visa (subclass 482), Core Skills stream, with an employer sponsor, is the realistic first visa.** Registered Nurse occupations are on the Core Skills Occupation List, and the department is currently processing healthcare 482 applications as a priority. Permanent residence can follow through an employer-nominated 186 or the points-tested 189 and 190 (an indicative 70 points for you for the 189, before invitation cut-offs; see the visa table).
- **Do not count on a Working Holiday visa as a bridge.** For a Japanese passport the age limit is 18 to 30 at the time of application. You are 30 now, so it works only if you apply before your 31st birthday, which is before 2028. It also does not let you work as an RN, because that needs Ahpra registration.
- **Money:** the budget below comes to A$26,775 / ¥2,916,503, of which A$10,886 / ¥1,185,810 is officially priced and A$15,889 / ¥1,730,693 is our own allowance. Against ¥3,500,000 of savings that leaves about ¥583,497, which is thin once a retake or the visa fee is paid by you.
- **Realistic start date:** registration around the second half of 2028 if each step goes right the first time; a first RN shift is more likely from late 2028 to early 2029. Ahpra advises not to resign or move until registration is approved.

## Which registration pathway applies to a Japan-qualified RN

| Pathway | What it needs |
| --- | --- |
| **Fast-track standard for internationally qualified RNs** (in force from 23 April 2025) | Pathway 1 needs a nursing qualification from an approved comparable jurisdiction plus at least 1,800 hours as an RN there since 1 January 2017. Pathway 2 needs a qualification from outside those jurisdictions obtained since 1 January 2017, plus having passed an exam process for internationally qualified RNs in a comparable jurisdiction, general RN registration there, and 1,800 hours of practice there. A Japan-qualified nurse practising in Japan meets neither. The approved list is UK, Ireland, US, British Columbia and Ontario, Singapore and Spain. The list is "as amended from time to time" and the standard is due for review by April 2028; the list we read is dated April 2025 and we found no later list as of 8 November 2026, so re-check before you start. |
| **Existing assessment for internationally qualified nurses (the route we expect for you)** | The Self-check assigns Stream A, B or C by testing the qualification against three criteria. **Stream B** means the qualification meets criteria 1 and 3 (recognised for registration in its country, and academic level at least an Australian bachelor level) without meeting criterion 2, the quality-assurance criterion. Stream B candidates complete Orientation Part 1, the Portfolio, then the outcomes-based assessment: the NCLEX-RN (the multiple-choice exam) and the OSCE. |

**Counterintuitive finding:** a route through a comparable country (Pathway 2) sounds like a shortcut, but it would mean passing a regulatory exam and working 1,800 hours in the UK, Ireland, the US, Canada (British Columbia or Ontario), Singapore or Spain first. For someone starting in Osaka that is longer and likely costlier than the NCLEX-plus-OSCE route, so it is not a substitute.

Two related points. A passed NCLEX-RN can be reused: if you pass within 10 years of establishing your Portfolio, evidence of the pass satisfies the multiple-choice stage. And Ahpra will not give visa advice and does not endorse any NCLEX or OSCE preparation course.

## Step-by-step timeline

Process times below come from Ahpra and NMBA pages. The calendar months are our planning estimate, built backwards from a 2028 start; they are not official dates.

1. **Now to mid 2027: English test (planning estimate).** Sit once by about mid 2027 and keep a second sitting in reserve. Computer IELTS Academic at an Eiken centre costs ¥29,700 per sitting from 1 September 2026. The IELTS One Skill Retake (¥19,250, within 60 days, once per original test) is accepted by the Boards. Results must be within two years of the day you lodge the registration application.
2. **Self-check on the Ahpra portal (planning estimate: August 2027).** An agent cannot do the Self-check for you. Enter the exact wording from your degree certificate. If you are assigned Stream A or B, pay the A$410 assessment fee, which covers Orientation Part 1 (about 1 hour; 90 days to finish it).
3. **Portfolio:** qualification and identity documents. After the Portfolio is established you have 180 days to finish the NCLEX stage.
4. **NCLEX-RN in Osaka (planning estimate: October to December 2027).** Ahpra confirms eligibility within 4 weeks; you then have 90 days from the authorisation to test to sit; results come within six weeks; the Pearson VUE registration is valid for 365 days. Fees are US$350 plus US$150 international scheduling in the base case.
5. **OSCE fee, visa for the trip, then the exam (planning estimate: February to May 2028).** You have 120 days to pay the A$4,000 fee once the OSCE stage opens. Ahpra then gives you a letter for the visa application (up to 4 weeks) and will not offer a date until you send the granted visa. After you pay you have 180 days to sit; the confirmation letter arrives 4 to 8 weeks before the date; results come within 8 weeks. The OSCE is 10 stations of 10 minutes each. **Do not book travel before the confirmation letter.**
6. **Registration application (planning estimate: mid 2028).** Application fee A$346 and registration fee A$201. Ahpra aims for 4 to 6 weeks on a complete application, and an estimated 8 to 10 weeks where an NMBA decision is needed. English evidence, criminal history, recency of practice and indemnity standards must be met. Orientation Part 2 (2 to 3 hours) is due within six months of registration.
7. **Skills assessment (ANMAC) and visa.** A skills assessment is needed for the 189 and 190, and the 482 requires one only where the rules make it mandatory for the occupation. Ahpra's own advice is registration first, then visa, then employment.
8. **Employer and arrival (planning estimate: late 2028 to early 2029).** The 482 requires an approved employer sponsor to nominate you at or above A$79,423 (for nominations lodged from 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027).

## English test scores that are accepted

Minimum scores for tests taken on or after 23 April 2026 (the Boards updated them on that date to align with Home Affairs). Results come from one sitting, or at most two sittings within 12 months from the same provider.
