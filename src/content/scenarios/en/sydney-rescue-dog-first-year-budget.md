**Worried that adopting means stretching both your budget and your working day? It can work, conditionally. For this Marrickville couple, I would start with a well-matched adult rescue, stagger office days and allow $9,123.61 in the first year for the plan with one daycare day a week. Keep at least $3,000 in separate emergency cash. Neither amount guarantees that a particular dog or vet bill will fit.**

With chosen 10% breathing room, the rounded saving targets are $850/month in the first year and about $700/month thereafter. If all three office days overlap and each needs daycare, the first-year allocation rises to $15,363.61; the rounded target becomes $1,450/month. These are planning targets, not provider or insurance quotes.

This example is for a couple renting a two-bedroom apartment, each working three office days, planning adoption in early 2027. All costs are AUD. Published prices and rules were checked on November 6, 2026; they have not been refreshed for this guide. Recheck prices, permissions and the chosen dog's needs before committing. Without your income, debt, rent and savings, there is no unconditional affordability answer.

## Start with your roster, not the cheapest adoption fee

If one person works Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday and the other Wednesday, Thursday and Friday, someone is home on four weekdays. Wednesday still needs care. Working from home also needs room for toileting, exercise, feeding and settling around meetings.

At $65/day over 48 weeks, reducing shared office days from three to one saves $6,240/year. That matters much more than choosing a cheaper adoption fee. But the saving only works if the individual dog can manage the routine and your work arrangement is dependable.

Try moving the relevant monthly target into a dog fund for a few pay cycles. Use surplus after household bills, debt payments and normal savings. If it needs credit or drains the household emergency fund, wait.

Have around $4,710 available initially: $600 adoption allowance, $450 equipment, $360 course, $300 future-cleaning savings and the separate $3,000 reserve. Routine bills and insurance or daycare prepayments come on top. The first four amounts are already in the annual allocation below; do not add them twice. The monthly targets do not build a fresh emergency reserve for you.

## Get rental permission before bringing the dog home

For an existing ordinary NSW tenancy, complete Part A of the [standard pet application](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/rental-forms-surveys-and-data/resources/form-to-apply-to-keep-a-pet-a-rental-property). Every named tenant must sign. Send it to the landlord or agent and keep delivery evidence. The landlord uses Part B to respond.

The [NSW Fair Trading guidance](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/rules/pets-rentals) gives the landlord 21 calendar days, counted from the day after delivery. Weekends and public holidays count. Posting needs an additional seven working days for delivery; an application without all tenant signatures is invalid and does not start the clock. No response within the deadline means approval without conditions.

Consent lasts for that pet while it lives at the property, even if the landlord, agent or agreement changes. Moving needs new consent. Strata is separate: read the by-laws and seek owners-corporation approval if required. A blanket strata ban on all pets is not valid, but landlord consent does not replace a required strata approval. Purpose-built student accommodation has different refusal rules; this example is an ordinary tenancy.

### What can a landlord refuse on?

Fair Trading lists eight grounds: unreasonable animal numbers; unsuitable fencing; insufficient open space; inability to keep the animal humanely; highly probable damage costing more than the bond; breach of another law or applicable valid rule or by-law; the landlord living in the same rental home; or refusal to agree to reasonable pet conditions after discussion. The response must explain why the ground applies.

Four or fewer animals cannot be refused on number alone. Fencing is not a refusal ground for a dog kept indoors and taken outside under physical control. No private garden is not automatically insufficient space if proper toileting and exercise happen elsewhere. Other grounds can still apply. A landlord simply disliking dogs is not enough.

### No pet bond, but some cleaning conditions can apply

The landlord cannot demand extra bond, higher rent or other security such as compulsory insurance as a condition of pet consent. Reasonable written consent conditions may require professional end-of-tenancy carpet cleaning for a suitable indoor animal/property, or fumigation for an indoor mammal. If validly imposed, these can apply even when carpets look clean or no infestation is visible. They are not universal charges for every dog owner.

You remain responsible for damage and nuisance, with normal fair-wear-and-tear rules. Do not offer an extra pet bond or promise that a dog will never bark. Describe only safeguards you can actually provide.

### Keep the two dispute deadlines separate

For an existing-tenancy refusal or unreasonable condition, Fair Trading says to apply to NCAT within 28 days of receiving the response, unless an extension is granted. Get help promptly. Do not bring the dog in while disputing an existing-tenancy refusal.

The [change from October 2, 2026](https://www.nsw.gov.au/departments-and-agencies/fair-trading/news/changes-to-rental-laws) is different. When entering a new tenancy, applying within seven days of entering the agreement permits the pet to stay while consent is considered. After refusal, the pet can stay for 21 days. An NCAT application within that 21-day period preserves the stay during the case, with a further 21 days after dismissal or withdrawal. This is not a general 28-day permission to keep a pet pending appeal, and it does not remove strata requirements.

### A microchip is not completed lifetime registration

[NSW microchipping guidance](https://www.olg.nsw.gov.au/pets/nsw-pet-registry/owners/microchipping) requires microchipping before sale or gift, or by 12 weeks, whichever comes first. The giver or seller arranges it. Registration is also due by 12 weeks or immediately on first transfer, whichever comes first—not the old six-month deadline.

[Published registration fees](https://www.petregistry.olg.nsw.gov.au/pets/nsw-pet-registry/registration-and-permit-fees/pet-registration-fees) include $0 lifetime registration for an eligible desexed dog from a council pound, shelter or approved rehoming organisation. Nil fee still means registration is required. Ordinary dog registration is $84; eligible pensioner registration is $37; late payment after 28 days overdue adds $25. Lifetime registration is not annual or payable again simply because ownership changes.

The [additional non-desexed-dog fee](https://www.petregistry.olg.nsw.gov.au/pets/nsw-pet-registry/registration-and-permit-fees/non-desexed-cat-and-dog-fees) is a once-only $198 for ordinary non-desexed dogs over six months, subject to exceptions. Eligible rescued dogs desexed after six months do not automatically incur it. The budget assumes an included microchip and eligible nil-fee registration. It does not claim implantation is universally free. Confirm paperwork and any missing service cost.

## Match the dog to the life you can actually offer

A medium size or an “independent” description does not prove comfort alone. [Norman's profile](https://sydneydogsandcatshome.org/animal/norman-2/) is one concrete example of a dog who cannot cope alone. Bunny and Asher also need more home presence than a fee comparison might suggest.

[RSPCA guidance, updated September 6, 2022](https://kb.rspca.org.au/categories/companion-animals/dogs/behaviour/what-can-i-do-if-my-dog-is-anxious-when-im-not-at-home), supports gradually increasing time away below the dog's anxiety threshold. It does not give every rescue a universal safe 4-, 6- or 8-hour limit. A food toy or short walk does not cure significant separation distress.

Before placement, explain your real commute, apartment, lack of yard, office overlap and backup care. Ask for an adult whose assessed energy, noise tolerance and alone-time history fit. Protect an initial settling period; as a planning suggestion, try to keep the first couple of weeks flexible, knowing the dog may need longer. Start with tiny absences, not a full unattended workday.

Plan morning and evening walks, several toileting opportunities, short reward-based practice, enrichment and a quiet resting place. Tailor activity to health and age. A crate is not all-day care. If you see pacing, barking, escape attempts, destruction or toileting when alone, reduce isolation and seek veterinary and reward-based behaviour help. If group daycare is unsuitable, arrange in-home company instead.

### Nearby parks are not blanket off-leash permission

[Inner West Council's exercise-area guidance](https://www.innerwest.nsw.gov.au/dogs/dog-exercise-areas) says public places are on-leash unless designated otherwise. Keep effective control and pick up waste. Dogs are prohibited in or within 10m of playgrounds and within 10m of food preparation/consumption areas, plus other signed or prohibited places.

- Enmore fenced dog park permits off-leash use inside the enclosure any time; use a lead before entry and after exit.
- Johnson Park's designated area allows 6–9am and 4–7pm, with evening hours 5–8pm during daylight saving. Otherwise use a lead.
- Henson Park's designated eastern hillside is outside the sporting ground; sporting events affect permission. The whole ground is not off leash.
- Hawthorne Canal Reserve permits designated boundaries only. Nearby Blackmore Park is always on leash.

Check current signs, maps and sporting restrictions. For a newly adopted dog, start on lead and build comfort and safe recall. Permission does not mean your dog is ready.

## If you are not sure, explore fostering carefully

A matched adult foster placement can help you understand the routine, once permissions and daytime care are ready. It is not guaranteed to be short, free or an automatic route to adoption.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can we adopt without a private garden?

Possibly. No garden is not an automatic rental refusal for a dog whose toileting and exercise are properly provided elsewhere. But a particular rescue may require a garden or more companionship. Rental permission and a suitable animal match are separate questions.

### Is a midday walk enough on an office day?

Only if the individual dog is comfortable with the rest of the absence and toileting intervals. It is not equivalent to daycare or in-home company. Build alone time gradually and arrange more support if distress appears.

### Does insurance replace the emergency fund?

No. Excess, co-payments, exclusions and paying before reimbursement still need cash. The premium allowance is not a quote, and routine care is not automatically covered.

### Does included microchipping mean registration is finished?

No. Confirm ownership/contact details and the lifetime-registration record. Eligible desexed rescues can have a nil fee without being exempt from registration.
