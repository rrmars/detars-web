## Choose the operating system before chasing the discount

Ask your course and employer about local Power BI Desktop, Excel add-ins, examination software, VPN/security agents and peripherals. The [Microsoft Power BI Desktop download](https://www.microsoft.com/en-gb/download/details.aspx?id=58494), checked at 05:38 GMT and again at 08:20 UTC, lists a Windows x64 installer. If you need it locally, my low-friction choice is Intel/AMD Windows, rather than budgeting for a Mac workaround.

You need not rule out Snapdragon. The [Microsoft Windows on Arm FAQ](https://support.microsoft.com/en-gb/windows/experience/platform-variants/windows-arm-based-pcs-faq), checked November 5 at 05:34 GMT, lists native everyday apps including Microsoft 365/Teams, Chrome and Zoom, plus emulation for other software. But drivers need Arm support. Ask your employer and course to confirm your particular VPN, scanner and exam tools before choosing it.

For this workload, I would aim for **16GB RAM and 512GB SSD**, a light chassis and a keyboard you like. That leaves useful headroom; it does not mean 8GB cannot work. A confirmed 32GB deal around £900–£1,000 could change the recommendation, but there is no confirmed offer like that in this shortlist. Choose the apps, memory, ports and support you need before paying for an “AI PC” badge.

## Questions you may still have

Use these questions to check memory, student eligibility and the disputed display before choosing.

**Is 8GB enough, or should I insist on 16GB?** Ordinary work may run on 8GB, but my preference for calls, tabs, Canva and several years of use is 16GB. The Neo and £899 Surface are not like-for-like memory alternatives to the two leads. This is a workload/headroom judgment, not a new benchmark or prediction that 8GB will fail.

**Can a part-time master's student use Apple education prices?** The [policy](https://www.apple.com/uk-edu/shop/browse/open/salespolicies/edu), selectively reread November 5, says UK tertiary attendance or acceptance; it does not impose a full-time-only condition in that eligibility passage. My interpretation is likely yes, subject to verification. It does not promise a lower price than an older retail offer, nor a discount on every product.

**Is the £749 blue Zenbook definitely 3K?** No. Currys advertising, same-suffix John Lewis specifications and a Feefo-verified owner's complaint conflict. Budget and decide as though it is WUXGA unless the legal seller confirms the exact delivered panel in writing. An older 3K-family review cannot settle it.
