**You do not need to spend this holiday chasing clear water or paying for a beach club every day. For a Texas couple, I would stay five nights in central Playa del Carmen, put Cobá and one cenote into a single driver-led day, and keep the other days loose. The working budget is US$2,400 for two, excluding flights. No March hotel quote is verified, and the latest US travel-advisory page still needs your own check before you commit.**

The example runs Wednesday, March 17, 2027 to Monday, March 22, 2027: **five nights, six calendar dates and four full intervening days**. The five-day outline includes arrival, not five full sightseeing days. Houston is the departure-city example; flights are assumed to use Cancún airport (CUN), but flight times are not supplied. Prices and source access below are dated November 6, 2026, Asia/Singapore, not locked-in travel prices.

Despite the regional Yucatán label, the selected holiday is on the Mexican Caribbean in **Quintana Roo**. It is not a self-drive circuit across Yucatán state: use an airport bus, walk locally and hire a dispatched driver for the jungle loop. That keeps the trip simpler without pretending the transport is already booked.

If you want to walk to dinner rather than return to a resort for every meal, Playa is the best fit here. Beach quality is a practical comparison, not a measured survey or a prediction for March. All room ranges below are **estimates for one double room per night before taxes**, not offers for your dates.

## March improves the odds, not the promise

The [University of South Florida Sargassum Watch System](https://optics.marine.usf.edu/click_saws.html) describes an Atlantic belt that usually peaks in June/July and reaches a winter minimum. My seasonal read is that March generally offers better odds than summer, but it is already a spring transition—not a reliably seaweed-free month. There is no defensible individual-base risk percentage here.

USF's [October 31, 2026 bulletin](https://optics.marine.usf.edu/projects/SaWS/pdf/Sargassum_outlook_2026_bulletin10_USF.pdf) describes declining autumn amounts, still unusually high in several regions. It does **not** forecast your March beach. Wind, tides and circulation control strandings, and the bulletin warns against forecasting particular beaches months ahead.

Check SaWS again in late February and early March, then look for recent, dated beach photos immediately before departure. If the shore is brown, the surf unsafe or rain changes the day, swap beach and jungle days. Keep a pool option and do not prepay several non-refundable beach-club days.

## Safety and entry: resolve these before paying

All three bases share Quintana Roo's state-level advisory; there is no official “safe winner” among them. The readable [OSAC reproduction of State Department guidance](https://www.osac.gov/Content/Report/89194885-afe5-4253-bfab-27eda52cd3cf), dated August 12, 2025, places Quintana Roo at **Level 2: exercise increased caution, due to terrorism and crime**, and Yucatán, where Chichén Itzá sits, at Level 1. It warns about bystanders harmed by gang shootings and extra vigilance after dark in downtown Cancún, Playa and Tulum.

**That is not a verified latest advisory.** The [current State Department Mexico page](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/mexico.html) and its [legacy advisory address](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/traveladvisories/traveladvisories/mexico-travel-advisory.html) could not be read past verification. A search-index result showed May 29, 2026 as an update date; the latest page itself was not fully inspected. Open it yourself before committing money and again just before departure; adjust or abandon this itinerary if its current restrictions require that.

Use lit pedestrian and tourist routes at night, dispatched transport rather than street-hailed taxis, and daylight intercity travel. The readable guidance prohibits intercity travel after dark for US government employees and advises citizens to follow those restrictions. Avoid drugs, isolated late-night beach walks and unattended drinks; obey swim flags. A resort gate does not make these risks disappear, and these precautions are not a safety guarantee.

**Texas residence does not establish either traveller's passport or Mexican entry eligibility.** Passport validity, visa or exemption eligibility, permitted stay and any airline documentation have not been verified for this couple. Check them for both actual passports before buying non-refundable travel. VISITAX is not entry permission. No visa cost or separate immigration-fee amount is established in this budget; confirm whether a federal immigration charge is already in the airline ticket rather than paying it twice.

This is deliberately **not a rental-car recommendation**: no rental quote, insurance terms, deposit, licence acceptance or self-drive route assessment was obtained. If you prefer to drive, resolve those separately and rebuild the budget; do not treat the driver allowance as a verified rental-car package. Keep intercity travel in daylight whichever transport you choose.

## Your five-day outline, plus the departure morning

### Wednesday, March 17: arrive and stop planning activities

Take ADO from CUN to Playa Turística. The dated official reference is **about MX$270/person one-way, MX$540 together**. Allow 1½–1⅚ hours aboard, roughly 60–120 minutes for immigration and bags, 20–40 minutes waiting and a 5–15-minute hotel walk: **landing to room is about 3–5 hours, estimated**. Sequential terminal calls can add friction. Do not put a paid activity here.

If daylight remains, walk the shore; otherwise eat near the hotel. A daylight arrival fits this plan better. For a late arrival, reconsider the daylight-travel guidance and arrange appropriate transport rather than rushing a bus connection. A private transfer costs extra and does not remove after-dark risk.

### Thursday, March 18: beach first, inland food second

Walk south toward Playacar or north toward a clearer stretch, about 15–35 minutes each way from the central grid, estimated. Swim only where flags permit. Bring a towel: a beach club is optional.

Look inland around Avenues 10–30 for a busy taquería or small set-menu fonda. Allow **MX$150–250/person for a simple lunch** or **MX$250–450/person for an ordinary sit-down dinner**, before drinks and tip—estimates, not menu quotations. The food pot averages MX$1,350/day for both across all six dates; tips are separate. Opening times and tables at individual vendors are not guaranteed.

### Friday, March 19: Cobá and Nicte-Ha, one proper jungle day

| Time | Plan and realistic allowance |
|---|---|
| 07:30 | Leave Playa with a dispatched private driver. Playa–Cobá is approximately 1¾–2¼ hours each way; arrival at dawn is not promised. |
| 09:30–12:00 | Explore Cobá's forest paths, monuments and sacbé roads. A local guide is worthwhile; MX$900 for the couple is an estimate, not a quote. |
| 12:00–13:30 | Drive toward Parque Dos Ojos, allowing 75–100 minutes including access roads. Carry water; make a quick lunch stop only if time permits. The nominal slot is shorter than the upper driving estimate, so keep the swim flexible. |
| 13:30–15:00 | Swim in open-water Nicte-Ha. Last entry 15:30; closing 16:00. If late, skip the swim instead of arriving at closing and move it to Saturday using transport reserve or contingency. |
| About 16:00–16:30 | Return to central Playa, allowing 60–75 minutes from the cenote including access road and traffic. Keep the road travel in daylight. |

**Allow MX$5,400 for the driver day, not a verified quote.** Get a written all-in price covering waiting, fuel, parking and the internal cenote road. Admissions, guide and tip are separate below. If the price exceeds the allowance, use contingency deliberately or reprice a shared tour with every exclusion visible. ADO and Tren Maya do not deliver this two-stop loop to both gates; do not piece it together from unconfirmed return buses.

### Saturday, March 20: a genuinely slow day

Try the opposite beach direction from Thursday. Keep this day swappable with Friday for seaweed, rain or a site closure. If swimming looks poor, use the hotel pool and have a long lunch. There is no need to add a ferry or a Tulum taxi marathon.

For a regional dinner, consider Carboncitos on Calle 4: cochinita pibil or another Yucatán dish. Its [operator menu](https://carboncitosplaya.com.mx/m/menu-in-english/) says **cash only**; the [drinks address](https://www.carboncitosplaya.com.mx/drinks.html) redirects there. Allow **MX$900–1,200 for two with modest drinks**, an estimate within the food pot, not a verified dish-price quote.

### Sunday, March 21: keep the last swim unhurried

Beach, coffee, a pool hour or a second attempt at clear water—do not add another ruin. Try tacos al pastor and an agua fresca at a busy inland taquería; **MX$350–550 for two is an estimate**. Read the posted menu first.

Confirm Monday's bus, exact departure station and airport terminal. Fifth Avenue is fine for a stroll; decline sales pitches without stopping. Check bills for service already included and pay in pesos rather than accepting a merchant's dollar conversion.

### Monday, March 22: leave enough room for CUN

This is departure, not another activity day. Allow 1½–2 hours by road, transfer and wait buffer, and about three hours at the airport for an international flight. **Leave the hotel roughly 5–6 hours before departure**, adjusted to the actual bus timetable. An early flight may require a private transfer, outside the ADO line and paid from contingency; resolve the daylight-travel issue before choosing that flight.

## Why Cobá rather than Chichén Itzá?

Choose Chichén Itzá if El Castillo is your main reason for coming. Otherwise I would trade that icon for less road time: approximately **2¾–3¼ hours each way** from Playa to Chichén, against Cobá's 1¾–2¼. Those are road estimates, not train times or a time-zone conversion. Equinox-period crowds are a sensible expectation, not a confirmed March access program.

Use the [official Tren Maya reservation portal](https://reservas.ventaboletostrenmaya.com.mx/) if the train experience itself matters; do not add it just to tick a box.

### VISITAX is separate from the hotel bill

The [official Quintana Roo portal](https://visitax.gob.mx/sitio/) published **MX$293 per adult foreign visitor, MX$586 for two**, through its [registration page](https://visitax.gob.mx/sitio/registro.php) and [public calculator code](https://visitax.gob.mx/sitio/js/validaciones/registro.js?12). Readable text sometimes showed an error; the amount was checked in the public HTML and MXN calculator, not inferred from that error. No personal data, registration or payment was submitted. **The 2027 amount remains unconfirmed.**

Use the government portal rather than a marked-up lookalike and retain the receipt after paying. VISITAX is separate from room VAT/lodging tax, the environmental charge and any federal immigration charge already included in the flight. Ask for those components explicitly in the full bill.

Flights, shopping, diving and unlimited cocktails are outside this budget. Insurance is only an allowance, not a policy: price it for both travellers' ages, medical needs and planned activities before committing. The driver, guide, meal costs, unknown access charge and environmental fee likewise need real prices. Keep the room total near US$850 and resolve those costs before spending the contingency on extras.
