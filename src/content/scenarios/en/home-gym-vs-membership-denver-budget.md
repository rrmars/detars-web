## Make sure your apartment works for the movements

Allow roughly 6 × 8 ft (48 sq ft) of clear floor space, plus room to extend your arms overhead. This is a planning estimate, not a building rule. Try the movements without weights first. Lifepro gives approximate base storage dimensions of 20.5 × 16.2 × 8.7 inches; fitting the bases into a corner does not mean you have room to exercise. Store weights low and stable, keep tubes out of sunlight, and keep children and pets outside the workout area.

Stand on a firm, non-slip surface and use the soft mat for floor exercises. The mat is not a rated platform for dropping weights. Set adjustable weights down gently. If your floor, ceiling or noise rules make that difficult, the gym may be the better choice even when you have storage space.

Think ahead about the 25-lb-per-hand limit and 5-lb jumps. You may need smaller steps or heavier weights as you improve. Keep all plates, bases and locks, but count on $0 from resale; there is no dependable Denver used-price estimate here. A kit that still challenges you may cost nothing more to keep using. If you outgrow it, leave room to change equipment or switch to a gym rather than expecting it to last forever.

## Start with movements you can repeat comfortably

You do not need a complicated routine to begin. [CDC guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html) calls for strength work for all major muscle groups on at least 2 days weekly, alongside at least 150 minutes of moderate aerobic activity. The [ACSM update](https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026/) emphasizes consistency and adapting training to you; home, band and bodyweight work can all help. The routine below puts those ideas into practice. It is not an official CDC or ACSM programme, and it does not promise a particular result.

For a calendar you can follow, start on 2027-01-04 and train on Monday, Wednesday and Friday through 2027-03-26, with the plan ending 2027-03-28. That gives 36 planned sessions over 12 weeks. Leave rest days between sessions and allow roughly 30–45 minutes once you know the movements. Choose a different start if needed; these dates are not equipment-delivery promises. Warm up with 5 minutes of walking or marching, then 5–8 unweighted squats and hinges, followed by light practice sets for the opening exercises. Move with control and keep breathing.

| Exercise, in order | Working target | How to start or make it easier |
|---|---|---|
| Goblet squat | 8–12 reps | Hold a weight at your chest; begin with bodyweight if that feels more manageable |
| Dumbbell Romanian deadlift | 8–12 reps | Keep weights near your thighs, soften your knees and push your hips back; stop before your back rounds; practise unloaded while waiting for weights |
| Floor press | 8–12 reps | Lie on the mat with knees bent; let upper arms touch the floor gently; use wall push-ups if getting weights into place is awkward |
| One-arm row | 8–12 reps each side | Support your free hand on your own thigh and keep your torso still; start with the weaker side and match its reps; unloaded practice does not give the same resistance |
| Standing overhead press | 8–12 reps | Avoid leaning back; use the lightest weights or stand securely on a tube with handles, following its setup and stretch limits |
| Calf raise | 10–15 reps | Start without weights and touch a wall lightly for balance |
| Dead bug | 5–8 slow reps each side | Extend opposite arm and leg in turn; shorten the reach if your lower back arches |

Rest 90–120 seconds between squat, hinge, press and row sets, and about 60 seconds for calf raises and dead bugs. Take longer if your breathing or technique has not recovered. For a one-sided movement, completing both sides counts as a set. Bodyweight options help you start while you sort out equipment; they do not provide identical loading or fully replace the weighted routine.

| Phase | Sets per exercise each session | How hard to work |
|---|---|---|
| Weeks 1–2 · 2027-01-04–2027-01-17 | 1; add a second only when comfortable | Finish with about 3–4 good reps still possible |
| Weeks 3–4 · 2027-01-18–2027-01-31 | 2 | Finish with about 2–3 good reps still possible |
| Weeks 5–8 · 2027-02-01–2027-02-28 | 2; a third set for squat and row is optional | Add it only if you are recovering well |
| Weeks 9–12 · 2027-03-01–2027-03-28 | 3 for the first 4 exercises; 2 for the remaining 3 | Keep about 2 good reps in reserve; stay at 2 sets if needed |

Choose a starting load that lets you reach the lower rep target with good form and the suggested reserve. Add a rep when you feel ready. Once every set reaches the top of the range with at least 2 good reps left on 2 consecutive sessions, try the smallest weight increase and return toward the lower target. If that jump feels too large, keep your current load and work toward 15 controlled reps, or try a lighter band. There is no need to force it. For dead bugs, extend your reach before adding reps; for calf raises, add weight after 15 easy reps.

Write down the load per dumbbell, your sets, reps and how many more good reps you could have done. Missed a session? Continue the sequence instead of doubling up. After a week off, return with a lighter load and one fewer set. If performance falls for 2 sessions or soreness changes how you move, reduce a set and the load for a week. When hinges or rows stay easy at the heaviest setting, look at heavier equipment or a gym instead of adding reps indefinitely.

**Know when to stop.** Stop an exercise for sharp or worsening joint pain. Stop the session for chest pain, faintness or unusual breathlessness and seek appropriate help. Before each set, check tubes for cracks and make sure weight locks are secure. Skip maximal-strength tests and explosive swings. Build walking gradually toward 150 minutes weekly; lifting does not automatically replace aerobic activity.

## Before you commit, try the space and check the total

Measure your apartment and rehearse the movements without weights. Then confirm the equipment's checkout total and stock, or get the nearest suitable gym's fees and cancellation terms in writing. Keep your spending limit, leave the bench for later and start with the routine you can repeat. Reconsider when space, habit or the available load becomes the problem. Old prices and estimated backorder dates are not promises for your purchase.
