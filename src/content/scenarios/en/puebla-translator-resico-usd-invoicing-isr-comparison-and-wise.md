You translate from Puebla for US agencies, you are paid in dollars, and you are not sure whether the Régimen Simplificado de Confianza (RESICO) would really cost you less tax, whether your invoices can carry IVA at 0%, or which way of receiving dollars loses the least. **Short answer: move to RESICO if your contador confirms you qualify. On the assumptions below, monthly ISR falls from about MXN 11,260.60 to MXN 894.23 at USD 3,200, and from MXN 18,526.18 to MXN 1,676.67 at USD 4,500 (this figure uses our own whole-month reading of the RESICO rate; unconfirmed). For receiving the money, Wise looks cheapest on the numbers found, with a cost of about 0.72% on one comparison snapshot (unconfirmed; eligibility for Mexican residents not confirmed).**

This guide was written on 8 November 2026 from public pages only: no account was opened, no one was contacted and no form was submitted. It uses the Banxico FIX rate of 18.6297 MXN/USD (6 November 2026) and the 2026 ISR tariff. "Unconfirmed" marks anything that did not come from an official page, and "assumed" marks our own planning figure. **It is general information, not tax, accounting or legal advice; settle the open points with your contador before you act.** The source materials are not published.

## The recommendation and why

**Regime: move to RESICO (personas físicas) if your contador confirms you qualify.** Monthly ISR, same income, same exchange rate (assumed FIX 18.6297, 2026 tariff, the contador's MXN 1,200 fee treated as fully deductible in the general regime):

- **USD 3,200 a month (MXN 59,615.04):** RESICO MXN 894.23 against MXN 11,260.60 in the general regime.
- **USD 4,500 a month (MXN 83,833.65):** RESICO MXN 1,676.67 against MXN 18,526.18 in the general regime.
- That is roughly MXN 10,400 and MXN 16,850 a month better off in RESICO, under the stated assumptions (exactly MXN 10,366.37 and MXN 16,849.51 on those inputs).
- **Deduction break-even:** the general regime only wins if your total deductible costs (the contador included) reach about MXN 48,096.52 a month at USD 3,200, which is about 81% of your income and not plausible for a solo translator. Under RESICO you deduct no expenses at all, so the contador's fee of MXN 1,200 becomes a pure cost.
- **Rate step to watch (our own reading, unconfirmed):** this guide reads the RESICO rate as applying to the whole month's income. Near USD 4,500 that matters, because the rate jumps from 1.50% to 2.00% at MXN 83,333.33 (about USD 4,473 a month). See the section on monthly ISR.

**Receiving route: Wise** (USD account details, free ACH in, conversion at the mid-market rate; paying out to a Mexican bank account, its route and any payout fee were not found) **looks cheapest on the numbers we could find.** The 0.72% is a single Wise comparison snapshot for USD 3,200 (unconfirmed). Whether a Mexican resident can open USD account details was not confirmed, and the Wise price table for Mexico could not be read. The alternatives, in the same worked example: Payoneer about 3% (upper bound), PayPal about 8% and direct SWIFT about 3–4% (USD 34.80 per wire at BBVA plus an assumed 1.5% bank exchange spread; the real spread is unpublished).

**Two open questions to settle with your contador before relying on this:**

1. The exact RESICO opt-in deadline and procedure. It was not found on a primary page (RMF rule 3.13.2 points to ficha 28/CFF). **Next step:** treat any date you hear as unverified until sat.gob.mx or your contador confirms it.
2. Whether your translation work qualifies for IVA at 0% under the last paragraph of LIVA art. 29, and whether payments that arrive through Wise (rather than a bank-to-bank transfer from a foreign account) meet the payment condition in RLIVA art. 58. **The IVA 0% treatment is unconfirmed.**

**Status of the rules.** The text of RESICO (LISR 113-E and 113-F), LIVA art. 29 and LISR 102 and 106 was read on a non-primary mirror (fisc.mx) because the official legislation sites (diputados.gob.mx and dof.gob.mx) timed out, so those items are marked unconfirmed. SAT pages, RMF 2026, the Anexo 8 and Anexo 20 catalogs and Banxico were read directly. A 2027 ISR reform reportedly passed the Cámara de Diputados on 13 October 2026 (to raise the RESICO cap to MXN 5M); only blog reports were found, with no primary proof of Senate approval or Diario Oficial (DOF) publication, so this guide uses the current MXN 3.5M cap.

## Monthly ISR compared: worked example

Assumed steady monthly income, so the cumulative provisional payments equal the single-month tariff each month. Annual-return adjustments, personal deductions and IVA are not modelled. Subsidio al empleo is assumed not to apply (no source checked). MXN.

| Item | USD 3,200 | USD 4,500 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gross income (USD × 18.6297) | 59,615.04 | 83,833.65 |
| **RESICO**: band and rate | ≤ 83,333.33 → 1.50% | ≤ 208,333.33 → 2.00% |
| **RESICO ISR** (no deductions) | 59,615.04 × 1.50% = 894.23 | 83,833.65 × 2.00% = 1,676.67 |
| **General**: taxable base after MXN 1,200 contador (assumed deductible in full) | 58,415.04 | 82,633.65 |
| **General ISR**: 10,457.09 + (base − 55,736.69) × 30% | 10,457.09 + (58,415.04 − 55,736.69) × 30% = 11,260.60 | 10,457.09 + (82,633.65 − 55,736.69) × 30% = 18,526.18 |
| General ISR if you also deduct MXN 3,000 of other expenses (assumption) | 10,381.44 | 17,626.18 |

**Watch the 2.00% step (our own reading, to be confirmed).** This guide reads the RESICO rate as applying to the whole month's income, the band's "tasa aplicable". At USD 4,500 the monthly income (MXN 83,833.65) is just above the 83,333.33 line, so the rate steps from 1.50% to 2.00% on every peso: a month of MXN 83,333.33 would cost about MXN 1,250.00, while MXN 83,833.65 costs MXN 1,676.67. At the 18.6297 FIX the line is crossed at about USD 4,473 a month (83,333.33 ÷ 18.6297). **Next step:** ask your contador whether the rate applies to the whole month or only to the part above each line, because the answer changes the USD 4,500 result.

## IVA on exported services: possibly 0%, unconfirmed

**Everything in this section rests on mirror texts and on an open reading; treat IVA at 0% as likely-but-to-be-confirmed.**

- **Rule:** LIVA art. 29, first paragraph: residents in Mexico apply the 0% rate to services that are exported (**unconfirmed**, mirror). Fracc. IV lists services "aprovechados en el extranjero" (technical assistance, maquila, advertising, commissions, insurance, financing, filming, call centers, IT services). Translation is not on that list.
- **The route that may apply to a freelancer:** the last paragraph of art. 29 extends the 0% rate to residents in Mexico who provide "servicios personales independientes" that are "aprovechados en su totalidad en el extranjero por residentes en el extranjero sin establecimiento en el país" (**unconfirmed**, mirror). RLIVA art. 61 (official gob.mx text, 2006 original, so a later amendment is not ruled out) implements it: such services count as used abroad when they are contracted and paid by the foreign resident and meet the payment conditions of RLIVA art. 58.
- **The aprovechamiento test in practice (RLIVA 58):** (a) the client is a non-resident without a permanent establishment in Mexico; (b) the client contracts and pays; (c) payment is by nominative cheque or transfer into the provider's account at a bank (institución de crédito) or casa de bolsa; and (d) the funds come from an account at a foreign financial institution. RMF 2026 rule 4.6.5 also accepts payment by the foreign client's own credit card from a foreign account (the three later 2026 modifications of the RMF were not read).
- **Risk with platforms:** no official text was found on Wise, Payoneer or PayPal as payers, so a platform route might not meet (c) and (d). **This is a real tax risk to raise with your contador.**
- **Is translation a "servicio personal independiente"?** Probably, but no SAT criterion naming translators was found. One contador blog (**unconfirmed**) argues that the fracc. IV list is exhaustive and that translation would carry 16%. If 16% applied, your US agencies would be invoiced IVA they might not accept.
- **Still required with 0%:** you must still file the monthly IVA return by the 17th (LIVA art. 5-D, mirror). Income at 0% means no IVA payable, and input IVA (for example on the contador's fee) can be credited or refunded (art. 6, mirror).

### Worked example: route cost only, before tax

Assumptions: FIX 18.6297 for the whole year; two agencies, so two payments a month; Wise 0.72% proportional (also at USD 4,500), assuming the agency pays by ACH (a wire would add USD 6.11 per payment); Payoneer 1% + 2% = 3%; PayPal 4.45% + 3.50% = 7.95% (assumes conversion inside PayPal; volume tiers from MXN 50,000 a month ignored) + USD 0.30 × 2; SWIFT = USD 34.80 × 2 + 1.5% assumed spread; the ISR base ignores route fees. ## Before you commit: what to ask your contador

- Whether you are eligible for RESICO (no excluded income types), the opt-in date and the ficha 28/CFF steps for someone already registered.
- Whether the annual return is still required in RESICO.
- IVA at 0%: is your translation work a "servicio personal independiente aprovechado en el extranjero", and does a Wise or Payoneer payer meet RLIVA 58?
- Whether the 2027 ISR reform (RESICO cap of MXN 5M, re-entry) is published in the DOF before January.
- Which exchange rate to put on each CFDI and which date to use when the funds arrive.
- Whether the RESICO rate applies to the whole month's income, which decides what happens near USD 4,473 a month.

## What is still unconfirmed

- **Statute texts:** LISR 113-E, 113-F, 102 and 106, LIVA 29, 5-D and 6, and CFF 20 were read on the fisc.mx mirror, not on diputados.gob.mx or the DOF.
- **RESICO switch deadline:** not found on a primary page.
- **IVA 0% on translation (LIVA art. 29, last paragraph):** unresolved, and whether Wise, Payoneer or PayPal payers meet RLIVA 58 is unresolved. The RLIVA text read is the 2006 original, and the three later 2026 RMF modifications were not read for rule 4.6.5.
- **Route costs:** the Wise Mexico fee table, the Payoneer Mexico table, the Santander fee and all bank exchange spreads were not found; the 0.72% Wise figure is one snapshot, and the 1.5% SWIFT spread is an assumption.
- **2027:** the reform (RESICO cap of MXN 5M) is known only from blogs, and the 2027 tariff is not published, so the 2026 table is applied.
- **Whole-month rate:** applying the RESICO rate to the whole month's income is our reading.
- **Arithmetic:** the money figures were recomputed from the tariff and the assumptions and matched. That recheck was done inside the project that wrote the guide, so treat it as a second calculation, not an outside audit.
- **No reviews or ratings** of any provider are included.
