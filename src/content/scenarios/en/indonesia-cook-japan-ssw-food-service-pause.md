If you are saving for restaurant work in Japan, a test pass or a recruiter’s promise can feel like the next step. **For Rizky, the safer move is to keep working in Yogyakarta, study Japanese and pause placement spending.** The new overseas food-service route and the relevant skills-test bookings are suspended in the notices checked. Late 2027 is a conditional goal, not a confirmed departure.

This example follows **Rizky, 26, a hotel line cook in Yogyakarta**, with **IDR 25 million saved** and a restaurant-work goal in **late 2027**. Rules, fees and test information are an **as-of 7 November 2026 snapshot**, not a fresh eligibility decision or a promise for 2027. The underlying source file is not published.

Even if the route reopens, the money plan depends on an actual contract, lawful cost allocation, housing and payday. **IDR 6,726,000 per transfer** and **IDR 67,260,000 across the chosen first-year transfer schedule** are conditional calculations—not guaranteed family income. Start with the reopening gate, then test readiness, then the contract and cash timetable.

## Keep earning while the route is paused

**Keep the Yogyakarta job and study Japanese; put placement spending on hold.** New overseas food-service SSW(i) entry is not presently an ordinary open route. Japan suspended issuance of new food-service Certificates of Eligibility (COEs) from **13 April 2026**, following its [27 March policy notice](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/ssw/03_00001.html). ISA’s [**19 October 2026** update](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/10_00259.html) still says applications received on or after 13 April 2026 are in principle not issued. [OTAFF’s 27 March 2026 notice](https://otaff.or.jp/tokutei/en/news/cat5/1894/) also suspends bookings for the food-service SSW(i) skills exam; [Prometric’s notice](https://www.prometric-jp.com/ssw/news/archives/311) explicitly includes Indonesia. There is no verified reopening date in the notices read. [S1–S3]

**Late 2027 is an aspiration, not a bookable departure.** Do not pay a broker to reserve a quota, resign, borrow for a placement package, or buy a non-refundable flight. Language learning remains useful across routes. Prefer **JFT-Basic** for flexible test opportunities when ready; an existing **JLPT N4 or higher** pass is equally acceptable for this field, so do not pay for both solely for SSW(i). Continue beyond exam-level Japanese toward safe kitchen communication. [S4, T2–T4]

**Counterintuitive finding:** passing the tests does not currently solve the entry bottleneck. Nor does the Indonesia–Japan government memorandum create a guaranteed government-to-government restaurant job. Wait for both the COE route and the skills exam to reopen before making recruitment commitments. Food/beverage manufacturing is a different sector/test and job; do not let an agency relabel factory work as restaurant work. [S2, S5]

## Requirements and tests

### Rizky’s basic fit

- SSW applicants must be **18 or older**; Japan sets no academic-degree requirement. At 26, he meets the age floor. Indonesian cook experience is valuable but does not itself waive tests.
- For this background: pass the **food-service SSW(i) skills test** and **one accepted Japanese test**. No qualifying Japanese TITP(ii) completion is established for Rizky; do not assume an exemption.
- SSW(i) generally permits up to **five years in total**; family accompaniment is generally not permitted. It is not an automatic SSW(ii) or permanent-residence pathway.

[S4, S6]

### Before lawful departure

- A genuine full-time, direct restaurant employment contract; pay at least equivalent to comparable Japanese workers. Food service is not a labour-dispatch route.
- Employer eligibility, sector council compliance and a mandatory support plan, plus medical fitness, passport, test certificates, COE and the appropriate work visa.
- Complete Indonesia’s SISKOP2MI verification, migrant-worker social protection, OPP and E-KPMI process. The COE alone is not permission to depart or work.

[S5–S8]

## What the government channels actually do

1. **Check the sector gate first.** Use ISA, MAFF and OTAFF notices, not a recruiter’s screenshot. The suspension concerns new food-service SSW(i) entry; existing-worker renewals and specified transfers have different rules. Rizky is a new overseas applicant. [S1–S3]
2. **Choose direct hiring or a properly authorised intermediary.** ISA says an Indonesian P3MI is optional. A training school (LPK), Indonesian P3MI, Japanese recruiter, employer and Registered Support Organisation (RSO) are different roles. Verify the P3MI in SISKOP2MI’s official list and any Japanese employment-placement permission; an RSO registration alone is not a recruitment licence. For 2027, also check compliance with the support-system changes effective **1 April 2027** ([ISA FAQ Q97](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/policies/ssw/faq.html)), not only the organisation’s registration. This is not a reopening announcement. [S5, S9]
3. **Use SISKOP2MI, not a paid “government slot”.** When the route is open and an employer exists, register through the official Indonesian system. The government’s individual-route guide calls for passport, employment agreement and COE upload, then BP3MI/P4MI/LTSA verification. Follow the current E-KPMI checklist, including applicable family/marital permission, medical and insurance documents. Complete required BPJS protection and free government-funded OPP. [S7, S8]
4. **Employer prepares the Japanese side.** The employer provides eligible employment/support documents and normally arranges the COE application; Rizky supplies personal and test documents. Apply for the work visa only after a valid COE and sending-country steps are in order. Ordinary COE processing has a published standard of one to three months, but this is *not* a promise during suspension/backlog. [S6, S10]
5. **Recheck the bilateral framework for 2027.** The 2019 MoC addresses proper sending/acceptance and abusive brokers. ISA records a renewal for six months from 25 June 2026. Check the subsequent renewal/procedural position before acting in 2027. Do not confuse this framework with Japan’s separate EPA nursing/care programmes. [S5]

## Legal-fee versus illegal-fee checklist

**Two countries’ rules apply.** Indonesia’s Regulation 17/2025 puts placement components on the employer by default, but Article 3 allows qualified exceptions. A worker signature alone does not establish a lawful exception or override Japan’s mandatory-support prohibition. Ask for the component, legal basis, applicable ceiling, payer, refund rule and invoice before accepting any quote. [S11]

### Reject / do not budget as normal

- Employment guarantee, “no-resignation” security deposit, or predetermined nonperformance/exit penalty—even if described as refundable.
- Worker charges or wage deductions for mandatory SSW support, necessary support interpreting, Japan airport pickup/dropoff, or the required rent-guarantee-company fee.
- “COE government fee”: the COE application itself is free. Separately contracted professional work must not be disguised as a government charge.
- An unexplained monthly support/management deduction or an invoice combining employer recruitment costs with worker costs.

[S9–S11]

### Possible worker spending—but verify

- Personal language study and official test entry fees may be paid directly; determine whether the placement arrangement must reimburse them.
- Ordinary agreed rent, utilities, food and personal living costs are distinct from support. Housing assistance does not automatically mean free rent.
- Passport, visa, medical, BPJS, training, domestic transport and international tickets are listed placement-related components under Indonesia’s 2025 rule: employer-default, not automatically worker-payable. Any exception needs a documented basis.
- Japanese statutory employee tax/insurance shares may be deducted. The employer’s own statutory shares are not Rizky’s contribution.

[S9, S11, W3–W5]

### Japan-specific Indonesian schedule: do not turn old maximums into a bill

Decision 148/2023 (P3MI placement with Japanese corporate employers; adopted 11 April 2023; official register still marks it effective) prints worker-column maximums of medical IDR 902,000, psychological IDR 550,000, social protection IDR 370,000 on a 24-month assumption, and Java domestic transport IDR 500,000. Their derived subtotal for Rizky’s Java origin is **IDR 2,322,000**; outside-Java transport is IDR 2m instead, giving IDR 3,822,000. These are **2023 attachment figures, not certified November 2026 worker-payable totals**, and are not a mandiri-route tariff. Current application must be reconciled with Regulation 17/2025 and any later applicable tariff/exception. [S11, S13]

The same decision allocates visa, departure ticket, company service and monthly supervision to the employer. Its historic visa IDR 400,000 is not today’s consular tariff. The embassy now lists single-entry visa **IDR 1,650,000 from 1 July 2026**, before any application-centre service charge; the exact SSW submission charge and 2027 rate must be rechecked. Japan’s own travel rule requires employer-paid return if the worker cannot afford it; Indonesian/contract rules can impose broader ticket obligations. **Do not pay an employer component twice.** [S10–S13]

Before paying, have the exact contract, any claimed exception and the full service-centre invoice checked. The figures above do not establish a lawful bill for you—or give a broker permission to invent one.

### Use this before signing

1. Match the exact legal employer, worksite, job duties, recruiter permissions and RSO registration/language coverage against official records.
2. Get an understandable written contract: base wage, scheduled hours, overtime terms, days off, housing address/cost, every deduction, airfare, renewal charges and support contact. Separate base pay from a headline that assumes overtime.
3. Retain copies and receipts; do not let a broker control the only copies of passport, certificates or online accounts. Have BP3MI or an appropriate official labour-advice service clarify unexplained charges *before* you pay.

### D. The actual arrival-year cash timing

**Use this more cautious cash case for the first 12 months after arrival:** if work starts immediately but monthly pay is one month in arrears, only **11 full salaries** are received in those 12 months. The 12-pay table above is an earnings benchmark, not permission to spend unpaid wages. With the same 12 months of living costs, tax earmark and 10 transfers:

11 × ¥183,770 = ¥2,021,470 cash pay (IDR 230,447,580).
¥2,021,470 − ¥1,080,000 living − ¥60,000 tax earmark − ¥600,000 transfers = **¥281,470 new cash buffer (IDR 32,087,580)**.
Arrival ¥131,578.947 − setup ¥30,000 + new buffer ¥281,470 ≈ **¥383,049 Japan unrestricted cash (IDR 43,667,580)**.

Family still receives the chosen ¥590,000 / IDR 67,260,000 over months 3–12, if the actual pay and costs match. One further month’s net pay (about ¥183,770 / IDR 20,949,780 in this model) remains receivable rather than cash at the anniversary. The IDR 5m home reserve and ¥60,000 tax earmark remain separate. Partial first-month wages, a later first payday or higher lawful costs require lower transfers or more arrival savings.

### Protect the first payday

After the preparation cap and home reserve, IDR 15m gives about ¥131,579 of arrival liquidity. Setup ¥30,000 + one month’s living costs ¥90,000 = ¥120,000, leaving only ¥11,579 before the first pay arrives. If the first full pay takes two months, the requirement becomes ¥210,000: a shortfall of about ¥78,421 (IDR 8,940,000). Get a written payday date and employer housing/advance arrangements, or save more; do not bridge this with a broker loan. The annual table already counts all 12 months of living expenses, so the initial bridge is not deducted twice.

**Preparation cap:** IDR 5m is an expenditure ceiling, not a lawful placement tariff. It is for self-directed tests, travel and personal contingencies only after the payer allocation is settled. Official listed test and visa amounts do not mean all are Rizky’s final liability. If legally payable costs exceed the cap, employer support or more savings is needed; do not raid the home reserve.

### Stress test before promising remittances

At ¥200,000 gross, ¥18,300 pension, ¥10,000 health, ¥1,000 employment insurance and ¥3,500 income-tax allowance, net is ¥167,200. With ¥45,000 rent, other living costs unchanged, total living is ¥100,000. After the ¥5,000 resident-tax reserve and ¥60,000 transfer budget, only **¥2,200/month** remains. Reduce the transfer budget to ¥40,000 and retain ¥22,200; with the same hypothetical ¥1,000 transfer cost, family receives ¥39,000 × 114 = **IDR 4,446,000**. These are scenario calculations, not promised premiums or tax bills.

If yen receipts convert at IDR 103 instead of 114, a ¥59,000 family receipt is IDR 6,077,000 rather than IDR 6,726,000—a decline of IDR 649,000 per transfer. Recalculate from the actual contract, rent quote, payroll band, tax treatment and transfer quote before committing to family support.
