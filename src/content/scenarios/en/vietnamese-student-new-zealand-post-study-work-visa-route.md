Minh Anh wants to study in New Zealand from mid-2027 with VND 1.6 billion. Should she take a diploma or a bachelor, and Auckland or Christchurch? **Short answer: of the routes read, only the 3-year Level 7 bachelor gives an open-work Post-Study Work Visa (PSWV), and it costs far more than the budget. A Level 5–6 diploma fits the budget, but general IT and business diplomas are not on Immigration NZ’s PSWV list as read. The 6-month Short-Term Graduate Work Visa, due to open from 16 November 2026 and not yet in force, is a job-hunting bridge, not a long-term pathway. Christchurch is the provisional city pick.**

Read on 8 November 2026 from public pages only; no schools, agents or officials were contacted. **This is general information, not legal or immigration advice.** The source materials are not published. Several budget lines are assumptions, marked “unconfirmed” next to each figure.

## The recommendation, with its conditions

- **City: Christchurch, provisionally.** University tuition in the two cities could not be compared like for like, because Auckland university fees were not read. Diploma fees are the same at Yoobee in both cities. Rent looks lower in Christchurch, but the inputs are uneven: the Auckland planning rent of NZD 363 a week is an AUT residence price, while the Christchurch NZD 235 a week is an **unconfirmed** midpoint of a shared-room range from a non-primary site. On those inputs Christchurch is about NZD 20,000 cheaper over 3 years. **Treat that as a planning difference, not proof of a city gap.**
- **Route: the 3-year Level 7 bachelor is the only route found that gives an open-work PSWV.** Immigration NZ (INZ) says the visa can run “up to 3 years”; our reading is that 3 years of bachelor study gives about 3 years, but INZ does not state “3 years for a bachelor” in those words. **It does not fit VND 1.6 billion.** Tuition alone (NZD 117,690–122,730, about VND 1.68–1.76 billion) already exceeds the budget; all-in it is about VND 2.8–3.0 billion.
- **What fits: a Level 5–6 diploma.** All-in about VND 0.73 billion for 1 year, or about VND 1.52 billion for a 2-year Christchurch Level 6 diploma. **But on the pages read, a general IT or business diploma is not on INZ’s PSWV list.** From 16 November 2026 a new Short-Term Graduate Work Visa (6 months) is due to open for Level 5–7 qualifications that do not qualify for a PSWV. It helps with job-hunting; it is not a long-term pathway. Its details are not yet published, so confirm them after launch.
- **If a long post-study stay is the goal,** the family should either (a) find extra funding or scholarships for the bachelor (about VND 1.2–1.4 billion more) or (b) treat a diploma as a first stage and ask the provider, in writing, whether its credits count toward a degree. **Credit transfer was not verified.**

**Read this first.** These lines are our assumptions, not sourced figures: non-rent living costs, flights, insurance, Christchurch rent and the VND rate (VND 14,300 per NZD, a cross-calculation, not a bank quote). The student-visa application fee for Vietnamese citizens was **not obtained**; only the NZD 100 levy is included. AUT, Lincoln and Auckland university fees could not be read. Where the page infers rather than quotes, it says so.

## Post-Study Work Visa by route

| Route | PSWV? | Length | Work conditions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Level 7 bachelor, 3 yrs (e.g. Canterbury BCom, Massey BBus) | Yes: degree Level 7+, full-time for at least 30 weeks in NZ | Visa equals time studied (INZ: Level 8 or below). Our reading: up to 3 years | Open: any job, anywhere in NZ (legal work) |
| Level 5–6 diploma in business or IT (e.g. Ara L5 IT; Yoobee L6 Software Development) | Not on INZ’s eligible list as read (the list covers construction, engineering strands, some teaching and graduate diplomas, a Level 5 farm/dairy diploma and some Level 4 trade certificates) | None | Not applicable. From 16 November 2026 she may qualify for the Short-Term Graduate Work Visa: 6 months of open work, Level 5–7, at least 24 full-time weeks. INZ says further details will be published closer to when the visa becomes available |
| Listed non-degree qualification (Level 7 or lower) | Yes, if on the list | Equals time studied | The job must relate to study (a visa condition) |
| Level 7 graduate diploma (post-degree only) | From 16 November 2026, if she also holds a bachelor’s | Up to 1 year | Open work only if she also holds a bachelor’s; a listed graduate diploma without a bachelor’s must work in the listed occupation. Not available straight after school |

**Does a diploma alone qualify?** Not unless it is on the list. Business and IT diplomas were not on it when read (our inference from the list). **Check the list again before enrolling.**

“In New Zealand for X weeks”: degree-level and Level 8+ routes need at least 30 weeks of full-time study in NZ; listed non-degree routes need full-time study in NZ for the full length of the qualification; the new Short-Term Graduate Work Visa needs at least 24 weeks. Only one PSWV is ever granted. Apply no later than 3 months after the student visa expires; NZD 5,000 in funds is needed. The new Short-Term Graduate Work Visa is employer-only work (no business ownership), needs NZD 5,000, must be applied for within 3 months of the student visa expiring, and is not in force yet: it is a future rule, so confirm before relying on it.

**Partner rights (if relevant later).** A PSWV holder’s partner can apply for a work visa and children can study as domestic students. From 16 November 2026 INZ says PSWV holders can support partners and children for visitor, work or dependent-child student visas if requirements are met. Short-Term Graduate Work Visa holders cannot support a partner for a work or student visa, or children for a dependent-child student visa. **Partner rights while on a student visa were not researched.**

**Does the provider need NZQA Category 1?** The INZ pages read require an approved provider that is a signatory to the Pastoral Care Code of Practice; they do not say Category 1, and universities have no category. Category appears in some rules, such as English-language work rights. NZQA is moving to a new framework (iQAF) in 2026, so check the provider’s status at enrolment. No page making Category 1 mandatory for PSWV-qualifying study was found, but **absence of a rule on the pages read is not proof.** Example: Yoobee is NZQA Category 2 and a Code signatory.

## Student visa essentials (Immigration NZ)

- **Living-cost funds:** NZD 20,000 per year (study of 1 year or more); NZD 1,667 a month if shorter. Vietnam can use the Funds Transfer Scheme.
- **Tuition proof:** a receipt showing fees paid for 1 course or 1 year, whichever is shorter. Applying from outside NZ, “approval in principle” lets her pay after approval (usually 10 days; 15 for the Funds Transfer Scheme). Screenshots of bank transfers and agent receipts are not accepted. Tuition is additional to living funds.
- **Health and character:** a chest X-ray or medical may be needed; a police certificate if aged 17+ and total NZ stay will be 24 months or more; a genuine-intentions test; medical and travel insurance is a visa condition. IELTS results are optional but can help show genuine intent (Minh Anh reports an IELTS score of 6.0).
- **Visa charges:** IVL (international visitor levy) NZD 100, from an INZ page dated 8 November 2024 with rates as of 1 October 2024 and not re-verified for 2026. **The application fee for Vietnamese applicants was not obtained.** INZ’s fee tool is interactive, and the non-primary figures found (NZD 850, 485, 286 + 156 and 375) conflicted, so none is used.

## Work while studying

- Up to 25 hours a week in term for full-time study at NZQF Level 4 or above (so a Level 5–6 diploma qualifies on the wording read). **Check the actual eVisa conditions.**
- Full-time in scheduled breaks needs at least 1 academic year of study, a course of at least 120 credits and at least 2 semesters over at least 8 months; full-time over Christmas and New Year needs full-time study and the 2-semesters-over-8-months course. **One eligibility line on INZ’s page was cut off when read, so check the page.**
- No self-employment: INZ says you must work for an employer under an employment agreement.
- Minimum wage: adult NZD 23.95 an hour from 1 April 2026 (starting-out and training NZD 19.16 where they apply).

Worked example (gross, before tax; assumptions are ours):

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Term: 20 h a week × NZD 23.95 | NZD 479.00 a week |
| 30 teaching weeks × 479.00 | NZD 14,370.00 |
| Holidays: 4 weeks × 40 h × 23.95 (if the visa allows) | NZD 3,832.00 |
| Total gross per year | NZD 18,202.00 ≈ VND 260 million (× 14,300) |
| At the 25 h cap: 25 × 23.95 | NZD 598.75 a week |

**Tax and KiwiSaver were not researched, so take-home will be lower.** Jobs at exactly minimum wage with 20 hours are not guaranteed. The budgets below **do not subtract any work income.**

## Total budgets (NZD and VND)

**Rate: NZD 1 = VND 14,300, unconfirmed** (Vietcombank USD sell 25,780 × mid-market NZD/USD 0.554247 ≈ 14,288, rounded; Vietcombank’s public rates list had no NZD line; mid-market NZD/VND on 7 November 2026 was 14,080.76). VND 1.6 billion = NZD 111,888 (1,600,000,000 ÷ 14,300); at the mid-market rate it is NZD 113,631.

Assumptions per year: rent × 52 weeks + NZD 9,000 other living (**unconfirmed**). Auckland rent 363 × 52 = 18,876, so living is 27,876. Christchurch rent 235 × 52 = 12,220, so living is 21,220. Insurance NZD 500 a year (**unconfirmed**; a non-primary snippet said 899, which would add about NZD 400 a year; another non-primary guide said 450–550); IVL NZD 100; flights NZD 2,000 per return trip (**unconfirmed**, one a year). The visa application fee is excluded (not obtained). Fees are held at published values (UC 2027, then 2028 repeated for year 3).

**Even the visa minimum does not rescue the bachelor.** At INZ’s NZD 20,000 a year funds floor, the UC degree is 122,730 + 60,000 = NZD 182,730 (≈ VND 2.61 billion) before flights and insurance. Tuition alone exceeds VND 1.6 billion at any rate above about VND 13,037 per NZD for UC (1.6 billion ÷ 122,730) or 13,595 for Massey (1.6 billion ÷ 117,690); the rate used (VND 14,300) would need to fall roughly 5–9% for tuition alone to fit.

Visa-day cash need (route C): first-year tuition paid (27,237) + 20,000 funds = NZD 47,237 shown to INZ. Route A: 39,590 + 20,000 = NZD 59,590.

Routes C, D and E do not give a PSWV as read; the plausible post-study option is the 6-month Short-Term Graduate Work Visa, subject to the rules published at launch. Part-time work (about NZD 18,200 gross a year, see above) could cover a large part of living costs, but it is not counted here.
