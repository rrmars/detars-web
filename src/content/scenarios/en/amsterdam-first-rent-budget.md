**Moving as a couple for work on April 1, 2027, with €95,000 combined gross income and about €2,200 for rent? Start around €1,800–€2,000 basic rent, then check the extras and each landlord’s income formula. A €2,000 basic-rent example becomes €3,925 in monthly household spending; at €2,200 basic rent, it becomes €4,125. Keep roughly €10,100–€13,600 in moving cash separate from that monthly budget. These are planning amounts, not offers or a personal payroll quote.**

This guide is for two adults with no children seeking one bedroom, usually advertised as 2 rooms. Your first filters are whether both salaries count, whether both of you can register at the exact address, and how much the full monthly bill will be. The prices and rules below retain a November 6, 2026 snapshot; this page was adapted on November 6, 2026 without new external checks. None of the adverts promises April availability, and two are short-stay comparables only. All money is EUR, without conversion.

## “Private landlord” does not mean “unregulated rent.”

The Affordable Rent Act took effect on 1 July 2024. For your new tenancy, the WWS points system matters. It values the home, not your salary. A privately owned home can still have a regulated rent. The advertised price alone does not prove that it belongs in the free sector. [[R1]](https://www.huurcommissie.nl/support/beleidsboeken/waarderingsstelsel-zelfstandige-woonruimte/inleiding)

Published 2027 reference amounts in the November 6, 2026 snapshot — basic rent, not utilities

| Sector | Points / rent reference | What it means for you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Social / low-rent | Up to 143 points; 2027 boundary €959.71/month | Regulated. Allocation and income conditions are separate. Do not base an April relocation on getting a social home. |
| Middle rent | 144–186 points; standard maximum at 186 points €1,263.32/month in 2027 | Regulated, with a maximum linked to the actual points. Some permitted property surcharges can affect the final maximum. A lower-point home does not automatically qualify for the top amount. |
| Free sector | 187+ points and an initial basic rent above the applicable boundary | Usually the practical search lane at €1,800–€2,200. “Free” does not mean no tenant protection. |

The 2027 figures were published on 6 October 2026 and take effect on 1 January 2027. They are not estimates. For comparison, the 2026 boundaries are €932.93 and €1,228.07. Contract-signing date and the applicable assessment rules matter; ask for the WWS calculation rather than assigning a sector from a portal card. [[R2]](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/stcrt-2026-35093.html) [[R1]](https://www.huurcommissie.nl/support/beleidsboeken/waarderingsstelsel-zelfstandige-woonruimte/inleiding)

**April-specific wrinkle:** 2027 changes include a WOZ-value surcharge that can raise a regulated home’s lawful rent above €1,263.32 without making it free-sector. Huurcommissie gives an example of €1,489.26 with 186 capped points. So ask for the 2027 calculation and any surcharge—not just a price-band label. [[R10]](https://www.huurcommissie.nl/actueel/nieuws/2026/10/29/aanpassingen-puntentelling-woningwaarderingsstelsel-per-1-januari-2027)

The [enacted decree](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/stb-2026-267.html) underlies these WWS changes. Ask for the property-specific calculation rather than assuming every regulated home has the same ceiling.

**A useful surprise: don’t write off middle rent just because you earn €95,000.** The published national 2027 middle-income figure for a household of two or more is €97,048. You are below that headline figure. But this is not automatic access: local allocation rules, the income definition, reference year and the particular property still matter. Amsterdam’s currently displayed 2026 two-person permit cap is €93,531, below your stated gross. Recheck the City’s 2027 rules before applying. Its permit system covers certain homes, not every regulated flat. [[R2]](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/stcrt-2026-35093.html) [[R3]](https://www.amsterdam.nl/en/housing/housing-permit/)

Ask for the basic rent (*kale huur*), service charges, heating/energy advance, water, furniture charge and any compulsory parking separately. An “inclusive” €1,950 can be cheaper than €1,800 plus everything. Service costs should be accounted for, not treated as a mysterious extra rent. A short-stay label also deserves a proper contract check; do not assume it has the same terms or registration rights as an ordinary home. [[R4]](https://www.government.nl/themes/building-and-housing/housing/rented-housing/step-by-step-plan-for-tenants)

## Your income can work. How it is counted is the catch.

€95,000 ÷ 12 is **€7,916.67 gross/month**, annualised. If that total includes annual holiday pay or bonuses, your regular monthly payslip will be lower. For illustration, if the entire €95,000 includes an 8% holiday allowance on base pay, base monthly pay is about **€7,330**. These are arithmetic illustrations, not your payroll quote.

Screening examples — landlord policies, not statutory income requirements

| Policy | At €2,200 basic rent | Your annualised €7,917 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3× gross rent | €6,600 gross/month needed | Passes if both incomes are fully counted |
| 3.5× gross rent | €7,700 needed | Narrow pass; holiday-pay exclusion could change it |
| 4× gross rent | €8,800 needed | Does not pass; at 4×, annualised rent ceiling is about €1,979 |

Actual ads show the variation. Bercylaan asks 3× rent for a single earner but 4× for dual earners. Hisgenpad and Elzenhagensingel say 3×. Startbaan says 4× the basic rent. Charley Tooropgracht instead asks €5,000 **net joint** income in its prose, with a conflicting €5,500 field. Do not confuse net income with “net rent,” which means rent excluding extras. See the linked ads below.

Before spending time on a viewing, ask: “Do you count both salaries fully? Is holiday pay included? Will you accept signed Dutch employment contracts and an employer statement before our first payslips?” A future start, probation or fixed-term employment may need extra explanation. No Dutch payslips is a documentation problem to solve, not a reason to invent them.

**Prepare one tidy application pack:** employment contracts for both of you; employer letters stating salary, start date and contract/probation terms; residence-permit or application evidence; recent payslips if available; and references if you have them. Share identity documents only through a verified channel, with unnecessary details protected where possible. Put both intended occupants and the right to register both in writing. The highly-skilled-migrant permit is not a rent guarantee or an automatic tax ruling.

### The money you should not be surprised by

- **Deposit:** for a new contract, at most **two months’ basic rent**. At €2,200 basic rent that is €4,400, separate from the first rent payment. Newcomer status does not raise the cap. Normal return deadline is 14 days after the tenancy ends; where permitted deductions apply, the remainder is due within 30 days with a written itemised explanation. Normal wear is not tenant damage. Photograph the check-in condition and meter readings. [[R5]](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/documenten/vraag-en-antwoord/woning-huren/woning-huren-waarborgsom)
- **Agency fee:** an agent already representing the landlord cannot also charge you mediation fees for that home, even if the landlord pays no fee. Renaming it “administration,” “contract” or “registration” does not fix that. If you separately hire your own search agent to find a home outside its existing offering, a tenant-paid search fee can be valid; agree the service, fee and VAT beforehand. No paid search-agent fee is assumed in the budget. [[R6]](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/documenten/vraag-en-antwoord/huurwoning-zoeken/moet-ik-bemiddelingskosten-betalen-voor-een-kamer-of-huurwoning)
- **Rent versus furniture:** *gemeubileerd* means furnished; *gestoffeerd* usually means floor/window finishes, not a sofa and bed. *Kaal* can mean you need to supply finishes too. Ask for an inventory rather than relying on the English translation; the ads below contain real contradictions.

## “No registration” is not a small inconvenience.

If you move to Amsterdam for more than four months, the City says to register in person within five days of arrival. Both of you must attend. Registration produces your BSN if you do not already have one. The City says you need it for work, a bank account, health insurance and benefits. Register with the municipality where you actually live—Diemen, Amstelveen and Haarlem have their own procedures. [[R7]](https://www.amsterdam.nl/en/civil-affairs/first-registration/)

Before you pay, get written confirmation that **both adults can live and register at this exact address**. Amsterdam’s published address-proof routes include a copy of a valid rental contract, or its original resident-consent form signed by the longest-registered resident with their ID copy. Keep the signed tenancy agreement; ask the municipality which route fits both of you. A blanket extra landlord-consent letter is not stated as necessary when using the rental-contract route. Bring passports, residence documents and relevant original civil-status documents; check legalisation/translation requirements before leaving home. An agent saying “expats welcome” is not registration evidence. [[R7]](https://www.amsterdam.nl/en/civil-affairs/first-registration/)

For an April move, a legitimate temporary address that permits registration is better than a bargain that forbids it. Do not assume a hotel, holiday rental or six-month serviced apartment allows registration. Ask the provider and the municipality before booking. Non-resident registration is not a substitute for registering where you live when you are settling for more than four months. If you cannot provide an ordinary residential address, ask the municipality about the proper solution rather than using someone else’s address on paper.

## The “30% ruling”: plan for up to 27% for a new April 2027 arrival

The **Belastingdienst’s payroll newsletter, edition 2 dated 4 November 2026**, says the maximum expat allowance falls from 30% to **27% from 1 January 2027**. It identifies this as a Belastingplan 2025 measure, not merely a new proposal. Older employees can have transitional protection; that does not describe a first-time April 2027 entrant. The same publication says higher salary norms apply in 2027 and the final figures will be published in its December 2026 numerical appendix. Do not use an old 30% calculator as your April payslip. [[T1]](https://download.belastingdienst.nl/belastingdienst/docs/nieuwsbrief-loonheffingen-2027-lh2091t72fd.pdf)

This is a tax-free allowance on the **qualifying employee’s** remuneration, subject to the rules and employer agreement. It is not 27% extra household pay and it does not automatically cover the other partner. A highly-skilled-migrant permit and the expat tax scheme are separate tests. Recruitment from abroad, the previous-distance/residence test, individual taxable salary and a valid decision matter. The normal application is made jointly with the employer; filing within four months of starting allows retroactive application from the first working day. Start that discussion with HR before arrival and use the [joint application](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/bldcontentnl/themaoverstijgend/programmas_en_formulieren/verzoek-loonheffingen-30-regeling). For an April 1, 2027 start, aim for receipt well before July 31, 2027 rather than leaving the timing to chance. [[T2]](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/buitenland/content/ik-kom-in-nederland-werken-30-procent-regeling-aanvragen) [[T3]](https://download.belastingdienst.nl/belastingdienst/docs/handboek-loonheffingen-lh0221t62fd.pdf)

For context only, the general **2026** taxable salary norm is more than €48,013 excluding the tax-free allowance; a qualifying under-30 master’s graduate has a different norm. **That is not the final 2027 threshold.** Combined household gross of €95,000 cannot establish either person’s eligibility. The October handbook announces €51,899 as the raised general base, with further indexation still to come; the announced under-30 qualifying master’s base is €39,450, also not a final indexed threshold. The taxable salary must stay above the applicable floor: full 27% on a €70,000 inclusive salary leaves only €51,100 taxable, already too low under that announced base. A smaller allowance may work instead. The usual prior-residence test is more than 16 of the previous 24 months living more than 150 km from the Dutch border; exceptions exist. The maximum term is five years and previous Dutch stays/work can shorten it. [[T2]](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/buitenland/content/ik-kom-in-nederland-werken-30-procent-regeling-aanvragen) [[T3]](https://download.belastingdienst.nl/belastingdienst/docs/handboek-loonheffingen-lh0221t62fd.pdf)

### How it changes the money in your account

Illustration: if one person earns the entire €95,000 inclusive of the allowance, a full 27% allowance would be €25,650 tax-free and leave €69,350 taxable. Using **2026 tax rates and credits as a proxy**, average household net is roughly **€5,040/month without the allowance** versus **€6,200 with it**—about €1,160 more. This assumes eligibility, employer agreement and the final 2027 salary norm allowing the full amount. It is not a 2027 payroll quote. [[T4]](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/voorlopige-aanslag/content/voorlopige-aanslag-tarieven-en-heffingskortingen)

With two salaries of €47,500 and no ruling, the same proxy gives about **€6,320/month combined**. That is why the salary split changes the answer so much. These annual-net ÷ 12 illustrations assume adults below pension age, full-year earnings/residence, no employee pension deductions, no other deductions or income, and no transferable unused partner credit. Health premiums are paid separately. Holiday pay may arrive as a lump sum, and an April arrival creates a part-year tax situation. Ask payroll for a regular-month net estimate *without* the allowance first; treat any approved benefit as extra breathing room.

One adjacent tax trap: a new 2027 ruling does not restore the old partial non-resident treatment for overseas assets. Have payroll or your adviser separate that question from the salary allowance. [[T2]](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/buitenland/content/ik-kom-in-nederland-werken-30-procent-regeling-aanvragen)

## €2,200 rent is not a €2,200 housing budget.

I would search around **€1,800–€2,000 basic rent** first. If your €2,200 ceiling means the whole housing bill, aim around €1,800–€1,900 basic rent and check every extra. The example below is for two adults, no car and no children. **All non-rent figures are planning estimates, not supplier quotes or confirmed 2027 tariffs.**

Monthly example — euros for the household

| Item | Plan | What can change it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic rent | €2,000 | Chosen target, within the live-ad sample; not a market average |
| Building service costs | €100 estimate | Many ads omit the amount; ask before comparing |
| Heating / electricity | €180 estimate | Contract, insulation and usage; district/block heating can be separate |

Housing alone in this example is **€2,305/month** before internet and local charges, or €2,505 at €2,200 basic rent. Avoid double-counting inclusions: Strekkerweg’s advertised €1,950 already includes advances for gas, water, electricity and internet. Keep annual bills and utility reconciliation money aside.

Against the conservative single-earner no-ruling proxy of €5,040, the €3,925 plan leaves about **€1,115/month** before savings goals, travel, furniture and unexpected costs. Employee pension deductions and a lower regular-month payment can shrink that. Do not commit the tax benefit before it is approved.

### Keep moving cash separate from monthly spending

At €2,200 basic rent, first month plus a maximum two-month deposit is **€6,600**. Add an estimated €1,500–€3,000 for setup/furniture and €2,000–€4,000 for a temporary bridge if needed: roughly **€10,100–€13,600 ready cash**, excluding international moving costs, flights and any independently hired search agent. The deposit is tied-up cash, not a recurring expense. A bare flat can cost more to set up. Temporary accommodation estimates are allowances, not live quotes.

People coming to live or work in the Netherlands generally need Dutch basic health insurance promptly and within four months, with cover from the applicable start date. Do not treat those four months as free cover. Check your individual situation. [[R8]](https://www.government.nl/documents/question-and-answer/health-insurance/when-do-i-need-to-take-out-health-insurance-if-i-come-to-live-in-the-netherlands)

## Be quick with documents. Be slow with money.

- Do not pay to unlock a viewing, reserve keys that will be mailed to you, or satisfy an “owner abroad” story. Verify the actual apartment and the person authorised to let it.
- Find the agency’s real website independently. Check its company details and domain. A copied logo and a portal listing are not proof; genuine agency accounts can be compromised.
- For a private landlord, check ownership through the Kadaster or the authority of the representative. Match the contract, recipient and payment instructions before transferring anything.
- Ask for a real viewing, or a live viewing by a trusted person acting for you. Reverse-search suspicious photos. Do not send an unprotected passport pack to a random chat account.
- Insist on written terms: both occupants, registration, basic rent and extras, deposit, inventory, start/end dates and notice. No-registration offers fail an essential need for this move.
- A cheap regulated home is not automatically a scam. Equally, a €2,200 ad is not automatically lawful free-sector rent. Check the WWS score and the explanation for the price.

Practical safeguards informed by [Pararius’s scam guidance [R9]](https://www.pararius.com/info/avoid-scams). No platform can guarantee every ad.

## Use the next few months to remove friction.

- **November–December 2026:** confirm where both jobs are based, the income split, holiday-pay treatment, probation, relocation support and commute reimbursement. Ask HR for a no-ruling net-pay estimate and the tax-application plan. Prepare original registration documents.
- **January 2027:** recheck final tax norms, municipal housing-permit rules and insurance premiums. Decide whether €2,200 is basic rent or all housing costs. Prepare your document pack and your shortlist of stations.
- **February–March:** make the active April search your priority. Respond promptly to suitable new ads, after checking income criteria. This is a suggested search window, not a promise that listings only appear then. Have a registration-compatible temporary bridge as a fallback.
- **Before signing:** confirm two-person occupancy and registration, the WWS breakdown, total monthly amount, photos of the actual unit, inventory, deposit and contract/notice terms. Ask why a fixed end date or minimum term applies; do not assume every short-stay offer is an ordinary tenancy.
- **On arrival:** document the handover and meters; register where you live; arrange insurance and utilities as applicable. Keep enough cash for the gap before your first salary and any delayed tax decision.

**My pick for your starting strategy:** efficient one-bedroom homes around €1,800–€2,000 in Noord, Diemen or Amstelveen, with Haarlem as an alternative if the full commute works. Keep middle rent on a separate “eligibility to check” list. Don’t pay four extra months of rent just to secure a November ad for April.
