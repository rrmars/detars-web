**Worried that a candle will sell but barely pay for your time? At an $18 item price, this example leaves only about $0.81 per sale after labour and fixed costs. Start by paying yourself, then see what is left. The Ohio / US Etsy example below is not a recommended launch price, a safety certification or a promise of demand.**

*Use this as a planning starting point, not tax or legal advice; a qualified adviser can help check what applies to you.*

Planning a January 2027 launch? Use the prices and rules checked on 2026-11-05 as a starting point, then check them again before selling. All amounts stay in USD, without conversion. Your actual supplier bill, parcel and sales channel need their own checks.

## Start with the work you actually do

Start with one candle per order and an 8 oz net fill by weight. Allow 12 active minutes at $18/hour, 5% material loss, $0.50 for inbound freight and nonrecoverable tax, and $0.30 for other variable costs. For this example, assume 100 sales/month, $50 monthly overhead plus $1 for extra unsold renewals, and 20% of orders attributed at a 15% Offsite Ads rate. There is no discount. Buyer sales tax is an illustrative 7.5%, with all shipping taxed for the processing-fee calculation. Replace these estimates with your own costs; they do not establish demand.

For postage, assume a 24 oz packed parcel going to zone 5, with $14.85 retail postage charged to the buyer. Set aside $3.60 labour per candle. Monthly fixed costs total $51, or $0.51 per candle at the expected sales volume. The $0 entries for startup recovery and tax on seller fees mean not filled in yet, not free. Paid Etsy Ads at $0 means switched off.

| Item price | Left after labour, before fixed costs | Profit after fixed costs | Break-even candles/month | Candles for $500 total owner pay | Active hours for that target |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $18.00 | $1.32 | $0.81 | 39 | 112 | 22.4 |
| $22.00 | $4.81 | $4.30 | 11 | 66 | 13.2 |
| $26.00 | $8.30 | $7.79 | 7 | 47 | 9.4 |

The table is rounded and before personal income taxes; keep full precision when you calculate. At 100 sales, the $18 case leaves about $81.02 monthly profit plus $360 labour pay, or $441.02 total owner income. Pay for your work is not the same as profit after your work. Selling less spreads fixed costs over fewer candles, so do not treat the assumed volume as a sales forecast.

## Buy the right quantities, and budget beyond the wax

With an 8% fragrance-to-wax ratio, an 8 oz total fill uses about 7.407 oz wax + 0.593 oz fragrance, not 8 oz wax with oil added afterwards. Weigh both; fluid ounces are different. Calculate pack price / pack quantity × what you use, then add material loss and the allowance for landed costs.

That gives about $7.27 in base materials, $7.65 after loss and $8.15 including the landed-cost allowance per candle. Supplier freight and tax are not in the pack prices. Budget cash for whole packs, equipment and spare stock, not just what each candle uses. The shipper’s [public product data](https://flushpackaging.com/products/studio-tumbler-shipping-box.js) supports its unit price, not a confirmed multi-unit pack offer. Ask about the purchase quantity before budgeting an order.

Remember wick adhesive, label ink, tape, carrier labels, tools, insurance, burn tests, failed batches, overfill, breakage, refunds and heat damage. Include setup, cleanup, messages, photography, packing and bookkeeping in active work time. Curing wait needs space and affects lead time, but is not itself hourly work. **Before selling, burn-test your exact wax, fragrance, wick and jar combination. Confirm safe fill and headspace; a cost calculation cannot establish safety.**

## Check shipping before offering free delivery

**Charging the buyer exactly the postage still leaves some shipping costs with you: percentage fees also apply to that charge. With free shipping, you still pay the carrier.** In this example, the expected fee share on shipping is 12.725%. To recover it, divide postage by the share you keep and round up. That is a cautious allowance when the ad cap applies, not a quote for every order.

| Item price | Left if every order has 15% Offsite Ads | Left if every order has 12% Offsite Ads | Left with free shipping at expected ad mix |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $18.00 | −$2.62 | −$1.64 | −$11.64 |
| $22.00 | $0.39 | $1.49 | −$8.15 |
| $26.00 | $3.40 | $4.63 | −$4.66 |

These amounts have paid for labour but not fixed costs. They are not the profit figures in the earlier table. If an amount is negative, selling more deepens the loss. If it is zero, sales still cannot pay fixed costs. Neither case has a finite break-even volume. Even a positive amount must cover overhead and the startup costs you still need to enter.

The [USPS retail tariff](https://pe.usps.com/text/DMM300/Notice123.htm) effective 2026-10-04 already includes the temporary peak adjustment. The [USPS announcement](https://about.usps.com/newsroom/national-releases/2026/0825-usps-announces-temporary-price-change-for-2026-holiday-shipping-season.htm) puts its end at 12 a.m. Central on 2027-01-17. **Check postage before your January 2027 launch and again after 2027-01-17. Do not add the peak charge twice, or assume the same tariff covers all of January.**

Pack a prototype, weigh it, and get a quote using the origin and destination ZIPs. The pricing calculation covers 16–48 oz, rounded up to 1–3 lb, in zones 1–9. The assumed 6 × 6 × 6 inch carton is not the confirmed size of the Flush box. Use [Ground Advantage guidance](https://www.usps.com/ship/ground-advantage.htm) to check dimensions: this example assumes a rectangular parcel under 1 cubic foot, with no side over 22 inches. Get a separate quote for larger, unusual or bundled parcels. Commercial or Etsy labels may cost less than retail; confirm the actual price before counting the saving.

## Turn the figures into your next decision

The pricing-workbook instructions below describe the original model. Only the workbooks listed under Downloads are public; original supporting evidence remains private.

- Start with your measured work time and actual shopping basket. Add supplier delivery and tax, insurance, tools, testing and setup. If an important cost still says zero, find out whether it really is free. Budget whole-pack cash separately from what each candle consumes.
- Choose how you will charge shipping: 1 charges postage only, 2 adds enough for expected fees, 3 offers free shipping, and 4 lets you set a manual charge. If using the pricing workbook, enter your prices in B4:D4. Compare what is left after labour in row 19, profit after fixed costs in row 23, and the tougher ad and shipping cases in rows 29–31. Change the inputs and recalculate before using the results.
- Decide what you want to earn. Row 25 shows break-even after labour. Row 26 aims for $200 profit after labour, needing 191 / 53 / 31 candles at the comparison prices. Row 27 aims for $500 total owner pay including labour, needing 112 / 66 / 47 candles. Choose the target you mean; do not add the rows or count labour pay twice.
- Check your time as well as your target. With 30 active hours/month and 12 minutes per candle, capacity is 150 candles. Allow for curing, storage, sourcing and fulfilment too. Having time to make them does not mean buyers will want them. Test discounts, ad attribution and returns before scaling up.

Once the unknown costs are filled in, try the $22 and $26 cases and compare genuinely matched delivered offers. If postage makes the price hard to sell, check a commercial label quote or a lower-cost package that passes testing before cutting your own pay. Pause if nothing is left after labour, or if safety and cash needs are still unresolved.

When you change your costs, recalculate before relying on the totals. Check that formulas still work and that missing costs have not been treated as free. A tidy spreadsheet is a starting point, not a substitute for safe products, cash and a price buyers will pay.
