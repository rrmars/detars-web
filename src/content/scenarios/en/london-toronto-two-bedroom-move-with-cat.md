You want your Toronto home to feel familiar, without spending the relocation allowance before you've bought a bed. **For this south London couple, I'd sell ordinary bulky furniture, carry the irreplaceables and send a carefully edited 99 kg air shipment. Budget CAD 12,951.54 for that plan with cat cargo—CAD 4,951.54 above the CAD 8,000 employer allowance, before adult flights or housing.** A shared container preserves more favourites, but it is not the budget escape hatch.

This plan assumes a two-bedroom London flat, two adults and one privately owned domestic cat over 3 months, arriving around **April 1, 2027**. It uses prices and rules checked **November 6, 2026**, not confirmed April availability. Your immigration status, employment duration, earlier Canadian residence and the cat's actual flight still need settling before you commit.

There is a cheaper possibility, but don't buy tickets on the strength of it: **if the exact flight accepts your cat in cabin and the owner-managed cost assumptions hold**, the light plan becomes **CAD 8,771.54**. Deferring the guest bed and trimming other purchases can bring only the initial stage below the allowance. That is postponed spending, not a fully furnished move for less.

- **Carry:** passports, immigration/job papers, original customs and cat documents, medicines, jewellery, laptops, backups, 2–3 weeks of clothes and a familiar cat blanket. Keep immediate necessities out of freight.
- **Consider shipping:** heirlooms, art, instruments, a genuinely good ergonomic chair, quality furniture that fits the Toronto home and compact favourite kitchenware. Photograph, value and insure them.

Ask whether you would pay each item's *extra* freight, insurance and handling cost to buy it again today. For 25 m³, request both sole-use and shared-load scopes: a 20ft container is plausible, not guaranteed once wrapping, awkward shapes and access are included. Avoid flying furniture.

## Customs: your job offer does not choose the route

**Check each person's customs status before signing a settler declaration.** Immigration status and customs status are not identical. Under [CBSA's settler memorandum](https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca/publications/dm-md/d2/d2-2-1-eng.html), first residence must be for at least 12 months, but employment of no more than 36 months and students are excluded. Employment exceeding 36 months can qualify despite temporary immigration status. Earlier Canadian residence may mean former-resident rules; an earlier PR landing without establishing residence is not necessarily the settlement event.

### If you qualify as first-time settlers

1. Prepare **two copies** of a valued inventory, with separate **accompanying goods** and **goods to follow** lists. Use [BSF186, formerly B4](https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca/publications/forms-formulaires/bsf186-eng.html), plus [BSF186A, formerly B4A](https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca/publications/forms-formulaires/bsf186a-eng.html) or an accepted detailed attachment.
2. Use approximate **CAD values**, quantities, descriptions and make/model/serial numbers where possible. Group ordinary household articles sensibly; identify valuables separately. Photograph/appraise jewellery and keep box IDs consistent with the mover's list. The [CBSA moving guide](https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca/travel-voyage/mrc-drc-eng.html) explains identification and declarations.
3. At your **first Canadian entry when actually settling**, personally declare later shipments even if no household goods accompany you. If connecting domestically, this may not be Toronto. Get the processed/stamped original and attachments; a mover's list does not replace this declaration.
4. Keep that original for later clearance. A properly authorised agent may clear later freight under [CBSA's accounting guidance](https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca/publications/dm-md/d17/d17-1-3-eng.html); the carrier is not automatically your authorised customs agent.

Ordinary qualifying goods must have been **owned, possessed and used abroad before settlement**. There is no general six-month minimum for first-time settlers, nor a universal CAD 10,000 per-item settler cap. New, leased, commercial or omitted goods may be assessed; no universal tax percentage can be calculated without your items and classification.

| Clock | What it means for your shipment |
|---|---|
| Before settlement | Meet ownership, possession and use conditions; declare intended later goods at the settlement entry |
| Goods to follow | **No import time limit** for qualifying goods on the original list; not permission to add later purchases |
| After each good physically arrives | A **12-month retention period** begins for that good; early sale/disposal can trigger duties |
| Goods arrive before you | CBSA describes a **40-day sufferance-warehouse period**, then an extension/unclaimed-goods process; commercial storage and demurrage run separately |

Aim for goods to arrive with or after you. No-time-limit goods to follow does not mean free storage.

### If temporary-worker rules apply

[Temporary importation under 9803](https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca/publications/dm-md/d2/d2-1-1-eng.html) is conditional duty/tax relief, possibly with security—not permanent settler treatment. Nonconsumables normally must leave by employment completion or 36 months after arrival, whichever comes first. Extensions are not automatic. Keep a duplicate inventory, serials, approximate values and eligibility documents for unaccompanied goods; ordinary temporary goods do not use the same mandatory first-arrival settler-accounting process.

If you intend to change to employment beyond 36 months or apply inland for PR, **report the transition to CBSA rather than waiting silently for approval**. The temporary and settler memoranda describe different triggers/cutoffs: inland PR wording refers to goods owned/possessed/used before the PR application date, not a shopping window through approval. Ask CBSA to settle your exact transition before buying or shipping more.

### Keep awkward contents out until cleared

- **Food, plants, seeds, soil, wood and animal products:** [declare them](https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca/services/fpa-apa/regs-eng.html) and use [CFIA AIRS](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/importing-food-plants-animals/airs) for the actual item, origin, Ontario destination and end use. Don't ship pantry contents or houseplants by default. Clean outdoor tools, bikes and shoes thoroughly. Wood furniture is not universally banned; protected-species requirements remain separate.
- **Wood crates/pallets:** these are not the furniture itself. The [CFIA packaging directive](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/plant-health/invasive-pests-and-plants/directives/forest-products/98-08) requires compliant treatment/debarking/marking or accepted certification for regulated solid-wood packaging. Processed-wood-only and thin-wood exemptions do not automatically cover mixed packaging. Have the mover confirm its materials.
- **Alcohol/tobacco:** ordinary settler goods-to-follow relief does not cover them. Accompanied allowances are separate; arrange provincial permission and assessments before a wine-cellar shipment.
- **Weapons/ammunition, replicas, protected-species items and controlled medicines:** tax relief is not admissibility. Obtain item-specific clearance; start with [CBSA's firearms guidance](https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca/import/iefw-iefa-eng.html) for weapons, not the household packing list.
- **Used mattresses:** the [specific March 2026 memorandum](https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca/publications/dm-md/d9/d9-1-7-eng.html) exempts qualifying 9807 settler mattresses from the general prohibition; ordinary temporary 9803 is not listed. Confirm classification rather than relying on a blanket fumigation claim.

## Your cat has three separate gates

The following assumes a privately owned domestic cat over 3 months, not a wild-cat hybrid or commercial transfer. Its age, breed, health, measurements and recent country history still need checking. A human job offer does not settle household customs status; nor does a Canadian cat document settle UK export or airline acceptance.

### Canadian entry

The [CFIA domestic-cat requirements](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/animal-health/terrestrial-animals/imports/import-policies/general/reference-document) accept an English/French veterinarian's certificate identifying the cat and establishing **one** of current rabies vaccination, qualifying rabies-free-country residence in the preceding six months, or the specified antibody-test alternative. A titre is **not routinely additional to vaccination**. The [rabies-free list](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/importing-food-plants-animals/pets/rabies-free) includes the UK but is under review; a recently imported cat cannot assume six months' UK residence.

The adult domestic-cat clause does not impose routine quarantine, microchipping, tapeworm treatment or a separate import permit. That does not waive carrier/export conditions. Microchipping is sensible and may be required by the provider. Let the vet choose and document the appropriate certification route, including vaccine/date/validity where applicable.

The [CFIA fee schedule](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/about-cfia/acts-and-regulations/list-acts-and-regulations/cfia-fees-notice) gives **CAD 36.95** for the first animal at the checked rate, not a guaranteed April fee. Allow **CAD 50–75** for routine inspection/tax uncertainty; cargo release/brokerage costs are separate. The cargo model already includes arrival provision—don't add this reserve again.

### UK export and the examination window

[GOV.UK non-EU guidance](https://www.gov.uk/taking-your-pet-abroad/travelling-to-a-noneu-country) calls for an **Export Health Certificate and EXA application**, with an Official Veterinarian. Use the [Canada EHC 2923 route](https://www.gov.uk/export-health-certificates/export-cats-and-dogs-to-canada-certificate-2923), not the EU AHC workflow. The [official guidance notes](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/653f91c9e6c9680014aa9cfa/2923NFG.pdf) require the clinical exam **within 72 hours of export**. The certificate's 10-day validity does not extend that examination window.

For an **April 1, 2027 departure**, the exam must be no earlier than the corresponding time on **March 29, 2027**. A March 31 departure needs its own calculation. Work from actual export time, not Toronto arrival. Book the OV well ahead and use the currently issued certificate. Discuss health and any medication with the vet; do not assume sedation is the solution to travel stress.

It is not the base plan.

**Cabin fit may be the blocker even if the route works.** Air Canada's mainline 777/787/A330 table permits a flexible soft carrier up to **40 W × 43 L × 20 H cm**, with room to stand, turn and lie comfortably; Premium Economy is excluded. Crouching into a bag is not enough. Register promptly after booking, within the stated 24-hour refund-protection window where applicable. A generic online fee does not reserve a pet space.

For cargo/hold, choose the crate from the cat's measurements. [IATA's 2026 requirements](https://www.iata.org/contentassets/b0016da92c86449f850fe9560827bbea/pet-container-requirements.pdf) use internal length **A + ½B** (nose-to-tail-root plus half ground-to-elbow), width **2C** (widest body width), and height **D + bedding** (standing head/ear height). The cat must stand, sit erect, turn and lie naturally. Use secure bolted construction, a leak-proof floor, absorbent bedding, ventilation on four sides, handles/spacers, a secure door, an externally refillable fixed water bowl and live-animal/upright labels. Remove/disable wheels and follow stricter airline variations. IATA does not certify crate brands; recheck the 2027 edition before buying. Routine sedation is discouraged; ask the treating vet about your cat.

Start gentle carrier practice now. Arrange cat-friendly accommodation and a quiet arrival room. Give the shipper age, breed/flat-faced status, weight, A/B/C/D measurements, health/medications, chip/vaccine records, six-month country history, pickup/destination, dates and intended owner flight. Ask for a weather/flight-disruption recovery plan, boarding, cancellation, rerouting, payment and quote-validity terms—not just a freight price.

## Questions you may still have

### Can the employer allowance cover this move?

Not with the cargo-cat baseline, even for the light plan. The below-allowance initial stage needs confirmed cabin acceptance, the owner-managed cost assumption, a deferred guest bed and a further before-tax trim. Flights, housing and other excluded costs remain outside it.

### Must settler goods arrive within a year?

No: qualifying goods on the original goods-to-follow list have no import time limit. The 12-month rule concerns retention after each good's physical arrival. First-entry declaration and eligibility still matter; this is not a storage concession or permission to add new purchases.

### Does the cat need vaccination and a titre test?

Not routinely both under the adult domestic-cat clause. Canada provides alternative documentary routes, while UK export certification and the airline are separate requirements. Ask the OV to choose the correct route using your cat's history and current rules.

### Should we ship in March if we arrive in April?

It can work if you deliberately let goods follow and can live without them. Shared loads can take much longer than your flight; allow for the May–early August planning span and agree delivery/storage arrangements rather than promising an arrival-day home.
