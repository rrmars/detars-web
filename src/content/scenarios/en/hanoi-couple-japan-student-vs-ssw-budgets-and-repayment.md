You want to work in Japan, you have VND 180,000,000 saved and relatives may lend up to VND 250,000,000, and you are afraid of paying an agent for a route that closes or buries you in debt. **Short answer: plan for both of you to go as Specified Skilled Workers (i), known as SSW (i). Lan goes in the accommodation (hotel) field; Huy goes in industrial product manufacturing, unless he can prove an Engineer/Humanities-eligible credential before he pays anyone. The language-school route for Huy costs roughly VND 340–416 million for two years and still does not fix his real obstacle.**

This guide follows Huy and Lan, a married couple in Hanoi. Lan has hotel front-desk experience and no Japanese. Huy holds a 3-year Vietnamese college (cao đẳng) IT diploma and the JLPT N5. It was written on 8 November 2026 from public pages only; no agency, school, employer or official was contacted. "Unconfirmed" marks anything that did not come from an official or school page, and "assumed" marks our own planning figure. **It is general information, not legal or immigration advice.** The source materials are not published.

## The recommendation and the three facts that change the usual advice

- **Lan: SSW (i) in the accommodation (hotel) field.** She has no Japanese, but she has hotel front-desk experience, and this field has a Vietnam skills test. The most recent embassy notice found is from April 2024, so confirm current sitting dates and the fee on Prometric before relying on it. She needs JFT-Basic (or JLPT N4) at level A2.2 plus the hotel skills test.
- **Huy: also SSW (i), in industrial product manufacturing, as the base plan, unless he can prove an Engineer/Humanities-eligible credential before paying anyone.** The language-school detour costs roughly VND 340–416 million for two years (see 'Budgets and repayment timelines'), uses most or all of the family loan, and still does not fix the obstacle: a 3-year Vietnamese college IT diploma is not shown, in any Japanese immigration text read, to count as "university degree or equivalent". **Next step:** before paying a school or an agent, get a written view, from a Japanese employer or a lawyer, on whether your actual diploma and transcript count.
- **Counterintuitive finding:** the common agent pitch is "study first, then convert to an Engineer visa". But the Engineer/Specialist in Humanities status can also be applied for from abroad by an employer (the immigration agency's Engineer page describes a route for a Certificate of Eligibility; this page was not read in full, so **unconfirmed**). The requirement is the credential (a related degree, a Japanese vocational-school (senmon-gakkō) diploma, 10 years of related experience, or for IT a designated IT qualification), not having been a student. A language school, at most about 2 years, gives none of those. So the student path helps Huy only if he then also enters a Japanese vocational school or university, which adds years and cost this plan has not priced.

Three facts change the usual advice:

- **Food service SSW (i) is closed to new overseas entrants.** Japan stopped issuing Certificates of Eligibility for it for applications received on or after 13 April 2026. The Vietnam food-service skills test stopped taking new bookings on 6 April 2026, with "no prospect of resumption" (official pages). Ignore any agency still selling it.
- **Vietnam is not yet on Japan's published list of countries with an Employment for Skill Development (ESD, 育成就労) memorandum.** The system starts on 1 April 2027, but the list, updated 20 October 2026, names Uzbekistan, Sri Lanka, Thailand, Bangladesh, Timor-Leste and Laos (official page). ESD takes workers in principle only from such countries, so do not plan on it. Direct SSW (i) entry from Vietnam is a separate, existing route.
- **No spouse can accompany an SSW (i) worker.** Each of you can hold your own SSW (i) status (our reading; not stated outright by Japan's immigration agency), but nobody guarantees you are placed in the same city. See 'Can the spouse come?'

**Honest limits:** the Vietnamese service-fee article could not be read from an official portal (only secondary quotes), the Prometric Vietnam test calendar could not be read as a table, and no dated SSW job advert was found. These are flagged where they matter.

## Can the spouse come?

- **As the dependent of an SSW (i) worker: no.** The immigration agency's FAQ (Q9) says that in SSW (ii) accompaniment by family is allowed but in SSW (i) it is not. Narrow discretionary exceptions exist: a child born in Japan to two SSW (i) workers, and a spouse already in Japan as a dependent when a student changes to SSW (i) (official page). The ESD system also says family accompaniment is "in principle not allowed" (official page).
- **Both as separate SSW (i) workers: probably yes.** No official text was found forbidding a married couple from each holding SSW (i). Each must pass Japanese A2.2 and a skills test and have a contracting employer. **Unconfirmed; this is our inference.** There is no mechanism to put you in the same city. **Next step:** agree the work location in writing with each employer before departure.
- **As the dependent of a language-school student: allowed by the statute wording** ("spouse or child supported by a person holding ... College Student", official page), but the supporter must show his own savings or a scholarship for the dependent, and Vietnam is in the heightened-scrutiny group (inference). Only the first half of the Dependent page was read, and the dependent's own work-hour rule is **unconfirmed**. In practice this would roughly double Huy's living costs, which this budget does not price.

## Student path: rules, fees and switching to Engineer

**Visa rules.**

- **Certificate of Eligibility (COE):** normally filed with the immigration bureau by the language school on the student's behalf; required documents differ by school and nationality. After the COE, the applicant applies for the visa at the Japanese embassy or consulate. This comes from the Study in Japan site as a search-result snippet only, the page was not opened, and the Hanoi embassy page could not be read (**unconfirmed**).
- **Financial sponsor proof:** documents proving ability to pay all costs during the stay; if someone else pays, that person's proof of means and how they came to have the money (transaction history, passbooks, income proof). The immigration agency warns that temporarily depositing a large sum may be treated as cause to doubt proper residence (official page). From April 2027, entrants to language schools need an additional ability-to-pay confirmation form (official page). A VND 250,000,000 family loan arriving in a bank account just before filing is exactly the pattern the agency scrutinises. **Next step:** explain the source of the money honestly in the documents.
- **Part-time work:** "within 28 hours per week; during long school holidays within 8 hours per day" after permission (official page). Many agents say "40 hours in vacation"; that figure is not in the agency text that was read (**unconfirmed**). Exceeding the limit can mean no renewal or deportation. Working to pay all tuition and living costs "is not accepted" (official page).
- **If attendance drops** (school-side rules from the language-school standards notice, official page): monthly attendance below 80% means the school must give guidance; below 50% means the school must report the student (and the part-time employer, where permitted) to the immigration bureau; a school whose six-month average attendance falls below 70% can be removed from the recognised list. A renewal refusal for a poor study record counts against the school. No official sentence says "below 70% (or 80%) means renewal refused"; those cut-offs circulate from agents and social posts (**unconfirmed**). Treat 80% as the safe line.
- **Heightened scrutiny:** Vietnam is not on the agency's list of countries exempted from "careful examination", so we infer it is in the heightened group, with school-prepared confirmations and language proof required (**unconfirmed**; inference from the list's wording).

**Switching to Engineer / Specialist in Humanities / International Services.** The landing standard (immigration agency, official page) requires one of: (i) graduation in a related subject from a university "or equivalent or higher education"; (ii) completion of a Japanese vocational-school (senmon-gakkō) specialist course meeting notified conditions; (iii) 10 years of practical experience (years studying the related subject at university or senmon count); and for IT, an exception for holders of a designated information-technology qualification. Pay must be equal to or above that of a Japanese person doing the job (no fixed yen floor). For language-heavy jobs at smaller employers, applications from 15 April 2026 need CEFR B2 proof, for example JLPT N2 (official page).

**Huy's diploma:** no official text says a Vietnamese 3-year college diploma equals a university degree, so whether it counts is a case-by-case decision (**unconfirmed**). His realistic routes are a Japanese vocational-school diploma, a designated IT exam (the list was not read; check the agency's "special notification"), or accumulating experience. Study years in a related major count towards the 10 years, so about 3 years of college could count, leaving about 7 more. That is our reading of the agency's wording (**unconfirmed**).

## SSW (i): fields, tests, dates, fees, and the new ESD system

- **Fields:** 19 fields now. Linen supply, Warehousing and Resource circulation were added by cabinet decision on 23 January 2026, with acceptance there starting "once preparations are completed" (official page). No agency statement restricting Vietnam to a subset was found; Vietnam's procedure runs through a send-out company approved by DOLAB (Vietnam's overseas-labour department) and a worker-supply contract (official page). The 2019 memorandum itself was not read. The agency's per-field headroom PDF, listed on its page as updated 6 November 2026, was not read either, so check headroom before paying.
- **Your two fields:** accommodation (Lan) and industrial product manufacturing (Huy) are open fields with Vietnam test routes. Food service is closed (see above). Care needs an extra care-Japanese test (agency outline, official page).
- **Japanese test:** the JFT-Basic fee in Vietnam is VND 870,000, computer-based, 60 minutes, age 17 and over, Hanoi test centre at IIG Vietnam, Trung Yen Plaza (Embassy of Japan in Vietnam page, official). JLPT N4 is an accepted alternative (official page).
- **JFT-Basic dates in Vietnam:** the official ssw.go.jp calendar shows Vietnam sittings on 4 to 8, 13 and 26 to 31 October 2026, and on 1 to 2, 4 to 6 and 8 to 11 November 2026 (official page). The Prometric calendar (page listed as updated 15 October 2026) was reported to cover Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City into January 2027, but its grid could not be read, so no date is confirmed from it (**unconfirmed**). A prep school's transcription lists Hanoi on 5, 6, 9 and 10 November, 7, 8, 10, 11, 21 to 25 and 27 December, and 8 to 10 and 12 to 15 January, with "no schedule after 15 January 2027" (**unconfirmed**). **Next step:** confirm on Prometric before booking.
- **Skills tests in Vietnam:** manufacturing costs VND 1,350,000 and takes 80 minutes, with sittings in Hanoi on 9 to 10 November 2026 and in Ho Chi Minh City on 9 to 11 November 2026 and booking closed on 4 November 2026, so the next sitting is not yet announced (official page). Accommodation is VND 1,200,000, 60 minutes, rolling sittings, but that fee is from a notice dated 8 April 2024 and must be re-verified (official page, dated). Food service is suspended.
- **Exemption:** only people who completed Technical Intern Training No. 2 skip both tests. This does not apply to you two (official page).
- **Employer duties:** pay at least equal to Japanese workers; no passing support costs on to the worker; no deposit-taking or penalty contracts; the employer pays the return fare if the worker cannot (official page).

**The ESD (育成就労) transition.**

- The law was promulgated on 21 June 2024 and operation starts on 1 April 2027: 3 years of work to reach SSW (i) level, then SSW (i) for 5 years, then SSW (ii). 17 of the 19 SSW fields have an ESD counterpart (official page).
- Entry needs Japanese at A1 level (JFT-Basic or JLPT N5, or an equivalent course); A2.2 at the end; family accompaniment "in principle not allowed" (official page).
- Send-out fee cap under ESD: 2 months of monthly salary; anything above must be borne by the employer or supervising body (official page).
- Bilateral rule: workers are taken "in principle only from countries with a memorandum". Vietnam is not on the 20 October 2026 list (official page); whether it was added after that date could not be checked. Entering SSW (i) directly from abroad "without going through ESD" remains valid (official page).

## Vietnamese service-fee rules, agency quotes and debt warning signs

**What the law says.**

- **Law 69/2020/QH14, Article 23 (in force since 1 January 2022):** the service fee charged to the worker is capped at 1 month of contract salary per 12 months worked; for contracts of 36 months or more, no more than 3 months; 0.5 month per 12 months of extension. The first part was seen only as a search-result snippet of thuvienphapluat.vn, and the 3-month and 0.5-month parts only through a study-abroad consultancy page (**unconfirmed**). The portal blocked the fetch. So "3 months" is a ceiling for long contracts, not a flat rate.
- **Brokerage and deposits:** collecting brokerage money from workers is prohibited; deposits go into a blocked account in the worker's own name and are returned with interest (**unconfirmed**; secondary source).
- **Law 22/2026/QH16** amends Law 69/2020. It was signed on 24 August 2026 and takes effect on 1 March 2027 (government legal portal, official page). Reported changes: waiting time to leave capped at 180 days after selection, with refund of fees plus compensation if the company breaks it (government newspaper report of the draft explanation; the final article number is unverified); training and language fees only after the foreign employer has selected the worker and signed (**unconfirmed**); no delegating selection to third parties (**unconfirmed**).
- **Japan-specific annex caps** (Circular 21/2021 as amended by Circular 09/2026/TT-BNV, effective 30 June 2026; this citation itself comes from a consultancy page, **unconfirmed**) were not obtained. Decrees 112/2021, 38/2020, 12/2022 and 86/2021 were not read. Whether Article 23 explicitly names SSW workers is not verified; our reading is that it applies to any contract placement through a licensed Vietnamese service company (**unconfirmed**; inference). Students going on their own are not "workers under contract", so this fee cap does not protect them.

**Agency quotes against the cap.** All are commercial sites (**unconfirmed**), read on 8 November 2026. The cap column uses ¥200,000 of monthly pay at VND 166.4 per yen, about VND 33.3 million per month of pay.

**Debt-bondage warning signs.**

- Total fees to the Vietnamese side above the contract-term cap (table above), or any fee described as "brokerage" or "introduction".
- Training or language fees demanded before a Japanese employer has selected you (illegal after 1 March 2027 under the amended law, **unconfirmed**).
- A deposit paid in cash to the agency rather than into a blocked account in your own name.
- No itemised written contract stating the service fee, other sums, the deposit and termination terms.
- Loans arranged by the agency or by someone linked to it; you sign for a loan you did not choose.
- A penalty clause for quitting or going home. Japan's SSW standards say an employer must not sign a contract with penalty clauses or knowingly hire workers who paid deposits (official page).
- Your passport or bank card held by anyone but you; being told to pay "support costs" or housing set-up to the employer (employers must bear the support costs, official page).
- Fake or temporary bank balances for a student visa; the agency treats these as grounds for doubt (official page).
- Context: the US State Department's 2025 report (Vietnam Tier 2, read through a mirror) says some recruiters "charge excessive fees, increasing workers' vulnerability to forced labor through debt-based coercion", and that some Vietnamese are exploited under Japan's technical intern programme (foreign government report).

**Where to complain in Japan:** the Foreign Residents Support Center (FRESC) on 0570-011000, weekdays 9:00–17:00, or 03-5363-3013 from abroad (official page). The technical-intern organisation runs a native-language hotline for technical interns only; its Vietnamese number was not found. A Vietnamese DOLAB complaint line was not found either.
