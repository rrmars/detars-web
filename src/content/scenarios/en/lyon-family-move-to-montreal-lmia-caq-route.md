You have a job offer in Montréal, two children and a spouse who is a hospital pharmacist. Is September 2027 realistic, and can one salary carry year one? **Short answer: the route exists and runs through the employer (LMIA, then CAQ, then the work permit); September 2027 is plausible but has little slack; and on Julien’s salary alone the first year ends about CAD 3,464.02 short once setup costs and a 10 % contingency are counted.**

The guide follows Claire (38, hospital pharmacist), Julien (40, software project manager) and their children aged 7 and 11. A Montréal software company has offered Julien CAD 115,000 a year (about €73,099), from September 2027. Written on 8 November 2026 from public pages only; nobody was contacted. “Unconfirmed” marks anything not from a primary source or not read in full. **It is general information, not immigration, legal or tax advice.** The source materials are not published.

## The recommended route: employer-led LMIA, CAQ, then an employer-specific work permit

For Julien, the only route confirmed on official pages is Quebec’s Temporary Foreign Worker Program. His employer obtains a positive LMIA (Labour Market Impact Assessment), issued jointly with Quebec’s immigration ministry (MIFI). Julien then obtains a **CAQ** (Quebec acceptance certificate) through the employer, and applies to IRCC (federal immigration) for the employer-specific work permit. At CAD 115,000 (about CAD 55.29 an hour on 2,080 hours, our arithmetic) the job is far above Quebec’s high-wage threshold of CAD 36.00 an hour for LMIAs received from 17 July 2026.

- **Ask the employer now; it is the biggest lever on timing.** Does the company qualify for the **Global Talent Stream** (average 9 business days) or Quebec’s facilitated process, rather than the high-wage stream (average **98 business days**, September 2026 figures)? And which NOC and TEER code will the LMIA use for Julien? That code decides whether Claire can get a spouse open work permit.
- **Do not count on a shortcut.** International Experience Canada is for ages 18–35, so neither parent qualifies. No LMIA exemption for a salaried IT job turned up on any page read, which is not proof that none exists.
- **Counterintuitive finding:** common advice says “settle later through the Quebec Experience Program (**PEQ**)”. PEQ’s window ran from 2 July to 31 October 2026 and is **closed**, with no decision on reopening (page dated 29 October 2026). The live permanent route is the PSTQ via Arrima, whose 2026 main invitation stream needed spoken French level 7; criteria can tighten between rounds, so plan for it but do not assume it.
- **Adjacent problem:** Claire’s licence will probably take until 2028 or later, so plan the household on one salary for at least the first year.

**Timing risk.** The LMIA can be filed up to 6 months before the start date, so about 1 March 2027 for a 1 September start. At the 98-business-day average (weekends and advertising time excluded), the decision lands around mid-July to mid-August 2027. That is *before* the CAQ, the work permit and the children’s study permits are processed, and IRCC processing times could not be retrieved, so no estimate is made for them. Quebec’s ministry advises against resigning, buying tickets or taking travel insurance before all authorisations are in hand. **Next step:** ask the employer for the stream, the NOC and TEER code, and who pays the CAQ fee, before anything else.

## Claire and the children: permits and fees

**Claire’s spouse open work permit.** IRCC’s eligibility depends on the worker’s occupation level: spouses of **TEER 0 or 1** workers qualify, plus certain listed TEER 2 and 3 occupations. A search snippet adds that the worker’s permit must be valid at least 16 months from the spouse’s application (**partly verified**). Julien’s TEER code is unknown because the employer decides the NOC: **this is the single fact to get from the employer.** The fee is CAD 155 plus a CAD 100 open-permit holder fee, so CAD 255, plus biometrics. Whether Claire needs her own CAQ was not found. An open permit is not tied to an employer, but practising as a pharmacist still needs the Ordre’s permit; no source linking the two to the internship was found.

**The children (7 and 11).** Minors who want to study must apply for a study permit **before entering Canada**, including when travelling with parents who apply for a work permit; no acceptance letter is needed then. The fee is CAD 150 each (from a snippet of IRCC’s fee list, so unconfirmed). Quebec’s page says children generally need a CAQ from elementary school and “a work permit”, almost certainly a typo for study permit: **confirm with MIFI.** The CAQ-for-studies fee was not found.

**Fees we can total (CAD):** Julien’s permit 155 + Claire’s permit 255 + children’s study permits 300 + biometrics 170 (the family cap; whether it covers the children’s applications was not confirmed) + CAQ 233 = **CAD 1,113**. The LMIA fee of CAD 1,000 is the employer’s and may not be recovered from Julien.

## Schooling under Quebec’s Charter of the French Language

1. **Default: French public school.** Instruction is in French except where the Charter allows otherwise.
2. **English public school.** The permanent-resident route needs a Canadian-citizen parent educated in English in Canada, so a French-citizen family does not fit. Children of someone staying temporarily to work can get a *temporary* authorization, and the ministry says “special rules” limit its length. The “maximum 3 years, one time, non-renewable” figure comes only from McGill’s family-care page (**unconfirmed**), and the regulation text could not be loaded. McGill also says it ends if the family later gets a CSQ, which **matters if you plan to settle**. The school passes the request to the ministry (about 15 working days for the current school year, 20 for the next).
3. **French school enrolment.** First registration generally needs the birth certificate and proof of residence. The Montréal service centre CSSDM asked for two original proofs of Quebec address in the parent’s name and one identity document for the child. Its 2026-27 regular window was 7 January to 27 January 2026; 2027-28 dates are not yet published. Registration also happens in-year and in summer, by appointment at the neighbourhood school. **Which centre serves Rosemont, Villeray or Outremont was not verified**: use the Ministry’s “find my service centre” tool with your address.
4. **Language assessment.** A child who does not speak French is placed in a reception class after assessment.
5. **Cost.** Quebec says tuition may be free for workers’ children, except in private, college or university institutions. School-day care (service de garde) was quoted at CAD 10 a day in a search summary (**unconfirmed**).
6. **Grade placement.** Not found officially. By our arithmetic on the 30 September cut-off (confirmed only for kindergarten), a child aged 7 on 30 September 2027 would be in Grade 2 and an 11-year-old in Grade 6 (**inference**).

**Next step:** look up the service centre for your address, then ask the school for a summer or in-year registration appointment.

## Health cover (RAMQ) and the France–Quebec social security agreement

- **Eligibility.** A person temporarily in Quebec qualifies with an eligible work permit valid for more than 6 months, and must not be absent more than 21 consecutive days. Children under 18 staying more than 6 months also qualify. **Open work permits are generally ineligible “with some exceptions”**, so Claire’s cover may depend on her permit type, and RAMQ’s dependant wording (“spouse or dependant age 18 to 25 who holds an authorization of stay”) is ambiguous for a 38-year-old. **This is an important unresolved point: confirm with RAMQ.**
- **Waiting period.** Normally up to 3 months after registration, not applied to children under 18. RAMQ also states you can be insured as soon as you register if you arrive “from a country that has signed a social security agreement with Québec”, and France is one of 11. The official text therefore points to **no 3-month wait for French arrivals**, subject to paperwork.
- **Paperwork.** RAMQ’s own step list for French arrivals was not retrievable. A French-side form (SE 401-Q-207, completed by the CPAM) appears only in forum posts and a CLEISS snippet, and that you must be in Quebec to register is also **unconfirmed**. Verify the current list on RAMQ and ameli before leaving France.
- **Safety net.** RAMQ “strongly recommends” private insurance from arrival, since care before your start date is not reimbursed; keep a short policy for the first weeks.
- **Drug insurance (1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027).** Public-plan premium CAD 0–789 per person, monthly deductible CAD 21.25, coinsurance 30 %, monthly cap CAD 105.25. If the employer offers a group plan you are generally required to join it (a CLEISS page dated 2024; structure only). Premiums for 2027-28 were not found.
- **Social security agreement.** It covers health-insurance continuity and pensions through Retraite Québec. Detachment is irrelevant: Julien will be an ordinary Montréal employee. How to leave the French system (CPAM, CFE) was **not found** on official pages.

## Claire: recognition of a French pharmacy degree by the Ordre des pharmaciens du Québec

A French pharmacy doctorate can go through the France–Quebec recognition arrangement (in force since 3 March 2011), but it is **not automatic**: the Ordre requires a compensatory measure plus a 600-hour internship. **Licensure is therefore likely 2028 or later.**

1. **Open the online file** with the Ordre. Documents: an official copy of the diploma, and proof of registration or de-registration with the French Ordre stating hospital or community practice and dates. The evaluation fee is **CAD 532.11** including taxes, non-refundable; review takes about 7 days.
2. **Pick a compensatory option** (both can be ticked at filing):
   - **Option A, exam route:** pass the PEBC Part II (ECOS), offered in May and November; the Ordre wants the complete file at least 5 months before the exam; then a self-study law course (PHM 6510); then the internship, about 4–8 months after the exam.
   - **Option B, Université de Montréal programme:** 16 months, 64 credits plus internship, about 20 months in total. The deadline is 1 November for the following autumn, so 1 November 2026 has already passed for autumn 2027.
3. **Internship and French.** She must find the site and supervisor herself (hospital or community pharmacy). No OQLF French exam: her 3 or more years of full-time pharmacy study in France satisfy the Charter.
4. **Permit.** After the internship report, the Ordre emails the permit application (registration, dues, FARPOPQ liability fund); the permit follows within 7 days of a complete application. The pages list only a recognised diploma and French as conditions. No residency condition appears, but that is absence of evidence, so **confirm with the Ordre**.
