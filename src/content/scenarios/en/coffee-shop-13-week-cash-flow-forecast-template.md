## Fill in your own costs before worrying about a restaurant average

The example starts with approximate sales, six workers, rent and existing debt. You still need your real opening bank balance, wages, loan rate and tax bill. **Do not treat the example as another operating café’s verified finances.** Use the cells below to enter your own numbers.

| Inputs cells | Actual example | How to customize |
|---|---:|---|
| B4 | November 9, 2026 | Forecast Monday; roll exactly seven days after reconciliation |
| B5 / B6 | $6,500 / $1,500 | Eligible total opening cash / reserve already included in that total |
| B7 | $5,000 | Your minimum spendable cash, not an industry or lender standard |
| B8 | $38,000/month | POS sales excluding catering, sales tax and tips |
| B9 / B10 / B11 | 90% / 2.8% / 1 business day | Card share / assumed effective card fee / assumed settlement lag |
| B12:B16 | 6 people × 22 hours × $18 × 2 weeks × 1.12 = **$5,322.24** | Replace staffing and employer costs; total is funding cost, not employee net pay |
| B17 / B18 | November 13, 2026 / $6,500 | First biweekly payroll funding date / monthly rent |
| B19:B22 | $40,000 / 10% APR / 60 months / **$849.88 monthly** | Existing debt and illustrative amortization; use the lender statement |
| B23 / B24 / B25 | 3% / 15% / 32.4% | Tax-reserve assumption / POS downside / supplier-budget proxy |

**Start with $6,500, not $8,000:** the $1,500 reserve is already inside that cash balance. Spendable opening cash is $6,500 − $1,500 = $5,000. Reconcile operating and reserve accounts together, then take out held sales tax and employee tips. Their receipts and remittances are outside this forecast. If they share your bank account, subtract those liabilities from eligible opening cash. You still owe them; keeping them out here helps you avoid spending money that is not yours.

**A loan payment takes cash out; it does not bring new finance in.** `Inputs!B22` uses `IF(B20=0,B19/B21,-PMT(B20/12,B21,B19))`. Count the full principal-and-interest installment as an outflow. The 10% and 60 months are example assumptions, not SBA terms; replace them from your lender’s statement. There is no assumed new loan, automatic overdraft, cash interest or dedicated finance-inflow schedule. A shortfall flags pressure, not available money. For new funding, add a properly designed inflow and repayment plan, not a made-up catering receipt.

**Price your own orders instead of aiming at a restaurant average.** The Sources tab links the [National Restaurant Association’s September 10, 2025 analysis](https://www.restaurant.org/research-and-media/research/restaurant-economic-insights/analysis-commentary/restaurant-operators-kept-food-cost-ratios-in-check-in-2024/), read and checked again November 5, 2026. It gives a **32.4% median 2024 food/nonalcoholic-beverage cost ratio for limited-service respondents**, explicitly not a standard or target. This workbook borrows it to estimate a purchasing budget. Inventory purchases and accounting cost of sales can fall at different times. Replace the estimate with your orders and unpaid invoices.

Daily sales begin with $38,000 × 12 ÷ 365, then monthly seasonal and weekday weights. The monthly indexes are normalized to an average of 1; weekday weights sum to 100%. Saturday/Sunday receive 38% combined, and winter is stronger. These are **example assumptions**, not a demand forecast. Column C replaces the plan when filled—including a valid zero-sales day.

Cash sales use the assumed 10% non-card share on the sale date. Card deposits aggregate sales due to settle that date × 90% × (1 − 2.8%). Fees are already netted from deposits; do not charge them again as a separate payment. Prior-day card sales still unsettled at the opening date need actual history; deposits already in opening cash must not be counted twice.

**Check when card cash really arrives.** [Square’s transfer page](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/3807-deposit-options-with-square), read November 5, says standard timing depends on the cutoff: before **5pm PT/8pm ET**, payments are available next business day; later payments by the second business day, subject to bank processing. Next-business-day transfers are not sent on Saturdays. A single one-business-day lag is therefore only a shortcut. Put known exceptions in Daily Sales E and update Holidays for your processor. The 2.8% here is an assumption, not a Square quote.

Supplier estimates are dated Thursdays for a Monday–Sunday purchase budget, split 65% net 15 and 35% net 30. Actual committed invoices should normally be **Fixed**, not automatically reduced because sales fall. Use due-date override E when the expected cash date differs from invoice date plus terms. Separate fixed catering-supply examples total **$1,360.80**, alongside **$4,200** of committed catering receipts; both remain unchanged in the downside. Replace or clear these example orders.

Baseline payments are **$5,322.24 payroll + $2,856.65 suppliers + $849.88 loan**. November’s rent falls before the start, so do not charge it again in week 1. Over the 13 weeks, rent adds up to **$19,500** and seven payroll debits to **$37,255.68**. Dates decide which week needs the cash, not a monthly average. Displayed figures round separately; the formulas keep full precision.

Keep the bridge simple: **opening total cash + receipts − payments = ending total cash**. Subtract the reserve to see what you can spend. The daily calculation sets aside 3% of receipts without treating it as another cash expense. A Tax payment reduces total cash once and releases reserve down to zero; anything beyond that uses operating cash. The 3% is a saving assumption, not your tax calculation.

**A healthy-looking Sunday can hide a tight weekday.** In the last downside week, Sunday spendable cash is **$5,076.25**, but the daily low is **$4,936.08**, leaving a **$63.92** gap to target. The lowest downside point is **$3,575.39** in the week beginning November 30, a **$1,424.61** target gap. These are buffer shortfalls, not negative bank balances, approved credit or a cumulative borrowing need. Do not add the weekly shortfalls as separate debts.

Read **CASH GAP** as a negative daily spendable low, **BELOW MIN** as below your target, and **OK** otherwise. “OK” passes only this daily-closing check. It does not cover which debit or deposit happens first during a day, or the opening balance before transactions. Check those with your bank schedule. Positive cash is not the same as profit or solvency; you still need a profit-and-loss account, balance sheet and any debt-covenant checks.

The 15% case lowers POS sales from your selected start, not card sales already earned and awaiting deposit. Only **Flex supplier invoices dated on or after that start** fall by 15%, with relief delayed until their payment dates. Payroll, rent, loan, tax, other fixed bills, owner draws and committed catering do not automatically fall. That is why first-week supplier payments are the same in both cases. Try other pressures too; this is not a guarantee of the worst possible outcome.

## Customize once, then reset every Monday

1. **Archive and compare.** Save a dated copy; compare last week's estimate with actual bank cash and explain variances. Initial setup is more than a ten-minute exercise; the short reset assumes reconciled records.
2. **Reconcile before rolling.** Enter actual Sunday-close eligible total cash in Inputs B5 and reserve already included in it in B6. Keep sales tax/tips outside the pool. Set a deliberate B7 operating buffer.
3. **Roll B4 exactly seven days.** Do not drag forecast columns or change the fixed calendar anchor B26. The dated ledger stays in place.
4. **Replace schedules, do not duplicate them.** Update POS history, card deposit dates, actual AP, catering collections, payroll funding and bank debit dates. Mark cleared rows Paid Yes once included in reconciled opening cash; move genuinely unpaid/uncollected expected cash dates forward. Past dates are not automatically paid or collected.
5. **Check both warnings.** B27 tests Monday/start/calendar setup and whether reserve exceeds total cash. B28 counts overdue uncleared supplier/catering/payment items; it does not check stale card settlements. READY is not a completeness guarantee: inspect every actual schedule.
6. **Decide before the squeeze.** Review both daily lows; investigate late collections, confirmed payment flexibility and funding options without assuming credit is available. Reforecast after changing a date or amount.

**Check the calendar before you keep rolling:** Daily Sales and Engine cover **October 5, 2026–January 2, 2028**, rows **5:459**. Suppliers, Catering and Payments formulas include **5:304**; Holidays uses **A5:A54**. You need room for the 91-day forecast and 30 days after its end. Before coverage runs out, extend calendars, schedules and formula ranges together. Pasting outside included rows will not safely extend the calculations. There is no automatic bank/POS feed, actual-versus-budget dashboard or dedicated new-financing calculator.

## Two tax questions to settle with your own figures

These questions come from the [IRS estimated-tax FAQ](https://www.irs.gov/faqs/estimated-tax), read November 5, 2026. They are official guidance questions, not coffee-shop customer submissions. Use them to check what applies to your business.

**“When are quarterly estimated tax payments due?”** For this calendar-year individual federal example, [Publication 505 (2026)](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p505) gives **January 15, 2027** for September–December 2026, with exceptions. Use that for the example’s date, **not to justify the $3,000 amount**. Your entity, state and applicable relief may change the schedule. Replace Payments with confirmed dates and amounts.

**Do you need individual quarterly estimated payments?** The IRS question on this asks you to consider expected tax after withholding and credits, as well as current/prior-year payment tests and exceptions. **The 3% reserve cannot work this out for you.** Have the obligation calculated for your circumstances, then enter the payment dates. Setting money aside is not the same as paying it.
