**Worried that Mum needs more help than you can give from two hours away? If she wants to stay home and can be safe between visits, start with assessed daily care and a falls/recovery review. Explore extra-care housing and a residential fallback alongside it. You can make the longer-term choice together, rather than trying to settle everything after one difficult week.**

This example is for an 82-year-old mother living alone in Leeds, with £38,000 savings, £1,450 pension per calendar month and a £240,000 home. Her adult child works full time. Those are the family's supplied figures, not a financial or care assessment.

At the illustrated home-care starting price, two billed hours a day plus assumed living costs leave £23,412.80 savings after the first year. That only works if the assessed care is sufficient, the quoted price is available and needs do not increase. Your working day and travel time are real limits; they should not quietly become the backup care package.

The prices and England funding rules below were checked on November 6, 2026, for planning in early 2027. They have not been refreshed. Ask for written prices and the applicable rules before committing, especially around April fee and benefit changes.

## Start with what makes today manageable

Ask Mum what matters most: her own front door, familiar carers, meals with company, nearby friends or someone close at night. Then ask Leeds for a free needs assessment, even if she will initially pay herself. Describe washing, dressing, meals, medicines, mobility, falls, night-time needs and the help you cannot reliably provide.

After a fall, ask her GP or the council about occupational therapy, physiotherapy and reablement/intermediate care. Assessed recovery support can be free, usually for up to six weeks. It is not an automatic six-week free care package for everyone. Do not delay essential help while funding paperwork is underway. If she is unsafe now, seek urgent clinical or social-care help; call 999 for an emergency. See the [NHS recovery-care guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/social-care-and-support/care-after-a-hospital-stay/care-to-support-recovery-after-leaving-hospital-or-after-a-fall-injury-or-illness/).

### Could live-in care fit the savings?

At the starting fee plus an assumed £700/month household budget, spending is £7,958.33/month. After pension, the gap is £6,508.33/month. Savings reach £23,250 after about 2.27 months; an unchanged mechanical calculation reaches zero in 5.84 months. Use the earlier funding checkpoint, not zero, to plan.

The occupied house is ignored, but council help depends on assessed eligible needs and a sufficient personal budget. It does not guarantee continuation of a chosen £1,675/week private package. Discuss the alternative and funding transition before starting.

## What Leeds funding would—and would not—mean

The [NHS financial-assessment guide](https://www.nhs.uk/social-care-and-support/help-from-social-services-and-charities/financial-assessment-means-test/) explains the needs assessment and means test. Your earnings and savings are not Mum's assets for her assessment. A top-up or guarantor agreement you sign is a separate commitment.

Under the [2026/27 charging circular](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/social-care-charging-for-local-authorities-2026-to-2027/social-care-charging-for-care-and-support-2026-to-2027-local-authority-circular):

| Countable capital | Usual treatment |
|---|---|
| Over £23,250 | Normally pays the full cost initially. |
| £14,250–£23,250 | Income contribution plus £1/week assumed income for every £250, or part, above £14,250. |
| £14,250 or less | No capital contribution, but income can still be charged. |

For example, £20,000 savings produces £23/week assumed capital income: 23 blocks of £250. It is not a £23 weekly grant. Low savings does not automatically mean free care.

For council-supported care at home, the single pension-age minimum income guarantee is £241.45/week, with relevant housing and disability-related costs considered. Keep receipts for extra heating, laundry and equipment. Leeds' [home-care means test](https://www.leeds.gov.uk/adult-social-care/how-to-get-adult-social-care-and-support/help-to-pay-for-care/help-paying-for-care-and-support/how-we-look-at-your-money) explains these costs. For council-supported permanent care-home residents, the minimum personal expenses allowance is £31.80/week. These are different protections.

### The house: three different rules

- **Care in the home she occupies:** its value is ignored in the care means test.
- **Permanent residential or nursing care:** the house normally counts unless a disregard applies. A partner or certain qualifying relatives remaining there may protect it, including specified relatives aged 60+ or incapacitated, or her child under 18, subject to occupation conditions. An adult child living elsewhere does not protect it just by being family. Temporary stays are different. See the [property-disregard rules](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2014/2672/schedule/2).
- **The initial 12-week property disregard:** this is conditional breathing space, not free care. Income and other savings still count. With £38,000 other savings, Mum normally still pays initially. Ask Leeds for eligibility and exact dates in writing, especially if savings change or she is already paying privately. See the [care-home means test](https://www.leeds.gov.uk/adult-social-care/how-to-get-adult-social-care-and-support/help-to-pay-for-care/help-paying-for-care-homes/how-we-look-at-your-money).

### A deferred payment agreement delays the bill; it does not remove it

A DPA is a secured council loan towards agreed care-home costs, not normally a home-care loan. Leeds normally requires an assessed permanent placement, savings no more than £23,250, sufficient equity, acceptable security and legal authority to sign. The house must remain insured and maintained; a mortgage or equity release can obstruct approval.

The [Leeds DPA terms](https://www.leeds.gov.uk/adult-social-care/how-to-get-adult-social-care-and-support/help-to-pay-for-care/help-paying-for-care-homes/deferred-payment-agreements) publish 4.5% interest from July 1, 2026, reviewed each January/July. The January 2027 rate is not confirmed here. Published fees include £199 setup, £100 annual, £157 termination and legal charges usually around £544, plus registry/search charges and possible valuation. Interest and debt must be repaid; Leeds says within 90 days after death. Ask about the equity limit, retained income, fee deferral and what happens if the house does not sell.

### Be careful with a family top-up

A more expensive accommodation choice than a sufficient council personal budget normally needs a willing, affordable third-party top-up. It is separate from Mum's assessed contribution. Get a written agreement covering increases, reviews and missed payments; inability to pay can risk a move. Residents can pay in limited exceptions, including the 12-week disregard or an agreed DPA covering the additional cost, under the [accommodation regulations](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2014/2670/regulation/5/made).

If no suitable affordable placement is actually available, the council must adjust its budget to meet assessed needs. You should not have to top up simply to make up that shortfall. Ask it to identify a suitable available placement before agreeing; the [statutory guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/care-act-2014-supporting-implementation/care-and-support-statutory-guidance) addresses this. Do not casually sign a provider guarantee.

### Do not budget on an old care-cost cap

No £86,000 care-cost cap is in force for this plan. The former £100,000 upper-capital proposal was not implemented either: the [charging reforms were cancelled](https://www.gov.uk/government/speeches/chancellor-statement-on-public-spending-inheritance). No replacement 2027 cap was confirmed. A [specific October 2026 amendment](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/1028/made) concerns Irish compensation payments, not a general cap. Recheck 2027/28 limits and provider increases before April 2027.

## Benefits and NHS support worth checking

### Attendance Allowance: useful, but not automatic

The [2026/27 rates](https://www.gov.uk/attendance-allowance/what-youll-get) are £76.70/week lower rate and £114.60/week higher rate, equivalent to £332.37 and £496.60 per average month. It is not means-tested. Qualifying daytime help/supervision or night-time needs may support the lower rate; both day and night can support the higher rate.

Normally qualifying needs must already have existed for six months. That is not an extra six-month wait after applying. We do not know when Mum's needs began, so none of the baseline budgets assumes an award. Residence and other [eligibility conditions](https://www.gov.uk/attendance-allowance/eligibility) also apply; special end-of-life rules differ. A benefits adviser can help describe difficult days and nights.

Self-funders in care homes can generally retain AA. Publicly funded accommodation/personal care normally stops it after 28 days; tell DWP about changes using its [care-home benefits guidance](https://www.gov.uk/going-into-care-home-benefits). The [Age UK Attendance Allowance factsheet](https://www.ageuk.org.uk/siteassets/documents/factsheets/fs34_attendance_allowance_fcs.pdf?epslanguage=en-GB) specifically distinguishes FNC-only self-funders, who can retain AA, and repayable council advances such as a DPA. Confirm the actual funding arrangement; do not treat FNC-only funding as full CHC.

### Pension Credit: check it, but do not count it as income yet

On these assumptions, pension is about £334.62/week and countable savings add £56/week deemed income, giving £390.62/week assessed income. That exceeds the £238 ordinary guarantee and £324.05 with the conditional £86.05/week severe-disability addition. Illustrative Guarantee Credit and Savings Credit are both nil. At age 82, Savings Credit still needs consideration; nil Guarantee Credit alone is not the reason to dismiss it.

AA is ignored as Pension Credit income. The disability addition normally also requires living alone and nobody being paid a relevant carer benefit or UC carer element for her; it is not automatically £86.05 extra cash. There is no general upper savings cutoff for Pension Credit. Recheck as savings fall or allowable housing costs and capital disregards change. See [eligibility](https://www.gov.uk/pension-credit/eligibility), [amounts](https://www.gov.uk/pension-credit/what-youll-get) and the [technical calculation guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/pension-credit-technical-guidance/a-detailed-guide-to-pension-credit-for-advisers-and-others). This illustration assumes all savings count and no extra qualifying housing costs, additions or other income.

### Council Tax and support for you

Check Mum's existing bill for the [25% single-person discount](https://www.leeds.gov.uk/council-tax/discounts-and-exemptions/single-person). It is separate from [means-tested Council Tax Support](https://www.leeds.gov.uk/council-tax/discounts-and-exemptions/council-tax-support), which £38,000 savings normally prevents on these facts. Pension-age rules differ from working-age rules. Qualifying adaptations may allow a [disability-related band reduction](https://www.leeds.gov.uk/council-tax/discounts-and-exemptions/discounts-for-disabled-people); a permanent move into care leaving an empty home may allow an [empty-property exemption](https://www.leeds.gov.uk/council-tax/discounts-and-exemptions/empty-properties/discounts-and-exemptions-for-empty-properties). Notify the council rather than assuming the bill stops.

Carer's Allowance is £86.45/week, normally requiring at least 35 caring hours/week, Mum receiving a qualifying benefit such as AA, and earnings no more than £204/week after permitted deductions. Full-time work and distance are not automatic exclusions, but typical full-time earnings make eligibility unlikely. A paid award can remove her severe-disability addition, not AA itself. Check [eligibility](https://www.gov.uk/carers-allowance/eligibility) and the [effect on other benefits](https://www.gov.uk/carers-allowance/effect-on-other-benefits) before claiming. Your own carer's assessment does not depend on claiming an allowance.

### CHC and FNC are different kinds of help

[NHS Continuing Healthcare](https://www.nhs.uk/social-care-and-support/money-work-and-benefits/nhs-continuing-healthcare/) funds an assessed primary health need without a means test, at home or in a care home. Nature, intensity, complexity and unpredictability matter, not age, savings or diagnosis alone. Ask the GP, community nurse or social worker whether a CHC Checklist is indicated. A positive Checklist leads to fuller assessment, not automatic funding. Request copies and written reasons.

Decisions normally take place within 28 days once a full assessment is required, not from the first GP conversation. Rapid deterioration approaching a terminal phase has a fast-track route, usually with a package within 48 hours. CHC at home does not pay ordinary household bills; there is no ordinary top-up for the NHS-assessed package.

[NHS-funded nursing care](https://www.nhs.uk/social-care-and-support/money-work-and-benefits/nhs-funded-nursing-care/) is narrower: £267.68/week standard rate from April 1, 2026, paid directly to a nursing home for assessed registered-nursing needs where CHC does not apply. It is not cash for the family or funding for ordinary visiting carers. Ask about CHC first, then FNC where nursing care is needed. Request **gross fee − NHS payment = Mum's bill** in writing. Seacroft's FNC-inclusive advertised fee is not proof of her net invoice; subtract the contribution once only, never again from an already confirmed net quote. Do not assume the rate lasts throughout 2027.

**A: stay home.** This assumes two billed care-hours daily at £32.40/hour, without travel or short-call supplements. They can be split across the day only if assessed safe. The £700 covers food, utilities, Council Tax, insurance, maintenance and personal spending. Using £850 instead raises the monthly draw to £1,365.60 and brings the £23,250 checkpoint forward to 10.80 months. Council help then depends on income, eligible needs and allowable expenses—not simply cash crossing a line. An unchanged zero-cash calculation gives 31.26 months, but is not a care plan.

**B: extra-care flat.** Fixed charges are £322.98/week; two care-hours daily add £275.80/week. Together that is £598.78/week before the assumed £550 private bills/food/personal budget and £200 for the old house. This assumes the former home stays empty, retained and countable, with no disregard. Reaching the cash threshold does not itself unlock council funding. No Housing Benefit or Council Tax Support is assumed; their property rules need a separate check. Moving, furnishing and deposits are extra. Cash alone lasts 20.06 months at unchanged costs, but the whole asset picture matters.

**C: residential placement.** Savings cannot cover the £54,140 first-year gap: the cash-only runway is 8.42 months. Because the house normally counts, the council does not automatically start paying at the cash threshold. Discuss a sale, DPA and written bridging plan well before month three.

For C only, if Leeds agreed to defer the entire ongoing gap after savings reach £23,250, spending £14,750 first would leave £39,390 illustrative debt at year end, plus interest and fees, with £23,250 savings retained. This is a cash-flow illustration, not DPA approval or an assessed contribution. The gap includes personal and empty-house spending; the actual deferrable amount, retained income and provider agreement need assessment. No automatic 12-week subsidy is included: starting savings exceed the limit and the threshold is reached after 12 weeks.

**If lower-rate AA were awarded for months 7–12 only**, it would reduce the first-year gap by £1,994.20. A would end with £25,407.00 savings; B with £17,257.64; C's total year-one gap would fall to £52,145.80, still requiring property-backed funding. ## Questions families often ask

### Should we wait until savings reach the council limit?

No. Request the free needs assessment now and ask Leeds how early it needs a funding discussion. At £38,000 she normally pays initially, but essential support should not wait for paperwork. The threshold is a review point, not a promise of free care.

### Does owning her home rule out help at home?

No. The home she occupies is ignored for that care means test. Her savings, pension, eligible needs and allowable expenses still matter. A permanently vacated former home after moving requires a fresh assessment.

### Are the first 12 weeks in a care home free?

No. The conditional rule ignores property value, not income and other savings. With £38,000 cash she normally still self-funds initially. Ask Leeds to confirm eligibility and dates rather than treating it as a free-care offer.

### Can we count Attendance Allowance in the budget now?

Not until entitlement and timing are clear. Normally qualifying needs must already have existed for six months, and public funding can change payment. The baseline deliberately excludes it; the separate example shows what an award could change.

### Will the advertised nursing fee be Mum's final bill?

Not necessarily. Needs, contract extras, room choice and FNC treatment affect liability. Get the gross total, NHS contribution and resident's invoice in writing, and check the contribution is deducted only once.
