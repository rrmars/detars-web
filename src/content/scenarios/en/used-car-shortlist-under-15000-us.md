**Worried that a cheap car will leave you short of cash? Start with a Mazda3 sedan or Corolla LE, and keep the sticker price below your ceiling. A $12,000 Corolla here comes to $13,987.15 with acquisition costs, inspection and history—not $12,000 all in. Keep money for running it, too.**

## Start with what your budget needs to cover

If you plan to buy with cash in Phoenix, Arizona, in January 2027 and drive 15,000 miles/year, this is a useful starting budget. The $15,000 ceiling covers acquisition, including tax, title and registration; the examples also include independent inspection and history. There is no trade-in or financing. Fuel, insurance and maintenance come on top. The figures are dated 2026-11-05: November advertisements are examples, not confirmed stock or a promise of January availability.

Choose the car with better records and a sound independent inspection, rather than automatically taking the newer year or lower mileage. Aim for around $14,000 acquired and leave about $1,000 unspent. If your ceiling must cover first-year running costs too, you will need a lower purchase target.

## Which car leaves you enough cash?

Use these hypothetical purchases to compare your cash needs. They are not quotes for the advertised cars below.

| Configuration to look for | Example vehicle price | Acquisition with inspection and history | First-year cash with running costs | Representative combined MPG |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Mazda3 2014–2018, 2.0L sedan, 6-speed automatic | $11,500.00 | $13,441.65 | $18,164.25 | 34 |
| Corolla 2014–2017, 1.8L automatic; confirm CVT/campaign status | $12,000.00 | $13,987.15 | $18,829.92 | 32 |
| Fit 2012–2013, 1.5L, 5-speed automatic | $9,000.00 | $10,714.15 | $15,622.81 | 31 |
| CR-V 2012–2014, 2.4L, 5-speed automatic; preferably 2WD | $11,000.00 | $12,896.15 | $18,710.32 | 26 |

For a conventional automatic commuter, start with Mazda3; consider Corolla with documented transmission work. Fit leaves more cash and has flexible cargo space, but try it on the freeway before deciding. Choose CR-V only if the space is worth the extra fuel and repair money. It is an older space-first fallback, not an equivalent-age replacement for a compact.

### Look past the sticker price

Here is where the Corolla money goes: $12,000.00 vehicle + $500.00 document fee + $1,137.50 tax + $65.00 estimated VLT + $13.50 fixed state fees + $10.00 miscellaneous allowance + $16.15 emissions if due = $13,742.15 dealer subtotal. Add $200.00 for inspection and $45.00 for history to reach $13,987.15. Ask for your own written buyer order; these are allowances, not the seller's quote.

The [Phoenix tax chart](https://www.phoenix.gov/content/dam/phoenix/financesite/documents/privilege-use-tax/Combined-Tax-Rate-Chart-0126.pdf) gives the 9.1% rate used below $14,338 for 2026–2027. A seller elsewhere in the metro area may use a different jurisdiction. This budget conservatively taxes the document fee; remove that fee if the advertised total already includes it. Fixed fees are $4 title + $8 registration under the [state fee statute](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/28/02003.htm), plus $1.50 under the [air-quality fee statute](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/49/00551.htm). The [emissions schedule](https://myazcar.com/testing-info-and-fees) lists $16.15 for Phoenix OBD testing, only if required and not already covered.

VLT is not based on the price you pay for a used car. It depends on the original manufacturer base retail and elapsed registration periods. The [VLT statute](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/28/05801.htm) gives the formula. Treat the $65.00 per vehicle in the table as money set aside until you get an actual VIN calculation. Do not assume an older CR-V automatically gets a lower bill.

For annual running costs, set aside an estimated $1,800.00 for insurance, $1,000.00 for compact maintenance and repairs or $1,500.00 for CR-V, plus fuel. These are not quotes or repair-cost caps. Fuel uses 15,000 miles divided by representative MPG, multiplied by $4.3579/gallon: the Arizona regular price dated 2026-11-05 from [AAA](https://gasprices.aaa.com/?state=AZ). Annual fuel works out to $1,922.60 / $2,042.77 / $2,108.66 / $2,514.17 in table order. Allowing 20% more fuel money gives you a useful what-if check, not a January price forecast. Check the actual year, body, trim and drivetrain before relying on MPG.

These totals show cash spent, not the full economic cost of ownership. They leave out depreciation, resale, financing, parking and tolls. A major A/C, cooling-system or transmission repair can exceed your reserve. A good A/C test in January also cannot prove summer performance.

## Check the defect, not just the year

- Mazda3: [17V-393](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/rcl/2017/RCLRPT-17V393-7065.PDF) concerns affected 2014–2016 Mexico-built cars with hand-operated parking brakes, not every car from those years. The advertised sedan's JM1 VIN does not match that report's 3MZ scope. The separate [23V-487 camera letter](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/rcl/2023/RCONL-23V487-4657.pdf) concerns certain 2014–2018 hatchbacks, not every sedan. Check applicability to the car you are buying.
- Corolla: for affected 2014–2017 CVTs, ask for completion proof for [Toyota JSD](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2018/MC-10197440-9999.pdf), which replaced J0D. A platform saying no open recalls does not confirm this separate service campaign was done. Insist on a clean hot road test and diagnostic scan. Historical remedy terms do not promise coverage today.
- Fit: a 2013 conventional automatic can still have airbag-recall risk. Ask for current VIN-specific completion proof for applicable inflator campaigns. A repairable 2015 CVT recall is not a reason to rule out all later Fits.
- CR-V: pause if it judders when warm under light throttle. [Honda bulletin 15-086 reproduced by OEMDTC](https://oemdtc.com/tsb/10099348/) describes software and ATF action for 2012–2014. It is not a safety recall or a promise of free repairs today. The available text is a reproduction, without a separately checked primary-host PDF. Ask for repair records and VIN checks for applicable latch and interlock recalls, even in Phoenix.

You need not reject a repaired car just because its year appears in a campaign. But a completed repair does not tell you whether an old transmission is healthy overall. Let the records, diagnostic checks and test drive make that decision.

## Before you pay

1. Before January, narrow your preferred cars and choose your own independent shop. Refresh ads, insurance and transaction charges near purchase. Ask for an itemized written out-the-door total.
2. Match VIN across the car, title and history. Check seller identity, lien release, crash and flood history, and consistent mileage. Get current recall status and repair invoices.
3. Ask for a lift inspection, diagnostic scan, cooling and A/C checks, tire and brake assessment, and a cold-to-hot city and freeway drive. If inspection is refused, safety issues remain unresolved or fees eat the buffer, walk away.
4. Read the Buyers Guide, warranty exclusions and written promises. The [FTC dealer guide](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/buying-used-car-dealer) explains why history cannot replace inspection and why there is no federal automatic 3-day cancellation right. Arrange insurance and legal temporary driving permission before collecting the car.
