You are renting in Taipei, you have NT$3.2M saved, and you are looking at a two-bedroom resale in Banqiao or Zhonghe at NT$18–22M. You are asking whether the youth mortgage makes buying in 2027 sensible. **Short answer: don't buy at NT$18–22M in 2027. Youth-loan eligibility is not established: combined take-home pay cannot prove the borrower's income test. Even assuming approval, you would not have the cash. Keep renting through 2027 and save, with the 31 July 2029 application deadline in mind.** This is our read of the numbers, not a guarantee.

This guide follows Yu-ting (32) and Chia-hao (34), with combined net income of NT$145,000 a month and NT$3.2M in savings. It was prepared on 8 November 2026 from public pages and government open data; no bank, agent or developer was contacted and no personal quote was obtained. "Unconfirmed" marks anything that did not come from an official page, and "assumed" marks our own planning figure. **It is general information, not financial, tax or legal advice.** The source materials are not published.

## The short answer and the numbers behind it

At the maximum 80% loan, a NT$20M home needs about NT$4.81M in cash: a NT$4.00M down payment, about NT$0.51M closing costs and about NT$0.3M for fix-up and moving (exactly NT$4,814,600). Against NT$3.2M in savings, that is NT$2,214,600 short once a NT$0.6M safety reserve is kept.

- **Today's savings:** with a NT$0.6M reserve, NT$3.2M supports a purchase price of only about NT$10.13M.
- **End of 2027 (assumed):** if you save NT$40,000 a month for 13 months, you reach NT$3.72M, which supports about NT$12.45M. The NT$40,000 a month is **our assumption**; your household budget was not provided.
- **Timing at NT$40,000 a month:** a NT$14M home becomes affordable (cash plus reserve) in about 22 months, around September 2028. A NT$16M home needs about 33 months, around August 2029, which is just after the 31 July 2029 application deadline. It would need a slightly higher saving rate (about NT$42,000 a month) or a smaller reserve.
- **Meeting the deadline alone does not establish eligibility; the bank must confirm all the conditions when you apply.** Prices, closing costs and rent drift while you wait are not modelled.
- **Financially, buying is also the weaker bet at these prices** unless the home appreciates about 2.0%–2.5% a year for 10 years (NT$20M case, see the comparison below).

**One practical step:** ask a bank for a written pre-assessment before you spend anything else (see "One concrete first step").

## What changed: 新青安 ended on 31 July 2026

The 新青安 round (1 August 2023 to 31 July 2026) no longer exists under that name. It was replaced on 1 August 2026 by 青安3.0, which accepts applications from 1 August 2026 to 31 July 2029, with disbursement by 31 October 2029 (National Treasury Administration, Ministry of Finance). This guide uses the 青安3.0 rules throughout. The rules in the table below are official; whether they apply to you is our reading.

## Bank practice and the Central Bank's credit controls

- **Central Bank selective controls (in force from 18 September 2026, official).** The caps in the Central Bank's table are for companies, high-priced homes and buyers who already own homes: second home 70% with no grace period (raised from 60% on 18 September), third home and above 30%, high-priced homes 30%. The part of the release we read does not define "high-priced". The table lists no loan-share cap for a buyer who owns no home. **Our reading** is that such buyers fall outside those caps; this is unconfirmed because the full regulation text was not read. For you the binding limits are the youth-loan rules above and each bank's credit assessment. The policy rate is unchanged (rediscount rate 2%). **Next step:** ask the bank to state in writing which limits apply to you.
- **Queues and quota (partly unconfirmed).** We found no official source saying the youth-loan quota is exhausted or that public banks are queueing. The Financial Supervisory Commission's order excluded youth loans disbursed after 1 September 2025 from the bank-lending cap in Banking Act Article 72-2 (official). That the same treatment continues for 青安3.0 rests on a media report and is **unconfirmed**. Other media reports from early November 2026 say some public banks control mortgage quota centrally, with disbursement expected from late December to January, and some private banks have paused or queue first-home applications (unconfirmed). Treat that as a reason to ask your bank for a written timeline, not as established fact.
- **Affordability test (unconfirmed).** Banks assess income and repayment ability case by case. We found no regulator-published debt-service-ratio rule, so payment-to-income is shown below as a rule of thumb only.

## What the market looks like where you are looking

- **Prices.** Registered actual-price data for Q1 to Q3 2026, resale apartments and elevator buildings with 2–3 rooms, aged 20–40 years, excluding special-circumstance deals (our calculation from Ministry of the Interior open data; Q3 is a snapshot and late registrations are missing): Banqiao n=112, median NT$527,000 per ping (52.7萬; quartiles NT$437,000–650,000), median total NT$15.3M. Zhonghe n=143, median NT$529,000 per ping (52.9萬), median total NT$16.2M. Within NT$18–22M, Banqiao has n=18 (about 30 ping) and Zhonghe n=37 (about 33 ping). The Banqiao sample is small.
- **What that means.** NT$18–22M buys a larger-than-typical unit. A median 2–3-room resale costs NT$15–16M, close to our NT$16M example, where the cash shortfall is 1,317,340 on today's savings and 797,340 on end-2027 savings.
- **Rents.** A whole-unit 2-bedroom (25–40 ping) in Banqiao and Zhonghe has a median rent of NT$26,000 a month (middle half 22,000–30,000), about what you pay in Taipei. Moving to New Taipei would not cut your rent much.
- **House-price direction.** There is no confirmed official index reading. Media-reported quarterly figures (unconfirmed) show New Taipei prices flat to lower in 2026 Q2. The Central Bank says transactions are cooling and speculation is fading.
- **Rates.** The policy rate is on hold, but the Central Bank notes that market rates have risen recently and that the Fed, ECB and BoJ have raised rates. Your youth loan floats with the Post Office rate, and from year 7 you pay the full rate.

## Ten-year comparison: buying a NT$20M home or renting

Assumptions (ours unless stated): you buy at NT$20M with a NT$16M loan (10M youth plus 6M bank part at 2.55%), 40-year amortising from month 1. The renter pays NT$26,000 a month, rising 2% a year (the Central Bank's CPI forecasts are 2.03% for 2026 and 1.83% for 2027), and invests the cash the buyer would spend up front plus every month's difference between the owner's total outlay (loan payment plus holding costs growing 2% a year) and rent, earning the stated return after tax. The same rent of NT$26,000 is used for every price tier. For the renter's savings, an annual-effective return is converted to a monthly rate with end-of-month contributions. After 10 years the buyer sells at the stated price with 4% selling cost and repays the loan balance. **Capital-gains tax on the sale is not modelled** (a self-use exemption may cover it; unchecked). A shorter horizon would change the result.

## One concrete first step

Ask Bank of Taiwan or Land Bank for a written pre-assessment (預審) for a NT$16M purchase. First ask for its eligibility-income definition, assessment period and document checklist for each proposed borrower; provide the requested income or tax records, your marriage-registration date, property record and credit report. Combined take-home pay is not enough. Ask the bank to address eligibility first, then: (a) whether your cap would be NT$10M or NT$12M if eligible, (b) its proposed amount and rate for the part above the cap, and (c) its realistic disbursement timeline. Pre-assessment is not final loan approval. Only if eligibility and the assumed loan terms are confirmed, use the savings target: about NT$4.52M for a NT$16M home including the NT$0.6M reserve (3,917,340 + 600,000). Review again in mid-2028.

## What would change this advice

- **Savings above about NT$5.41M** (NT$20M home: 4.81M cash plus 0.6M reserve) or family help with the down payment. Then the NT$20M case becomes feasible, though the 10-year comparison still needs growth of 2.0%+ a year.
- **A loan share below 80% from the bank** means more cash needed, not less.
- **A rent shock in your area or loss of your current rental.**
- **Policy.** The Central Bank says it keeps reviewing credit controls; no 2027 change has been announced as far as we found. Youth-loan terms are reviewed by the Ministry of Finance before the 2029 end date.

## What is still unconfirmed

- **Rates:** the live Post Office base rate (official pages still say 1.72%); the bank-part mortgage rate (2.55% is assumed); Bank of Taiwan's and Land Bank's ordinary-mortgage rate and fee pages. **Next step:** get the bank's written rate for the part above the cap.
- **Fees and assumed costs:** the agent fee (2% buyer side), the scrivener fee, insurance premiums, the stamp-duty base in practice, 書狀費, the mortgage-registration base, the renovation allowance, the management fee, the repair allowance, the NT$40,000 a month saving, the NT$0.6M reserve, and the assessed-house-value and declared-land-value ratios are all assumptions.
- **Tax on sale:** capital-gains tax on selling the home is not modelled in the 10-year comparison.
- **Queues and prices:** queueing or quota claims in the media, and house-price index readings.
- **Your own facts:** eligibility remains unestablished until the bank assesses each proposed borrower's income using its required definition, period and documents, and verifies ownership; combined net pay cannot establish it. Your marriage-registration date decides the NT$10M or NT$12M cap if eligible.
- **Tax and fee rules:** only the 6% deed tax was re-read against its statute; the other tax and fee rules, and the house-price index readings, were not independently re-read.
