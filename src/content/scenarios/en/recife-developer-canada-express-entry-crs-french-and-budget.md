Thiago is a software developer and Camila is a nurse. You have no Canadian work and no job offer, and you are not sure whether Express Entry is realistic or whether your savings will last. **Short answer: Thiago should be the principal applicant, and he should invest in French (NCLC 7 in all four skills). French adds +62 CRS points and is the only route we found that matches a profile with no Canadian experience to the kinds of round that are actually happening (a provincial nomination is a separate path this guide does not cover).** The money works on paper, but several budget lines are placeholders.

This guide follows Thiago (29, about 6 years as a developer) and Camila (28, about 4 years as a registered nurse), both with a bachelor's degree, BRL 140,000 saved, and Calgary as the first city. It was written on 8 November 2026 from public pages only; nobody was contacted and no application, account or quote was made. Their English levels are our estimates (Thiago CLB 8–9, Camila CLB 7), not test results. **It is general information, not legal or immigration advice.** The source materials are not published.

Tags used below: "official page" means a Government of Canada, test-body, bank or provincial page that was read; "unconfirmed" means a third-party page, a search snippet or our own memory; "placeholder" and "assumed" mean our planning figure with no source.

## The recommendation and why

**Thiago is the principal applicant. Invest in French. Re-check the rounds page before you rely on any cut-off.**

- **Why Thiago:** the principal's own language, age, education and work history carry the big points. With his estimated English (CLB 8–9) he scores 358–423 on the Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS). Camila as principal, at her estimated CLB 7, scores 334–342 (342 if Thiago as her spouse is at CLB 9; 334 if he is at CLB 8). Swap only if Camila's English ends up higher than his.
- **Her nursing job is not a scoring advantage:** the healthcare category draws needed 462–476 in the last year. Details are below.
- **Why French:** it adds +62 points (12 for a second language plus 50 bonus). More important, there has been no general round since 23 April 2024 and no STEM round since 11 April 2024. French-category rounds in 2026 had cut-offs of 382–435 (median 400) and invited 4,000–8,500 people each. Thiago at CLB 8 + French scores 420; at CLB 9 + French, 485.
- **Biggest risk:** the French timeline. Going from zero to NCLC 7 in all four skills probably takes 9–18 months of study (our estimate, assumed), and Canada changed the CRS on 3 November 2026 with no round held under the new rules when the page was read. **Next step:** start French lessons now, and look at the official rounds page again before you plan around any number here.
- **Money:** BRL 140,000 is about C$35,910 at BRL 3.8986 per CAD. Estimated costs before applying are about C$8,138 (R$31,725), leaving about C$27,772 against the C$19,001 of settlement funds IRCC's table shows for two people (that table is dated July 2025; the 2026 figure was not confirmed). Three months in Calgary with no income would cost about C$17,301 more. Several lines are placeholders.

## What changed on 3 November 2026

The CRS now has a Labour Market Integration factor (at most 150 points without a spouse, 140 with one). It merges Canadian work experience, with extra points for high-wage occupations, and a high-wage job offer, which no longer needs an LMIA. For Thiago and Camila, who have no Canadian work and no job offer, this factor is 0. Rounds were paused for up to two weeks while IRCC (Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada) recalculated, and the latest round visible when we read the page was on 28 October 2026.

Our inference, not confirmed against the old grid, is that the change does not move their scores, so the 2026 cut-offs stay broadly comparable. **Treat every cut-off in this guide as indicative:** all of them predate the change. **Next step:** after the first round under the new rules, compare its cut-off with the tables below before you commit money to a test date.

## Can you qualify at all? The Federal Skilled Worker 67-point grid

The Federal Skilled Worker (FSW) program needs minimums first: 1 year of continuous full-time skilled work (TEER 0–3) in the last 10 years, CLB 7 in all four skills for TEER 0/1 jobs, an education credential assessment (ECA) for foreign education, proof of funds, admissibility, and a plan to live outside Quebec. Software developers (NOC 21232) and registered nurses (31301) appear in IRCC's lists; that they are TEER 1 is from our own knowledge and unconfirmed. You must then score 67 of 100. FSW points are separate from CRS points.

| Factor | Max | Thiago, CLB 8 all, no French | Pts | Camila as principal, CLB 7 all | Pts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First language (4 skills) | 24 | CLB 8 = 5 × 4 | 20 | CLB 7 = 4 × 4 | 16 |
| Second language | 4 | none (CLB 5+ needed) | 0 | none | 0 |
| Education | 25 | Bachelor's, 3+ years = 21 (unconfirmed: from memory of the IRCC grid) | 21 | same | 21 |
| Skilled experience | 15 | 6 years = 15 | 15 | 4 years = 13 | 13 |
| Age | 12 | 29 (18–35) = 12 | 12 | 28 = 12 | 12 |
| Arranged employment | 10 | no offer | 0 | no offer | 0 |
| Adaptability | 10 | spouse CLB 4+ in all four = 5 | 5 | spouse CLB 4+ = 5 | 5 |
| Total (pass = 67) | 100 | 20+0+21+15+12+0+5 | 73 | 16+0+21+13+12+0+5 | 67 |

Variations: Thiago at CLB 7 = 69, at CLB 9 = 77, and at CLB 8 + French NCLC 7 (second language 4) = 77. Camila at CLB 8 = 71. **Camila's margin at CLB 7 is zero, and the 21 points for education could not be confirmed on a rendered official page, so one point either way decides her eligibility.** That is another reason not to make her principal. **Next step:** check the education line in IRCC's FSW tool before you rely on either total. Adaptability points also require Camila's test results.

## CRS worked examples (rules in force from 3 November 2026)

With a spouse coming along, the principal's core points are capped at 530 and the spouse's own factors add at most 40: education (a bachelor's = 8 of 10), language (CLB 7–8 = 3 per skill; CLB 9+ = 5 per skill, max 20) and Canadian work (0 for her). Skill transferability (max 100) uses the principal's education × language and foreign work × language. Thiago has 6 years of foreign work, so that block gives 25 points (CLB 7–8) or 50 (CLB 9 in all four skills), plus 13 or 25 for education. French adds 50 (NCLC 7 in all four skills plus CLB 5+ English).

Fixed inputs: Thiago, 29, gets 100 age points (20–29 with a spouse), dropping to 95 at 30. The CRS says age counts on the date of invitation; we did not find that wording on the page text, so treat it as unconfirmed. A bachelor's is 112. Camila at CLB 7 gives 3 × 4 = 12, plus 8 for her bachelor's and 0 for Canadian work, so her subtotal is 20.

Table B assumes Thiago at CLB 9 (8 for education + 20 for language). If Thiago were at CLB 8, B1 would be 334, which is where the low end of the 334–342 range comes from. Camila's 4 years of foreign work give the same skill-transferability tier as Thiago's 6, because the top band starts at 3 years. If both had the same language level, the totals would be almost identical (A4 = 423 with Camila at CLB 7, B3 = 431 with both at CLB 9). So the principal should be whoever has the stronger test scores; the spouse's improvement from CLB 7 to CLB 9 is worth only +8 points (12 to 20).

**Counterintuitive finding:** common advice says "make the nurse the principal to use the healthcare draws". That assumes healthcare cut-offs are low. They are not: 462–476 across the eight healthcare rounds from July 2025 to September 2026, higher than the French rounds (382–435). Camila at CLB 7 (342) is 120 to 134 points short, and even with French (404) she would be short. The healthcare route only wins if she reaches about CLB 9 plus French (493), and at that point Thiago as principal with the same French is within 8 points (485). One French certificate is needed either way. Healthcare also requires at least 1 year of full-time work in a listed health occupation within the previous 3 years; registered nurses (31301) are on the list.

## What French adds, and what it takes

| Item | Points | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Second official language, NCLC 7–8 | +12 | 3 per skill × 4 (CLB 4 or less = 0; 5–6 = 1; 9+ = 6) |
| Second official language, NCLC 9+ | +22 | 6 per skill, capped at 22 with a spouse |
| French additional points (NCLC 7 in all four, English CLB 5+) | +50 | +25 if English is CLB 4 or below |
| Total for NCLC 7 in all four skills | +62 | 12 + 50. Skill transferability uses the first language only, so nothing extra there |

To enter French-category rounds you only need NCLC 7 in all four skills on an approved test less than 2 years old. The accepted French tests are TEF Canada and TCF Canada, and results stay valid for 2 years. We found no published TEF Canada price for Recife. TCF Canada is given at Aliança Francesa Recife, but the price is quoted on request and is not public.

**One more English level versus French:** going from CLB 8 to CLB 9 in all four skills (A2 to A4) is +65 points with no French (358 to 423), slightly more than French's +62. But it does not unlock any draw: without French, a candidate with no Canadian experience has no matching round type at present. French changes access, English CLB 9 changes rank. Do both if time allows; if you must choose, French first.

## Settlement funds for a couple

A family of 2 needs C$19,001, which is about R$74,077 at 3.8986 (19,001 × 3.8986 = 74,077.3). The table on IRCC's page is dated 7 July 2025, and IRCC says it updates yearly in July, so the 2026 figure is probably higher and could not be confirmed. **Next step:** re-check the official page before you transfer or document anything.

- The funds must be available at application and at visa issuance. Bank letters need balances, the 6-month average, account-opening dates and debts. Borrowed money and property equity do not count; joint accounts do.
- They are not required under the Canadian Experience Class, or with a valid job offer while authorized to work in Canada.
- **Sensitivity:** savings after pre-application costs are R$108,275 (see the budget below). That equals C$19,001 at about BRL 5.70 per CAD (108,275 ÷ 19,001), against 3.8986 today. The real would have to lose about 32% of its value in Canadian dollars to reach that point (3.8986 to 5.70 is a 46% rise in BRL per CAD). The margin of about C$8,771 would also absorb a rise of up to about 46% in the required amount, if the 2026 table is higher.

## Documents: ECA and police certificates

**ECA (education credential assessment).** The principal needs one to be FSW-eligible, and it is also needed to earn points for foreign education, including the spouse's. Only the highest credential needs assessing. Designated organizations are WES, ICAS, CES (University of Toronto), IQAS and BCIT ICES. This guide uses WES: the ECA is valid 5 years and can be reused in a new profile. We could not read WES's own ECA price: a third-party page says C$264 (unconfirmed), WES notes a 3% price rise from 1 January 2026, and the international priority courier is C$97 (column order inferred, unconfirmed). Steps: create a WES account, order the ECA for IRCC, have your university send verified documents, pay, wait, then enter the reference number in the Express Entry profile. We did not confirm the ECA turnaround.

**Nursing is separate.** An ECA gives immigration points but not a licence. Camila must register with a provincial regulator (through NNAS for most provinces). We could not find the NNAS fee and did not read the process pages. **Next step:** research this before choosing a province.

**Police certificates (Brazil).** Both of you need one for every country lived in for 6 months or more in the last 10 years (for you, only Brazil). The document is the Certidão de Antecedentes Criminais from the Brazilian Federal Police; state certificates are not accepted. It is free, issued online and valid 90 days. For the country where you live now, the certificate must be issued no more than 6 months before you submit, so request it after the invitation, inside the 60-day window to file. We found no public price list for sworn translations and apostilles of other documents (marriage certificate, diplomas, work letters).
