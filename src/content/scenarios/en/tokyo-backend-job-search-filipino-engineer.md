You are probably wondering whether N4 will shut every door. Whether six years of backend work is enough. And whether the move will eat your savings before the first payday.

My read: this is realistic. Go and Python give you a useful starting point. Target teams that already work in English and bring engineers from abroad. Your biggest unknown is not Japanese. It is whether your education or qualifications satisfy the work-status route.

For a Filipino backend engineer living in the Philippines, with six years’ experience and JLPT N4. Target: employed in Tokyo by **1 October 2027**. Chinese-speaking engineers can use the translated plan too, but it retains this Filipino profile and departure requirements. This is friendly information, not immigration or legal advice; have the employer’s qualified adviser check your actual documents.

## The short version

- **Salary target: ¥8–11m gross a year** — my negotiating estimate, not a guaranteed offer. Consider ¥7m if the team, workload and relocation support are genuinely good. Six years does not automatically mean senior. Here, m means million yen.
- **Keep about ¥1.5m accessible before departure** — estimated for one adult, including setup and a separate three-month buffer. Reduce it only for support confirmed in writing.
- **Check the degree/qualification gate this week.** Six years alone is below the usual ten-year experience alternative. Do not spend months interviewing before settling this.
- **Search now. Move when the paperwork works.** “By October” can mean a spring or summer start. If October is the only possible start, shift the main 16-week campaign to March 1, 2027–June 20, 2027.

## Use the ordinary engineer route first. Check HSP second.

### Engineer / Specialist in Humanities / International Services

This is the normal work-status route for a qualifying backend engineering job. You need a real contract with an organisation in Japan, technical duties that fit the status, and pay at least comparable to a Japanese worker doing comparable work. See the [ISA application page](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/status/gijinkoku.html) and its [landing criteria, page 1](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/content/001366995.pdf).

- **Relevant university degree/equivalent?** Gather diploma and transcript. The degree/major has not been supplied, so eligibility is still unconfirmed.
- **No qualifying degree?** The alternative is normally ten years of relevant experience, including certain qualifying relevant study periods. Six work years alone is not enough. Do not count every school year or overlap twice.
- **A qualifying Japanese vocational credential is a separate branch.** It is not a blanket acceptance of every overseas short diploma. Have the employer's adviser assess the exact institution, course and documents.
- **There is an IT-specific exception.** The [official designated-exam notice, item 3](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/policies/bill/nyukan_hourei_h09.html) lists Philippine PhilNITS FE and AP exams. A qualifying pass can replace the education/ten-year branch for relevant IT work. A generic cloud certificate does not automatically qualify. Exam availability, pass status and fit still need checking.

**N4 is not a blanket visa blocker for backend work.** The technical criteria do not impose a universal N2 test. The ISA page’s **15 April 2026** change asks category 3/4 cases mainly doing language-based interpersonal work for B2-equivalent language evidence; it is not an across-the-board N2 rule for software engineers. ISA can still request evidence case by case. Employer requirements are separate.

### From the Philippines: the order matters

- Get a qualifying written offer. Confirm who will own sponsorship and Philippine-side paperwork.
- The employer’s authorised representative in Japan prepares the **Certificate of Eligibility (COE)** application. You supply passport, education/experience evidence and other requested documents; employer-document lists differ by category. ISA’s [published standard COE processing period](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/procedures/16-1.html) is **1–3 months**, not a deadline or guarantee.
- With the COE, use the competent Japanese mission’s current working-visa channel. [MOFA](https://www.mofa.go.jp/j_info/visit/visa/long/visa1.html) explains that a COE supports the application but does not guarantee a visa. Confirm the Philippine submission channel at application time.
- Complete applicable Philippine overseas-employment clearance, then travel and undergo landing examination. Do not treat an offer, COE or tourist entry as permission to start work.

**Two easy-to-miss Philippines checks.** [ISA’s pre-entry TB rules](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/10_00219.html) generally require a designated-clinic TB clearance for a Filipino national living in the Philippines applying for new medium/long-term entry. Mandatory submission began **23 June 2025**; usual validity is **180 days from the examination**. Coordinate it with the COE, not a year early. Residence outside target countries and specified programmes can change applicability.  [MWO-Tokyo’s direct-hire exemption checklist](https://mwo-tokyo.dmw.gov.ph/index.php/2025/08/04/exemption-on-the-ban-on-direct-hiring/) (4 Aug 2025) requires employer cooperation and separate contract verification. It publishes **10–15 working days for verification**, not total departure clearance. Ask DMW/MWO to confirm direct-hire exemption versus licensed-agency route, current medical/orientation/insurance steps and exact OEC/exit-clearance requirement. The end-to-end 2027 process remains **verification required**; a Japanese visa does not settle it.

The cited MWO checklist says contract verification itself has no fee. That does not make medicals, insurance or the whole deployment free. Have the employer confirm the applicable route and each cost; the flight/documents allowance below may not cover your actual Philippine deployment costs. Do not assume a returning-worker exemption applies to a first deployment.

### Highly Skilled Professional 1(b): possible, not automatic

For advanced specialised/technical work, the points route needs **70 points**, qualifying underlying activities/eligibility and at least **¥3m annual remuneration**. That minimum is not a salary recommendation. Use the [ISA points table, page 1](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/content/001398882.pdf), [current evaluation page](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/resources/newimmiact_3_evaluate_index.html) and [official FAQ](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/resources/newimmiact_3_qa.html). The linked table is undated; accessed 6 Nov 2026.

| Factor | Rough points for this case |
| --- | --- |
| Education — unknown | Bachelor/equivalent 10; master 20; doctorate 30. Use the applicable highest category, not all three added together. |
| Relevant experience | Six years = 10. Seven completed years = 15; ten = 20. The extra point band is not earned merely because 2027 arrives. |
| Age — unknown | 29 or younger: 15; 30–34: 10; 35–39: 5; 40+: 0. Use age at assessment. |
| Japanese | N4 = 0. N2 = 10; N1 = 15. Not cumulative; N2 also has an exclusion when the Japanese-higher-education bonus applies. |
| Offer remuneration | ¥8m = 30; ¥9m = 35; ¥10m+ = 40 across ages. ¥7m = 25 below age 40, but 0 salary points at 40+. Lower thresholds are age-dependent. Use qualifying accepting-organisation remuneration, not speculative equity or uncertain overtime. |

**Illustrations, not your established score:** age 28 + bachelor + six years + ¥8m + N4 = 15 + 10 + 10 + 30 + 0 = **65**. At seven completed years, that becomes **70**. Age 32 with the same bachelor, six years, ¥8m and N4 gives **60**; documented N2 would bring it to **70**. Age 42 + bachelor + seven years + ¥10m + N4 gives **65**.

No employer, university, research or exam bonuses are assumed. Age, degree and salary are unknown, so your real score cannot be established yet. If eligible, submit the HSP self-calculation and supporting evidence with the HSP COE process; it is not an automatic upgrade. Falling short of HSP does not itself prevent an ordinary engineer status. Do not postpone a good move just to chase points.

## The first apartment is the expensive part.

This model is for **one adult, no dependents, no car**. A modest studio/1K and a commute, not a central luxury apartment. Every budget amount is an **estimate in November 2026 yen**, not a quote for October 2027. Reprice housing and flights before accepting.

That is historical occupied-stock rent, not today’s asking rent. I would budget **¥90,000 rent + ¥5,000 building fees** instead. Choose the train line after you know the office.

Basis: [Selectra, updated 27 Oct 2026](https://selectra.jp/en/life-in-japan/first-cost-in-japan), gives key money/deposit commonly 1–2 months each, guarantor 50–100% of rent plus fees, and basic furnishing ¥100,000–250,000. It is a commercial affiliate guide. [Housing Rep Tokyo’s undated rental guide](https://www.houserep-tokyo.com/rent/cost) explains advance rent, cleaning deductions and utilities outside rent. Its luxury examples are not our rent benchmark. A “3–5 months upfront” headline is not a cap: this example is already about 5.1 months of base rent.

### First 12 months: about ¥3.45m cash outflow

**¥2,760,000** living costs (12 × ¥230,000) + **¥364,500** lease costs excluding the first ¥95,000 housing already counted + **¥150,000** basic furnishings + **¥120,000** temporary accommodation + **¥50,000** flight/documents allowance = **¥3,444,500**. Temporary housing is an additional transition cushion; shorten it if it overlaps the permanent lease. The ¥90,000 refundable deposit is included as cash outflow, not a permanent expense.

**Departure fund: about ¥1.5m.** Setup cash of ¥779,500 (lease + furnishing + temporary stay + flight/docs), plus an untouched ¥690,000 three-month reserve, totals ¥1,469,500. The reserve is not another annual expense. Ask about the first payday and whether relocation is reimbursed only afterwards.

Not included: payroll income/resident taxes, pension/health/employment-insurance deductions, remittances, dependents, major dental work or trips home. Ask payroll to show both arrival-year and following-year net pay; do not build the plan on a tax-free first year. Save a further **10–15% cost cushion (estimate)** if moving late in 2027. An extra ¥10,000 monthly rent adds ¥120,000 yearly, before larger rent-linked fees.

## Sixteen weeks. Then a decision, not endless applying.

**Main campaign: 9 November 2026–28 February 2027.** Budget 8–10 hours a week (estimate) alongside work. Keep your current job during the search. Start earlier than October if a good employer can bring you over sooner. Current vacancies are leads for now, not inventory held until 2027.

| Week | Do this | Concrete checkpoint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · 9–15 Nov | Check degree, transcript, job-history proof, passport and notice period. Build a cash target. Identify the qualifying work-status route. | One eligibility folder; missing qualifications escalated to employer/adviser before assuming sponsorship works. |
| 2 · 16–22 Nov | Write a two-page English CV. Lead with Go/Python services, production ownership, scale, latency/reliability and measurable outcomes. Use only real metrics. | CV + six short project/incident stories. Put “Philippines; relocation required; JLPT N4” clearly. |
| 3 · 23–29 Nov | Build a 25-company list. Tag no-Japanese, overseas eligibility, Tokyo office, stack and salary. Check the ten leads above again. | First four tailored applications, only to live, eligible roles. |
| 4 · 30 Nov–6 Dec | Practise a Go or Python coding interview and one English system-design mock. Explain trade-offs aloud. | Four more applications; one mock reviewed for communication, not just correctness. |
| 5 · 7–13 Dec | Prepare SQL/indexing, API design, testing, concurrency and failure handling. Show one sanitised public project or architecture write-up. | Four applications; portfolio demonstrates production judgment, no employer secrets. |
| 6 · 14–20 Dec | Handle screens. Ask about team language, overseas sponsorship, timing and range before long take-homes. | Four applications; every active lead has a clear next step. |
| 7 · 21–27 Dec | Send four more tailored applications and review responses. Follow up once after about 7–10 business days, allowing holidays. | Roughly 20 tailored applications by now. If fewer than two screens, fix targeting/CV before sending more. |
| 8 · 28 Dec–3 Jan | Expect slower replies. Rest, practise one design case, and tighten weak CV bullets. | No panic mass-applying. Updated January shortlist. |
| 9 · 4–10 Jan | Refresh listings and resume the pipeline. Practise queues, idempotency, caching and observable services. | Four applications; two coding/design practice sessions. |
| 10 · 11–17 Jan | Prepare deployment, AWS/GCP and incident stories. Own gaps honestly; do not claim unfamiliar tools. | Four applications; ask active teams what “senior” means to them. |
| 11 · 18–24 Jan | Use one technical mock to attack the failure pattern from real interviews. | Four applications. Screens but no technical passes → practise; no screens → retarget. |
| 12 · 25–31 Jan | Keep final-round preparation and new leads running together. | Four applications; about 36 total. Aim for two late-stage tracks, not a promised conversion rate. |
| 13 · 1–7 Feb | Ask finalists about COE owner, Philippine departure compliance, relocation cap and start-date flexibility. | Written process/timing outline. If no late stage, widen company size and adjacent backend/platform roles. |
| 14 · 8–14 Feb | Compare any offer on guaranteed pay, work hours, probation, on-call, office days and relocation clawbacks. | Salary + payroll illustration + sponsor/qualification check. No offer yet? Four targeted applications. |
| 15 · 15–21 Feb | Negotiate from scope and evidence. Gather reference permissions and immigration documents when requested. | One comparison sheet. No resignation based on a verbal promise. |
| 16 · 22–28 Feb | Choose the strongest viable offer, or diagnose and restart a narrower campaign. | Written offer and paperwork plan, or explicit reset. Do not buy a flight to manufacture momentum. |

### Japanese: a parallel habit, not the admission ticket

Spend **30–45 minutes a day (recommended)** on listening and practical speaking. Add two short conversation sessions weekly if affordable. Work on introductions, clarifying a task, housing, clinics and ward-office visits. Aim toward N3 during 2027 as a learning goal, not a guaranteed exam result. N4 is not evidence of business conversation; apply to “no Japanese required” roles rather than treating N2/N3 requirements as optional.

### The calendar after the campaign

- **March–May 2027:** close outstanding interviews or join earlier if ready. Refresh closed leads. If a fixed October start is essential, run the same weekly sequence from 1 March through 20 June instead.
- **By 30 June 2027:** aim for a signed offer and confirmed sponsor/document route. This is a planning deadline, not an official immigration deadline.
- **July–August:** employer handles the COE process; you handle requested evidence and Philippine departure compliance. Allow a multi-month buffer. Neither an offer nor a COE alone authorises work.
- **September:** travel only after the required visa and departure clearance are in place. Use temporary housing, then choose a lease near the actual commute. If approval is late, agree a later start rather than working on a tourist entry.
- **1 October 2027:** target start. If no qualifying offer by July, revise the date while staying employed. A rushed bad contract is not a successful move.

**Before accepting, ask:** Which language is used in stand-ups and incident response? Who files and pays for sponsorship? Can someone applying from the Philippines join? What is the guaranteed cash salary? How much fixed overtime is included? When is the first payday? Is housing paid upfront or reimbursed? Who handles DMW/MWO requirements?
