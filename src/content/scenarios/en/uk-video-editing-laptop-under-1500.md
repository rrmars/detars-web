## Check your Sony files before choosing the operating system

Decoding unpacks compressed camera frames. GPU processing handles the grade, effects and compositing. A machine can be strong at the second and still struggle with—or lack software permission for—the first.

Bring an original clip. Check **H.264 versus H.265, 8-bit versus 10-bit, 4:2:0 versus 4:2:2, frame rate and recording mode**. “H.265 supported” does not answer all of those questions.

### Why the Air is the lower-friction first choice

Apple lists an H.264/HEVC media engine, but does not spell out the Air's exact 10-bit 4:2:2 profiles. [Adobe's guidance](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/get-started/technical-requirements/hardware-accelerated-decoding-and-encoding.html), updated January 7, 2026, names Apple silicon M1 Pro/Max/Ultra “or newer” for 10-bit 4:2:2 decoding. That is Premiere guidance, **not an M5-specific Resolve playback benchmark**.

The September 2026 [Resolve 21.1 codec table](https://documents.blackmagicdesign.com/SupportNotes/DaVinci_Resolve_21_Supported_Codec_List.pdf) lists macOS H.264/H.265 GPU acceleration without Studio-only wording on those rows. That makes the Air the more straightforward choice here, not a guarantee for every Sony profile. Test your exact files.

### Why Windows needs a proxy plan

The same table gives Windows Free H.265 **8/10-bit OS-supported profiles**; more profiles and GPU acceleration are in Studio. Windows Free H.264 starts with 8-bit OS-supported profiles. Neither “no 10-bit works” nor “all 10-bit 4:2:2 works” is a safe rule.

For this budget, plan **ProRes or DNxHR proxies/intermediates** rather than quietly adding an unpriced Studio licence. If Resolve cannot import the original, transcode externally first. Free still uses the GPU for image processing; that is separate from codec acceleration.

[Intel's March 2023 media table](https://cdrdv2-public.intel.com/773553/onevpl_developer-reference-media-intel-hardware_1.1-772607-773553.pdf) marks HEVC 10-bit 4:2:2 decode for 10th-generation Ice Lake, not other 10th-generation platforms, and for the 11th-, 12th- and 13th-generation tables. The Legion's 13th-generation platform is relevant only if its integrated GPU stays available to the application. A dGPU-only mode can remove that route. Do not generalise this to every Intel chip or Core Ultra.

[NVIDIA's decode matrix](https://developer.nvidia.com/video-encode-decode-support-matrix) marks RTX 4060/4070 laptop HEVC 4:2:2 unsupported. Its [SDK 13 Blackwell announcement](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-video-codec-sdk-13-0-powered-by-nvidia-blackwell/), dated February 24, 2025, includes HEVC 8/10/12-bit 4:2:2 decoding. But the inspected matrix did not establish every 5050/5060 laptop row. Exact laptop and application integration remain unconfirmed. Studio cannot add a profile absent from the hardware, and capable hardware does not remove an edition restriction.

**Counterintuitive finding:** the strongest gaming GPU is not automatically the best purchase for Resolve Free. For this travel-and-codec mix, the lighter Mac can be more useful even when a Windows RTX laptop has more effects headroom.

### Check what your “4K” delivery actually requires

[Blackmagic's product page](https://www.blackmagicdesign.com/products/davinciresolve) specifies Free output up to **Ultra HD 3840×2160 at 60fps**. The codec PDF separately has a broad “≥4K” Studio-only encoding footnote. This recommendation assumes UHD delivery and a successful test of your actual export preset—not DCI 4096-wide output, higher frame rates or every codec's 4K export.

## Make the small setup work well

- **Memory:** 16GB is a workable minimum, not generous. Prefer 24–32GB for longer timelines, Fusion and Premiere alongside other apps only if the whole basket fits. Unified memory is shared with the GPU; it is not directly equivalent to 16GB system RAM plus 8GB VRAM. For a discrete GPU, 8GB VRAM is a sensible 4K-effects target, not an FPS promise.
- **Cooling:** keep the Air's layers and effects modest. Use render cache and proxies when needed. Long, effects-heavy renders expose its fanless limit. Plan mains power and fan noise for demanding Windows GPU work. Neither choice promises effortless effects-heavy native 4K multicam.
- **Storage:** 512GB can work with an external SSD; 1TB gives cache more room. Keep internal free space. Proxies can be much larger than camera originals. A working external drive is **not a backup**.
- **Colour:** for web/Rec.709 work, prioritise a calibrated near-100% sRGB display. Gamut coverage is not accuracy; OLED is not calibration. Disable True Tone/Night Shift for colour decisions and control the viewing environment. None of these screens is certified here for HDR mastering.
- **Ingest:** use an existing camera USB transfer cable or an appropriate reader. Sony CFexpress needs its own suitable reader; the A14's microSD slot does not accept full-size Sony SD cards. A 10Gb/s USB SSD does not need Thunderbolt to reach its intended interface speed. On the Air, MagSafe can handle power while the drive occupies a data port. USB-C shape alone does not establish USB4 or Thunderbolt.
- **Bag weight:** count the charger too. The Air's 1.23kg and fanless design suit travel; the A14's 1.46kg is appealing for an RTX machine, but its adapter adds weight. Comparable measured editing-runtime and noise results are missing across all four exact configurations.

Before paying, confirm the **exact SKU, UK keyboard, seller, charger, memory, screen gamut, delivered total, warranty and return terms**. In your actual installed Resolve edition, test native playback, scrubbing, a grade, export and proxy relinking with your own original clip. A decoder check alone is not a whole-workflow test.

## FAQ

### Is the Air setup definitely under £1,500?

Only conditionally. The laptop and adapter had direct dated prices, but the SSD price and delivery remain unconfirmed. Use the delivered-drive ceiling above and leave room for any essential reader.

### Will an RTX 5060 remove the need for proxies?

Not necessarily. The exact hardware/application path still needs checking, and Windows Resolve Free has codec restrictions distinct from GPU processing. Keep proxies or external transcoding in the plan.

### Are the review battery hours a guide to editing all day?

No. Those are Wi-Fi or video-rundown tests. They do not establish Resolve runtime, sustained export speed or Sony timeline playback.

### Should I pay extra for Studio now?

No Studio cost is included in this plan. Start with the actual Free workflow and clip test. Do not assume paying for software repairs a missing hardware codec profile or leaves the basket within budget.
