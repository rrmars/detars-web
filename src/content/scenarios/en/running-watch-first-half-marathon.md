**For a first half marathon, I would choose the Garmin Forerunner 165 for clearer training guidance, or the COROS PACE 3 for a light, running-first watch with dual-frequency GPS. The sampled prices were $191.90 and $199 before tax and shipping—not checkout quotes. You do not need to spend the whole $250 budget to get useful run tracking.**

This example is for an Austin runner using Android, running three or four times a week toward an April 2027 half marathon. It is **desk research, not hands-on testing**: specifications, public US offers and attributed reviewers' experiences, not our own runs or battery measurements. Prices were captured on November 6, 2026 in Singapore, November 5, 2026 in Austin and UTC. They have not been refreshed for this draft; stock and delivery to your address need checking.

## Start with the help you actually want

Choose **Garmin Forerunner 165 non-Music, Black/Slate** if you want help turning a race goal into a manageable week. Garmin Coach half-marathon plans, race-aware daily suggestions, intervals, recovery time and PacePro pacing guidance are more useful here than another premium dashboard. Buttons and a bright AMOLED screen are a friendly combination. The Walmart offer was low stock; it was sold and shipped by Walmart.com, not a confirmed Austin delivery.

Choose **COROS PACE 3, Chalk** if you prefer a light, always-on running watch and value dual-frequency GPS more than smartwatch polish. Free plans, EvoLab fatigue/recovery analysis, race estimates and breadcrumb routes give you room to grow. Wareable found its training presentation less intuitive than Garmin's. Its screen is transflective, not vivid AMOLED, and breadcrumb guidance is not a full street map.

If price matters most, **Amazfit Active 2 Round, Black Sport Silicone at $99.99** is a reasonable compromise. I would not choose it primarily to trust wrist-heart-rate zones: the independent reviews disagree about workout accuracy and software smoothness. The Round and Square models are not interchangeable.

## Compare the mode you will use, not the biggest battery number

All these watches have built-in GPS, so a phone is not required to record the route. “Multi-system” means several satellite constellations; it does **not** automatically mean multiband or dual frequency. All prices below are sampled new US-market offers, before sales tax and shipping. Public availability flags are not delivery promises.

These are manufacturer ceilings using different settings, **not matched real-world test results**. Music, maps, screen choices and connected sensors change runtime. My read is that each offers ample published endurance for a first half when charged beforehand; that is a planning judgment, not a battery guarantee. Do not treat COROS's 38h as dual-frequency endurance or Suunto's 40h saving mode as its highest-accuracy setting.

Suunto advertised **$199 with STAWON50**, but coupon acceptance and terms were not checkout-tested. Budget $249 unless your own final basket proves otherwise. App support is established, not compatibility with your exact handset; minimum Android versions were not established for the other watches.

## What the reviews mean for your runs

**Garmin: wait for a GPS lock.** Wareable's historical review sometimes waited 4–5 minutes for satellite acquisition, while otherwise finding tracking solid and HR generally good, with lag when effort changed. That does not establish a current firmware fault. The base model has no offline music; LiveTrack and incident messages also need a compatible connected phone.

**COROS: expect useful data with less hand-holding.** Wareable liked GPS and workout-HR accuracy, including intervals, but found analysis and workout planning less neatly explained. COROS revised daily battery estimates to include sleep tracking, so do not rely on old “24 days” expectations for everyday wear.

**Amazfit: the feature list cannot settle accuracy.** Wareable reported inaccurate average/max exercise HR and map lag. Notebookcheck found better sampled HR and smoother software, but its HR checks were bike rides, not a complete running-interval validation. Neither result proves every unit behaves alike. If wrist readings look implausible, do not let that number dictate the workout; use easy effort and lap pace while checking fit and settings.

**Suunto: read old criticism alongside later changes.** DC Rainmaker's May 2025 review found HR trouble in demanding scenarios and weak charging magnets. Suunto later changed fonts/UI and HR. Version 3.27.18 began rollout on August 21, 2026 with improved optical HR; the release list inspected then showed 3.28.26 dated October 26, 2026. Those are dated software observations, not a claim about today's latest release. Update before judging the watch. Manufacturer improvement notes are not independent retesting; no independent post-August-2026 HR retest was obtained.

## Keep some money for the actual running

I would skip premium readiness scores, running-form dashboards, solar charging, titanium and ultra-distance battery for this goal. Prioritize a plan you can follow, readable lap pace and interval prompts. The Garmin already gives recovery time without Training Readiness.

Multiband can help around buildings and difficult reception, but I would not reject Garmin for ordinary road training just because it lacks that label. COROS already offers it at $199. That is a use-case judgment, not an Austin route test. Full maps are also less important on familiar routes and a supported road half.

If you carry your Android anyway, offline music may not be worth paying extra for. Likewise, do not buy a chest sensor automatically on day one. Check comfort and fit first. Contact, movement, skin and tattoos can affect wrist HR; a compatible chest sensor is a targeted fix if readings stay erratic and accurate zone work matters to you.

**A simple setup:** display elapsed time, distance and lap pace. Add easy run/walk or interval prompts from your plan. Before buying, check the final tax/shipping total, return terms, exact model/color, phone compatibility and delivery. Before racing, practise with the watch on your own long runs rather than discovering its settings at the start line.
