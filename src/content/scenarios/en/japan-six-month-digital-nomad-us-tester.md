You can make room for Japan without giving up your US job—but “fully remote” is only the beginning. For this 35-year-old US citizen, earning $118,000 as a software QA employee, Japan's digital-nomad route looks plausible. Start with written employer approval, insurance that meets the application checklist and a tax review before committing to housing.

Set aside ¥2,252,800, about $14,217.28, for the trip in this example, plus separate refundable-deposit cash. That is a planning allowance for one person, not a quote or an after-tax affordability verdict. Immigration permission, employer permission and Japanese tax exemption are three separate decisions.

The plan assumes a single employee with no dependents, keeping a US job and US tax residence, arriving in April 2027 and splitting the stay between Tokyo and Fukuoka. Prices and rules below come from material collected on November 6, 2026; nothing has been refreshed for your application or travel dates.

## Does the digital-nomad route fit you?

The retained [ISA requirements](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/status/designatedactivities53_00001.html), [nationality chart](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/content/001416932.pdf) and [July 2024 Q&A](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/content/001422249.pdf) support this starting point:

| Requirement or limit | What it means for this US employee |
| --- | --- |
| Eligible nationality | The United States is on the principal-applicant list. Other passports need their own check; a tourism-waiver list is not a substitute. |
| Overseas remote work | Internet-based QA work for a foreign employer's foreign office appears to fit. Keep the overseas employment arrangement; do not add Japanese employment or contracts with Japanese organizations or individuals. Ordinary side-job permission is not available in principle. |
| Annual income | The application threshold is ¥10,000,000 annual income, not savings or six months' pay. The $118,000 salary is about ¥18,697,701 at the dated reference rate below; current income still needs documentation. |
| Private insurance | Cover the entire stay for death, injury and illness, including at least ¥10,000,000 in medical treatment for injury and illness. The national Q&A allows remains transport or a death benefit, with no specified minimum death-benefit amount. Your mission may ask for stricter documentation. |
| Length of stay | Maximum six months, with no extension or renewal. After leaving, wait six months before another entry under this same status. Read the actual landing permission for the expiry date. |
| Residence services | No residence card, ordinary resident registration or jūminhyō, and no enrollment in Japanese public medical insurance on this status. |

The [resident-registration categories](https://www.soumu.go.jp/main_sosiki/jichi_gyousei/c-gyousei/zairyu/english/resident_record.html) are another reason not to plan around ordinary long-term-resident services. Renting is not categorically forbidden, but a landlord's documentation and screening can make an ordinary long lease impractical. Look for an expressly passport-accepting monthly contract.

Moving from Tokyo to Fukuoka needs no special immigration procedure. If you temporarily leave Japan, arrange the applicable re-entry permission before departure; returning before expiry does not restart the six months. A supported spouse or child would need a separate application, qualifying nationality, family proof and insurance, with no automatic work permission. This solo budget does not cover them.

## Apply through the mission serving your US address

Your state and county are not supplied, so a specific consulate cannot be chosen. Start with the [embassy consulate guide](https://www.us.emb-japan.go.jp/jicc/consulate-guide.html) and [jurisdiction map](https://www.ny.us.emb-japan.go.jp/itpr_en/jurisdiction.html). Only official search extracts were available for those pages in the collected material; direct access was denied. Confirm your jurisdiction before relying on a checklist.

The [MOFA digital-nomad guidance](https://www.mofa.go.jp/ca/fna/pagewe_000001_00046.html) and national Q&A support applying from abroad through the appropriate embassy or consulate. A Certificate of Eligibility, if already held, can replace some supporting documents; it is not a compulsory preliminary trip to Japan. The Q&A describes direct overseas application where there is no agent in Japan for a COE application.

Prepare:

- Valid passport, signed application form and photo.
- A dated activity plan with cities, accommodation intentions and qualifying remote work.
- Annual-income evidence such as tax or income certificates and your employment contract; add an employment/salary letter, recent payslips and W-2 as required by your mission.
- Insurance certificate and policy summary showing every Japan date, medical limits and death/remains benefits.
- Proof of US jurisdiction, financial documents and return-mail materials where the local checklist requires them.

The [San Francisco February 2026 checklist](https://www.sf.us.emb-japan.go.jp/files/101001925.pdf) is a useful example, not a nationwide procedure. It specifies mail/drop-off rather than an in-person application, prepaid USPS passport return and a minimum five business days after a sufficient packet, without expedited service. It says US citizens pay a $0 visa issuance fee; postage, photos and copies are separate. Check your own mission's 2027 fee and submission rules.

San Francisco also describes single-entry visa validity of three months from issuance and application within three months before entry. For an April 5, 2027 arrival, that suggests an application window around January 5, 2027 under that example. Visa validity is the entry window, not extra time in Japan. Allow 4–8 weeks for questions, referrals, holidays and mail as a planning buffer—not an approval or processing promise.

### Why not just use visa-free entry?

The retained [Miami temporary-visitor search extract](https://www.miami.us.emb-japan.go.jp/itpr_en/visa_temporary_visitor.html) describes up to 90 days for permitted non-paid activities such as tourism and business meetings; its full page could not be fetched. That does not establish authorization for this six-month, full-time productive remote-work plan. Use the dedicated route rather than treating a tourist eVisa, border run or visitor extension as a workaround. A genuinely non-working trip of 90 days or less is a different plan.

## Keep the visa and tax decisions separate

### Your US filing obligations do not disappear

The [IRS worldwide-income guidance](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/us-citizens-and-resident-aliens-abroad) keeps US filing and tax obligations in view. Do not budget for the foreign earned income exclusion on this trip alone. It requires a foreign tax home plus either [330 full days abroad in a 12-consecutive-month period](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-earned-income-exclusion-physical-presence-test) or qualifying [bona fide foreign residence spanning an entire tax year](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-earned-income-exclusion-bona-fide-residence-test). Six months in Japan followed by a return to the US, with no other qualifying overseas period, satisfies neither route. A longer overseas history could change the facts; none is supplied.

### Japanese treaty exemption is plausible, not automatic

The [NTA nonresident guidance](https://www.nta.go.jp/english/taxes/individual/12006.htm) treats work physically performed in Japan as a potential Japan-source wage payment even when the employer and bank account are overseas. A genuinely temporary stay ordinarily leaves you nonresident absent a Japanese domicile, but 183 days is not Japan's general tax-residence definition. No residence card does not mean no tax liability.

For a qualifying US treaty resident, [Article 14 of the US–Japan convention](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/131/Treaty-Japan-11-6-2003.pdf) exempts the employment income only if all of these hold:

1. Presence in Japan is no more than 183 days in aggregate in any 12-month period commencing or ending in the taxable year concerned.
2. Pay is by or on behalf of an employer not resident in Japan.
3. Pay is not borne by that employer's Japanese permanent establishment.

US citizenship alone does not establish treaty residence. Payroll needs to confirm the legal employer, any Japanese permanent establishment or salary-cost recharge and all your Japan travel. The [IRS treaty index](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/japan-tax-treaty-documents) distinguishes the older convention; the [2013 protocol](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/131/Treaty-Japan-Pr2-1-24-2013.pdf) does not replace this employment article.

The six-month trap is easy to miss: April 1–September 30, 2027 is 183 calendar days inclusive; adding October 1 makes 184. The sample April 5–September 25, 2027 stay is 174 days using conservative inclusive counting. Other Japan visits can consume the same rolling allowance. These are calendar calculations, not an authoritative tax day-count ruling; confirm the convention and leave a buffer below both tax and immigration limits.

If treaty relief fails, NTA describes a 20.42% nonresident baseline for the relevant employment income and a quasi-final-return rule for offshore-paid wages without withholding. That is a risk to resolve, not an assessed tax amount in this budget. NTA also describes treaty applications through the payer and a late refund route. Have a Japan tax professional establish the correct claim/filing procedure for a US employer without a Japan withholding presence; do not assume no paperwork. If Japanese tax is properly due, review US foreign-tax-credit relief rather than assuming FEIE applies.

### Get a written employer answer

Ask the employer to authorize dates, Tokyo/Fukuoka locations and US-hours meetings, then review payroll, Japanese employment-law and permanent-establishment exposure. QA work does not itself settle those issues. Check client/test-data access, privacy/export restrictions, cybersecurity/VPN, workers' compensation, corporate insurance and emergency support.

[US overseas-employment withholding guidance](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/persons-employed-abroad-by-a-us-person) and [Social Security/Medicare guidance](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/social-security-tax-consequences-of-working-abroad) mean you should not simply stop federal withholding or FICA. The [Japan Pension Service](https://www.nenkin.go.jp/international/english/agreement/mechanisms/workjapan.html) describes temporary-detachment conditions and coverage coordination; a self-chosen six-month workation is not automatically a qualifying assignment. Ask payroll whether a certificate of coverage is needed. US state domicile/withholding remains unresolved because your state is unknown.

One practical equipment check matters for the longer stay: the ISA Q&A describes only a 90-day visitor exception for certain overseas Wi-Fi/Bluetooth devices without Japan's conformity mark. Check your phone and laptop; compliant rented equipment or wired connectivity may be needed beyond that exception.

## FAQ

### Is my salary enough to qualify?

It appears above the dated annual-income threshold, but salary alone does not settle the application. You still need acceptable income proof, qualifying work, insurance and mission-specific documents. The yen equivalent is exchange-rate dependent.

### Can I rent without a residence card?

Potentially. Oakhouse's passport-or-card policy is the clearest route among these examples, subject to screening and a valid status. JapanHeya platform marketing does not establish each host's acceptance, and the Fukuoka sharehouse examples remain unconfirmed.

### Does staying less than six months make the salary tax-free in Japan?

No. Treaty residence, the rolling presence test, a non-Japanese employer and salary not borne by a Japanese permanent establishment all matter. Immigration permission and treaty relief are different questions, with filing procedures still to establish.

### Can I choose insurance just because its medical limit is high enough?

No. Check the issued policy's exclusions, death/remains benefits and all stay dates against your mission's checklist. San Francisco's example also asks for independently held insurance and accidental death and dismemberment cover. Neither candidate has personal acceptance confirmed here.
