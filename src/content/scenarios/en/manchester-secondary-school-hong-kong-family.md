If your son is unhappy at school, a famous name is not the whole answer. You need a manageable journey, enough challenge in maths, help with academic English and room to keep Cantonese and written Chinese. **Plan for Year 9 in September 2027, with non-selective schools alongside uncertain grammar routes.** Consider independent schools only as a separate full-fee affordability decision.

This guide follows a Hong Kong BN(O) family near the Stockport/Trafford border, with a boy currently in Year 8, aged 13, strong at maths and developing academic English. Normal progression puts **Year 10 in September 2028 and GCSEs in summer 2029**. Confirm his exact birthday and current placement: age alone does not determine the route. Year 10 in September 2027 would be an out-of-normal-cohort request, not a routine fresh intake.

My recommendation is to move before GCSE courses if a genuinely better place becomes available. Some schools start preparation or options earlier, so compare subject sequences rather than year labels alone. If bullying or safety is the problem, seek the current school's safeguarding help now rather than waiting for a transfer.

**Keep the current place and attendance until a transfer is agreed.** A test pass, waiting-list position or appeal is not an offer. No individual vacancy or admission probability is established here. Rules, fees and statistics below were read on November 7, 2026; this is not a refreshed forecast for the move.

## Use the right council route, even across the border

A September start in a non-normal entry year is still an in-year application. Neither borough creates a general fresh Year 10 intake. [DfE overseas-admissions guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/schools-admissions-applications-from-overseas-children) and the [BN(O) visa overview](https://www.gov.uk/british-national-overseas-bno-visa) support school attendance; the public-funds restriction is not a state-schooling ban. It does not override selection, capacity or address priorities. Immigration conditions still need checking for the family.

### If the answer is no

The [School Admissions Code](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/1001050/School_admissions_code_2021.pdf) protects a formal application: you cannot be restricted to a waiting-list enquiry. Competing applications follow published oversubscription rules, not arrival order. The aim is 10 school days and a written outcome within 15. For grammars, within 15 school days the authority must instead notify an assessment date, or explain refusal with appeal rights if it chooses not to assess because another pupil would prejudice provision/resources. **That is not a promise of a test or place within 15 days.**

The [Appeals Code](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/1108077/School_Admission_Appeals_Code_2022.pdf) requires refusal reasons, appeal information, deadline and contact route; at least 20 school days to lodge and normally at least 10 school days' hearing notice. An in-year appeal is heard within 30 school days of lodging, not the normal-round 40-day timetable. Holidays are not school days; hearing notice can be waived by agreement.

Accepting a suitable backup does not prejudice an appeal; a waiting list and appeal can run together. [Trafford's appeals overview](https://www.trafford.gov.uk/schools-and-learning/starting-school-and-admissions/school-admissions-appeals/secondary-school-admission-appeals) and [appeal administration](https://www.trafford.gov.uk/schools-and-learning/starting-school-and-admissions/school-admissions-appeals/secondary-school-admission-appeals/appeals-process) distinguish council-administered AGSB, Sale and Stretford appeals from Urmston's own process. Follow the refusal letter. [Stockport's independent panel decision](https://www.stockport.gov.uk/admission-appeals) is binding.

[DfE advice for parents](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/admission-appeals-for-school-places/advice-for-parents-and-guardians-on-school-admission-appeals) distinguishes an academically qualified-but-full grammar case from non-qualification, which needs evidence of the required academic standard; a local review can affect the panel's role. An appeal is not an English-test waiver or a reliable alternative entry route. Usually only one appeal per school per academic year is possible, with a significant-material-change exception. A disagreement about year group alone when a place is offered follows the complaints route, not a statutory admission appeal.
