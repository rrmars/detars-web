You are 29, you work at a hotel front desk in Fukuoka, your TOEIC score is 780, you have ¥1,600,000 in savings and you hope to arrive in Canada in May or June 2027. You want to know when to apply, which city to start in, and whether the money lasts. **Short answer: apply the day the 2027 Japan pool opens; the invitation must come before your 31st birthday. Treat May 2027 as tight and plan for mid-May to June 2027. Toronto is a low-confidence lean for a hotel front-desk job. Landing cash is about CAD 6,644 (Vancouver) or CAD 6,664 (Toronto), which is workable but not roomy.**

This guide is based on public government and company pages read on 7 November 2026 and 8 November 2026. Nothing was applied for and nobody was contacted. "Unconfirmed" marks anything that did not come from an official page that was read, or that is an assumption. **It is general information, not legal, tax or immigration advice.** The source materials are not published. The guide was re-read against the official government pages once after it was written, and the corrections are included; that is a check on the guide, not an official review.

## The recommendation and why

**Apply: submit your IEC profile the day the 2027 Japan pool opens.** As of 7 November 2026 the Canadian immigration department (IRCC) says the 2026 pools are closed and the 2027 pools will reopen, but it gives no date. Working Holiday selection from the pool is random with equal chances, and you may get no invitation at all. Being in the pool from the first round means you are in every draw and lowers the risk of ageing out.

- **Age:** Japan is 18 to 30 inclusive. IRCC says that if you are invited before your birthday you have passed the age check, and you can enter later while the port-of-entry letter is valid. At 29 you are eligible now, but **the invitation must come before your 31st birthday; if you turn 31 while still waiting in the pool, you drop out.** Your birthday is not known to this guide, so the earlier you submit, the safer. **Next step:** check your birthday against the date the pool opens and submit the same day.
- **City (low-confidence lean):** start in **Toronto** if the goal is a hotel front-desk job quickly. Choose Vancouver if a slightly higher pay floor and an explicit public-health route matter more. The basis is Job Bank (the Government of Canada job site): it rates the outlook for hotel front desk clerks "Good" in the Toronto region but "Limited" in Lower Mainland–Southwest (Vancouver area), and rates servers "Limited" in both. Pay differences are small (CAD 18.25 against CAD 17.95 minimum wage). No official source measures time-to-hire, so this is a judgement, not a finding.
- **When:** "May 2027" is tight. In 2026 Japan's first round was the week of 2 March 2026, but that comes from a search-result snippet and is **unconfirmed**. An invitation then gives you 10 days to accept and 20 days to apply, and IRCC's 56-day service standard puts a permit decision around early May at best. **Plan for mid-May to June 2027 and do not buy flights until the port-of-entry letter arrives.**
- **Money:** ¥1,600,000 is about CAD 13,841 at the planning rate used here. After upfront costs you land with about CAD 6,644 and about 3.6 months of living costs if you earned nothing. If you find work after 3 or 6 weeks, cash stays above CAD 2,579 (see the runway table). It is workable but not roomy.

**To-do list**

- Confirm your passport covers May 2027 plus 12 months, and ideally more.
- Create your IRCC secure account and complete the questionnaire as soon as the 2027 pool opens.
- Keep your documents (CV, photo, family information form IMM 5707) ready for the 20-day window.
- After the letter arrives: buy 12-month insurance, book the flight and get a bank statement dated within a week of departure.

## Health coverage

Private insurance is mandatory at entry in both cities and must run for the whole permit.

- **British Columbia (MSP):** Working Holiday permits valid for 6+ months "may be deemed residents". Coverage starts after the rest of the arrival month plus 2 months, and the enrolment page says to get private insurance while you wait; it also says coverage "may start three months after arrival". The Working Holiday sub-page adds conditions: six consecutive months of employment, at least 18 hours a week, plus an employer letter and departure date (that page was last updated in October 2022). A 20 hour a week job qualifies on hours; a patchwork of shifts below 18 hours may not. MSP premiums were eliminated on 1 January 2020.
- **Ontario (OHIP):** there is "no longer a waiting period", but eligibility for work-permit holders needs full-time work for an Ontario employer for at least 6 months, plus presence rules of 153 of 183 days. The page does not name IEC, so whether ServiceOntario accepts an open IEC permit is **unconfirmed**. **Next step:** ask ServiceOntario in writing before relying on OHIP.

## SIN, bank account and tax basics

- **SIN (Social Insurance Number):** free; apply online (about 5 business days), by mail (about 20) or at a Service Canada office. It needs your work permit and passport, starts with 9 and expires with the permit. IRCC says to apply as soon as possible after arrival, and the Service Canada page says you may start work as soon as you apply.
- **Bank account:** the newcomer pages of RBC, TD, Scotiabank, BMO and CIBC were blocked or showed no fee or ID details, so no fees or ID rules are quoted here. Ask the bank in person. The tax agency (CRA) lists a Canadian bank account as a secondary residential tie, and CRA direct deposit only works for Canadian accounts (otherwise a cheque or wire), so keeping the account open until the refund arrives is practical.
- **Residency for tax:** CRA weighs residential ties and the length and purpose of stay. A 12-month stay with a home and a job most likely makes you a part-year resident in the arrival and departure years (our reading, not CRA's ruling). CRA credits are prorated for part-year residents.
- **Leaving:** file a Canadian return for the year you leave if you want a refund, using the province you lived in on departure.
- **Deadline:** the 2025 return was due on 30 April 2026; the 2027 return should follow the same pattern (inference). The earliest e-filing date and NETFILE rules were not verified.
