You make good cloth. The worry is that the first parcel to Tokyo or Melbourne costs more to send than it earns. **Start with a small, honest Etsy shop and put off a paid Shopify store.** But read this next part before anything else: **no all-in shipping lane for a 200 g packed parcel at the modelled shipping charge of IDR 400,000 has been found, and at the published DHL base rates even the first two lanes, Japan and Australia, lose money unless the charge you collect changes.**

This plan is for **Dewi, who runs a batik workshop in Yogyakarta with two artisans**, has **IDR 20 million** to start, and wants to sell silk and cotton scarves abroad from **January to December 2027**. Her local business sells at about **IDR 450,000–1,800,000** per piece, and the plan is meant to protect that business, not to be paid for by it. Every fee and rule below was read on **7 November 2026**. Treat them as dated observations, not January 2027 offers. No accounts were opened, no one was contacted and no parcel was sent.

The source materials are not published.

## Recommendation: Etsy first, Shopify later

**Launch a small maker-led Etsy collection, aim first at Australia and Japan, and add the United States only once a carrier can declare and collect current duties.** Stage the EU later, after product-safety representation and destination rules are sorted out. Keep a simple brand presence and a customer list. Defer a paid Shopify store until repeat or direct demand and an approved international payment gateway justify it.

- **Why Etsy.** It has the clearest confirmed Indonesian seller eligibility and a payout route to an Indonesian bank account in IDR.
- **Why not Shopify yet.** Shopify is a shop builder, not a marketplace, so it brings no buyers by itself. An Indonesian storefront is possible, but Shopify Payments is not available in Indonesia. A separate payment gateway must approve Dewi, and whether it will accept foreign cards for her is **not confirmed**.
- **This is a recommendation, not a demand forecast.** Nothing here predicts how many people will buy.
- **Two old rules are now dangerous shortcuts.** US purchases under US$800 are **not** automatically duty-free. And Indonesia’s 0.5% regime for individual small businesses no longer has a blanket seven-year end date: a regulation effective 22 April 2026 changed that. Recheck both before a 2027 launch.
- **What success means.** About 40 international orders a month by December 2027, with positive contribution after labour, shipping, platform costs and sensible reserves, not simply 40 discounted orders.
- **First gates before any listing goes live.** Cost and time three cotton and three silk scarves. Weigh each packed parcel. Settle maker eligibility, bank and payout identity, business and tax status, and courier acceptance. Publish only stock, or a lead time, that the workshop can actually meet.

## Shipping: a published DHL rate is higher than the shipping charge

**Read this section before you price anything.** The cheapest published direct-DHL base for a 0.5 kg parcel from Indonesia is already more than the IDR 400,000 shipping allowance used in the unit-economics test below. **No all-in 200 g lane at that price has been established. If you keep the shipping charge unchanged, Japan and Australia, the two lanes recommended first, lose money too.** Don’t switch on any destination until you have a verified tracked lane and a shipping charge that covers it.

A 200 g parcel means the whole packed shipment, not just the scarf. DHL compares actual weight with volume weight (length × width × height in cm ÷ 5,000) and bills the higher, rounded up to 0.5 kg steps. A 25 × 20 × 4 cm parcel has a volume weight of 0.4 kg and bills at 0.5 kg. A 30 × 25 × 5 cm parcel is 0.75 kg and bills at 1 kg. These sizes are examples, not Dewi’s packaging.

### An EU buyer’s bill, step by step

Customs duty and VAT are different layers. Take an illustrative non-IOSS order: goods €80 plus transport €20, one customs item, no preference, and an assumed 20% destination VAT rate used only for the sum. Duty €3 plus the €2 handling fee gives modelled import VAT of 20% × (€80 + €20 + €3 + €2) = €21. The buyer pays €80 + €20 + €3 + €2 + €21 = €126, plus any carrier collection or clearance fees. The €20 freight is hypothetical, not converted from DHL, and the real member-state rate, tax base and rounding must be confirmed.

**Say who pays at the door.** Separate DAP (the buyer generally pays import charges on arrival) from a properly set-up DDP or landed-cost service (the seller arranges payment). No DDP carrier service was booked or confirmed. “Free shipping”, marketplace VAT collection or writing “gift” on the parcel doesn’t erase customs costs, and values and origin must be truthful.

## Indonesia: small parcels simplify customs, not the whole business

- **Business identity (NIB).** Use the OSS individual micro and small business pathway and check the workshop’s current business classification code (KBLI), risk level and any operating or environmental obligations. The exact code is **unconfirmed**; don’t copy old codes during the KBLI 2025 migration.
- **Tax identity and records.** Confirm Dewi’s NIK and NPWP status and her real eligibility. Reconcile orders, refunds, platform fees, currency conversions, shipping receipts and bank deposits. Keep invoices, materials and labour records and export evidence. File required returns even if the relief leaves zero final tax. Deadlines and her prior elections need individual checking.
- **Exporter customs access.** A relevant NIB with customs access supports ordinary customs registration, which is free and linked to OSS and the tax office. That is not a rule that every individual postal parcel needs its own registration. Confirm the route before the first shipment.
- **Declaring a retail parcel.** Under the 2025 customs rule, an individual sender’s parcel up to and including 30 kg goes by consignment note (CN); a business at that weight uses CN or an export declaration (PEB). Dewi stays the exporter. Prepare a truthful invoice with items, quantity, fibre content, origin, value and currency, and keep the accepted declaration and proof of dispatch. A carrier-specific template is **unverified**. Never mark a sale as a gift or understate value.
- **Classification and restrictions.** Get the correct finished-scarf tariff classification for silk versus cotton and the construction, and check current restricted-goods lists on INSW. No tariff subheading, export-duty exemption or “no permits needed” ruling has been established. Samples aren’t automatically exempt from restrictions.
- **Returns and VAT.** Keep export proof in case a returned parcel is re-imported; relief is not automatic. Check VAT registration (PKP) separately: 0.5% income tax is not VAT, and overseas sales aren’t automatically outside Indonesian tax. Don’t charge or describe Indonesian VAT as if Dewi were confirmed as PKP.

**When does the 0.5% regime end?** For an eligible individual, not automatically after seven years. A regulation effective 22 April 2026 deleted the old duration article, and a tax-office release of 24 June 2026 confirms that duration now depends on eligibility. The rate applies to qualifying turnover, not profit, and the broad annual eligibility ceiling is Rp4.8 billion. An individual’s first Rp500 million of qualifying turnover stays relieved; that relief isn’t the same as for a one-person company. It ends if Dewi elects ordinary taxation or stops qualifying, and the new rules prevent re-entry after specified elections or threshold exclusions. Crossing the threshold in an otherwise eligible year generally moves her to ordinary taxation the following year. Eligibility counts other businesses and services, one-person companies and applicable family rules, not only this Etsy shop. Her registration history, other income and elections are **unknown**.

An illustration only: eligible individual turnover of IDR 840,000,000 gives 0.5% × (840,000,000 − 500,000,000) = IDR 1,700,000 a year. Overseas buyers don’t by themselves make receipts tax-exempt. The model below treats charged shipping as part of receipts; confirm the real accounting treatment.

## Photos and listings: show the cloth and the real item

This is an editorial workflow suggestion, not a promise about ranking or conversion. Spend on cloth and fulfilment before an expensive camera.

- Start with a phone, indirect window light, a neutral background, a tripod and consistent colour.
- Take one uncluttered hero shot of the whole scarf, plus flat front and reverse views of the full pattern.
- Add close-ups of canting lines, the wax-resist character, weave and hem.
- Add a worn or draped view for scale, with the model’s permission, and a ruler view with dimensions in centimetres and inches.
- Show the real colour and texture in neutral light, plus the packaging.
- Include a real making-process image or short clip, and say whose hands and work it shows.
- Keep the same light and white balance, avoid filters that change silk sheen or colour, and keep the cloth visible in a square thumbnail crop. Show genuine variation; don’t use “handmade” to excuse undisclosed damage.

**Listing details that prevent returns.** Example title: “Hand-drawn batik silk scarf — [verified motif/colour], [measured size] — made in Yogyakarta.” Replace the brackets and don’t invent dimensions, dye provenance or certifications. State fibre percentage, size and tolerance, finished weight, hand-drawn tulis versus stamped or printed, who did what in the workshop, care instructions based on tests, colour variation, ready-to-ship versus made-to-order lead time, return conditions, tracking and buyer import charges. Show the actual unique scarf, or explain that images are representative. Don’t claim “natural dye”, “fair trade”, “sustainable”, “heritage-certified”, sales counts or reviews without evidence.

**EU sales need more than VAT.** Before targeting EU buyers, resolve the EU-established responsible economic operator under the General Product Safety Regulation, product traceability and risk documentation, and online contact details and warnings. Fibre labels must be firmly attached and in the required destination languages. US textile guidance also expects fibre, origin and business identification. Care, chemical-safety, consumer-return and national packaging rules need a destination-specific review before launch. This is a launch gate, not a claim of certification.

**Test your capacity.** If two artisans each have 120 net export-production hours a month, 240 ÷ 40 = 6 artisan-hours per scarf. That is an assumed test, not measured output. If real tulis work takes longer, sell a smaller ready-stock collection, lengthen honest lead times or revise the target. Don’t quietly outsource or pass off printed cloth as hand-drawn.

## Unit economics: price the scarf and the delivery separately

Everything in this table is a planning assumption in IDR, not Dewi’s actual cost or a courier quote. One scarf per order; product prices of IDR 900,000 (cotton) and IDR 1,800,000 (silk). **The shipping charge of IDR 400,000 is a placeholder** for testing the model. Replace it with the real all-in amount for a packed lane before offering shipping, and remember the section above: published DHL bases are higher, and US or EU duties and clearance can make this allowance inadequate.

List and get paid in IDR. For this arithmetic only, US$1 = IDR 16,000, so a US$0.20 listing is IDR 3,200. Assume one paid listing per sold item, no stale-listing renewals, no buyer tax in receipts and no Offsite Ads in the base case.

**These are contributions, not profit, and they rest on the IDR 400,000 shipping assumption.** After provisions that is IDR 308,300 per cotton scarf and IDR 557,800 per silk scarf, and it turns negative as soon as the carrier’s published base replaces the placeholder. General overhead, Dewi’s management time, customer-acquisition spend, subscriptions, one-time setup and compliance costs are still to come. The ad examples assume the ad base is this order’s P + S and no separate buyer tax; tax on ad fees may use up the allowance. Refunds, fee credits, reserves and currency can differ.

**Don’t pass shipping through at cost.** The base percentage fees are 6.5% + 4.5% = 11%. To recover IDR 400,000 of delivery without subsidy from the product price, you would charge 400,000 ÷ (1 − 0.11) = IDR 449,438.20, before ads, buyer-tax effects and other costs. The table charges only 400,000, so the product margin absorbs IDR 44,000 of fees on shipping. If the real carrier cost rises by IDR 100,000 and the customer charge stays fixed, contribution falls by IDR 100,000. If both rise by IDR 100,000, contribution falls by IDR 11,000 from the base percentages (IDR 11,500 once the model’s 0.5% provision is included), before other reserves and ads.

**Buyer tax isn’t free to process.** If Etsy collects tax of IDR 200,000 equivalent from the buyer, processing may add 4.5% × 200,000 = IDR 9,000. Keep buyer tax out of your sales earnings but inside the payment fee base. Marketplace VAT collection doesn’t cover customs duty, courier clearance or all new EU fees.
