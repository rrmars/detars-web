If charging would become another errand between work and the school run, start with a hybrid. **Choose Toyota Corolla Cross HEV Premium as the lower-friction default, with Honda HR-V e:HEV EL the close alternative, until your condo charging arrangement is confirmed.** BYD ATTO 3 Extended has the cheapest base financed ownership estimate, **THB 972,693**, but that does not make it the easiest car for your family.

This comparison is for a Bang Na family of four buying in early 2027, keeping the car until early 2032, driving 18,000 km/year including monthly Hua Hin trips: 90,000 km over five years. One assigned condo bay exists, but an approved charger does not. Treat the THB 1.0–1.3m budget as a ceiling, not a target. Chinese-language readers get the same Thai market and finance assumptions, not different eligibility.

Prices and rules are the November 7, 2026 snapshot, not refreshed quotations or guaranteed delivery offers. All amounts are nominal THB, undiscounted. **An EV becomes a stronger choice when permission, safe installation, fair billing and the delivered price are written down—and you accept uncertain resale.** Public-only charging remains an option if your family willingly accepts the routine.

## Compare ownership costs separately from cash flow

**Insurance, service, tyres, installation and resale below are planning allowances, not quotes.** Small differences are not precise forecasts. The EV base assumes 80% approved residential-meter AC and 20% public DC, plus THB 45,000 installation: a conditional possibility, not your condo’s present position.

TCO = purchase + loan interest + voluntary insurance + energy + maintenance + replacement tyres + annual tax + compulsory insurance + installation − resale. Purchase appears once: down payment plus principal repayments equals the price. Do not add the entire loan repayment again.

Also excluded: inflation, investment return, loan fees, registration/admin charges, accidents, major repairs, charging-site idle/access/parking fees and the value of your time. **Excluded does not mean free:** add actual charges when known.

## Work out which electricity bill you would actually pay

Bang Na is in MEA’s service area; PEA is relevant elsewhere, including Hua Hin. Your building’s supply and master-meter category determine the tariff—not the car badge or the fact that you have an assigned bay. Exact MEA rates, current Thai VAT implementation and your condo’s billing have not been confirmed.

The [PEA residential schedule](https://www.pea.co.th/sites/default/files/users/user34/attachments/Electricity_Tariff_SEP_2026_3.pdf) is effective September 2026; [Ft](https://www.pea.co.th/our-services/tariff/ft) is +0.1623 THB/kWh for September 2026–December 2026. Residential calculations conditionally assume 7% VAT, not a checked current Thai legal instrument. January 2027–April 2027 Ft is unknown. The [VOLTA tariff](https://www.pea.co.th/knowledge/ev-charging) applies from May 1, 2026 and already includes VAT; do not add Ft or VAT again. The fuel row comes from [PTT/OR](https://www.pttor.com/th/oil_price).

Residential figures are marginal energy costs, not an average whole-house bill. Existing service charges are not incremental with the existing meter; a separate service or meter adds charges. Demand fees, load-management fees and condo markups are outside that calculation. The residential schedule shows THB 24.62/month service. Weekday TOU peak is 09:00–22:00, off-peak 22:00–09:00; use the utility calendar for weekend/holiday and substitute-holiday rules. Elective TOU has switching costs and reprices household daytime use too.

Public rates depend on the charger’s rated-power bracket, not the power your car achieves. THB 6.90 is a PEA peak proxy, not every operator’s rate. No current PluZ tariff was confirmed; the EGAT indication was not established well enough to use. PTT/OR’s Bangkok row is timestamped November 6, 2026 at 05:00, with any applicable local maintenance tax excluded. Hua Hin fuel prices can differ; gasohol95 is not gasoline95.

**Counterintuitive finding: cheaper electricity does not necessarily repay a new charger within your ownership period.** At these assumptions, replacing 80% of public charging with residential charging saves THB 5,347/year. It does not price queues, detours or evenings spent charging. If compliant installation is genuinely included, remove only the avoided installation cost, not every building infrastructure charge.

### Give public-only charging a realistic place in your week

Annual demand is 18,000 × 18/100 = 3,240 billed kWh; at 6.90 that costs THB 22,356/year. Assuming 40 kWh bought per visit means 81 visits/year, about 1.56/week. A planning allowance of 45 minutes each means 60.75 hours/year before extra detours or queues. These are scheduling assumptions, not a measured charging curve or queue survey; both EVs share the same assumed consumption.

Choose a primary and backup CCS2 site near home, plus another pair along the Hua Hin route. Check connector, power, opening hours, live status, access/parking/idle fees, payment and app requirements. No individual station’s live equipment, free bay or reliability is confirmed. Try the routine against your actual family timetable before ordering.

For the monthly Hua Hin trip, allow an estimated 500 km round trip: 90 kWh at 18 kWh/100 km, or 110 kWh at a highway stress assumption of 22 kWh/100 km. A roughly 60-kWh battery is not a reason to assume an uncharged return. Confirm destination charging and retain a usable reserve. These trips are already within annual mileage, not added again. Public-only charging is not inherently prohibited or proof of excessive degradation; read the actual warranty restrictions.

## Get the condo answer before paying the deposit

An assigned bay is a useful start, not an automatic right to alter common property. **If permission or workable all-in installation is still unresolved when ordering, choose the hybrid unless you explicitly accept public-only charging.** Do not buy on “management will probably allow it”.

| Check | What to settle before commitment |
| --- | --- |
| Parking right | Read allocation, title/lease and condo rules. Establish whether the bay, walls, risers and cable route are common property and who can authorize works. |
| Written permission | Agree charger location, route, contractor, hours, fire-stopping, liability and reinstatement. Management must determine whether committee consent or an owners’ resolution is needed; no universal voting threshold is assumed. |
| Electrical survey | Qualified designer/installer checks transformer capacity, maximum demand, earthing, voltage drop, protection, load management and future neighbours’ loads. A nominal 7-kW box must respect circuit and vehicle limits. |
| Meter and tariff | Determine unit meter, separate MEA service or condo master meter; record category and every billing charge. Cheap residential TOU does not automatically apply to a common-area commercial meter. |
| Safe commissioning | Dedicated circuit, isolation, overcurrent and residual-current/DC leakage protection, compliant equipment, earthing and test records. Installer confirms applicable MEA/EIT/TISI requirements. No extension lead or improvised common-area connection. |
| Insurance and use | Agree building-insurer requirements, emergency isolation, access control, meter accuracy, upkeep, outage response, idle/parking policy and moving/selling responsibilities. |
| Complete installation scope | Include charger, labour, cable/trunking, submeter, protection, survey/testing, meter/utility changes, tax, civil/fire-stopping work and load management. |

The installation allowance is THB 45,000. THB 30,000–80,000 is only an unconfirmed planning range, not a condo quotation; long cable routes, switchboard or transformer upgrades can exceed it. This checklist is prudent project planning, not an electrical design or legal opinion. Actual building consent, inspection requirements and capacity remain open.

## Keep policy risk out of your arithmetic until it is clear

The [Excise EV3.5 release](https://webdev.excise.go.th/act2560/files/secretary/press3-2567.pdf) and [claim form](https://webdev.excise.go.th/act2560/files/thaweesak/แบบฟอร์มภาษีสรรพสามิต_2560/01/ยฟ.01-03-ระยะที่-2.pdf) describe qualifying domestic passenger BEVs through 2027: price no more than THB 2m, support THB 25,000 for 10–<50 kWh or THB 50,000 for ≥50 kWh in 2026–2027, with 2% promotional excise for qualifying cars. Both EV batteries exceed 50 kWh, but capacity alone does not prove eligibility.
