Worried that leaving payroll means a tax bill you will not see coming? Start with a separate reserve and a payment calendar. **On $95,000 of 2027 receipts, this example estimates $18,474.09 of federal tax. It assumes an illustrative $6,000 of eligible Schedule C business costs plus $2,000 of eligible personal health premiums—not a confirmed breakdown of your $8,000.** Reserve **25%** of each paid invoice. Budget **$4,650** per payment date, then check that target against your completed 2026 return. It is not a verified prior-year safe harbor.

This is for a single US resident taxpayer in Austin, Texas, planning to leave an $88,000 salaried job and start full-time UX freelancing on January 1, 2027. As of November 6, 2026, that start was still ahead; no 2027 estimated-tax payment was due then. The scenario assumes you are under 65, have no dependants or employees, use cash-basis sole-proprietor accounting, and have no 2027 wages, other income, capital gains, credits, itemized deductions or baseline retirement contributions. Retirement sizing separately assumes under 50 and no other employee deferrals.

All rules and publication-status statements below are the November 6, 2026 snapshot, not a new check of current tax law. [Texas has no personal income tax](https://comptroller.texas.gov/economy/fiscal-notes/archive/2016/february/starting.php). That does not make every business activity exempt from other state taxes.

## Split the expenses before trusting the estimate

Health insurance usually belongs in a different part of the return from business costs. The distinction affects self-employment tax, so a single combined expense total is not enough.

| Treatment of the $8,000 total | Modeled federal tax | What must be true |
|---|---:|---|
| $6,000 Schedule C + $2,000 health | $18,474.09 | Illustrative main split; every deduction eligible as allocated |
| $8,000 Schedule C + $0 health | $18,205.06 | All costs genuinely deductible on Schedule C |
| $0 Schedule C + $8,000 health | $19,281.17 | Range endpoint only; all premiums eligible |

The health share can move from $0 to $8,000 while the remaining eligible costs stay on Schedule C. That gives roughly $18,205–$19,281, not a guaranteed range for your return. An ineligible office corner, personal laptop use or disallowed premiums can raise the bill further.

Under the [Form 7206 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i7206), eligible proprietor health premiums generally go on Schedule 1. They reduce income tax and adjusted qualified business income (QBI), **not self-employment earnings or SE tax**. Business earned-income limits apply. Months when you are eligible for an employer-subsidized plan can be disallowed even if you decline it. Marketplace premium tax credits need a coordinated calculation. Never deduct the same premium twice. The general IRS SE page has conflicting health wording; this example follows the more specific Form 7206 treatment.

## Put these four dates on the calendar

The full-tax budget is $4,650 × 4 = $18,600, about $126 above the modeled liability. This is a cash-planning choice, not proof of penalty protection. Tax payment periods are not four equal three-month quarters.

| Income period | Estimated-payment deadline | Day |
|---|---|---|
| January–March 2027 | April 15, 2027 | Thursday |
| April–May 2027 | June 15, 2027 | Tuesday |
| June–August 2027 | September 15, 2027 | Wednesday |
| September–December 2027 | January 18, 2028 | Tuesday; still tax year 2027 |

These dates are derived from the [standing IRS due-date rules](https://www.irs.gov/faqs/estimated-tax/individuals/individuals-2) and [OPM's holiday calendar](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/pay-leave/federal-holidays/), not quoted from a 2027 Form 1040-ES. January 15, 2028 is Saturday. January 17, 2028 is Martin Luther King Jr. Day, so the fourth deadline moves to January 18, 2028.

For every $1,000 collected, transfer $250 to a separate tax reserve. At $95,000 receipts, that builds $23,750—about $5,276 above the example's tax. The 25% is a conservative buffer, not a statutory rate or a promise that it will always cover you.

Before each deadline:

- Reconcile collected receipts, business costs and eligible health premiums.
- Update the forecast. Check the scope warnings in the calculator.
- Compare your chosen payment target with the completed 2026 return.
- Make the payment for tax year 2027 and keep its confirmation.
- Revisit the plan if income rises, arrives late or becomes less predictable.

## Safe harbor and a full-tax budget do different jobs

With no withholding and an expected balance over $1,000, this scenario needs estimated payments. Generally, the required annual prepayment is the smaller of **90% of actual 2027 tax** or **100% of adjusted 2026 tax**. The prior-year percentage becomes **110%** if your 2026 AGI exceeds $150,000 as a single filer. The prior-year return must cover 12 months. Installments must be timely. See [Publication 505](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p505) and the [IRS safe-harbor FAQ](https://www.irs.gov/faqs/estimated-tax/individuals/individuals). Publication 505 is the 2026 edition here, rolled forward for standing mechanics, not presented as a 2027 edition.

| Payment approach | Planning amount | Important limit |
|---|---|---|
| 100% of adjusted prior-year tax | Unknown until your 2026 return is complete; annual benchmark ÷ 4 | Prior tax is not salary, withholding, refund or balance due |
| 110% of adjusted prior-year tax | Applies only if final 2026 AGI > $150,000 | Salary alone does not establish AGI |
| 90% of projected current tax | $16,626.68 annually; $4,156.67 per installment | Actual year-end tax controls; the forecast can fall short |
| Full projected tax | $18,474.09 ÷ 4 = $4,618.52; round up to $4,650 | Reforecast; not a known prior-year harbor |

My pick is the correctly calculated prior-year benchmark for penalty certainty once your 2026 return is final. Keep the full projected 2027 liability as a separate cash budget. If the prior benchmark exceeds the $4,650 payment plan, raise payments to meet it or establish a valid current-year or annualized alternative.

**Fully withheld does not mean zero tax.** Withholding paid the liability. Start with Form 1040 total tax, generally line 24, then apply the 1040-ES and Publication 505 adjustments. Do not enter your $88,000 salary, W-2 withholding, refund or $0 balance due as prior-year tax. Your 2026 withholding also does not become 2027 withholding.

A safe harbor protects against an underpayment penalty, not the remaining tax bill. Hold the difference for your 2027 return in 2028. A large payment at year-end generally does not erase an earlier missed installment.

## Where the $18,474.09 comes from

The [IRS announcement](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-releases-tax-inflation-adjustments-for-tax-year-2027-taxpayers-continue-to-benefit-from-the-working-families-tax-cuts) and [Revenue Procedure 2026-35](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-26-35.pdf), released November 5, 2026, supply the 2027 brackets, standard deduction and QBI thresholds. The [SSA table](https://www.ssa.gov/oact/cola/cbb.html) supplies the $192,300 wage base. The main estimate does **not** substitute 2026 limits. Only the later retirement-sizing example uses a clearly marked older-year proxy.

| Step | Calculation or treatment | Amount |
|---|---|---:|
| Client receipts | Expected cash collected in 2027 | $95,000.00 |
| Schedule C deductions | Illustrative eligible costs, excluding health | $6,000.00 |
| Net business profit | $95,000 − $6,000 | $89,000.00 |
| SE earnings | $89,000 × 92.35% | $82,191.50 |
| Social Security | $82,191.50 × 12.4%; below $192,300 cap | $10,191.75 |
| Medicare | $82,191.50 × 2.9% | $2,383.55 |
| Self-employment tax | Social Security + Medicare | $12,575.30 |
| Half-SE deduction | $12,575.2995 ÷ 2 | $6,287.65 |
| Eligible health deduction | Illustrative Schedule 1 deduction | $2,000.00 |
| AGI and adjusted QBI | $89,000 − $6,287.64975 − $2,000 | $80,712.35 |
| Standard deduction | Published 2027 single amount | $16,600.00 |
| Taxable income before QBI | $80,712.35025 − $16,600 | $64,112.35 |
| QBI candidate | 20% × $80,712.35025 | $16,142.47 |
| Taxable-income cap | 20% × $64,112.35025; no capital gains | $12,822.47 |
| Allowed QBI deduction | Smaller of the two amounts above | $12,822.47 |
| Taxable income after QBI | $64,112.35025 − $12,822.47005 | $51,289.88 |
| Income tax: first band | $12,800 × 10% | $1,280.00 |
| Income tax: next band | $38,489.8802 × 12% | $4,618.79 |
| Federal income tax | $1,280 + $4,618.785624 | $5,898.79 |
| Total federal tax | Income tax + SE tax | $18,474.09 |

The [Schedule SE form](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f1040sse.pdf) supports the 92.35% mechanics; its older-year wage limit is not used. The [IRS SE-tax guidance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/self-employment-tax-social-security-and-medicare-taxes) gives the ordinary rates and half-SE deduction. Additional Medicare uses a $200,000 single threshold and 0.9% rate; it is $0 here. Full precision is retained until display. Actual return tax tables and whole-dollar rounding may differ slightly.

A genuine domestic UX-design business can qualify for QBI. Pre-QBI taxable income of about $64,112 is below the 2027 single threshold of $208,300. Even if some work is consulting, a specified service business, that threshold permits the deduction in this example. Under the [Form 8995 mechanics](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8995), attributable half-SE, health and deductible retirement amounts reduce QBI. The taxable-income cap then binds. QBI is not simply 20% of $89,000 profit, and it reduces income tax, not SE tax. The $415 minimum deduction, with $1,030 minimum active QBI eligibility, is immaterial here; the calculator does not model that minimum-floor case.

Counterintuitive finding: the modeled taxable income after QBI is $51,289.88, within the **12%** band, not 22%. Rates apply only to dollars in each band, not all receipts. Ordinary SE tax still applies. The half-SE deduction lowers income tax; it does not reimburse half your SE tax.

## Pay through Direct Pay, and keep the receipt

Use the official [IRS Direct Pay page](https://www.irs.gov/payments/direct-pay-with-bank-account). It supports free bank payments without an account sign-in. Choose personal or individual payments, then **Estimated Tax → 1040-ES → 2027**. Keep 2027 selected even for the January 2028 installment.

Supply identity-verification information and bank details yourself. Schedule early enough to resolve problems and have funds available. Save the confirmation number, date, amount and tax year. One estimated payment covers income and SE tax; an electronic payment normally needs neither a separate SE transfer nor a mailed voucher.

Do not build this plan around a new individual EFTPS enrollment. The [IRS payment-transition notice](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/questions-and-answers-about-executive-order-14247-modernizing-payments-to-and-from-americas-bank-account), released January 27, 2026, says new individual enrollments ended October 17, 2025, with individuals required to transition later in 2026. A definitive completed shutdown date was not verified. Direct Pay is the practical route in this plan.

If clients pay in bursts, keep dated income and expense records. The equal prior-year method is simplest if cash allows. If income genuinely comes later, the annualized-income installment method can reduce early required payments. It uses cumulative January–March, January–May, January–August and full-year results, with Publication 505 and Form 2210 Schedule AI. It is not permission to pay an arbitrary share whenever convenient. Recalculate remaining installments if the forecast changes; late amounts may still attract a penalty. The attached calculator has no annualized-payment engine.

## Deduct business costs without counting them twice

### Laptop and software

Deduct only substantiated business use. Recurring work-software subscriptions are generally business expenses; personal use is not. A laptop or purchased software may need asset or depreciation treatment, or an available expensing election. A full immediate deduction is not automatic. [Publication 946](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946) covers section 179, generally requiring over 50% business use, and current-law 100% bonus depreciation for qualifying property acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025. Keep invoices, use records and placed-in-service dates. Confirm eligibility before claiming the deduction.

### The rented office corner

A separate room or permanent partition is not required. But the space must be identifiable, used regularly and exclusively for business, and meet a qualifying business-use test. A common route is the principal place for administration or management, with no other fixed location for substantial administrative work. A dining desk shared with personal life generally fails. See [Publication 587](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p587).

| Home-office method | Full-year eligible-use example | Care point |
|---|---|---|
| Simplified | 100 sq ft × $5 = $500; cap 300 sq ft or $1,500 | Business-income limit; no carryforward of unused simplified deduction |
| Actual | 100 / 1,000 sq ft = 10%; $24,000 rent × 10% = $2,400 rent component, plus eligible allocated costs | Records, reasonable allocation, income limits and carryforward rules; generally Form 8829 |

Choose one method for the year, not both. Both need eligibility. As a renter, you do not depreciate your landlord's building. The [simplified-option guidance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/simplified-option-for-home-office-deduction) explains the alternative methods. Do not add either example again if it is already in the $6,000 Schedule C total.

Against the main illustration, an **additional** eligible $500 office deduction saves about $115 combined tax; the alternative additional $2,400 actual-rent deduction saves about $553. They are not cumulative. Eligible $2,000 health premiums save about $278 versus the same scenario without the health deduction. A deduction is not a dollar-for-dollar refund. Do not buy something just for a write-off.

## Retirement: compare the plans, but verify the contribution limit

A SEP-IRA is generally the simpler employer-only option. At the usual maximum 25% employer rate, a sole proprietor uses the reduced rate 25% ÷ 125% = 20%. Here, 20% × ($89,000 − $6,287.64975) = **$16,542.47**, subject to plan and annual limits. Do not subtract health insurance again from this contribution base. The [IRS calculation guide](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/self-employed-individuals-calculating-your-own-retirement-plan-contribution-and-deduction) explains the adjustment.

A [Solo 401(k)](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/one-participant-401k-plans) can combine employee deferrals and employer contributions for an owner without employees other than a spouse. It often permits more saving at this income. Employee deferral limits aggregate across your plans in the same year.

**The retirement sizing uses 2026 proxies, not confirmed 2027 limits.** At the November 6, 2026 snapshot, no 2027 retirement-limit announcement had been verified. The [IRS COLA table](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/cola-increases-for-dollar-limitations-on-benefits-and-contributions) inspected ended at 2026: $24,500 employee deferral and $72,000 combined or SEP annual cap. An under-50, no-other-deferral Solo example is $24,500 + $16,542.47 = $41,042.47. Check official 2027 limits, eligibility and plan rules before contributing; this is not a legal 2027 maximum.

| Deductible traditional contribution | New income tax | Combined federal tax | Tax saved now |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $0 baseline | $5,898.79 | $18,474.09 | — |
| $10,000 eligible contribution | $4,938.79 | $17,514.09 | $960.00 |
| $16,542.47 SEP illustration | $4,310.71 | $16,886.01 | $1,588.08 |

SE tax stays $12,575.30 in these comparisons. Contributions also reduce QBI. A $10,000 deduction saves $960 here, not $2,200. Roth contributions generally do not give this current deduction. Retirement money is no longer available for everyday spending, and later traditional withdrawals are generally taxable.

A [SEP can generally be established and funded](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/retirement-plans-faqs-regarding-seps) by the federal return deadline, including extensions. Do not assume Solo employee elections share that deadline. Arrange the plan early and check establishment, election and funding rules. Age, catch-ups and other deferrals can change the answer.

## Questions you may still have

### Does Texas's lack of income tax mean I only owe federal income tax?

No. This estimate includes federal income tax **and** self-employment tax. Texas has no personal income tax, but other business taxes can still matter. This is not an assessment of every state business obligation.

### If my old job withheld everything, can I enter zero prior-year tax?

Not for that reason. Fully paid and zero liability are different. Use the adjusted prior-year tax calculation from the completed return and enter actual AGI too.

### Can I just pay 25% whenever a client pays me?

Use that transfer to build your reserve, not as a substitute for installment rules. Meet the dated payment target, or calculate a valid annualized-income alternative when income arrives later.

### Can I deduct my laptop, office and health premiums together?

Only with correct eligibility and allocation. Business costs and health premiums have different treatment, and office methods are alternatives. The example already assumes eligible business deductions in its Schedule C total; adding them twice overstates the saving.
