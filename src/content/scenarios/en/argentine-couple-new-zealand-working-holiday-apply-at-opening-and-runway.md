You want to go to New Zealand together from around September 2027 on a working holiday, you have USD 9,000, and you are worried about two things: missing the one short window when places open, and running out of money before the first pay arrives. **Short answer: apply the day the 2027 round opens, each of you with your own application, and treat "September 2027" as "apply at opening, arrive soon after". The USD 9,000 is tight: after visas, insurance and flights, about USD 740.86 may be missing if the NZ$4,200 funds rule applies to each of you (our assumption) and that money must stay untouched.**

This guide follows Martín (27) and Sofía (26), who are Argentine citizens. It was written on 8 November 2026 from public pages only. Nothing was applied for, booked or quoted, and no one was contacted. "Official page" marks a fact from an official page; "unconfirmed" marks a secondary source or our own planning assumption. **It is general information, not legal, tax or immigration advice.** The source materials are not published.

## The recommendation and why

**When to apply.** Argentina's scheme has a cap of 1,000 places a year, filled online first-come, first-served, and it closes when the places are full. In 2026 it opened on 24 September 2026 (Immigration New Zealand, published 15 January 2026). **The 2027 opening date had not been announced when we read the pages on 8 November 2026**, and the 2026 dates were only published in January. Next step: check the Immigration New Zealand news page from January 2027, and get ready before then. If the 2027 opening falls in late September, flying in October–November 2027 is more realistic than early September. You must enter New Zealand within 12 months of the visa being issued, so you do not need to fly the same week.

**Both partners apply separately and both need a place.** Each person lodges their own application and pays their own fees. We found no couple or partner provision on the official pages. If only one of you gets a place, the other has no working holiday visa, and what the second person could do instead was not researched. Next step: agree beforehand whether you would go on one visa (not recommended without checking your options) or wait for the next round.

**First base: Auckland for the first 3–5 weeks, then Central Otago (Cromwell area) from late November.** Auckland is the largest city, so we expect the most hospitality openings (our expectation; we found no vacancy data by city), and rooms ran about NZ$230–275 a week in the listings we saw. Queenstown is the most expensive (about NZ$410–475 a week for a room or a couple's room) and the Immigration New Zealand work table lists ski-slope staff in September but not October, so we read ski hiring as finished by then (our reading; ski-field dates not checked). Central Otago has cherry work from roughly early December to the end of January, which also starts the 3 months of horticulture work needed for the extension. **This is our judgement from the evidence below, not an official recommendation.**

**Money.** The NZ$4,200 funds rule, which we assume applies to each of you (the official page does not say; NZ$8,400 for two, about USD 4,656), plus our assumed costs leaves a gap of about USD 740.86 if that money must stay untouched. The flight price is the biggest unknown. The full working is in the budget section.

## Both of you need a place

**There is no way to apply as a couple**, and the New Zealand embassy page says places "are filled within a short period of time". Treat the two applications as independent events. Be ready before opening day: passports, funds evidence and your Immigration New Zealand online accounts set up, and both submit at once. The official pages do not say how many hours or days the quota lasts; we have no figure.

Next step if one application fails: do not buy flights on the strength of one approval. Decide beforehand whether to wait for the next round together.

## Pay, holiday pay and tax: a weekly example

The adult minimum wage from 1 April 2026 is NZ$23.95 an hour (NZ$958 for 40 hours; official page). The rate from 1 April 2027 had not been published when we read the page; assume it is higher.

| Example: 40 hours at NZ$23.95 | NZ$ a week |
|---|---|
| Gross pay | 958.00 |
| PAYE including the ACC earners' levy, tax code M (IRD IR340 table, tax year to 31 March 2027, ACC 1.75%; official page) | −163.41 |
| **Take-home** | **794.59** |
| Same, with the 2027–28 ACC levy of 1.83% (our calculation, rates otherwise unchanged): PAYE 164.18 | net 793.82 |
| If 8% holiday pay is paid on top each week: gross 958.00 + 76.64 | 1,034.64 |
| PAYE on that (our calculation, ACC 1.83%) | −179.72 |
| Net with holiday pay on top | 854.92 |

On 40 hours at the minimum wage, take-home is NZ$794.59 a week. Only the NZ$163.41 comes straight from IRD's published table. **The other amounts are our calculations, not IRD table lookups, and IRD's table would round slightly differently.** Our formula gives 163.415 before rounding, so it matches the IRD table to within a cent; IRD's table rounds down to 163.41.

Tax rates (IRD, from 1 April 2025): 10.5% to NZ$15,600; 17.5% to NZ$53,500; 30% to NZ$78,100. We found no newer table, so we assume these hold for 2027–28 (assumption). The 2027–28 ACC earners' levy of 1.83% is published (maximum earnings NZ$160,244).

**Holiday pay.** Pay-as-you-go 8% is allowed only for irregular work or a genuine fixed-term agreement under 12 months, if agreed in the contract; a "casual" label alone is not enough. The Employment Leave Act 2026 replaces the Holidays Act only on 6 August 2028, so the current rules apply for essentially your whole stay.

**KiwiSaver.** Employees on temporary, visitor, work or student visas cannot join and should not be auto-enrolled (employer page, 21 October 2026; applying it to a working holiday visa is our reading). Sick leave: 10 days paid a year for eligible employees; the qualifying period was not confirmed.

**IRD number.** The easy route is as a new arrival through a personal myIR account, using passport details and your Immigration New Zealand application number; IRD says the number arrives by text or email "in 2 days". The overseas route needs a New Zealand bank account or form IR997 and takes about 20 days. Apply by the visa's must-arrive-by date. Tax code M is our assumption: we found no working-holiday-specific rule.

**Bank account.** Only ANZ was read (unconfirmed, bank page): apply online up to 90 days before arriving with a visa that has 6+ months left; you can receive deposits but cannot withdraw until an in-branch identity check in New Zealand; no monthly fee on everyday accounts. ASB, BNZ, Westpac and Kiwibank were not confirmed.

**Getting tax back when leaving.** If all your income was wages already taxed, request an income-tax assessment by message in myIR and keep your bank details there current; file an IR3 if you had more than NZ$200 of untaxed income. Refund timing, and whether an overseas account is accepted, were not found.

## Health insurance

The visa requires full cover including hospital. A 12-month working holiday visa is not among the groups listed for publicly funded care (our reading of the Health New Zealand list), and ACC covers accidents only, not illness. The only published price we could use without giving personal details is SafetyWing Essential at US$62.72 per 4 weeks (ages 10–39), which is about USD 815.36 per person for 52 weeks (13 × 62.72), or USD 1,630.72 for two (52 weeks is 364 days; check the policy covers the whole visa period) (unconfirmed, insurer page). **We did not verify that Immigration New Zealand accepts it as "full medical insurance"**, and it excludes pre-existing conditions, maternity and cancer treatment. Another insurer's working-holiday page has no public price (its quote form needs personal data, which we did not use). Treat the figure as a placeholder. Next step: before you buy, ask Immigration New Zealand or the insurer whether the certificate meets the visa wording.

## The first weeks

- **Now to December 2026:** check passports (valid 3+ months beyond the visa's expiry); watch the Argentina page and news for 2027 opening dates; check whether any 2026 places remain open.
- **Before opening day:** funds evidence for NZ$4,200 each (plus ticket money on top), an insurance certificate plan, your Immigration New Zealand online accounts, and who does what.
- **After approval:** book flights; buy insurance with hospital cover; make an ANZ (or other bank) online application within 90 days of arrival; complete the New Zealand Traveller Declaration (tick the temporary-entry-visa option) within 24 hours before flying.
- **Week 1:** myIR account and IRD number (new-arrival route); a local SIM; a short stay; the bank branch identity check; start CVs (hospitality, plus healthcare-assistant roles for Sofía if eligible).
- **Weeks 1–4:** sign written employment agreements (minimum wage, holiday pay terms, tax code); give employers your IRD number and tax code; report underpayment through Employment New Zealand (0800 20 90 20).
- **November:** line up Central Otago seasonal work and keep payslips and employer letters for the extension.

| Insurance, 2 people (placeholder) | 2 × 815.36 | −1,630.72 |
| One-way flights Argentina to New Zealand (our assumption, unsourced) | 2 × USD 1,300 | −2,600.00 |
| **Cash left at arrival** | 9,000 − 5,084.40 | **3,915.60** |

You arrive with USD 3,915.60. In New Zealand dollars: 3,915.60 ÷ 0.55434 = NZ$7,063.54; less a 2% conversion and card-cost allowance (assumption) = NZ$6,922.26 usable.

**The funds-gap point.** The visa needs NZ$4,200 per person (assumed per person), so NZ$8,400 for two, about USD 4,656.46. If that cash must still be in your account when you show it, only USD 4,343.54 is free for visa, insurance and flights, which together cost USD 5,084.40 here: a shortfall of about USD 740.86. The official page says proof can be bank statements and that Immigration New Zealand may ask after you apply. It does not say the money must stay untouched on arrival, so **we cannot say how strictly this is checked**. Safest: show the funds at application time, and plan for more savings, cheaper flights or a lower insurance quote.

Nine weeks stand in for about 2 months (8.7 weeks, rounded up): 640 + 8 × 579 = NZ$5,272 (about USD 2,922). One-off costs: bond 500 (refundable) + bedding, basics and clothes 300 + SIMs 60 + work clothes 100 = NZ$960. Cash after one-offs: 6,922.26 − 960 = NZ$5,962.26.

**C. How long the savings last**

With no income at all: (5,962.26 − 640) ÷ 579 + 1 is about 10.2 weeks (about 2.4 months) before cash runs out.

With income, the job search takes 2 or 5 weeks, the first pay arrives one week after starting, pay is the minimum wage plus 8% holiday pay, PAYE uses 1.83% ACC, and the window is 9 weeks. Net per person at 30 hours a week = 775.98 gross − 129.00 PAYE = NZ$646.98.

**Reading it:** with both of you working 30 hours at the minimum wage, cash at week 9 is about NZ$8,454 (start after 2 weeks) or NZ$4,572 (after 5 weeks), and weekly income exceeds the NZ$579 spend, so the savings are not being used up. ## What is still unconfirmed

- **The 2027 opening date,** whether a fee or quota change applies, and how fast the quota fills.
- **Whether the NZ$4,200 is per person** and whether it must remain in the account on arrival. **Next step:** ask Immigration New Zealand before you move the money.
- **The NZ$770 fee basis:** the fee comes from a row in the May 2026 fees guide; matching it to Argentines is our reading.
- **Exchange rates:** the Reserve Bank rate was not readable, so a secondary aggregator rate is used; the black-market and MEP dollar rates and current Argentine foreign-exchange rules for individuals were not researched.
- **Insurance:** one insurer's price only, and acceptance of any policy by Immigration New Zealand is unverified.
- **Flights (USD 1,300 each), living costs, the 2% conversion cost, and a couple sharing one room at NZ$275** are unsourced assumptions. The cash at arrival, the 10.2 weeks with zero income and the USD 740.86 gap all move directly with the flight price.
- **Rents:** the city medians are second-hand readings; the listings are examples, not availability, and cannot predict September 2027 rents.
- **Other gaps:** bank details beyond ANZ; tax-refund timing; 2027–28 public holidays; tax brackets after 1 April 2027; the content of the Employment Leave Act changes; the sick-leave qualifying period; care-sector job rules for Sofía; live Trade Me and Flatmates listings; flight prices; food and transport costs.
- **Arithmetic check:** the money figures were recomputed with a separate calculation and matched on the final run (one earlier one-cent rounding mismatch was fixed). That check was run by the same author as the guide, not by an outside reviewer, and the pay examples other than NZ$163.41 are our calculations rather than IRD table lookups.
