If freelance turnover is climbing, it is easy to worry that staying micro means leaving money behind. For a developer with modest costs, **my provisional choice is to stay micro for 2027 and check VAT first—not to incorporate just for a predicted tax saving.** The illustration below leaves **€45,776.01** after contributions, income tax and business expenses under micro. CFE and unmodelled common costs remain outside that amount. It does not establish the best company structure or a guaranteed saving.

Meet Léa, a **hypothetical** Paris web developer, aged 29, single with 1 tax part and no children or other income. Imagine she has been a non-regulated, non-Cipav BNC micro-entrepreneur since 2024, rents a studio, has about €68,000 of turnover in 2026, expects €80,000–€85,000 in 2027 and spends €6,000 a year on business costs. The central case uses €82,500 of turnover excluding VAT (HT). No actual reader has supplied these personal facts. Her earlier turnover, tax-reference income and VAT history are unknown.

**These are annualized after-tax, full-distribution illustrations, not a forecast of spendable cash in 2027.** Company distributions and contribution adjustments can arrive later. The calculation reuses the **2026 income-tax scale for 2025 income** for **2027 income taxed in 2028**. That future scale, social ceilings and applicable rates still need checking. The rule snapshot is November 8, 2026; it is not current-law certification.

## First, find the date VAT actually started

The micro ceiling and the VAT exemption are different tests. Under the [official VAT-franchise guidance](https://entreprendre.service-public.gouv.fr/vosdroits/F21746), services and ordinary liberal activities use a €37,500 prior-year threshold and a €41,250 current-year upper threshold.

| Situation, assuming the franchise was available initially | Consequence under these rules |
|---|---|
| Prior-year turnover exceeds €37,500 | VAT from January 1 of the following year |
| Current-year turnover exceeds €37,500 but not €41,250 | Franchise can continue to December 31; VAT starts the following January 1 |
| Current-year turnover exceeds €41,250 | VAT from the first day of crossing, not just the following month |
| Léa's assumed €68,000 in 2026 | VAT no later than the 2026 crossing date, possibly earlier; VAT applies throughout 2027 under these thresholds |

**Unknown 2025 turnover matters: VAT may already have been due from January 1, 2026.** Earlier liability or an earlier option is also possible. Reconstruct dated receipts, transactions and invoices, including the crossing-day invoices; do not simply tax the amount above the threshold and call that the bill.

At an assumed 20% VAT rate, the €26,750 difference between €68,000 and €41,250 gives **about €4,458–€5,350 on that excess alone**: €4,458 if treated as VAT-inclusive (TTC), or €5,350 if VAT is added to an HT amount. **That is neither an arrears estimate nor a ceiling.** For scale only, a fully taxable €68,000 year would contain about €11,333 VAT if TTC, or require €13,600 if HT. Neither larger figure is Léa's assessed bill. Input credits, amounts recoverable from clients, transaction treatment, interest and penalties are not calculated.

Ask the service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) or an accountant to establish the effective date before issuing corrective invoices. Check VAT registration, invoice wording and returns yourself. Once liable, qualifying input VAT may be deductible: €6,000 of HT purchases all carrying deductible 20% VAT would mean up to €1,200, not an automatic credit. Do not assume every client can recover VAT or accept a price increase. Social contributions remain based on HT turnover.

## A strong year does not automatically end micro

The [tax authority's ceiling and exit rules](https://www.impots.gouv.fr/professionnel/questions/pour-rester-micro-entrepreneur-quel-montant-de-chiffre-daffaires-ou-de) set the services/BNC ceiling at **€83,600 HT for 2026–2028**, not the older €77,700 limit. The [micro regime guide](https://entreprendre.service-public.gouv.fr/vosdroits/F32353) also distinguishes these thresholds from VAT.

| Hypothetical turnover history | Micro consequence |
|---|---|
| 2026: €68,000; 2027: €85,000 | A first excess in 2027 does not itself end micro in 2028 |
| 2027: €85,000; 2028: €85,000 | Two consecutive excess years mean exit on January 1, 2029 under these rules |
| Exit from micro | A real-profit regime is required; incorporation is not required |

Keep a monthly HT turnover total. If another high year looks likely, compare **EI au réel / déclaration contrôlée as well as EURL and SASU**. A change of tax regime does not, by itself, change the legal form. The tax authority also notes the VAT-code recodification from January 1, 2027; do not confuse a code-reference change with a new micro ceiling.

## What micro leaves, and why VL is not a fresh option now

For this non-regulated BNC example, the [micro-social rules](https://entreprendre.service-public.gouv.fr/vosdroits/F36232) and [URSSAF's rate calendar](https://www.urssaf.fr/accueil/actualites/taux-cotisations-autoentrepeneur.html) give 25.6% for 2026. [URSSAF's status guide](https://www.autoentrepreneur.urssaf.fr/portail/accueil/sinformer-sur-le-statut/lessentiel-du-statut.html) says the 0.2% professional-training contribution (CFP) is additional. The model therefore uses 25.8% including CFP, reused for 2027 rather than confirmed for that future year.

The [micro-fiscal guide](https://entreprendre.service-public.gouv.fr/vosdroits/F23267) gives a 34% BNC allowance, with a €305 minimum. Actual costs are not deducted again for income tax, but they still leave your bank balance.

| Main micro calculation | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Turnover HT | €82,500.00 |
| Contributions including CFP: 25.8% | €21,285.00 |
| Taxable income after 34% allowance | €54,450.00 |
| Income tax using the older scale | €9,438.99 |
| Actual business expenses | €6,000.00 |
| Annualized personal amount after these items | €45,776.01 |

The [published progressive scale](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F1419) used here has bands at €11,600, €29,579, €84,577 and €181,917, with rates of 0%, 11%, 30%, 41% and 45%. It is the 2026 scale for 2025 income, not an announced scale for 2027 income taxed in 2028. The model does not implement all household adjustments, tax credits or low-income relief.

| Income-tax route at €82,500 HT | Default progressive scale | VL, only if validly elected and eligible |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income-tax basis | €54,450 taxable income | 2.2% of turnover |
| Income tax | €9,438.99 | €1,815.00 |
| Amount after tax, contributions and €6,000 expenses | €45,776.01 | €53,400.00 |
| Difference from default | — | €7,623.99; illustration only |

**For an existing activity, you cannot newly opt now for VL in 2027 unless the option was already filed by September 30, 2026.** Not currently using VL does not prove that no future-effective option was filed. Check the acknowledgement. The [official VL eligibility page](https://www.impots.gouv.fr/professionnel/questions/en-tant-que-micro-entrepreneur-sous-quelles-conditions-puis-je-opter-pour-l) also requires **2025 RFR no greater than €29,579 per part** for 2027, plus the applicable micro conditions. Léa's RFR is unknown. The next ordinary deadline for an existing activity is September 30, 2027 for 2028, subject to that option's eligibility rules; do not infer eligibility from turnover alone. VL is not automatically preferable for every household.

EURL means a single-member limited-liability company; SASU is a single-member simplified company. These examples use IS, not every possible tax election. EURL pay here is before manager contributions, not employee-style gross pay. The model assumes €1,000 capital and applies social contributions to EURL dividends above 10% of capital. It follows a 26% reduction of the social and CSG/CRDS bases, and assumes a further 10% professional-expense deduction for pay taxation. **The applicable EURL contribution regime, base and deductions need a professional simulation**; the [company-director social guide](https://entreprendre.service-public.gouv.fr/vosdroits/F38152) itself excludes professional libéraux from its stated scope. It does not independently certify this developer's treatment.

The [IS guidance](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/professionnels-entreprises/vosdroits/F23575) gives 15% on the first €42,500 of profit and 25% above, subject to turnover no greater than €10,000,000, fully paid capital and at least 75% individual ownership. The examples assume those conditions are met. SASU payroll uses the full employer rates in the [URSSAF private-sector table](https://www.urssaf.fr/accueil/outils-documentation/taux-baremes/taux-cotisations-secteur-prive.html), not an established entitlement to reduced rates. The [2026 PASS](https://www.urssaf.fr/accueil/outils-documentation/taux-baremes/plafonds-securite-sociale.html), €48,060, and [Agirc-Arrco rates](https://reglementation.agirc-arrco.fr/home/baremes/listes-area/baremes-1/cotisations-au-regime-agirc-arrco-en-2026.html) are reused. The accident-at-work rate of 1% is an assumption.

## Test the assumptions, not just turnover

With the same expenses, setup and rate assumptions, the turnover sensitivity is:

| Turnover HT | Micro | EURL: €30,000 pay, PFU model | EURL: €55,000 pay, PFU model | SASU: salary only, PFU model | SASU: €7,212 gross, PFU model | SASU: €18,000 gross, PFU model |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| €80,000 | €44,416 | €39,516 | €42,669 | €36,172 | €39,929 | €40,623 |
| €82,500 | €45,776 | €40,799 | €44,145 | €37,221 | €41,215 | €41,910 |
| €85,000 | €47,136 | €41,997 | €45,557 | €38,269 | €42,501 | €43,196 |

That table does not settle the dividend-tax choice. The [official dividend guidance](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/professionnels-entreprises/vosdroits/F32963) permits a progressive-scale option with a **40% allowance on qualifying dividends**, aggregated with actual other taxable income. The election has a wider scope than a single dividend. The PFU model uses the retained 2026 rate of 31.4%—12.8% income tax plus 18.6% social levies—without confirming its future application.

| SASU illustration at €82,500 HT | PFU model from the comparison | Progressive-scale variant |
|---|---:|---:|
| €7,212 gross salary plus dividends | €41,215 | About €43,825 |
| €18,000 gross salary plus dividends | €41,910 | About €42,897 |

The variants keep the same payroll and company costs, use the 40% dividend allowance and 18.6% social levies, aggregate salary taxable income, and add no extra deductible CSG adjustment. They are **not optimized or a certified tax election**. They already change which of these SASU mixes looks stronger. Do not decide using Léa's micro marginal rate as if it were her rate in every company scenario.

Other assumptions move the result too. An alternative EURL social reading gives about €41,230 at €57,500 pay, not a legal lower bound. With all-in company costs of €1,500 and no separate setup charge, the PFU illustrations give about €44,782 for EURL at €57,500 pay and €42,784 for SASU at €18,000 gross. That €1,500 is **total company cost**, not merely an accountant's fee. Full versus reduced SASU employer rates, the EURL social base, actual deductible costs and future rules still need resolving.

**There is no robust exact expense threshold at which a company becomes better here.** Larger expenses, a need to reinvest inside the business, genuinely lower administration costs or different cover needs justify a new comparison; they do not prove incorporation is right. Profit retained inside a company is business money, **not personal house-deposit savings**. In the €18,000 SASU illustration, retaining about €40,625 leaves only about €14,041 of personal net salary; extracting the retained money can trigger later tax.

## Compare protection as well as the personal amount

The [micro pension guide](https://entreprendre.service-public.gouv.fr/vosdroits/F23369) explains why validating quarters is different from building pension income. The contributions below are proxies for rights, not predicted pensions or benefit awards.

| Route | Pension trade-off in this model | Health and sick-pay point | Unemployment point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Micro | About €14,235 pension contributions; roughly €10,850 turnover for 4 quarters using the 2026 benchmark, not a confirmed 2027 threshold | Reimbursements and possible daily allowances; individual eligibility not established | No ordinary unemployment cover assumed; prior salaried rights need separate checking |
| EURL manager | About €12,625 / €12,906 at the two pay levels; classification and base unresolved | The director guide describes qualifying self-employed cover, including a 1-year contribution condition, 3-day wait and €65.84 daily maximum for 2026; applicability must be checked | No ordinary manager unemployment cover modelled |
| SASU salary only | About €15,011 pension-contribution proxy | Salary-based cover and accident-at-work protection; mutuelle and prévoyance excluded | A presidency is not assumed to create ordinary salaried unemployment rights |
| SASU mostly dividends | About €2,043 at €7,212 gross; €5,099 at €18,000 gross; dividends build no salary-based pension rights | Low salary means limited salary-based benefits; dividends do not replace insured salary | Same presidency limitation; specific rights require checking |

**€7,212 is a 2026 pension-quarter salary benchmark only**, using 150 hours × €12.02 × 4. It is not a guaranteed 2027 salary floor or proof of sufficient cover. A limited self-employed safety net such as ATI has not been assessed here. Do not cancel existing protection based on this table.

## A practical decision sequence

- Start with VAT: gather 2025 turnover and dated 2026 receipts and invoices; establish the liability date and any correction route with the SIE or an accountant.
- Keep micro as the provisional low-expense choice, while monitoring HT turnover and actual costs. VAT registration does not by itself force incorporation.
- If growth persists, compare EI au réel, EURL and SASU using actual fee quotes, barème/PFU alternatives, the correct social regime and a dated distribution/payment calendar.
- Check any already-filed VL election and RFR. Keep tax reserves separate from everyday money; a projected annual after-tax amount is not a payment schedule.
- Review pension and sick-pay protection, personal cash needs and business-retention plans together. Legal-form protection also needs advice; incorporation is not a blanket liability shield.

The useful next document is an accountant's scenario comparison, not incorporation paperwork. Ask it to separate personal cash, corporate retained funds, provisional contributions, later adjustments, VAT, CFE and one-off costs. The missing facts can change the choice; the provisional micro preference is a starting point, not an instruction to ignore them.
