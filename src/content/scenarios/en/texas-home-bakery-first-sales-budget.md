You can sell beautiful baking and still earn less than you hoped once shopping, cleanup and Saturdays at a stall count as work. For Maria, a San Antonio nurse planning a March 2027 launch with **$3,000 and 15–20 orders a month**, the sensible start is a narrow, shelf-stable menu sold by preorder, with scheduled personal handoffs. Try **$80 simple cakes, $33 cookie dozens and $25 sourdough loaves**, then test those prices against real receipts and timed batches. Keep **$1,000 untouched**, plus $360 contingency.

**Counterintuitive finding: weekly markets miss the $15/hour target at this volume.** One monthly event models $17.79/hour at 15 orders or $22.81/hour at 20. Four events at 20 orders fall to **$9.14/hour**. That is before personal taxes, benefits and recovery of startup spending. More selling days are not automatically a better business.

There is also a venue problem: **Pearl's published policy excludes cottage-law vendors**, not just applicants on a waitlist. No eligible alternative Saturday stall is confirmed. Treat the monthly-market figures below as a conditional comparison, not promised income. If admission or attendance rules do not fit, launch preorder-only and recalculate the handoff costs and time.

All amounts are USD. Legal, market and processor information was read on **November 7, 2026**; it has not been refreshed here. Costs, production times, prices and customer demand are planning assumptions, not quotations or measured results. Recheck the rules and terms before the future launch.

## Choose a menu you can safely make and hand over

Texas expanded cottage-food law through [SB 541](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/billtext/html/SB00541F.htm), effective **September 1, 2025**. It is not the old $50,000/nonperishable-only regime. But broader legal permission is not a reason to start with a refrigerated menu. The [Texas DSHS guidance](https://www.dshs.texas.gov/retail-food-establishments/permits-retail-food-establishments/texas-cottage-food-production) and [Health and Safety Code](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/hs/htm/hs.437.htm) give these planning boundaries:

| Decision | What matters for this launch |
| --- | --- |
| Cakes, cookies and plain sourdough | Start only with validated non-TCS recipes: foods not requiring time and temperature control for safety. Custard, cream cheese, whipped cream, cheesecake and moist fruit fillings can need temperature control. A frosting's name does not establish shelf stability; get a reliable recipe or safety assessment. |
| Excluded foods | No meat or poultry products; seafood, fish or shellfish products; ice or ice products, including ice cream and frozen custard; low-acid canned goods; CBD/THC products; raw milk or raw-milk products. Do not add meat inclusions to bread. Pasteurized milk, butter and eggs as ingredients are not the raw-milk exclusion, but final-product safety still matters. |
| Refrigerated/TCS expansion | Requires DSHS registration before sales, plus a separate cold-chain and label workflow—not included in this budget. Storage/delivery air temperature must achieve internal cold-food temperature ≤41°F or hot-food temperature ≥135°F. Put the made-on date on the label and the statutory safe-handling statement on the label or accompanying invoice/receipt in at least 12-point type. No TCS wholesale or donations. |
| Direct sales | Qualifying direct sales to Texas consumers can include markets and online orders. Government permission does not guarantee a private booth. Non-TCS wholesale is only through the registered, contractual Texas cottage-food-vendor route, not informal consignment. That route also needs the vendor's display sign and a made-on date on the product label. |
| Instagram ordering | Link to a public, legible product/label page on the operation's website before taking online payment. An incomplete Instagram photograph or DM is not a verified substitute. Address/actual registration ID may be supplied on the delivered label. |
| Handoffs | For online orders, the operator, employee or household member must personally deliver to the consumer. This plan uses scheduled personal handoffs, including Maria handing over online-paid pickup orders. That pickup approach is conservative, not a specific agency ruling. No parcel shipping, interstate sales, unrelated gig couriers or unattended porch drops are assumed. |
| Annual gross ceiling | DSHS displays $150,000 annual gross cottage-food income, not profit. The statute provides annual inflation adjustment. The adjusted ceiling, timing and partial-year treatment for the launch year remain unconfirmed; track all channels together and verify the published figure before selling. |
| Food-handler training | Maria must complete accredited basic food-handler training and keep evidence. An existing accredited food-manager certificate may substitute where applicable; nursing credentials alone do not. No verified course fee or renewal period is quoted. |
| Local health permits | A qualifying cottage operation is exempt from food-establishment licensing and routine inspection; local entities cannot charge the prohibited production/direct-sales/sampling permit fees. Emergency and recall powers remain. Private market fees are different, and sampling still needs safe handling. |

### Make the label and online page before accepting money

Package to prevent contamination. Use the business name, common product name, physical home address or actual DSHS registration ID, the major allergens actually present, and this exact residence disclosure:

> **Maria's Bakery — Chocolate Chip Cookies**<br>
> [Physical home address OR actual DSHS registration ID]<br>
> Contains: wheat, milk, eggs, soy [replace with the actual allergens; include tree nuts and sesame if present].
>
> **THIS PRODUCT WAS PRODUCED IN A PRIVATE RESIDENCE THAT IS NOT SUBJECT TO GOVERNMENTAL LICENSING OR INSPECTION.**

Do not copy the sample allergen list without checking your recipes. A label is not an allergen-free guarantee. Oversized or bulky products that cannot be conventionally packaged can have the information on the invoice/receipt. For this direct non-TCS menu, batch/made-on dates help traceability; the mandatory made-on rule described above applies to TCS and cottage-vendor sales. Full ingredients, net weight and contact details are helpful additions, not presented here as an exhaustive statement of every federal or claim-dependent labeling obligation.

**Address privacy has a registration consequence.** A direct non-TCS seller can use the physical home address without DSHS registration; using the ID alternative requires registration. Do not invent an ID or substitute a PO box. Avoid putting the home address on the online page unnecessarily; keep the required delivered label complete.

## Clear the launch gates without buying unnecessary permits

### Required, conditional—and still unresolved

- **Training:** finish the accredited food-handler route before launch, unless the applicable accredited food-manager substitute is already held.
- **DSHS registration:** required for TCS foods; optional for this direct non-TCS menu if choosing the ID instead of a home address. Selling your own baking does not by itself make you the separately defined resale “cottage food vendor.”
- **Assumed business name:** if Maria uses “Maria's Bakery,” plan the county DBA route for a sole proprietor. The [Secretary of State's name-filing guidance](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/corp/namefilingsfaqs.shtml) points to the county where the business office is maintained—Bexar County for a Bexar home. No LLC is needed for this individual cottage route. A DBA is not a business license or exclusive name right.
- **DBA fee:** do not treat $14 as settled. [Bexar's webpage](https://www.bexar.org/2958/Assumed-Business-NamesDBAs) lists $13 for one owner plus $1 clerk acknowledgment, but its [linked application](https://www.bexar.org/DocumentCenter/View/26459/Assumed-Name-Application-for-Certificate-of-Ownership?bidId=), revised April 20, 2023, lists $20 with outside notarization or $21 with clerk acknowledgment. Both list $0.50 per extra owner. Confirm the payable fee before filing. The $100 training/DBA/notary envelope below is an allowance, not a quote.
- **Sales-tax permit:** the final determination remains a prelaunch check. An exempt product does not establish an exempt-only business's permit exception. The [Comptroller's market guidance](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/publications/96-211.php) requires a permit for selling, taking orders for or promoting taxable items at an event. [Permit application is free](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/sales/faq/permit.php), but a bond can be required; a permit holder must file returns even with no sales or tax due. An express primary-source exempt-only exception for this exact arrangement remains unconfirmed. This is not a reason to collect tax on exempt products.
- **Product tax classification:** ordinary unheated, packaged bakery goods without eating utensils support the model's exempt-sales assumption. The [Comptroller's bakery distinction](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/publications/96-280.php) defines a qualifying bakery as a retail location with more than 50% bakery sales from a counter/display case, consumed off premises. For a nonqualifying seller, heating or supplying utensils makes bakery items taxable. Napkins, wax paper and foam/plastic clamshells are not utensils; plates, forks and knives are. A home Instagram business does not qualify merely because its name says “bakery.” Avoid hot service, plated desserts and taxable add-ons until the exact treatment is settled.

### Check the home separately from the food rules

The [municipal zoning provisions](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/lg/htm/lg.211.htm), §211.032, prevent a city from prohibiting qualifying cottage-food home use through zoning. Section 211.033 preserves nuisance and other tort actions. The [San Antonio health FAQ](https://www.sa.gov/Directory/Departments/SAMHD/Licenses-Food-Permits) recognizes home production by people meeting state cottage requirements. Do not buy a generic restaurant or temporary-food permit just because a general city page lists one.

That does **not** settle address-specific occupancy, signage, traffic or independent building requirements. Nor is municipal protection an established override of an HOA, deed restriction or lease. My read is that private restrictions may still limit the business; Maria's actual documents were not supplied. Review them before promising home pickup. Off-site handoffs can reduce traffic but cannot cure a covenant banning production at home. No commercial-kitchen conversion is assumed or established as necessary.

### Pearl is not the Saturday-market answer

The [Pearl Market Guide](https://atpearl.com/markets/market-guide/) says it is not accepting applications and does not take cottage-law vendors. Saturday hours are 9 a.m.–1 p.m.; its $25 application fee is **not** in this launch budget and should not be spent on this plan.

Its separate [vendor recruitment page](https://atpearl.com/markets/become-a-vendor/) describes producer-only goods within 150 miles, year-round attendance except seasonal products, licenses/permits/insurance, vetting, site visits and samples. It calls for annual membership payment within seven days of acceptance. Those general requirements do not cancel the cottage-vendor exclusion. Stall/membership amounts and insurance limits were not published in the inspected pages.

**No eligible alternative Saturday stall was verified.** Before spending on any market, obtain written cottage-food eligibility, acceptance, attendance minimums, all fees, cancellation terms, tent/weights/display rules, insurance/additional-insured requirements and sampling conditions. If one Saturday a month is not allowed, keep the launch preorder-only. Do not accept a weekly commitment just to make the original idea happen.

## Price the whole job, not just the flour

The opening asks are **$80 for a simple 6-inch two-layer cake, $33 for a dozen standard cookies and $25 for a nominal 800g finished sourdough loaf**. These are cost-based asks, not evidence that San Antonio customers will pay them. If bread will not sell at its required price, improve batching or drop it rather than quietly paying yourself less.

The conservative product table assumes **15 paid orders/month**, one priced unit per order, one conditional monthly event, one domestic card transaction per order and identical online/market prices. Ingredient, packaging, utility and waste figures are hypothetical. The fee uses [Square Free online/invoices](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/5068-what-are-square-s-fees): **3.3% + $0.30**, not its in-person rate. Recheck before launch; no shipping, tips, international cards or taxable utensils are assumed.

Monthly shared cash is $40 stall + $10 transport + $30 insurance provision = **$80**, or $5.3333/unit at 15 orders. Shared work is 3 hours shopping/admin/marketing + 6 hours travel/setup/selling/teardown = **9 hours**, or 0.60 hour/unit. Count mixing, baking attendance, decorating, packing, cleanup and failed batches. Exclude passive overnight fermentation only when free to do something else.

| Product / unit | Ask | Ingredients + packaging + utilities + waste | Production + admin + event time | Payment fee | Net available for labor | Effective pay | Whole-dollar minimum for $15/h |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| 6-inch two-layer simple cake | $80.00 | $12.00 + $3.00 + $0.50 + $0.6 = $16.1 | 2.0000 + 0.20 + 0.40 = 2.6000 h | 0.033 × 80 + 0.30 = $2.94 | 80 − 16.1 − 5.3333 − 2.94 = $55.63 | $21.39/h | $63.00 |
| One dozen standard cookies | $33.00 | $5.00 + $1.00 + $0.25 + $0.25 = $6.5 | 0.6667 + 0.20 + 0.40 = 1.2667 h | 0.033 × 33 + 0.30 = $1.39 | 33 − 6.5 − 5.3333 − 1.389 = $19.78 | $15.61/h | $33.00 |
| One 800g sourdough loaf | $25.00 | $2.50 + $0.75 + $0.50 + $0.125 = $3.875 | 0.3333 + 0.20 + 0.40 = 0.9333 h | 0.033 × 25 + 0.30 = $1.13 | 25 − 3.875 − 5.3333 − 1.125 = $14.67 | $15.71/h | $25.00 |

The whole-dollar $15/h floors are **$63 cake, $33 cookies and $25 bread**, not suggested selling prices. With `N` orders and production time for that product, the minimum is:

`P = (direct cost + 80/N + 0.30 + 15 × (production hours + 9/N)) ÷ (1 − 0.033)`, rounded upward.

Waste is 5% of ingredients. Batching is the important constraint: four dozen cookies need 2 active hours and eight loaves need 2 active hours. At 15 orders, six cookie dozens still require **two full batches, 4 hours**; six loaves use one full **2-hour** allowance. That is 4/6 hour per cookie dozen and 2/6 per loaf. At 20 orders, eight cookie dozens still need 4 hours and eight loaves 2 hours. These are **unverified timed-trial targets**, not measured efficiencies. Consolidate baking dates; separate bake days need recosting. The 800g finished weight is a target, not a measured yield.

### Charge separately for custom work and delivery

The base cake excludes fondant, elaborate piping, toppers and individual design sessions. An extra 30 minutes requires at least $7.50 ÷ 0.967 = **$7.76** extra revenue before materials; round up to $8. A separate 30-minute delivery with $5 travel cost needs ($7.50 + $5) ÷ 0.967 = **$12.93**; round up to $13 if paid in the same transaction. A separate payment also needs the fixed processing fee. Do not absorb either job into the base price.

The one-event case charges market costs to all monthly orders conservatively; visitors are not paid customers. An eligible, accepted monthly pilot may be worth testing, but preorder-first remains the practical alternative.

“Clears $15/hour” means cash available to compensate Maria **after operating costs but before personal income/self-employment tax, benefits and startup capital recovery**. It is not guaranteed take-home pay. For a provisional 25% personal-tax reserve and $15/h after that reserve, use a $20/h labor target: whole-dollar floors become $77 cake, $39 cookies and $30 bread at 15 orders. The 25% is an assumption, not an assessed tax rate.

Carry fractional cents through calculations and round only displayed totals, using half-up display; $1.125 becomes $1.13. Actual processor rounding per transaction can differ by cents. Test the wage threshold with actual settlements.

## What needs a fresh answer before you commit

The launch still depends on recipe safety, actual batch capacity, ingredient/packaging/insurance prices, customer demand, property clearance, the DBA fee, exact tax-permit treatment and an eligible market's complete terms. No current administrative-rule adoption history or adjusted future cap was conclusively established. DSHS's implementing-rule wording does not prove no later rule exists. A statutory cross-reference defect is not permission to sell excluded foods.

Use February's checks to close those gaps. If the market gate fails, protect the reserve and use preorders with safe scheduled handoffs; reprice the actual delivery/collection work instead of importing the market model unchanged. State permission, private admission and an acceptable hourly wage are three separate tests.
