**Worried about running out of money while sales build? At 75 mature cups/day, allow £57,070 before opening, including a £5,000 cash buffer. Starting with £45,000 leaves a £12,070 gap. In this slow case, neither monthly operating profit nor cash after your drawings reaches zero during the year.** Check demand and premises costs before relying on the expected case.

**Use this as a planning starting point, not tax, financial or legal advice; an adviser can help you check your own situation.**

## Start with the way you will actually work

This example is for a 15 m² Manchester takeaway hot-drink kiosk, opening on 2027-03-01 and covering the period to 2028-02-29. The information was checked on 2026-11-05, not refreshed for opening. It assumes a sole trader, 1 unpaid owner and 2 part-time staff. No premises have been selected and no supplier quotes secured, so replace the allowances before committing.

| What you need to decide | Starting assumption | What to keep in mind |
|---|---|---|
| Average ticket | £4.20 including VAT | 1 drink per transaction; no food sales |
| Mature demand: slow / expected / busy | 75 / 115 / 155 cups/day | Not validated; monthly ramp-up and seasonality change actual sales |
| Your work and withdrawals | 48 hours/week; £1,500/month drawings | Your salary is not an operating expense here; drawings still use cash |
| Staff coverage | 2 people × 20 productive hours/week | Add paid leave cover, employer NI and pension costs |
| Rent and service charge | £1,100/month + £100/month | Gross allowances, not quotes or Manchester rent comparisons |
| Cost of goods | 28% of net sales | Gross supplier cost, including milk, cups and waste |
| Start-up contingency | 15% of non-deposit outlay | Assumed spent before opening, not cash left for later |
| Business rates | £0/month | Depends on relief being awarded; it is not confirmed |

The first month allows 27 open days and 65% of mature demand. The trading calendar and ramp-up factors are assumptions, not a forecast. Plan staff and owner coverage around breaks, sickness and peak queues. The busy case includes no extra capacity cost, so test whether you could serve that demand.

## Profit, usable cash and getting your investment back are different

| Scenario | Funding including buffer | First operating-profit month | First cash-positive month after drawings | Start-up cash recovered | Annual cash after drawings |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---:|
| Slow: 75 cups/day | £57,070 | Not within 12 months | Not within 12 months | Not within 12 months | −£24,321 |
| Expected: 115 cups/day | £35,532 | April 2027 | June 2027 | Not within 12 months | £4,419 |
| Busy: 155 cups/day | £32,749 | March 2027 | March 2027 | December 2027 | £33,158 |

The amounts are rounded. Operating profit includes depreciation but not your salary or personal tax. A cash-positive month means the operating cash covers £1,500 drawings. Getting your investment back means cumulative cash after drawings covers all pre-opening cash, including the deposit. These are the first nonnegative months, not a promise of lasting break-even. No recovery date beyond 2028-02-29 is assumed.

**An early profit month does not mean you can relax about cash. In the expected case, profit starts in April 2027, cash after drawings first turns positive in June 2027, and it falls below zero again in January 2028.** Annual operating profit of £18,859 is not your take-home pay. After £18,000 annual drawings, £4,419 cash remains before personal tax, with start-up spending still unrecovered.

Work out funding as pre-opening cash + worst cumulative cash deficit after drawings + your chosen buffer. The rounded slow-case amounts are £27,749 + £24,321 + £5,000 = £57,070. The expected case has a smaller £2,783 early deficit. Start-up costs and the buffer are already included in the funding recommendation; do not add them again.

Starting with £45,000, the slow case runs down to about −£7,070 usable cash. The £12,070 funding gap also restores the £5,000 buffer. Covering those losses buys time; it does not make slow sales profitable or guarantee you can keep trading beyond the period shown.

## What you pay before opening

| Component | Rounded cash | How to treat it |
|---|---:|---|
| Non-deposit subtotal | £21,260 | Equipment, fit-out, setup, stock and paid staff preparation |
| Contingency | £3,189 | Assumed spent before opening |
| Refundable rent deposit | £3,300 | Cash tied up, not a depreciation expense |
| Total | £27,749 | Does not include prepaid opening-month rent |

The subtotal includes £6,000 for an espresso machine and £8,500 for fit-out. Procurement, rent and insurance premiums still need real quotes. Monthly rent comes out of operating cash; do not also count it as pre-opening rent. Opening stock stays invested, while monthly replenishment equals cost of goods sold.

## Check the rules before you commit

The guidance below was checked on 2026-11-05. Use it to prepare your questions, then confirm what applies to your opening date and actual premises. It has not been checked again for this page.

- This budget assumes voluntary VAT registration from opening, with a 20% hot-drink VAT reserve. Set output VAT aside monthly; costs are counted gross and no input-VAT recovery is credited. That leaves a conservative view of spendable cash, not a VAT-return calendar or a requirement to register immediately. Check [VAT rates](https://www.gov.uk/vat-rates), [HMRC takeaway guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/catering-takeaway-food-and-vat-notice-7091) and [registration rules](https://www.gov.uk/register-for-vat). Gross first-year receipts are not net taxable turnover.
- Staff pay is set at £12.71/hour in March 2027 and the announced £13.21/hour from April 2027. Recheck the [minimum-wage table](https://www.gov.uk/national-minimum-wage-rates) and [2027 announcement](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/minimum-wage-rates-for-2027) before hiring. The announcement's prose contains a conflicting year; the table and rates page support the timing used here. Do not let that typo set your payroll date.
- Allow for 5.6 weeks' leave with replacement cover, 15% employer NI and a 3% employer pension contribution on qualifying earnings. Dividing annual thresholds by 12 is a budget approximation, not exact payroll. Future NI and pension values are announced figures whose implementing rules need checking. Employment Allowance is not deducted here. See [holiday entitlement](https://www.gov.uk/holiday-entitlement-rights), [HMRC employer thresholds](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/rates-and-thresholds-for-employers-2026-to-2027) and [pension contributions](https://www.gov.uk/workplace-pensions/what-you-your-employer-and-the-government-pay).
- [Food registration](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/starting-a-food-business) is free and needed at least 28 days before trading. For this opening, that means no later than 2027-02-01. It does not replace site permissions. The [Manchester street-trading page](https://www.manchester.gov.uk/online-directories/licences-and-permissions-directories/licensing/markets-and-street-trading/street-trader-licence-and-consent) lists conditional £420 consent / £720 licence charges. The £500 permits allowance may not be enough. Private land does not automatically mean exemption, so ask about the actual site before signing.
- [Small-business rates relief](https://www.gov.uk/business-rates-relief/small-business-rate-relief) depends on rateable value and eligibility, not floor area or rent. Confirm an award before keeping £0 rates. [Employers' liability insurance](https://www.gov.uk/employers-liability-insurance) normally requires £5 million cover. That is an insured limit, not a quote for the £70/month premium allowance; ask an insurer what your business needs.

This budget leaves out personal income-tax/NIC reserves, debt interest, later equipment replacement, lease tax and extra busy-case staffing. It is not a complete opening-compliance checklist. Keep personal-tax money separate and ask for advice on your premises before taking on the commitment.

## Make the next decision with better information

- Count likely paid transactions at your site, including quiet periods. Replace the 75 / 115 / 155 demand assumptions and monthly ramp-up rather than choosing the case you would like to happen.
- Get the lease, deposit, service charge, rateable value, utility capacity, fit-out and equipment quotes. Resolve permitted use and street-trading status before signing.
- When working through the figures, keep your own copy, update Assumptions and inspect the lowest usable cash in every case. Recalculate when your inputs change.

- Test demand, ticket and staffing separately. With staffing and cost percentages unchanged, a 10% increase in volume or ticket adds about £8,263 annual expected operating profit; 10% extra staff hours costs about £3,622. Those comparisons leave out price-driven changes in demand and the cost of expanding capacity.
