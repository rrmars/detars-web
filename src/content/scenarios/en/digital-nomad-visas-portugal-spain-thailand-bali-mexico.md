**Worried that your income looks high enough, but the visa still will not fit your work?** If you are a solo freelance UX designer earning **USD5,500/month from foreign clients**, leaving the US in **March 2027** for roughly March–December abroad, start with **Portugal's temporary-stay D8**. It accepts independent services and suits a ten-month base. For a longer base, consider **Spain** after checking company clients, experience and social security. **Thailand's DTV** accepts freelancers, but you still need the required cash history. For **Mexico**, ask your actual consulate what freelance evidence and work arrangement it accepts. **Bali's E33G is not a confirmed fit:** annualized USD66,000 does not replace the separate foreign-company employment agreement.

**Before you choose:** compare income and savings, stay limits, unverified 2027 fees, work permissions and tax risks together. Passing an income test is only the start.

You can use this guide to weigh Portugal D8 against Spain's digital nomad visa, check Thailand DTV savings, understand Bali E33G's employment contract and prepare for Mexico temporary residence as a remote freelancer.

This example assumes **one applicant, no dependants, US citizenship and lawful US application residence**. Confirm your citizenship: being “from the US” is not enough. Treat USD5,500 as recurring personal **gross income**, not business turnover or spendable net cash. Before choosing, add your savings, US state/county, passport expiry, criminal history, client legal entities and relationship history, qualifications/experience and exact travel days. Dependants or another passport can change the answer.

## Tax: a permitted stay is not a tax holiday

**Keeping clients and payments abroad does not automatically make your work tax-free.** Where you perform it still matters. Check immigration eligibility, work permission, tax residence, income source, VAT/invoicing and social security separately. Staying “under 183 days” alone misses several of the tests below.

Use this table to prepare questions for a tax adviser, not calculate your personal bill. Its sources were checked on **November 5, 2026**. Ask the adviser to check the law versions noted below before you choose a base.

| Location | Residence and income-source checks | Practical issue before choosing a base |
|---|---|---|
| **Portugal** | **More than 183 days in a qualifying rolling 12 months**, or habitual-home test sooner; residence normally from first stay day subject to exceptions. Residents face worldwide-income exposure. [Article15][PT-TAX15], [Article16][PT-TAX16] | Review independent-activity registration, VAT/invoicing, SS and partial-year rules. No automatic old NHR benefit for a new 2027 arrival. [Activity registration][PT-REGISTER], [SSA Portugal][PT-SSA] |
| **Spain** | **More than 183 calendar-year days**, economic-interests base or family presumption; sporadic absences can count. Ordinary residence generally whole-year/worldwide-income. [AEAT][ES-TAX] | Article93/“Beckham” status is **not automatic for an ordinary UX freelancer**; narrower independent-professional categories require proof. Do not budget on a flat-tax outcome without confirmation. [AEAT special regime][ES-ART93] |
| **Thailand** | Newer specific Revenue Department guidance uses **180 days or more per calendar year**. Thai-performed work can be Thai-source even paid abroad; foreign-source remittance rules are a separate issue. [Revenue PDF][TH5] | A 180-day DTV entry can already reach the residence boundary. The legacy overview says “more than 180” and contains old rates; no current tax calculation is drawn from it. |
| **Indonesia** | Residing, **more than 183 days in any 12 months**, or intention to reside can trigger residence; first-day liability can matter. Locally performed services paid abroad remain relevant. [DJP criteria][ID3], [tax-law compilation][ID5] | E33G does not itself grant a foreign-income holiday. Do not assume you qualify for the narrow expertise treatment. The compilation runs through Law7/2021; check later changes rather than treating it as a current 2026 code. |
| **Mexico** | **No universal 183-day residence test.** Home/centre of vital interests, including principal professional centre, matters; independent services can be sourced where performed. A conditional **less-than-183-day nonresident-services exception** is not the residence rule. [SAT CFF][MXT1], [SAT LISR][MXT2] | Saved CFF is marked **November 14, 2025**; LISR **April 1, 2024**. Later amendments/current consolidation, treaty status, RFC/VAT and departure notice need checking. Have an adviser confirm your rate and any personal exemption. |

For a **US citizen**, worldwide reporting generally continues. FEIE requires a foreign tax home plus qualifying residence or **330 full foreign days in a consecutive 12-month period**; it does **not reduce self-employment tax**. March 1–December 31, 2027 is **306 calendar days**, before travel-day exclusions—insufficient alone for the physical-presence test. A period into 2028 might qualify, but neither foreign tax home nor eligibility is established by having a visa. Review the **2026 return due in 2027**, 2027 estimates and the 2027 return filed in 2028, credits/totalization and US state domicile. [IRS FEIE][US2], [physical-presence test][US7], [foreign tax home][US8], [estimates][US5].

Foreign-account reporting is separate: FBAR's **USD10,000 aggregate** threshold is across accounts at any time, not per account; Form8938 is distinct, and higher abroad thresholds cannot be assumed just from a March departure. [FinCEN][USF1], [IRS comparison][US10]. Obtain a cross-border professional review before committing to a long home base or taking local clients.
