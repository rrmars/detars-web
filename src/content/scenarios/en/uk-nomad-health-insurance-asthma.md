## If asthma is not covered, take this route instead.

- **Distinguish routine costs from catastrophic risk.** An exclusion only for maintenance prescriptions may be manageable if affordable and the acute-attack/hospital benefit is explicitly retained. A blanket asthma exclusion is a different—and much larger—gap.

- **Try the next fully underwritten health plan:** Cigna Silver, then Allianz Care Base, requesting explicit asthma acceptance. Avoid Allianz’s moratorium shortcut for this purpose: continuing inhaler use prevents the required symptom/treatment/medication-free period. [A2](#source-a2) [A3](#source-a3)

- **If quotations exceed £150:** compare a deductible you can actually fund and reduced routine outpatient benefits, retaining asthma admissions and evacuation. Keep money available for the deductible and pay-first outpatient claims. A higher deductible is not a saving if you cannot afford a claim.

- **If every affordable offer excludes asthma entirely:** do not treat Genki Traveler, SafetyWing Essential or World Nomads as the fix. Rework the budget or itinerary before committing. A specialist pre-existing-condition long-stay travel policy could be another route, but none has been verified here; it must explicitly accept asthma, the full duration and remote work. Do not self-insure an excluded hospital emergency by accident.

## Portugal and Thailand: what the insurance must do.

### Portugal: do not wait until the end of your Thailand stay to plan this.

Six months in Portugal cannot rely on ordinary British visitor permission of 90 days in any rolling 180 across Schengen. Remote work also needs the appropriate permission. For a finite six-month stay, investigate the national temporary remote-work route; residence is a different route. [P5](#source-p5)

**A naming trap:** often called the “D8 digital-nomad visa”, the current UK VFS checklists label remote temporary stay **E11** and remote residence **D9**; D8 there is an accompanying-family category. Use the full purpose/title. These are the November 6, 2026 UK application checklists, not guaranteed 2027 rules. [P1](#source-p1)

### Temporary stay: E11

The 6 March 2026 checklist asks for travel insurance covering necessary medical expenses, urgent medical assistance and potential repatriation for **the whole Portuguese stay**. For your six months, do not submit only a 120-day policy. [P2](#source-p2)

### Residence: entry visa, then AIMA

The D9 checklist asks for that insurance for the initial **120-day entry-visa period**. Later, AIMA’s general residence requirements ask for **health insurance or evidence of SNS coverage**. A short entry certificate does not solve ongoing healthcare; private insurance is not necessarily required forever if you have valid SNS evidence. [P3](#source-p3) [P4](#source-p4)

The separate Schengen short-stay insurance standard is at least €30,000, all Schengen territory and medical/repatriation benefits. The dated E11/D9 national checklists do not state that numeric minimum: do not automatically transplant every short-stay rule. Eligible S1 holders have an alternative in those checklists; a UK passport alone does not make you eligible. [P1](#source-p1), [P2](#source-p2), [P3](#source-p3)

These assessments are reasoned from the cited plan documents and P2–P4, not insurer endorsements or consular decisions. Travel cover is not automatically disqualified at the entry stage, and a broad health plan is not automatically accepted.

### Thailand DTV: no insurance minimum listed on London’s current DTV1 checklist.

The Royal Thai Embassy London page (updated 8 September 2026) lists remote workers/freelancers, 180 days per entry and one possible extension of up to another 180 days. It does **not** list an insurance certificate or minimum insurance amount for DTV1. Thus **all seven plans have the same DTV assessment: no published insurance test to certify against on that checklist**. Other visa categories’ insurance rules should not be imported into DTV. Additional documents or future changes remain possible. [T1](#source-t1)

**Check actual days:** an illustrative 1 February–31 July 2027 stay is 181 calendar days counting both dates. “Six months” can overrun the initial 180-day permission. Follow your admission stamp; arrange a qualifying extension or depart earlier as applicable. Five-year visa validity does not mean five years’ continuous stay. Insurance should still cover every day, whether mandatory or not.

## Travel insurance vs health insurance, in plain English.

### Travel medical: repair the unexpected trip problem.

SafetyWing Essential, Genki Traveler and World Nomads are principally about new illness/injury while travelling. They can pay for a hospital stay and some urgent outpatient treatment. That does not mean routine asthma reviews or an asthma emergency are covered. Repatriation may end the cover and leave you needing healthcare at home.

### International health: support ongoing life abroad.

Native, Complete, Cigna and Allianz offer broader ongoing care, but the chosen tier, outpatient module and underwriting still matter. “Chronic conditions covered” does not automatically include a condition you already have. Check whether evacuation is included or a paid module; a local ambulance is not the same as cross-border evacuation.

For you, hospital care for an accepted asthma attack matters more than luggage cover or a large headline maximum. None of these policies is a substitute for a visa, compulsory local insurance or lawful residence.

## Your short pre-purchase checklist.

- **Lock the dates and residence facts.** Cover the entire trip from leaving the UK to returning, including travel days. Tell the insurer about both countries and any residence change. SafetyWing Essential’s 364-day period may leave a gap in a 365-day year; a new policy can reassess what is pre-existing.

- **Read the actual asthma endorsement.** It should address asthma attacks, hospital/ICU care, GP and specialist reviews, rescue/preventer inhalers and related evacuation. Check exclusions, loading and whether the same protection continues after moving to Portugal. “Approved application” without these details is not enough.

- **Match your budget to cash flow.** Ask for the total including modules, payment-frequency charges and taxes. An annual premium divided by 12 is not necessarily the monthly-direct-debit price. Keep the deductible and a pay-first outpatient buffer accessible.

- **Get the right certificate before a visa application.** It must identify you, insured dates, Portugal/Schengen territory as required, medical/hospital and repatriation cover. Check both medical transport and return of remains. Certificate acceptance is separate from the insurer’s acceptance of asthma.

- **Save the assistance number and approval rules offline.** A serious admission should be coordinated with the insurer as soon as practical. Direct billing is not guaranteed at every hospital; exclusions and advance-approval requirements still apply.

## Two useful things beyond the premium.

**Chiang Mai air quality makes the asthma issue more than paperwork.** FCDO specifically warns that seasonal smoke haze there can worsen asthma. Before departure, discuss your travel/asthma plan and access to your prescribed inhalers with your clinician. [D3](#source-d3)

**Keep public healthcare entitlement separate from your passport.** If you remain eligible, a UK GHIC can help with medically necessary public healthcare during a temporary Portugal stay, including pre-existing conditions. It does not cover private care or repatriation, and it is not a substitute for insurance. Eligibility cannot be assumed once your residence circumstances change. Registered Portuguese residents can register for SNS public healthcare; bridge the gap rather than cancelling private cover in anticipation. [D2](#source-d2) [D5](#source-d5)

Likewise, British citizenship alone does not guarantee free NHS hospital care on UK visits after moving abroad. Check your actual entitlement rather than assuming that repatriation solves everything. [D5](#source-d5)

## Questions you may still have

### If I pay for inhalers myself, is a cheap travel policy enough?

Not if it also excludes asthma attacks or related hospital care. Genki Traveler and SafetyWing Essential do not solve that gap. Separate the routine-prescription bill from the much larger risk of an excluded admission.

### Does “chronic conditions covered” mean my existing asthma is accepted?

No. The condition must be disclosed and accepted without an exclusion that defeats the protection you need. Ask for the actual endorsement covering attacks, admissions, inhalers, reviews and related evacuation in both countries.

### Does a Portuguese visa certificate settle my healthcare needs?

No. Entry paperwork, ongoing residence evidence and actual asthma protection are separate tests. None of these plans is confirmed visa-approved for this applicant.

### Can I use my GHIC instead of insurance?

Only if you remain eligible, and it still does not replace insurance. It can help with medically necessary public care during a temporary Portugal stay, but not private care or repatriation. Residence changes can change entitlement.
