You have a Tokyo offer and a deadline, and the question is whether to ask for the Highly Skilled Professional status or take the ordinary engineer status and move on. **Short answer: ask the employer to file the Certificate of Eligibility (COE) as HSP(i)(b), with the standard status (Engineer / Specialist in Humanities / International Services) as the fallback.** The Immigration Services Agency (ISA) says HSP can be applied for directly at the first COE stage, and that a COE for another work status can be issued instead if the points are not accepted. The catch: the score has no margin, and the salary sits just above a points line.

This guide follows Wei-Ting: 31, backend engineer, master's in computer science, 7 years of related work, JLPT N2, single, TWD 1.8M saved (NT$ figures below are approximate), with an offer of JPY 9.2 million a year. It was written on 8 November 2026 from public pages read that day. Nobody was contacted and nothing was submitted. **This is general information, not legal, tax or immigration advice.** The source materials are not published.

"Official" below means a Japanese government page that was read. **Unconfirmed** means the figure comes from a page that is not a Japanese government page, from our own estimate, or from a page that was not opened. A recomputation of every figure on this page found no arithmetic errors; that is a check of sums, not of the rules behind them.

## The recommendation and why

**Ask for HSP(i)(b); keep the standard status as the fallback.**

- **Points:** on the assumptions below the base score is **80 points**, or **90 points** with JLPT N2. 70 qualifies for HSP. 80 shortens permanent residence (PR) to 1 year of HSP stay instead of the normal 10 years.
- **Cost:** HSP costs nothing extra at the COE stage, because the COE has no fee.
- **The risk:** the 80 has no margin. The salary of JPY 9.2 million is only JPY 0.2 million above the JPY 9.0 million line; below that line the salary points fall from 35 to 30. Age points drop from 10 to 5 at 35. The N2 (+10) is your buffer, so have the certificate ready.
- **What to check first:** that the points rows below match the legal ordinance PDF (it could not be read; see the caveat in the points section), how ISA counts "annual salary" (**unconfirmed**), and your employer's category (unknown).
- **If the points are not accepted:** the standard status is the fallback, and a COE for it can be issued instead.

## HSP(i)(b) points: a worked example

The table used is ISA's official point-calculation form, sheet "B 高度専門職第１号ロ" (header "from 1 April 2023"; file modified 13 June 2026).

Assumptions (yours to confirm): master's degree (not an MBA/MOT); 7 years of work directly related to the job; offered annual salary JPY 9.2 million; age 30–34 when you apply; no patents, papers or Japanese degree; JLPT N2 held; employer not known to be on an innovation-support list.

| Item | Your case and band | Points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Academic background | Master's | 20 |
| Professional career | 7 years (band 7 to under 10) | 15 |
| Annual salary × age | JPY 9.2 million, age 30–34 → band 9–10 million | 35 |
| Age | 30–34 | 10 |
| Base total (no bonus items) | 20 + 15 + 35 + 10 | 80 |
| Japanese ability | JLPT N2 equivalent | +10 |
| Total with N2 | 80 + 10 | 90 |

**Other bonus rows you might have (none assumed):**

- JLPT N1 or a Japanese-language major: +15 instead of N2's +10. (Other cells in the same form: age 35–39 = 5 points; salary 8–9 million = 30.)
- Graduated from a listed university (top 300 in at least two of QS/THE/ARWU, or other lists): +10. The cell read says 10, but a second column in the same file says 5, so **check**.
- Employer on the innovation-promotion support list: +10. Recognised IT licence: +5 (one) or +10 (several).
- Graduated from a Japanese university: +10 (not your case).

**Sensitivities:**

- If ISA counts less than JPY 9.0 million as "annual salary" (for example if part of the pay is a discretionary bonus), the salary row is 30 and the total is 75 (85 with N2). That is still above 70, but below 80 without N2. How ISA defines salary is **unconfirmed**; ask the employer to confirm what goes on the salary line.
- At 35, the age row is 5. PR on the 1-year route needs 80 both one year before applying and on the application date, so N2's +10 matters.

**Caveat:** the legal ordinance PDF (modified 8 October 2026) and the amended bonus notice (31 March 2026) use vertical text that would not extract. The Excel form (modified 13 June 2026) was used instead, and it could not be cross-checked against the PDFs; a visual check is recommended. The English form says "30 or below" for the youngest age band; the Japanese form says "under 30" (Japanese governs). J-Skip, a separate fast track introduced on 21 April 2023, was judged irrelevant at JPY 9.2 million, but its thresholds were not read (**unverified**).

## HSP(i)(b) against the standard status

| Point | Standard: Engineer / Specialist in Humanities / International Services | HSP(i)(b) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Period of stay | 5 years, 3 years, 1 year or 3 months; depends on employer category and record | 5 years, uniformly |

These rows come from ISA pages on preferential treatment, residence status and the PR guideline. The spouse-work entry for the standard status and the "not generally" entry for parents are our summary, not read on a page: **unconfirmed**. You are single, so the spouse and parent rows matter only in future. Two other changes: the HSP landing notification and residence guideline were abolished in March 2026, and from 15 April 2026 category 3/4 employers must add a declaration, and language proof for language-heavy roles, which is unlikely to apply to backend engineering.

## The COE, the visa and arrival

- **COE:** the employer (or an authorised agent) files it at the immigration office covering the employer's location. Online filing is available; there is no mail-in; there is **no fee**; standard processing is **1–3 months**. For HSP you supply the points sheet and evidence (degree, employment letters, JLPT result, contract). The employer has not been contacted.
- **E-COE:** it can be received by email (official). Showing it on a phone at the visa counter was not confirmed on the ISA page: **unconfirmed**.
- **Visa in Taipei:** Japan has no embassy in Taiwan. The Japan–Taiwan Exchange Association's Taipei office takes visa applications: passport, form, photo, ID card and COE; in principle 5 working days; no appointment. The fee is about JPY 15,000 for a single entry from 1 July 2026 (**unconfirmed**; the Exchange Association is not a Japanese government page, and the Taiwan-dollar fee table was not captured).
- **After landing:** register your address at the municipal office within 14 days, with the residence card (official).
- **Timing for an April 2027 start (our inference, unconfirmed):** ask the employer to file between December 2026 and January 2027. That covers the 1–3 month standard window in case HSP processing is slower than the 10-day target, and then 5 or more working days for the visa.
- **Not confirmed on an official page:** COE validity (commonly 3 months) and issuing the residence card at the airport. Visa-exempt entry for Taiwan passport holders is short-stay only, with no work (Exchange Association FAQ, **unconfirmed**).
- **Residence card, My Number, resident tax:** the residence card is expected on landing at a major airport (**unconfirmed**). My Number comes after address registration; no official page on it was read (**unconfirmed**), and employers commonly ask for it for payroll and insurance (**unconfirmed**). Resident tax is charged on prior-year income to people living at the address on 1 January (official). Arriving in April 2027, you are not resident on 1 January 2027, so there is no resident tax for 2027. The first bill comes in June 2028 (our inference from the 1 January rule), based on your 2027 income (about 9 months of pay).

## Income tax and social insurance: a monthly example

Assumptions: JPY 9.2 million paid as 12 equal months of JPY 766,667 (no bonus); employer enrolled in Kyokai Kenpo (Tokyo) and employees' pension; age under 40; no dependants. These are FY2026 rates. FY2027 rates (for April 2027) are not published yet (**unconfirmed for 2027**).

| Line | Calculation | JPY / month |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gross pay | 9,200,000 ÷ 12 (rounded) | 766,667 |
| Health insurance (your half) | Grade 38 = 750,000 × 4.925% = 36,937.5 → 36,937 (0.50 or below rounds down) | 36,937 |
| Child-support levy | 750,000 × 0.115% = 862.5 → 862 | 862 |
| Employees' pension (kosei nenkin, your half) | Capped at grade 32 = 650,000 × 9.15% | 59,475 |
| Employment insurance | 766,667 × 0.5% (rate seen only in a search summary: **unconfirmed**) | 3,833 |
| Social insurance total | 36,937 + 862 + 59,475 + 3,833 | 101,107 |
| Pay after social insurance | 766,667 − 101,107 | 665,560 |
| Income-tax withholding | NTA 2027 monthly table, row 665,000–668,000, no dependants | 56,850 |
| Take-home before resident tax | 766,667 − 101,107 − 56,850 | 608,710 |

On these assumptions the monthly take-home before resident tax is **JPY 608,710**. The health insurance rate is 9.85% in Tokyo (4.925% your half); nursing-care insurance (1.62%) applies only from age 40. Pension is 18.3%, split 9.15% each. Sources: the Kyokai Kenpo Tokyo table and the NTA withholding table (official).

Withholding of 56,850 × 12 = 682,200 is about 12,800 above 669,400, so a small year-end adjustment refund is likely in a full year. Rule changes: the 2026 tax reform raised the basic deduction to 620,000 for incomes like yours; from 2027 the reconstruction surtax falls to 1.1% and a 1.0% defence surtax starts, so the combined rate stays 2.1% (official).

**2027 part-year (April to December, 9 months).** Income 6,900,003. Deduction 6,900,003 × 10% + 1,100,000 = 1,790,000 (rounded), leaving 5,110,003. Social insurance 101,107 × 9 = 909,963 and the basic deduction 620,000 leave taxable income of 3,580,000. Tax 3,580,000 × 20% − 427,500 = 288,500; × 1.021 = 294,500. Withholding over 9 months is 56,850 × 9 = 511,650, so a year-end adjustment refund of about 217,150 is plausible in December 2027 (our estimate; not included in the budget below).

**Resident tax (first bill June 2028).** 10% of taxable income + 5,000 per-capita (Tokyo 23 wards, including 1,000 forest tax) − at least 2,500 adjustment credit (official). The basic deduction is assumed at 430,000 (**unconfirmed**). For the 2027 part-year: (5,110,003 − 909,963 − 430,000) = 3,770,040 → 3,770,000 × 10% = 377,000 − 2,500 + 5,000 ≈ 379,500, which is about 31,600 a month from June 2028. A full year of JPY 9.2 million would be roughly 563,100 (about 46,900 a month). This bill starts right after your first 12 months, so keep savings aside for it.

## If you leave within a few years

**Lump-sum withdrawal payment (脱退一時金).**

- **Who:** a non-Japanese person with at least 6 months of employees' pension, who has left Japan and claims within 2 years of leaving (official). Claiming wipes out the earlier pension periods (official).
- **Formula:** average standard remuneration × payment rate. For 36–41 months the rate is 3.3. Example, 36 months at the pension cap: 18.3% × ½ × 36 = 3.294 → 3.3; 650,000 × 3.3 = **JPY 2,145,000** (about NT$444,000 at 0.2072).
- **Tax:** 20.42% is withheld: 2,145,000 × 20.42% = 438,009 (about NT$90,800). A refund is possible by filing a return through a tax representative named before you leave. Our rough refund estimate is about 413,900, **unconfirmed** and dependent on how the retirement-income calculation applies.
- **Cap:** currently 60 months (rate 5.5). The law raising it to 8 years was promulgated on 20 June 2025, but the start date is set by cabinet order within 4 years; no April 2027 date was found. The same law bars claiming while a re-entry permit is valid (official). **Next step:** re-check the cap at departure.
