If you want a working year in Chiang Mai without organising your life around border runs, **apply for the DTV workcation visa from Germany before moving**. It expressly includes freelancers. LTR work-from-Thailand is not a fit for the facts here: several overseas clients are not the qualifying overseas employer it requires. Your income alone does not establish approval for either visa.

This plan is for Jonas, 38, a German freelance UX designer earning approximately €70,000/year from EU clients, spending most of 2027 in Chiang Mai and possibly staying through 2028. First clarify whether that figure means turnover or personal income/profit, how much savings is accessible, and what German home and insurance arrangements remain. Those answers matter more than choosing the cheapest visa fee.

**Working from Thailand ordinarily makes those design fees Thai-source income, even when EU clients pay into a German bank. Keeping the money offshore is not a tax escape for work performed in Chiang Mai.** Set aside tax funds and get German–Thai advice before giving up your flat, deregistering or moving the business.

Rules and fees below reflect the **November 7, 2026** information, not a promise about 2027–2028. Two dated changes already matter:

- From **August 31, 2026**, DTV supporting documents include permanent-residence evidence and criminal-record clearance. A tourist visit to a neighbouring country is not a reliable application plan.
- From **September 15, 2026**, the published German ordinary-passport exemption is **30 days**, tourism only, with one possible extension of up to 30 days. Older “60-day exemption” advice is not the basis for this move.

## Get the DTV documents ready while you are still in Germany

Use the current checklist for the mission responsible for your German state. Berlin is the documented baseline here; Jonas’s state is not specified. Its DTV1 list does not set a minimum annual income, large-employer requirement or health-insurance certificate. Age 38 presents no identified checklist issue.

Prepare:

1. Passport biodata page; Berlin’s general entry guidance requires at least 6 months’ passport validity on entry.
2. A photograph taken within the last 6 months.
3. Permanent-residence proof in Germany: for German citizens, both sides of Personalausweis **or** a German Meldebescheinigung. General e-Visa guidance also asks for identity/residence uploads; prepare both where available.
4. Last 3 months’ statements from one or more banks showing at least **THB 500,000** or equivalent, or sponsorship letter(s). This is evidence, not a payment to Thailand. Keep a currency buffer. Do not rely on a last-minute balance screenshot; exact treatment of fluctuating balances needs the mission’s confirmation.
5. Home-country employment contract/certificate **or** a professional portfolio showing remote/freelance status. For Jonas, add EU-client contracts, recent invoices and matching receipts to a UX portfolio. That supporting bundle is sensible preparation, not a separately mandated six-month income test.
6. Criminal-record clearance from the nationality or application country—Germany here. Berlin’s sheet does not specify a certificate-age limit. Check your mission’s current age, language and certification rules; do not import London’s 6-month limit into a German application.

Apply through [Thai e-Visa](https://thaievisa.go.th/). You **must be physically in Germany to submit and pay**; Berlin **strongly advises remaining there until the decision** because an interview may be requested. The German jurisdiction split is Berlin for its listed northern/eastern states, Frankfurt for Hesse, North Rhine-Westphalia, Rhineland-Palatinate, Saarland and Thuringia, and Munich for Bavaria and Baden-Württemberg. Check the procedure page rather than assuming Berlin overrides another mission.

Submission opens **90 days before intended arrival**. Berlin states **15 working days** after submission/payment and another **15 working days** after all requested additional material is complete and correct. Allow **6–8 weeks** as a planning buffer, not guaranteed service. Fees are non-refundable. Use clear scans of originals: screenshots of financial statements, flight bookings and hotel bookings are not accepted. Additional documents or an interview remain possible. Avoid non-refundable travel commitments before approval.

Already in Thailand? Do not file a German application as though you were still in Germany. Extend an existing DTV locally, or plan a lawful departure and application where the post accepts your permanent-residence evidence. Do not surrender German residence and then present an old registration as current proof.

**Keep work genuinely overseas-facing.** DTV’s published workcation purpose includes remote work/freelancing, but a blanket statutory work-permit exemption for every arrangement has **not been verified**. Before accepting Thai clients, local paid work or a mixed Thai/foreign arrangement, get case-specific labour/immigration advice and the appropriate visa/work-permit route, commonly Non-B plus permission. Paying Thai tax does not legalise unauthorised work.

## Why LTR does not become suitable just because income rises

The employment test is separate from the income test. BOI requires a full overseas employment contract with a listed company; or a private company operating at least **3 years**, with combined revenue of at least **USD 50 million over the last 3 years**; or an eligible wholly-owned subsidiary with corporate/ownership evidence. Being a freelancer serving those companies does not make you their employee.

Two BOI wording conflicts need resolution for borderline applicants. The summary uses USD 50 million while the detailed checklist says **more than** USD 50 million. The summary’s subsidiary ownership direction also differs from the detailed checklist, which requires the qualifying listed/private company to be the subsidiary’s **100% shareholder**. Ask BOI to confirm subsidiary cases and exact-threshold cases. Do not use older USD 150 million or mandatory 5-year work-experience summaries, or assert an unverified 2026 commencement date for the changed criteria.

Standard income is average **personal income USD 80,000/year in the past 2 years**. The **USD 40,000–under 80,000** route additionally needs a master’s or equivalent/higher qualification, full IP ownership evidence, or Series A funding of at least **USD 1 million**, as specified in the detailed checklist. The summary illustrates a master’s but does not list every alternative. None removes the employer requirement.

If actual employment changes, the application would need passport biodata and chronological Thai-stamp pages, at least **6 months’ validity and 2 blank pages**, a recent white-background formal photograph, relevant TDAC/stay records, CV and past **2 years’** official personal tax returns. Add the qualification evidence if using the lower-income route, full employment contract, signed position/start/end-date letter, remote-work permission issued no more than **3 months** before submission, corporate listing/audited revenue and ownership evidence, and health/social-security/deposit documents. Non-English/Thai material needs certified or notarised English/Thai translation. Police clearance can be requested case-by-case: home/residence-country letter no older than **3 months**, or Thai police certificate.

For insurance, the detailed list requires Thai hospitalisation/treatment cover of at least **USD 50,000**, with at least **10 months remaining**; a group policy must provide that amount per person. Travel-only and life-only insurance are rejected. Alternatives are current Thai SSO benefits or at least **USD 100,000** deposited in your name and maintained at least **12 months**. Keep qualifying conditions throughout the visa; a German EHIC is not Thai SSO evidence.

BOI’s online endorsement takes **20 working days** from complete valid documents, longer with queries, then **1–3 working days** pre-approval. Issue within **60 days** of the endorsement letter overseas or at TIESC Bangkok. Maintain lawful stay; do not cancel an existing visa before case-specific instructions. A pending application grants no general interim stay or work right. This LTR category has no Thai digital work permit and cannot cover Thai-employer part-time work. Other categories’ privileges are not transferable.

## Your work location matters more than the bank account

**180 days or more**, aggregated in a calendar year, makes you Thai tax resident under Revenue Code §41. Exactly 180 qualifies. Count **2027 and 2028 separately**; border trips do not reset the year. This is not the treaty’s 183-day test.

Thai-source work is a separate question. Section 41 and the Revenue Department’s guidance cover work performed/business carried on in Thailand whether paid inside or outside the country. Ordinary design services performed from Chiang Mai are therefore ordinarily Thai-source even with EU payers and a German receiving bank. **Fewer than 180 days does not itself exempt Thai-source work.** This is the application of the sourcing rules to the stated facts, not an individual Revenue Department ruling; treaty relief needs separate review.

For genuinely foreign-source income, P161/P162 and RD guidance apply a different rule. Assessable income arising from **January 1, 2024 onward**, earned in a year when you were Thai resident, is taxable when brought into Thailand, even in a later year. Waiting until the next year is not a general escape. Foreign income arising before 2024 and genuine foreign income earned in a nonresident year are outside that rule. Existing capital is not automatically income: trace it rather than treating every offshore balance as taxable or tax-free.

Separate records for pre-2024 capital, nonresident-year earnings, resident-year foreign income, Thai-performed services and later transfers. Document mixed accounts and partial remittances. Design done in Germany before moving needs a foreign-source/earning-year analysis; design done in Chiang Mai needs the Thai-source analysis even if retained offshore.

LTR is not a freelancer tax holiday. Royal Decree **743 §5**, with Notification **427** and continued qualifying conditions, exempts specified LTR categories’ qualifying foreign employment/business/property income brought into Thailand. Its English translation refers to income derived in the **previous tax year**; same-year receipts and particular remote-employment facts need adviser review. It does not relabel Thai-performed freelance work as foreign-source. No equivalent DTV concession is established here. The **17%** concession concerns qualifying highly-skilled employment, not DTV or every LTR holder.

### Do not rely on a generic “no Thai permanent establishment” shortcut

The official RD-hosted Germany–Thailand treaty text’s **Article 14** expressly covers personal services, including a liberal profession, and permits taxation where services are rendered. Its residence-state-only exception needs **all three**:

- Presence not exceeding **183 days** in the other state in the fiscal year.
- Payment by/on behalf of a payer not resident there.
- Remuneration not borne by that payer’s permanent establishment there.

A German treaty resident working most of the year in Thailand cannot rely on EU clients alone. **Article 7(5)** preserves the specific-income rules; a generic freelancer/fixed-base or no-PE shortcut misses Article 14.

If both countries’ domestic residence tests apply, **Article 4** goes through permanent home → centre of vital interests → habitual abode → nationality → competent-authority agreement. For ordinary qualifying Thai-taxable service income of a **German treaty resident**, **Article 22(2)(a)** generally gives German exemption with progression, not a universal automatic credit. Royalties and other categories can differ. Do not simply deduct every German tax payment from Thai tax; permitted credits depend on the treaty and are capped to attributable Thai tax, with supporting payment/source evidence.

**Current treaty amendments and MLI consolidation have not been exhaustively established.** Have an adviser check the operative treaty, German override provisions, actual Thai treatment and service/IP classification before claiming relief.

Both the reported “earning-year or following-year remittance exemption” and the claimed “worldwide taxation without remittance from 2027” remain **unconfirmed proposals here**. Recheck the actual instrument and commencement before moving and before filing; neither is used as law in this plan.

Reserve tax funds until the German–Thai allocation and relief are agreed.

## Decide the German home and health-cover questions before deregistering

Keeping an available German dwelling can preserve ordinary unlimited German tax liability under **EStG §1 and AO §8**. Spending more than 183 days abroad does not automatically end it. A postal address and a usable flat are different; registration is evidence, not the test. **AO §9** adds a separate habitual-abode test. Domestic liability and treaty residence can differ.

Genuinely giving up dwelling availability and German habitual abode can end ordinary unlimited liability. **Abmeldung alone is not enough** if the reality stays unchanged. German-source income may still create limited liability under **EStG §49**, including independent work exercised/exploited there or a retained German facility, subject to treaty limits. EU-client location alone settles neither source nor relief. Record the relocation date, home-access arrangements, business facilities and return-work days.

Before changing arrangements, ask about split-year residence, service/royalty treatment, conditional **AStG §2** expanded limited liability and **EStG §50d** relief conditions. This does not mean Thailand is automatically “low tax” or that Jonas meets the emigrant rule. If company shareholdings or transferred business assets exist, ask separately about departure taxes; none is stated here.

For health cover, answer three separate questions:

1. **Do German membership and contributions continue?** Address retention or deregistration does not decide this alone. DVKA directs relevant self-employed non-agreement cases to domestic Ausstrahlung rules and the existing Krankenkasse. A time-limited assignment can differ from relocating the practice; continuing German rules can also coexist with host-country obligations. Get a written assessment before cancelling contributions.
2. **Who pays for treatment in Thailand?** GKV benefits generally suspend abroad, with exceptions. DVKA’s agreement-country list does not include Thailand; EHIC is not ordinary Thai treatment cover. Paying German contributions does not itself buy comprehensive Chiang Mai care.
3. **Does a long-stay policy protect the whole move and return?** Check residence in Thailand, freelance remote work and the actual **2027–2028** duration, inpatient/outpatient care, pre-existing conditions/exclusions, deductibles, evacuation/repatriation, renewal and German visits. If already PKV- or KSK-insured, get that scheme’s assessment. There is no universal **two-year** KSK rule for every non-European country. Continuation, Anwartschaft and German return/re-admission need insurer-specific answers; no premium or automatic return entitlement is promised.

For DTV, ED and exemption, no insurance document on a visa list is not sensible protection for a working year. Even LTR’s USD 50,000 visa minimum does not prove the policy protects your health and German return position adequately.

- **November 7–14, 2026:** choose a provisional arrival date and filing mission. Clarify income/profit, accessible savings/history, clients/IP, German home/business access and GKV/PKV/KSK status. Forecast days separately for 2027 and 2028. Do not invent a financial history if the required evidence is missing; delay and confirm what is acceptable.
- **During November 2026:** arrange German–Thai tax advice and written insurer determinations before Abmeldung, giving up the flat or changing the business. Resolve source, treaty residence, German exposure, Thai filing/VAT and return cover.
- **Before submission:** assemble the DTV1 evidence and criminal clearance under your mission’s current conditions. Recheck rules and fees. Submit/pay in Germany within 90 days of arrival, ideally allowing 6–8 weeks; remaining until decision is strongly advised. For a January 2027 move, November preparation/submission can fit, depending on dates and financial history.
- **After approval:** verify passport details and validity, activate suitable health cover and complete the agreed German changes truthfully. Keep accessible copies of funds/work/accommodation evidence. If approval is missing, postpone the working move rather than relabel it as tourism.
- **Within 3 days before each entry:** complete the free official [TDAC](https://tdac.immigration.go.th/). It is not a visa; do not pay imitation sites.
- **On arrival:** check the actual admitted-until stamp. Arrange residence notification/TM30 with your accommodation provider and confirm the local reporting workflow. Diary any 90-day reports, the first 180-day deadline and tax days. LTR annual reporting is a different regime.
- **Well before the first stay expires:** confirm Chiang Mai’s then-current extension documents, financial seasoning, fee and processing time. Apply for the one discretionary up-to-180-day extension or leave on time; keep lawful fallback travel available. Re-entry is not guaranteed.
- **Throughout 2027 and before 2028 filing:** retain work-location days, contracts, expenses, invoice/payment dates and capital/income transfers. Coordinate Thai returns and German declarations/relief. If remaining in 2028, recheck laws, cover renewal, German ties and immigration dates before continuing.

**Day-count example, not permission:** entering **January 15, 2027**, with entry day as day 1, makes **July 13, 2027** day 180. A full further 180 days reaches day 360 on **January 9, 2028**. Staying January 15–December 31, 2027 without departures is **351 days** in 2027, above the domestic 180-day threshold. Follow the actual Immigration stamp, not this illustration: 360 days is not a full 365-day year.

## What still needs an answer before you commit

- **Extension fee:** THB 1,900 for DTV/ED/exemption is not directly primary-verified. The cost totals using it are conditional allowances only.
- **Work-permit scope:** no blanket statutory DTV exemption was verified. Thai-client/local arrangements need individual labour advice.
- **Local procedure:** exact 2027 Chiang Mai DTV/ED extension and ED-conversion eligibility, documents, fee, lead time, financial seasoning and reporting workflow are unresolved. Do not rely on a Bangkok checklist or a universal private-language-school year. Berlin ED3 asks for passport, recent photo, location evidence, tuition/living funds, relevant education/sports authority letter and signed enrolment with the signer’s ID/passport. Ordinary ED is distinct from degree-level ED Plus and needs a re-entry permit to preserve its stay.
- **Future law and personal facts:** check reforms, treaty/MLI consolidation, income allocation, German dwelling/business ties, exact travel dates, savings and policy terms. Degree/IP/funding and employment reality matter if LTR is reconsidered. A reliable personal tax bill cannot be derived from €70,000 alone.
