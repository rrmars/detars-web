**Worried that five days will disappear into transfers? Stay in My Khe / An Thuong for all nights.** Give Marble Mountains one morning, Hoi An its own afternoon and lantern evening, and the clearest safe day to **Hai Van Pass and Lang Co by private car**. Budget **VND 10,223,500 / AUD 601.38 for two adults**, including a 15% reserve but **not accommodation, flights, insurance or visas**. For late September 2027, treat beach time as a weather-dependent bonus. You can plan a good trip without promising sunny swimming days.

Use this five-day Da Nang plan to balance My Khe, Hoi An, Marble Mountains and Hai Van Pass, with a shared AUD budget and clear rain and ticket checks.

For a first 5 day visit, decide what matters most: beach time, a Hoi An evening or the Hai Van Pass drive. September weather makes that choice—and some flexibility—more useful than packing every day.

This plan is for **two adults travelling from Sydney in late September 2027** on ordinary Australian passports. Count five days **from arrival to departure, usually four nights**, not five full sightseeing days. Add your exact dates and flight times before using the timetable. The information was checked **November 5, 2026**; November 4 is the folder date. Selected official pages were checked again **November 5, 2026**, but you still need to confirm September 2027 availability and current rules. No hotel or transport here has been personally tested. ## Pick one base and keep your beach windows open

Choose a quiet side street near My Khe beach or An Thuong, ideally a 5–10-minute walk from the sand. Look for indoor common space, a reliable lift and flexible cancellation. You can then use a dry beach window without moving luggage. Ask hotels for rooms and prices on your dates; no specific hotel or future room offer is confirmed here.

Choose the Han River instead if city food and museums matter more than walking to the beach. Hoi An suits lantern-focused stays, but splitting these four nights adds transfers and seasonal old-town flood risk. If you add a fifth night, price the extra accommodation and spending separately; the five-day total does not include it automatically.

## Leave room for September rain

[Vietnam Tourism's climate guide](https://vietnam.travel/things-to-do/weather-and-climate-vietnam/), checked November 5, describes Hoi An's rainy period from September and central-coast storms and flooding. [JMA's Da Nang normals](https://www.data.jma.go.jp/tcc/tcc/products/climate/climatview/graph_mkhtml_nrm.php?e=7&k=0&m=9&n=48855&r=1&s=1&y=2026) give September **27.9 °C mean temperature and 410.4 mm monthly rainfall**. That helps you pack and plan; it cannot say which of your five days will be wet. Check a real forecast near departure rather than treating climate averages as a September 2027 forecast.

- **Ordinary rain:** move the Cham Museum into Day 2's mountain slot; use cafes and short food stops. Reassign museum admission rather than counting it twice.
- **Slippery steps/heavy rain:** postpone Marble Mountains. Do not force the caves to keep a timetable.
- **Hoi An flood warning or water in streets:** postpone/cancel, rather than use a lantern boat as a workaround.
- **Poor pass visibility, strong rain, landslide/typhoon warning:** cancel the scenic drive and remain local as advised. A cable-car mountain trip is not the safer substitute.
- **Unsafe sea flags:** no swimming. In severe-weather instructions, even a beach walk or travel to a museum may be inappropriate; follow local directions and closures.

Check forecasts 7–10 days before leaving and daily locally through the [National Centre for Hydro-Meteorological Forecasting](https://www.nchmf.gov.vn/kttv/). Ask your hotel to help interpret alerts. This is a place to check near travel, not a forecast already checked for your future dates. Keep driver plans flexible and read disruption and insurance terms before paying.

## Food and entry essentials

For food ideas, the [official Da Nang guide](https://vietnam.travel/things-to-do/foodie-guide-da-nang/) suggests Bà Vị for mì Quảng at 166 Lê Đình Dương; Bà Hờn for bún chả cá at 113/3 Nguyễn Chí Thanh; Bà Dưỡng for bánh xèo at 280/23 Hoàng Diệu; and avocado ice cream at Bắc Mỹ An market. **Check opening before making a special trip; these are guide suggestions, not personally tried restaurants.** Allow noodles 35–70k/person, bánh mì 25–45k and coffee 25–60k. Those are estimates, not menu quotes. A seafood feast can exceed 700k/day for two, so agree the weight, price/kg and cooking charge before ordering.

With an ordinary Australian passport, arrange an approved visa rather than assuming a short stay is exempt. [Vietnam Tourism's entry page](https://vietnam.travel/plan-your-trip/visa-requirements/), checked November 5 and marked updated 2026, leaves Australia off its exemption lists. It gives single-entry USD 25, six-month passport validity, two blank pages and 3–5 working days processing. Leave extra correction time; that is not a deadline guarantee. Use the [official e-visa portal](https://evisa.gov.vn/) and match your names, approved dates and real route. Da Nang Airport is on the retrieved border list, but connecting internationally elsewhere can change your entry point. Before applying and departing, recheck the rules and any September 2027 digital pre-arrival requirements.
