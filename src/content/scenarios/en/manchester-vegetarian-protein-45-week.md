Worried that reaching 120 g of protein means expensive powders and a crowded student kitchen? **This ovo-lacto vegetarian week averages about 125 g protein and 2,598 kcal a day. Whole-pack shopping comes to £23.65, leaving £21.35 of a £45 food budget. Protein powder is not needed.** The harder part is finding safe fridge and freezer space.

This is a starting menu for a 21-year-old Manchester student training four times a week, with a planned January 2027 start. Prices were seen on November 6, 2026. They are public UK listing examples, not confirmed Manchester branch stock, local checkout prices or January quotes. Peanut butter uses an older cached price whose freshness remains unconfirmed. Delivery, travel, equipment and cooking fuel are extra.

The calorie and protein totals are planning estimates, not a personal assessment of your energy needs or proof of a nutritionally complete diet. Allergens include milk, egg, wheat/gluten and peanut. Check every label, including spice blends.

## Check the kitchen before you shop

Allow roughly 4–5 litres of clear freezer space for five flat sauce portions and sliced bread, with room for cold air. That is an estimated fit, not a measured drawer. Use a proper freezer around −18°C, not just an ice compartment. Dry-fit your containers and bread first.

Your fridge shelf needs space for yoghurt, one open milk carton, eggs, sealed cheese, carrots and two sauce boxes. Keep unopened UHT milk in a cupboard. Buy the second yoghurt tub on Saturday so both tubs do not need shelf space at once.

If the food will not fit safely, reduce the Sunday batch and make the remaining sauce fresh midweek. Allow about 30–40 extra minutes. Do not replace freezer space with a week of refrigerated leftovers.

## Two Sunday pots

You need two roughly 2–3 litre pans, two hob rings, scales, a chopping board, sieve, spoon and seven small shallow containers or suitable freezer bags. No oven or blender is needed.

### A: tomato and red-lentil curry — four portions

Use 320 g dry red lentils, 800 g chopped tomatoes with juice, 280 g prepared onion, 120 g prepared carrot, 60 g weighed oil, 8 g curry powder and about 500 ml water, with extra splashes as needed.

1. Rinse the lentils. Chop the onion and grate or finely dice the carrot.
2. Soften onion and carrot in the oil for 6–8 minutes. Stir in curry powder for 30 seconds.
3. Add lentils, tomatoes and water. Bring to the boil, then simmer gently for 25–30 minutes, stirring and adding water if needed. Follow the lentil pack instructions and cook until fully soft.
4. Keep the sauce fairly thick to save space. Weigh the finished batch and divide equally into four portions. Cooking water loss varies; do not assume a fixed cooked yield.

Each sauce portion provides about 465 kcal and 24.2 g protein before rice.

### B: peanut, kidney-bean and lentil stew — three portions

Use 120 g dry red lentils, 480 g drained canned kidney beans, 400 g chopped tomatoes with juice, 210 g prepared onion, 90 g prepared carrot, 30 g peanut butter, 30 g weighed oil, 6 g curry powder and about 150 ml water, adding more as needed.

1. Rinse lentils. Drain and rinse the beans. **Use ready-cooked canned beans, not dry kidney beans.** They need different safety preparation.
2. Soften onion and carrot in the oil for 6–8 minutes. Add curry powder, then lentils, tomatoes and water.
3. Simmer for 20–25 minutes, stirring and adding water until the lentils are soft. Stir in peanut butter and beans.
4. Simmer for another 5–8 minutes until hot throughout, following the pack heating instructions. Weigh and divide equally into three portions. Check the tin’s drained weight and update quantities if it differs.

Each sauce portion provides about 505 kcal and 29.6 g protein before rice.

### Leave time to cool safely

A possible Sunday slot is 18:00–18:20 for cleaning, labelling, weighing and chopping; 18:20–19:00 for cooking both pots; and 19:00–20:00 for portioning, cooling, cold storage and washing-up. It is a working estimate, not a timed kitchen test. Start cooling each pot as soon as it finishes, rather than waiting for the other.

Divide into shallow portions promptly. A cold-water bath can help. Cover and refrigerate or freeze within 1–2 hours of cooking. Do not put a large hot pan into the shared fridge.

## Keep the week safe, not just convenient

The [FSA student guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/student-guide-to-food-safety-and-hygiene/student-guide-to-food-safety-and-hygiene), [chilling and defrosting guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/how-to-chill-freeze-and-defrost-food-safely/how-to-chill-freeze-and-defrost-food-safely) and [rice advice](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/home-food-fact-checker/home-food-fact-checker) support these limits:

- **Sunday split:** refrigerate Monday A and Tuesday B only. Freeze the remaining three A and two B portions after rapid cooling. Label the food, actual cooking time, freezing date and intended day.
- **Count 48 hours from cooking, not calendar days.** If B finishes at 19:00 Sunday, eat Tuesday’s portion by 18:30, after 47½ hours. An earlier cooking finish means an earlier deadline. If dinner cannot meet it, freeze Tuesday’s portion on Sunday too, allow extra freezer space and thaw it Monday night. Never refrigerate all seven sauces for the week.
- **Check temperatures:** fridge 0–5°C and freezer around −18°C. Use a thermometer, leave airflow and keep sealed ready-to-eat food away from raw meat, fish and drips in the shared fridge. About 3 litres for sauces plus roughly 1 litre for bread is only a rough allowance; packaging and recipe water change the fit.
- **Thaw in a leakproof container in the fridge** the night before. Defrost fully and use within 24 hours of full thawing. Alternatively, microwave-defrost immediately before heating. Reheat only once until steaming hot throughout; stir and follow microwave standing instructions. Heat only the portion you will eat.
- **Rice needs its own rule.** Cook the 100 g dry portion fresh using the pack method, about 15–20 minutes, and eat immediately. If any remains, cool ideally within one hour. Refrigerate for no more than 24 hours or freeze promptly. Reheat once until steaming hot throughout. Reheating cannot be relied on to make badly stored rice safe.
- **Follow dairy labels and use-by dates.** Open yoghurt tub 1 on Monday and finish it Friday, within its five-day opening guidance. Open tub 2 on Saturday; use the carried 600 g early next week within both the opening limit and use-by date. This yoghurt is not suitable for freezing. Keep cheese refrigerated and airtight once opened. The saved milk and cheese instructions do not give a numerical after-opening life; check the purchased packs rather than assuming one.
- **Open one milk carton at a time.** Unopened UHT cartons stay in a cool, dry cupboard; opened milk must be refrigerated and treated as fresh milk. Keep eggs in their carton in the fridge and cook them fresh, rather than keeping a week of boiled eggs.
- **Take a cold lunch to campus.** Use an insulated bag with an ice pack or a campus fridge. Do not leave cheese and cooked egg in a warm backpack. Wash hands and carrots and use clean utensils. Empty leftover canned contents into a suitable container rather than storing an open tin in the fridge.

## What the nutrition totals can tell you

The sums use dry, raw or prepared ingredient weights and drained beans. Cooking water changes weight, not the planned ingredient energy. Label rounding, fruit size and cooking losses make these estimates rather than laboratory measurements. Cooking nutrient losses are not modelled.

Most inputs come from the identified supermarket labels. Banana flesh, dry lentils, drained kidney beans, onions and carrots use clearly labelled generic UK [CoFID composition values](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/60538b91e90e07527df82ae4/McCance_Widdowsons_Composition_of_Foods_Integrated_Dataset_2021..xlsx), not invented Aldi labels. Per 100 g, those inputs are respectively 81 kcal / 1.2 g protein, 311 kcal / 25.6 g, 100 kcal / 8.6 g, 35 kcal / 1.0 g and 34 kcal / 0.5 g. Aldi’s bean nutrition table did not make its drained basis clear, so it was not used for those sums.

Rice needs care too: its label links 75 g dry to a cooked serving of about 205 g, giving 270 kcal and 5.9 g protein. The model derives 360 kcal and about 7.867 g protein per 100 g dry from that serving. It does not multiply cooked-per-100-g nutrition by dry rice weight. The chosen yoghurt has 6.2 g protein per 100 g, not an assumed 10 g just because the name says Greek-style.

## Questions you may have

### Do I need protein powder for this week?

No. The base menu already reaches the protein target. Powder may be convenient when time or appetite is the issue, but the provisional comparison above does not make it the cheapest protein here.

### Can I keep all the Sunday cooking in the fridge?

No. Keep only the portions that will meet the chilled-leftover deadline there. Freeze the later sauces on Sunday, not after they have spent most of the week refrigerated.

### What if my freezer drawer is too small?

Make a smaller Sunday batch and cook the remaining sauce fresh midweek. Keep the same rapid-cooling and safe-storage limits; do not squeeze in hot containers or extend fridge time to make the plan fit.

### Does £16.86 mean I only need that much cash?

No. It is the value allocated to food used. You need £23.65 for the whole-pack basket at the listed prices, including the later yoghurt purchase. The remaining food is carry-over, not cash in your pocket.
