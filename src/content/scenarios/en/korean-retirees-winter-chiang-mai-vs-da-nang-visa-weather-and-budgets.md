You are retired in Busan, you want to spend December to March abroad each year starting in 2027–28, and you are not sure which city fits: the visa, the smoke, the hospitals and a pension of KRW 2,900,000 a month all pull in different directions. **Short answer: lean toward Da Nang, entering on a 90-day e-visa for each of you, then one short exit and re-entry. This is a judgement call, not a settled fact. Cost is a tie-breaker, not the reason.**

This guide follows Seo-yeon (63) and Jae-ho (66). The pension income (KRW 2,900,000 a month), savings (KRW 220 million) and ages are your own figures. It was written on 8 November 2026 from public web pages. Nothing was booked, applied for or sent, and nobody was contacted. "Official page" means we read it on a government, official-body, insurer or hospital page. "Unconfirmed" means it came only from news, law-firm blogs, listing sites, aggregators or search snippets. Several key rules could not be confirmed on an official page, and this guide says where. **It is general information, not legal, tax, immigration, insurance or medical advice.** The source materials are not published.

## The recommendation and what could change it

**Lean toward Da Nang, entering on a 90-day e-visa for each of you, then one short exit and re-entry.** The reasons, and the conditions that could reverse them:

- **Da Nang looks about 9–10% cheaper on our assumptions.** Four months comes to about **KRW 11.0–11.4 million** in Da Nang against **KRW 12.2–12.5 million** in Chiang Mai (see the budget below). About three-quarters of the gap (KRW 872,856 of KRW 1,141,306) comes from applying a reported 20–40% short-stay rent premium to Chiang Mai only. We found no equivalent figure for Da Nang, so the gap may be overstated. Without the premium, Chiang Mai is still about KRW 268,450 dearer. Only rent and the exchange rates have sources; the other lines are our planning allowances, and insurance is our own estimate.
- **Chiang Mai's smoke season falls at the end of your stay.** Reports say the burning season runs February to May and March is usually the worst month; in March 2026 Chiang Mai was ranked the world's most polluted city several times (**unconfirmed**). A 1 December to 31 March stay ends in that window. A 2026 open-burning ban was reported to start on 1 January (**unconfirmed**). We found no comparable smoke season for Da Nang, but we did not look up Da Nang air-quality data.
- **Da Nang entry is cheap and well documented.** Koreans get 45 days visa-free, confirmed by the Korean foreign ministry (official page). A single-entry e-visa lasts up to 90 days and costs USD 25 each (**unconfirmed**: seen only in a search snippet of the official portal). Government fees for two are USD 50 (about KRW 68,000).
- **Da Nang has more direct flights from Busan** (roughly 4–6 a day against 1–2 to Chiang Mai in November 2026, **unconfirmed**), and Vinmec Da Nang says it offers Korean interpreters (official page).
- **What could change this:**
  1. Weather is not a deciding factor. Da Nang's February snippets (about 21.7 °C average and about 19 mm of rain, **unconfirmed**) cannot be compared with Chiang Mai, because we have no December to February temperature or rain data for Chiang Mai, so we cannot say which city is warmer, cooler, wetter or drier in those months. The only Chiang Mai figures are for March (35.4 °C average high, **unconfirmed**).
  2. Vietnam's 45-day exemption for Koreans is scheduled to end on **14 March 2028**, inside your first winter, and renewal is only "to be considered" (official page). The e-visa route does not depend on it.
  3. If you choose Chiang Mai, a stay limited to December and January would stay out of the reported February to May window, but smoke was already reported on 2 January 2026 (74 µg/m³ in one subdistrict) and we have no official December or January averages (**unconfirmed**).

## Thailand's stay length for Koreans is unresolved

The Korean embassy in Thailand (a Korean foreign-ministry mission) says Korea falls under the 1981 Korea–Thailand visa-exemption agreement, so Koreans get **90 days per entry** after Thailand's 15 September 2026 reform, with no cap on land-border entries, though border checks may tighten (official page, Korean side only). The text came from the page title and metadata of a Facebook post behind a login wall; the post date is not shown.

We could **not** find the same statement on a Thai official page. The Royal Thai Embassy Seoul page (updated 30 December 2025, before the reform) still says 60 days, extendable to 90, and the Thai foreign ministry list would not load.

**Unresolved: two readings.** If the 60-day rule applied, 60 days plus a 30-day extension would give 90 days. Either way, a 122-day stay needs one exit and air re-entry, or a longer visa. Whether a 90-day entry can be extended by 30 more days is not confirmed anywhere we could read. **Next step:** confirm with the Royal Thai Embassy Seoul before booking.

## 2. Korean health insurance (NHIS), pension and travel insurance

**National Health Insurance while you are away**

- **The threshold is 3 months, not 1.** Since 8 July 2020, staying abroad 3 months or more suspends benefits and exempts or reduces premiums (the law is NHI Act Art. 54). Before that it was 1 month (official page).
- **How days count:** suspension starts the day after you leave and ends on the day you re-enter. NHIS's example: leave 8 July, re-enter 9 October = 3 months or more, so premiums are exempt or reduced; re-enter 8 October = under 3 months, so premiums are charged (official page). A 122-day trip clears this comfortably, but a trip of about 90 days is borderline.
- **You must file.** From 1 September 2026 regional subscribers, voluntary continuing subscribers and dependents can file online or in the 건강보험25시 app; workplace subscribers file through the employer. Phone 1577-1000 (official page).
- **Come home early and the suspension is cancelled**, with premiums billed retroactively (official page). Care that NHIS paid for while you were suspended is clawed back (official page).
- **Visiting Korea mid-winter:** for entries from 1 July 2025, suspension ends on the entry day and the 3-month clock restarts when you leave again. An older NHIS page describes a different 1-month rule for short visits. The two pages conflict (official pages, **unresolved**). **Next step:** ask NHIS before planning a mid-winter trip home.
- **Which case applies to you:** we do not know whether you are regional subscribers or one of you is a dependent. For a regional subscriber, the premium excludes the absent member's income and property points (official page); that reduces the household bill rather than necessarily making it zero.
- **Not found:** an official statement on whether NHIS reimburses treatment abroad. The suspension rules suggest it does not, so treat NHIS as **not** covering you abroad and verify before relying on it.

**National Pension**

- The National Pension Service's overseas-remittance page says it is for emigration, loss of nationality or a foreigner's return home, and that "staying overseas alone" is not a reason to apply (official page). Our reading, which the page does not state in words, is that a 4-month trip does not change anything: payments keep going to your Korean account.
- We found no official page on a duty to report a short stay or on survival confirmation. **Next step:** call the pension service (1355) if you want that in writing. The private annuity depends on the provider and was not researched.

**Travel or long-stay insurance for ages 63 and 66**

- Many Korean travel policies are short-stay (90 days or less) and long-stay products start at 3 months (**unconfirmed**). Hyundai Marine Direct's long-stay product runs 3 months to 1 year and excludes residence abroad (official page). **We could not get Korean insurer prices** without entering personal data.
- **SafetyWing Nomad Essential** (its own page, read 8 November 2026): USD 62.72 per 4 weeks for ages 10–39, USD 250,000 overall limit, **no cover for pre-existing conditions** (official page). The 60–69 price was not readable. A January 2026 blog gave USD 138.31 per 4 weeks for 60–69, when the base price was USD 45.56 (**unconfirmed**). Scaling that to today's base (138.31 × 62.72 ÷ 45.56) gives about USD 190, *our estimate, not SafetyWing's number*. A secondary source says the maximum age is 69 (**unconfirmed**); Jae-ho will be 67–68 that winter.
- **Example, 2 people, 120 days (120 ÷ 28 = 4.29 four-week periods):** low, using the scaled price, 2 × 190.4 × 4.29 ≈ USD 1,632 (about USD 1,630); high, if billed in 5 whole 28-day cycles, 2 × 5 × 190.4 ≈ USD 1,904 (about USD 1,900). At USD 1 = KRW 1,357 that is **about KRW 2.21–2.58 million**. Using the outdated January blog price instead (138.31) would give about USD 1,186, so treat USD 1,630–1,900 as the working range. This is an example, not a quote.
- Cigna Global, Allianz Care, World Nomads and Genki prices for your ages were not found.

### Compared with your income

- Income: KRW 2,900,000 × 4 = **KRW 11,600,000** over four months.
- Four months in Chiang Mai: KRW 12.16–12.53 million = **105–108%** of that income (about KRW 3.04–3.13 million a month, above your KRW 2.9 million).
- Four months in Da Nang: KRW 11.02–11.39 million = **95–98%** (about KRW 2.76–2.85 million a month).
- These figures cover only the trip. Your Busan housing, bills, NHIS premium share (partly reduced while you are away) and any other costs continue. At these levels, the trip would take nearly all of your pension income for the four months in Da Nang (95–98%) and more than all of it in Chiang Mai (105–108%), so Busan costs would have to come from savings (KRW 220 million) or be reduced by sub-letting or other savings. Repeating it each year is a decision about how fast you are willing to draw on savings.
- Insurance is the biggest swing factor: the low-to-high range is about KRW 366,000 (USD 270), and the missing Korean-insurer prices could move it more. The city gap is about KRW 1.14 million in either insurance case. About three-quarters of it (KRW 872,856, or 76%) comes from the assumed Chiang Mai short-stay rent premium, and the rest (about KRW 268,450) remains even without it.
