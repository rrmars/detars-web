**Worried that a good rental yield could still leave you paying out each month?** If you are a UK citizen employed in Saudi Arabia, looking at an AED1.2–1.5 million apartment to rent now and perhaps use later, **start your checks with MBL Residence among these six adverts—not with a deposit**. Its estimated unlevered net yield is 4.36%, but the assumed mortgage leaves **AED9,295.13 negative annual cash flow**. Beverly Boulevard is cheaper but advertised rented; MAG 214 has much weaker rental returns in this calculation. Do not assume a nonresident loan will cover half: at 50% LTV, every ready-home loan here is below ADCB's published AED750,000 minimum.

## Check ownership, lending and residence separately

The [UAE government ownership page](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/moving-to-the-uae/expatriates-buying-a-property-in-the-uae), read November 5, allows nonresident foreigners to own in **designated freehold areas**. Living in Saudi Arabia does not make a British passport holder a GCC national. [DLD's sale-registration requirements](https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/eservices/property-sale-registration/), checked again for this guide, accept a valid nonresident foreigner's passport and require developer e-NOC in freehold areas. You still need to verify your exact unit's title and mortgage eligibility; these general pages cannot clear it.

The community pages call [JLT](https://jlt.ae/jlt-frequently-asked-questions-faq) and [Arjan](https://www.dubaiholdingcommunities.ae/communities/arjan) freehold. The Nakheel/Dubai Holding JVC pages checked here confirm the community name, not foreign ownership for every unit. Use the [DLD property-status enquiry](https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/eservices/property-status-overview/property-status/) with the actual title and plot identifiers, rather than relying on a neighbourhood label.

Hoping the purchase will also give you residence? The [DLD Golden Visa requirements](https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/eservices/request-for-golden-visa-investor/) and [ICP guide](https://icp.gov.ae/en/services/uae-golden-residency/), read November 5, set an AED2m property threshold. This budget is AED500,000–800,000 short. With a higher budget, ask the authorities how mortgage equity, valuation and off-plan purchases count: DLD still refers to AED2m paid on mortgaged property, while other official wording allows approved-bank financing. DLD/GDRFA/ICP say ten years; the UAE portal says five for real estate. Resolve those conflicting official descriptions for your case; they have not been reconciled here. Other property-residence products are outside this guide.

The budget conservatively puts all 4% on the buyer by agreement; that is not the default legal split.

## Ask the bank before relying on this mortgage calculation

The [CBUAE mortgage regulations](https://rulebook.centralbank.ae/en/rulebook/regulations-regarding-mortgage-loans), checked again November 5, put **expatriate investment property at a maximum 60% LTV**, separate from first owner-occupation and off-plan. Buying for the first time does not make an investment your home. Off-plan maximum LTV is 50%, but that ceiling does not mean construction finance is available. A lender may be stricter and use its appraised value, not your asking price.

The [ADCB product page](https://www.adcb.com/en/personal/loans/home-loans/standard-mortgage-loan.aspx) states 50% nonresident LTV. Its [mortgage FAQ](https://www.adcb.com/en/get-in-touch/faqs/loans/mortgage-services) previously listed a 750,000 minimum loan, 15-year maximum term, maturity age 60 and relationship AUM of 200,000, with a documentation route for UK nationals. All five ready homes here cost below AED1.5m: at 50%, none reaches the minimum loan. A later request returned 403. The browser recovered rate text **but not the eligibility tables**. Ask for these conditions in writing before planning around them.

[HSBC's nonresident page](https://www.hsbc.ae/mortgages/non-resident/), checked again, advertises up to 60% and requires Premier/Private Bank status or account eligibility. You still need its acceptance of Saudi income. The example uses **50% LTV, 15-year repayment and 6.5% nominal interest**, HSBC-style fees and insurance estimates based on ADCB information. **This combination is not an available bank product or a promise you can obtain it.** More borrowing saves upfront equity but worsens the rental cash flow shown below.

You also need to meet CBUAE's 50% maximum debt-burden ratio, seven-times-annual-income expatriate cap, +2–4 percentage-point rate stress and own-funds down payment. LTV alone will not settle approval. The 25-year regulatory ceiling is not ADCB's nonresident offer. Age, salary and debts are missing here. Confirm acceptance of the Saudi employer and currency, alternatives to tax returns, valuation, project eligibility and how the relationship-asset requirement works.

For comparison, HSBC's dated tariff gave APRs “from 5.35%” variable and “from 4.31%” fixed, excluding insurance. Those are not the nominal rate used here or your personal nonresident quote. EIBOR checked at about 07:23 UTC on November 5 showed the **November 4 fixing**, 3M 4.316060%, value date November 6—not a November 5 fixing. Ask about your reset date, margin and floor. No new rate quote or application has been requested.

## Gross yield, net yield and cash in your bank account are different

Compare these **full-year estimates after occupancy settles, before personal tax**, not with the cash received in your purchase year or guaranteed rent. Gross yield is headline rent divided by price. NOI subtracts vacancy and operating allowances, not debt or life insurance. Net yield here divides NOI by **the full unlevered purchase cost including fees**. For mortgage cash flow, then subtract **principal, interest and life insurance**. Principal builds your equity but still leaves your bank account. Neither appreciation nor sale proceeds are counted.

Life insurance is approximated at 0.2208% of the opening loan annually (0.0184% monthly ×12), not an insurer's declining-balance bill. Special assessments are set to zero only because they are unpriced; ask about them. Service charges and the actual lease need particular care. MBL's communal bill already includes insurance and a reserve. Reconcile bank cover and private-unit repairs with those inclusions to avoid counting the same protection twice. Amounts use decimal half-up rounding; rounded components may differ from totals by a cent.

Helvetia and Skyhills show **Not estimated** for income, yield and cash flow; do not read that as zero rent. Art House has no 2027 rental income or operating yield here. Estimated fixed costs for a ready home do not tell you what rent it can earn.

The table gives annual cash after debt and life insurance, rounded to cents from all 15 Sensitivity rows. The downside combines **15% lower rent, 7.5% nominal interest and 20% higher building charges**. The rate-only checks use 8.5%/10.5%, against 6.5% base, all at 50% LTV. The conditional 60% case keeps base rent, rate and charges. You must still secure approval, meet the minimum loan and check affordability. MBL's NOI stays unchanged when only interest rises, while your mortgage cash flow worsens. Its break-even headline rent is **110,636.68/year**, not the assumed 100,000. Do not judge the mortgage by unlevered yield alone.

Add personal tax, currency and remittance costs, travel, sale/exit fees, price changes, major assessments, furnishing/setup and the opportunity cost of relationship assets where relevant. They are excluded here. The [FTA natural-person guidance](https://tax.gov.ae/en/taxes/corporate.tax/corporate.tax.topics/basis.of.taxation.natural.person.aspx) separates real-estate investment income from natural-person business activity, but **does not make every arrangement tax-free**. Check again if UK/Saudi residence changes or you use a different ownership or holiday-home structure. These tax pages have not been freshly checked for this guide.

## Off-plan is a staged obligation, not a small deposit with guaranteed takeout

Art House advertises Q4 2028 handover, with 50% during construction and 50% **after handover**, plus a separate 10% booking statement. Here, the booking payment sits **within** the construction 50%. Ask for the signed instalment dates and post-handover period; neither is supplied here.

Count the 4% registration budget **once**, not again as an “Oqood tax.” The 1,020 Oqood/admin allowance includes a possible 1,000 developer self-registration pass-through plus 20 knowledge/innovation fees. Ask who owes them; they are not automatically the buyer's statutory liability. The 4,720 future-title allowance is a cushion. No ready-sale trustee charge is added at booking. Payment timing is assumed, not a signed schedule. Do not count on mortgage takeout or 2027 rent in this plan.

The [DLD initial-sale guidance](https://backoffice.dubailand.gov.ae/en/eservices/request-to-register-the-initial-sale/) covers provisional Oqood registration, not completed title. [Law 8/2007, especially Articles 7/9/15](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2007/Law%20No.%20(8)%20of%202007.html) provides project-specific escrow protection and noncompletion procedures, not a guaranteed delivery date or immediate refund. Before paying, verify the approved project, named escrow payee, SPA milestones and issued provisional registration. Those checks have not been completed for this unit.

## Before a deposit: the proposed diligence sequence

Work through this checklist before you pay; **the checks have not been done for you**.

1. Verify exact unit/plot foreign-ownership status, title, seller authority, permit, encumbrances and official completion status. Resolve size/location conflicts.
2. Get written lender conditions for Saudi income/documents, age, existing debts, accepted valuation, net loan and actual nominal/APR/reset/insurance terms before relying on financing. Read deposit/default/refund conditions with independent advice.
3. Obtain current service-charge budget, unit chargeable area, bill and arrears clearance; distinguish reserve funds from additional assessments and owner cooling obligations.
4. For Beverly or any occupied unit, read Ejari, the actual rent, expiry and notices. Your assumed rental income is not the contract you inherit on transfer.
5. Want to live there later? Buying does not cancel the tenant's fixed-term rights. The [Law 26/2007 Article 28](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2007/Law%20No.%20(26)%20of%202007.html) and [Law 33/2008 amended Articles 25–26](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2009/Law%20No.%20(33)%20of%202008%20Amending%20Law%20No.%20(26)%20of%202007.html), read November 5, require own or first-degree-family use, proof that you own no suitable alternative, the lease-expiry process and at least twelve months' notice through a Notary Public or registered mail. After recovery under an own-use ruling, residential reletting to a third party is constrained for two years. Do not treat that as a guaranteed move-in date. Ask your adviser whether an existing notice transfers and how a dispute could affect your dates; those questions are unresolved here.
6. Replace allowances with itemised closing and running-cost quotes. Keep liquid reserves separate from purchase cost, then test lower rent and higher rates again. Check the applicable 2027 rules before proceeding.

## Questions to settle before you buy

**Does a 7% gross yield mean the mortgage pays for itself?** No. MBL's 7.33% gross example becomes a 4.36% unlevered net yield on the purchase cost, then negative 9,295.13 annual cash after the assumed loan and life payments. Rent is not guaranteed, so check what your actual financing leaves in the account.

**Is AED1.5m enough if it is my whole budget?** All six estimated unlevered commitments fit below it, but furnishing, currency costs, tax or assessments could change that. A mortgage brings setup costs. Reserves and any relationship assets sit outside the price. A zero budget gap in a calculation is neither an all-in quote nor bank approval.

**Can I buy a rented home now and move in whenever I want?** Do not plan on that. You inherit tenancy obligations and must follow the formal own-use recovery conditions. Get advice for the actual lease rather than relying on the advert's general availability date.
