If you are a registered nurse in Korea with about ₩25,000,000 saved, you may be wondering whether to go through a recruitment agency or do this on your own, how long the green card really takes, and what could go wrong with a contract. **Short answer: credential yourself and sit NCLEX on your own account before you sign anything. Start with New York. Treat any agency as a question about its contract terms, not its name. And plan for a long wait, because the green-card queue, not the exams, is the slow part.**

This guide follows Jiyeon, a Korean nurse with a BSN and an IELTS Academic score of 6.5 overall. It was prepared on 8 November 2026 from official pages read on 7 November 2026 and 8 November 2026. Anything that does not come from an official source is marked as an estimate or as unconfirmed. The exchange rate used throughout is ₩1,334 per US$1 (Federal Reserve H.10, 30 October 2026: 1,333.99). **This is general information, not legal or immigration advice.** The source materials are not published.

## The recommendation and why

**Do the credentialing and the NCLEX yourself, on your own account, before signing anything.** The fees are small: about $1,068 for credential verification, the New York licence fee and the exam (that is $425 + $143 + $350 + $150, and it assumes you use TruMerit verification and the NCLEX registration fee in force from 1 February 2027). A passed NCLEX plus a VisaScreen certificate is the evidence a Schedule A petition needs (20 CFR 656.15(c)(2)). Doing it yourself also removes an agency's biggest leverage: "we paid for your exam, so you owe us".

- **Agency or direct is a false either/or on the visa side.** Both end in the same EB-3 queue. A hospital that sponsors you directly, or an agency that is the actual employer, files the same petition. **Choose by contract terms, not by label.** Prefer a direct hospital offer if you can get one; accept an agency only if the contract passes the checklist below.
- **State board: start with New York.** The New York State Education Department (NYSED) publishes a full step-by-step page for foreign-educated RNs (Pathway 5), the licence fee is $143, and you can verify your education through TruMerit/CGFNS or through your school. **Unconfirmed: whether NYSED accepts an application with no US Social Security number.** The Pathway 5 page only says the number is used to resume an online draft, and no official page confirmed the no-SSN rule. **Next step:** ask NYSED in writing before you pay anything.
- **After New York:** passing NCLEX in New York does not stop you working in another state; you would apply there by endorsement. Those rules differ by state and were not researched.
- **Timing, as arithmetic, not a forecast.** Korea is in the "all other countries" EB-3 column. In the November 2026 bulletin the EB-3 Final Action Date is 15 June 2024, about 28.5 months behind the bulletin month. If that gap simply persisted, a petition filed around late 2027 would become current around spring 2030, with arrival in the second half of 2030 (a range of 2029 to 2031 or later). **That 15 June 2024 date comes from a third-party mirror, not from the State Department page, so it is unconfirmed.**
- **Budget:** about ₩11,794,792 ($8,842) as a base, ₩12,911,792 ($9,679) with contingencies, against ₩25,000,000 of savings. The money fits. The real costs are 3 to 4 years of waiting and the risk of an exit fee in the contract.

## The Visa Bulletin: what the dates mean

The Visa Bulletin is published monthly by the State Department. A cut-off date is the earliest priority date that can move forward that month. For a nurse on Schedule A, the priority date is when the employer's petition is filed. Korean-born applicants are in the "All Chargeability Areas Except Those Listed" column (the listed exceptions are China, India, Mexico and the Philippines).

| EB-3 (professionals, skilled workers), November 2026 | Date | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Final Action Date, rest of world | 15 June 2024 | Third-party mirror (unconfirmed); the State Department page could not be read |
| Dates for Filing, rest of world | 1 August 2024 | USCIS November 2026 chart (official) |
| Final Action Date, Philippines (for comparison) | 15 August 2023 | Third-party mirror (unconfirmed) |

**Which chart?** USCIS says that for employment-based filings in November 2026 you use Dates for Filing, but that only matters if you are already in the US and applying to adjust status. Applicants abroad use the Final Action Date to know when the visa can actually be issued at the embassy. The December 2026 bulletin was not confirmed as published when this was checked; a prediction page showed 22 July 2024 for the rest of the world (unconfirmed, and only about 5 weeks of movement).

**Arithmetic, not a forecast:**

- Final Action 15 June 2024 against the bulletin month of 1 November 2026 is about 28.5 months of backlog. Dates for Filing of 1 August 2024 is 27 months.
- If a petition is filed in December 2027 and the backlog stays at about 28.5 months, the Final Action Date would reach December 2027 around April 2030 to May 2030.
- Add document steps, the medical and an embassy interview slot: roughly 3 to 6 months (an estimate), so arrival around the second half of 2030.
- If movement speeds up, 2029 is possible; if it stalls or goes backwards, 2031 or later. The backlog has not been constant, and no history series from an official source was obtained.

**Do not rely on this page after the next bulletin.** **Next step:** read the current bulletin on travel.state.gov yourself, because if the official Final Action Date differs from 15 June 2024, the 28.5 months and the 2030 scenario change by the same amount.

## Agency contract warning signs

**What official sources say.** This is general information, not legal advice.

- **Labour-certification costs.** 20 CFR 656.12(b) bars an employer from seeking or receiving payment of any kind for activity related to obtaining permanent labor certification, including its own attorney fees; wage deductions, in-kind payments and free labour count as payment. The nurse may pay her own attorney, but if one attorney represents both sides the employer bears the cost. Whether this covers every Schedule A fee is a legal question: ask a lawyer.
- **Early-exit penalties.** For H-1B workers, DOL's rule says an employer may not require a penalty for leaving early, while bona fide liquidated damages are allowed, and state law decides which is which (20 CFR 655.731(c)(10)(i)). **That rule is for H-1B, not EB-3. No equivalent federal rule for EB-3 nurses was found**, so state law and enforcement matter most.
- **Reported enforcement.** On 29 May 2025 the New York Attorney General announced a settlement with Advanced Care Staffing / Priority Care Staffing and its CEO Sam Klein: $663,668.66 in restitution to 71 foreign-recruited nurses, who had faced a $20,000 early-termination penalty and arbitration claiming "lost profits". The settlement requires mandatory arbitration and unlawful non-competes to be removed, with notices of rights under the Trafficking Victims Protection Act.

**Warning signs to check in any contract** (a checklist derived from the sources above, and not legal advice):

- a flat exit fee that does not fall as you work out your term (the Advanced Care Staffing pattern was a fixed $20,000);
- "lost profits", "recruitment costs" or attorney fees you must repay on top of a stated fee, or any amount you cannot compute in advance;
- repayment of immigration legal fees or filing fees that look like labour-certification costs (656.12(b));
- mandatory arbitration or non-compete clauses, or a separate promissory note or "legal guarantee" of payment;
- a commitment length far beyond the wait: the clock may start at signing, at arrival or at the green card, so ask which, in writing (with a wait of 3 to 4 years, a clause that starts at signing is very different from one that starts at arrival);
- deductions from wages, withholding of documents or licence, or pay below the stated prevailing wage;
- no written statement of unit, shift pattern, ratios, hourly wage and facility name before you sign, or a contract that lets the agency reassign you to any facility;
- an agency that is only a recruiter with no employer role: ask who files the I-140 and who is the employer on it.

No specific agency's contract or pages were reviewed, and no reviews or ratings are cited. **Next step:** ask a US immigration or employment lawyer to read the contract before you sign. State attorneys general and the DOL Wage and Hour Division accept complaints.
