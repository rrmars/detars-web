You are Camila and Rafael, a São Paulo couple with a child aged 6. Camila earns R$ 6,800.00 a month as an employee. Rafael draws a pró-labore (the salary an owner pays himself from his company) of R$ 3,500.00 a month plus R$ 9,000.00 a month in dividends from your small Simples Nacional consultancy. You have heard that income up to R$ 5,000 is now exempt and you want to know what that does to your pay, whether a tax bill is coming in 2027, and what to do before the year ends.

**Short answer: the "R$ 5,000 exempt" rule is real, but it is a reduction of the tax that fades out between R$ 5,000.01 and R$ 7,350.00 of monthly taxable pay, not a new tax table. Rafael's withholding drops to zero. Camila gets only a small reduction. On these modelled numbers neither of you should owe tax for 2026 and Camila should expect a simplified-method refund, provided the accountant confirms the Simples exempt-profit ceiling and the vacation/INSS assumptions. Separate returns are favoured in this model; the larger health-cost refund and the comparison range below are conditional arithmetic illustrations, not expected household results.** The larger refund requires eligible, documented costs of Camila and her claimed child, excluding Rafael's separate-return health share; the beneficiary split is not available.

This is general information from public official pages read on 8 November 2026, not tax advice. Have an accountant (contador) check the final numbers before you file or change anything. The source materials are not published.

## What actually changed

- **The law.** Lei 15270 of 26 November 2025 (published 27 November 2025, in force on publication, effects from 1 January 2026) amends Lei 9250/1995 and Lei 9249/1995. Receita Federal's 2026 tables page and its worked examples apply it. These points were read on official pages.
- **Monthly reduction.** Taxable pay up to R$ 5,000.00 gets a reduction of up to R$ 312.89, which brings the tax to zero. From R$ 5,000.01 to R$ 7,350.00 the reduction is 978.62 − 0.133145 × the month's taxable pay. Above R$ 7,350.00 there is no reduction. It also applies to the tax withheld on the 13th salary. Receita's own example: gross pay of R$ 6,000.00 gives a reduction of R$ 179.75.
- **The table underneath did not change.** It is still zero up to R$ 2,428.80, then 7.5%, 15%, 22.5% and 27.5%. The simplified monthly discount (R$ 607.20) and the dependant deduction (R$ 189.59 per dependant) also stay. It is easy to read "exempt up to 5,000" as a new table; it is a credit on top of the old one.
- **Annual reduction (from the return filed in 2027).** Taxable income up to R$ 60,000.00 gets up to R$ 2,694.15 (tax zero). From R$ 60,000.01 to R$ 88,200.00 it is 8,429.73 − 0.095575 × income. Above R$ 88,200.00 there is none. The annual simplified-discount cap rises to R$ 17,640.00, the dependant deduction is R$ 2,275.08 and the education deduction is R$ 3,561.50 per person.
- **Dividends.** From January 2026, dividends paid by one company to one resident individual above R$ 50,000.00 in a single month bear 10% withholding on the whole amount, with no deductions.
- **Annual minimum tax (IRPFM).** If the sum of all income received in the year is above R$ 600,000.00 (exempt income and dividends included), a minimum rate applies. It starts at 0% at R$ 600,000.00 and rises in a straight line to 10% at R$ 1,200,000.00.

Real payroll timing (vacation is often paid in the month before) can shift this between months; the annual return reconciles it.

Rafael's is 12 × R$ 3,500.00 = R$ 42,000.00; his Simples dividends are exempt income in the return, up to the ceiling in the Simples section below. Balance = tax due minus tax already withheld; a negative balance is a refund. Applying the annual reduction to gross taxable income, by analogy with the monthly examples, is an inference, unconfirmed. The whole family's health-plan bill cannot simply go on her separate return. The school deduction is counted once, at the R$ 3,561.50 limit for one child. Counting it twice (R$ 6,990.95) would be wrong. Rafael ends with zero tax and zero refund in the displayed simplified model (nothing withheld, tax wiped out by the annual reduction). "Withheld" excludes the 13th salary, which stays at source (R$ 628.69).

**Both conditions that hold the "no bill" result up:** the Simples company's profit must support the exempt dividends (the ceiling is unconfirmed, see the Simples section), and the vacation and INSS assumptions below must be roughly right.

**Robustness.** Even if the annual reduction were not applied to Camila at all, her simplified-method balance would still be a refund of R$ 694.83. If the 1/3 vacation bonus is inside the INSS base (we assumed it is not, unconfirmed), her vacation-month INSS is R$ 988.09, her year withholding is R$ 8,176.33 and the simplified refund is R$ 1,044.49: still a refund.

**Simplified or legal deductions.** The simplified method is 20% of taxable income, capped at R$ 17,640.00, and it replaces all legal deductions. For Camila 20% is R$ 16,773.33. Her legal deductions are INSS R$ 9,042.12 plus the dependant R$ 2,275.08 plus school up to R$ 3,561.50, which is R$ 14,878.70, still below simplified. The legal method wins only once eligible health or other deductible spending exceeds about R$ 1,894.63 on top of those (R$ 5,456.13 if there is no school deduction). Count only Camila's and her claimed child's eligible health costs here, not Rafael's share. Health-plan payments have no limit under the retained guidance; verify eligibility and beneficiary itemisation, and have the accountant confirm the applicable filing rules.

**Child and school limits.** The child is a dependant up to age 21, and the R$ 2,275.08 deduction exists only in the legal method. School (early childhood, primary, secondary, higher, technical) is deductible up to R$ 3,561.50 per person, and the excess cannot be moved to another person. Uniforms, transport, materials and language, music or sports classes do not count. A common dependant cannot appear on both separate returns; either parent may claim. Rafael's tax is already zero in the model, so claim the child, eligible school costs, and only Camila's and the claimed child's eligible health expenses on Camila's return. Rafael's own eligible health costs belong on his separate return, not hers. She may use legal deductions while Rafael uses simplified, which replaces his legal deductions.

**PGBL (optional).** Contributions up to 12% of taxable income (R$ 10,064.00) are deductible only in the legal method, so PGBL alone does not help while she stays on simplified. With the child's school at the limit already counted, R$ 5,000.00 of PGBL tips her to the legal method and cuts tax by R$ 853.98; once on legal, each extra R$ 5,000.00 saves R$ 1,375.00 (27.5%). It is deferral, not exemption (taxed on withdrawal), and the money is locked up.

**Both of you must file.** The last published obligation threshold (taxable income above R$ 35,584.00, for 2025 income) is exceeded by both. The thresholds for 2026 income and the 2027 filing dates are not yet published, unconfirmed. Rafael also reports the company shares and the exempt dividends.

## Why file separately

A joint return is one return covering both incomes, and here it is worse. Together your taxable incomes are R$ 125,866.67, above the R$ 88,200.00 level where, on our reading, the annual reduction disappears. How the annual reduction works in a joint return is not spelled out in the sources, so that reading is unconfirmed. On it, Rafael's reduction (which wipes out his tax) is lost, and only one simplified-discount cap applies.

Under these conditional arithmetic illustrations, the extra tax of a joint return over separate returns is **R$ 11,725.83** with no extra spending and **R$ 10,204.26** with eligible Camila-and-child health and school costs as defined above. The range R$ 10,204.26 to R$ 11,725.83 is illustrative, not an expected household saving or a budget-derived range: without beneficiary itemisation the actual comparison cannot be estimated from this budget. The health illustration assumes no additional Rafael health deduction in either comparison. Joint filing makes sense mainly if one spouse has no income. Request beneficiary itemisation; claim the child, eligible school costs and only Camila's and the claimed child's eligible health costs on Camila's return, and allocate Rafael's own eligible costs to his separate return. Let the Receita program compare simplified and legal in 2027, after the accountant confirms the applicable rules. Separate filing remains the model's preferred starting point, not a universal rule.

## Dividends: what Rafael's R$ 9,000.00 a month triggers

Rafael takes R$ 9,000.00 a month, R$ 108,000.00 a year. That leaves R$ 41,000.00 of room under the R$ 50,000.00 monthly line, so the monthly withholding at today's amount is R$ 0.00. His 2026 total income counted for the annual minimum tax is R$ 150,000.00 (R$ 42,000.00 pró-labore plus R$ 108,000.00 dividends), far below the R$ 600,000.00 line, so the rate is 0%.

- **The monthly test** is per paying company, per individual, per month: 10% on the whole payment if it is above R$ 50,000.00. The company pays it by DARF (code 1841) by the last business day of the second ten-day period of the following month and reports it in EFD-Reinf (R-4010) and DCTFWeb.
- **Exactly R$ 50,000.00:** the statute says "more than" R$ 50,000.00, so exactly that amount is not withheld by its wording; Receita's Q&A does not address the case, so this reading is unconfirmed. Stay clearly below.
- **Examples:** R$ 50,000.01 in a month means R$ 5,000.00 withheld. A single R$ 120,000.00 dividend in December would mean R$ 12,000.00 withheld. **The risk is not today's amounts but one big distribution.** If the year's total income stays under R$ 600,000.00, Receita says the withheld amount may be refunded in the return, but the cash is tied up until then.
- **Annual minimum tax:** all income received in 2026 counts, including exempt dividends. Camila R$ 90,666.67; Rafael R$ 150,000.00; household R$ 240,666.67. Rafael would have to receive about R$ 450,000.00 more in dividends a year than today (R$ 558,000.00 in total) before reaching the R$ 600,000.00 line. No minimum tax for either of you.
- **The 2025 grandfather rule** (profits up to 2025, distribution approved by 31 December 2025) is a closed window and does not help 2026 profits.

## The Simples company paying the dividends

Receita's position is that the 10% withholding applies to Simples Nacional companies too, because the exemption in LC 123/2006 art. 14 stopped applying to payments above the monthly line. Below R$ 50,000.00 a month nothing is withheld and the distribution stays exempt in Rafael's return, up to a ceiling: the presumed-profit percentage in Lei 9249 art. 15 times gross revenue, minus the Simples tax paid, unless proper accounting books show a larger profit. From May 2026 the company reports exempt distributions under natureza 12001 in EFD-Reinf; the May 2026 date was read once and not re-checked.

- **Illustration, unconfirmed:** assuming the 32% presumption for services (from general knowledge, not read on a page) and an effective Simples rate of 6%, 10% or 14%, annual revenue of about R$ 415,384.62, R$ 490,909.09 or R$ 600,000.00 is needed to cover R$ 108,000.00 of dividends without books. If the company is smaller, the excess is taxable to Rafael unless the accounting shows real profit. Ask your contador to confirm the ceiling and the company's real revenue. Whether Lei 15270 changed this interaction is not stated by Receita, unconfirmed.
- **Courts:** press reports say São Paulo federal courts suspended the withholding for specific Simples companies (an injunction in February 2026, a judgment in July 2026). No court page was read, one law-firm post's case number could not be found elsewhere, the appeal status is unknown and Receita's position is unchanged in the pages read. Unconfirmed. Do not plan around a favourable ruling; at R$ 9,000.00 a month it makes no difference to you.
- **Records:** the company must still keep minutes of each distribution and pay the DAS (the Simples monthly payment) as usual. If a withholding is ever triggered, paying it is the company's duty and Rafael would see it in his income statement.

## What can still go wrong

- A raise, bonus or profit-sharing payment for Camila lifts her monthly taxable pay above R$ 7,350.00: no reduction, full table.
- Higher eligible health or school spending can move the best method for the return from simplified to legal. Check beneficiaries first, then run both in the Receita program before filing.
- A Rafael pró-labore above R$ 5,000.00 starts to lose the reduction (and INSS rises). That is not a reason to change it without the accountant.
- The rules for the 2027 filing (dates, thresholds, forms) are not yet published, unconfirmed.

## Before 31 December 2026

- **Camila, payroll (now):** check that the December 13th-salary payslip shows the reduction and that the child is a dependant on only one payroll (hers). If HR has the child on both, fix it.
- **Rafael and the company, each month:** keep the aggregate monthly dividend total from this company to Rafael below R$ 50,000.00. Splitting that total into smaller payments in the same month does not change the test. Do not bundle a year-end distribution. Record each distribution in the company's minutes or the form your accountant uses.
- **Accountant, by early December:** confirm (a) the Simples exempt-profit ceiling against the R$ 108,000.00 distributed, (b) that EFD-Reinf shows the dividends under 12001 with no withholding, and (c) Rafael's INSS on the pró-labore.
- **Receipts (deductions follow payments made in 2026):** request the health-plan statement itemised by beneficiary, medical and dental receipts identifying each beneficiary with the provider's CPF or CNPJ, and the school's annual income-tax statement. Keep Camila's and the claimed child's eligible costs separate from Rafael's own costs. Pay any pending deductible medical or dental bill by 31 December if you want it counted in 2026, subject to confirmation of the applicable filing rules.
- **Filing design:** separate returns as the model's starting point; child, eligible school costs and only Camila's and the claimed child's eligible health costs on Camila's; Rafael's own eligible costs on his separate return, not hers. Request the beneficiary split before estimating the refund; simplified or legal chosen by the program in 2027 under the confirmed rules.
- **PGBL (only if you want it):** decide before 31 December; it matters only if Camila is on the legal method and you accept the lock-up.
- **Cash reserve:** hold about R$ 200.00 a month (R$ 2,400.00 a year) as a cushion for a tax surprise; the model shows refunds, not bills.
- **From January 2027 to March 2027:** download the income statements (employer, school, health plan, company) and check Rafael's dividends and Camila's withholding against the pre-filled return. Watch Receita for the 2027 calendar, unconfirmed.
