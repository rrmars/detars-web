You are Hiroshi (67) and Keiko (64), with about ¥42 million in savings and a pension of ¥290,000 a month, thinking about living in Malaysia from autumn 2027. You want to know where to go, which visa fits, and whether the money lasts. **Short answer: start with a trial stay in Kuala Lumpur (Mont Kiara), do not commit to MM2H yet, and put your questions to your ward office, the Japan Pension Service and a tax adviser before you buy anything.**

This guide was prepared on 8 November 2026 from public pages. It is general information, not legal, tax or immigration advice. Nothing was applied for and nobody was contacted. The source materials are not published. Listings are examples, not availability.

## Short answer: Mont Kiara first, trial stay before any visa

**City: Kuala Lumpur, Mont Kiara, as the base to test first.** The only Japanese-language support that could be confirmed is in Kuala Lumpur. The Japan Club of Kuala Lumpur’s own hospital list names Gleneagles KL, Pantai KL, ParkCity and Prince Court as having Japanese-language desks or staff. For Penang, a Japanese Consulate-General is confirmed, but no Japanese-speaking clinic or hospital desk was found. That is a gap in what could be read, not proof that none exists. Penang stays a serious alternative if rent matters more to you than Japanese-language care, because the older Tanjung Bungah blocks in the sample ask less. What to do: ask both Japanese-community groups and any hospital you might use what they offer before you choose.

**Visa: do not commit to a long-stay visa yet.** If you do go ahead, the federal MM2H **Silver** tier is the only route in this research that is on an official page and fits ages 67 and 64. Its money terms are heavy: a USD 150,000 fixed deposit and a compulsory home purchase of RM 600,000 or more, with a 10-year resale lock. At the exchange rates below, that ties up about **¥35.3 million of your ¥42 million**, before fees, purchase costs and the cost of keeping the Yokohama flat running.

**Safer sequence:** spend 1–3 months in autumn 2027, or earlier, in a rented Mont Kiara condo on a visa-free entry. A Japanese embassy page says visa-free stays are up to 90 days, but that page is undated, so confirm with Malaysian Immigration. Before buying anything, get answers to the questions in the adviser section below. Decide on MM2H Silver only after that.

**Sarawak and Sabah** run their own MM2H programmes, restrict residence to their own state, and their published numbers either conflict or could not be read. They suit you less unless you specifically want Kuching or Kota Kinabalu.

## What Silver would do to your savings

Exchange rates are the ECB reference rates published for 6 November 2026: 1 USD = 158.33 JPY and 1 MYR = 39.00 JPY. These are reference rates only; bank and card rates will be worse.

- Deposit: USD 150,000 × 158.33 = ¥23,749,500.
- Property: RM 600,000 × 39.00 = ¥23,400,000.
- If the maximum 50% of the deposit (USD 75,000 = ¥11,874,750) is withdrawn to help pay for the home, cash out is ¥23,400,000 + ¥23,749,500 − ¥11,874,750 = **¥35,274,750**. That leaves ¥6,725,250 of your ¥42,000,000.
- Officially listed fees, if all apply: RM 1,000 + RM 7,500 = RM 8,500 × 39.00 = ¥331,500, leaving ¥6,393,750. **Stamp duty, legal and agent costs for the purchase are not in the sources and are not included.**
- Remaining liquid money would fund roughly 29 months of the Kuala Lumpur case (¥6,393,750 ÷ ¥219,600 ≈ 29.1), which is less than the 3 years you are considering.

The home is an asset, but the pass is revoked if you sell within 10 years except to upgrade. Next step: if Silver still appeals after the trial stay, ask a licensed agent for a full written cost list including purchase costs before you pay a deposit.

## Healthcare and insurance

**Japanese national health insurance (国民健康保険) will probably end.** Yokohama’s pages say a move abroad means ending it at your ward’s insurance desk after the 転出届 (moving-out notice). Cover ends the day after departure, and you notify within 14 days. A rule that stays enrolled if you are away under a year, and leaves if you are away a year or more, appears on other cities’ pages (Ayase in Kanagawa was read in full) but not on the Yokohama pages that were read. Ask your ward. A 2–3 year stay falls on the “leave” side.

**海外療養費** (reimbursement for emergency care abroad) is limited, in the version read for 協会けんぽ: only treatment that Japan’s insurance covers, paid at Japanese price levels, with paperwork and months of waiting, and a 2-year limit. It would not replace insurance for a long stay. Yokohama’s own national-health-insurance version was not found.

MM2H lists health insurance among its renewal documents and a medical check-up at a panel clinic after approval.

**Private international cover at 64 and 67:** Cigna Global’s seniors page says there is no upper age limit and describes support for eligible pre-existing conditions. It shows no premium and no quote was requested. Other insurers and Japanese expatriate plans were not researched, so the budget uses a placeholder, marked as an assumption. Malaysian private hospitals are generally costly (Japan’s foreign ministry, read partially), and a secondary guide says private hospitals commonly accept overseas travel insurance cashless (unconfirmed). Next step: request written quotes for both of you, stating your conditions, before you fix a budget line.

## Questions to take to Japanese and Malaysian advisers

**Two separate notices.** A 転出届 at the ward ends resident registration and national health insurance (one year or more is the common standard on other cities’ pages). A 在留届 to the Japanese embassy is required for stays of 3 months or more, and filing one does not do the other. Keeping the Yokohama flat does not by itself settle whether you count as resident, so ask.

**Yokohama ward office (insurance and registration desks)**

- Is a planned 2–3 year stay with the flat kept treated as 転出? Is there a 1-year threshold?
- What happens to your 住民票, My Number card and 国保 on departure, and what is possible on return visits?
- How is the premium settled on exit, and does Yokohama pay 海外療養費 if you stay enrolled?

**Japan Pension Service**

- You must file the overseas address and bank form, and a 現況届 is due every year in your birth month with a 在留証明 from the embassy. In which currency is Malaysia paid (the list could not be read), and can a Japanese bank account be kept?
- Old-age pensions abroad are withheld as non-resident income unless a treaty exemption form (様式9) applies. Is Malaysia on the exemption list? The list and the treaty (a scanned PDF) could not be read.
- Keiko (64): voluntary 国民年金 enrolment is possible for Japanese living abroad, but the page read does not state the age limit, so she may have under a year left. Ask what her paid months and best option are.
- Does 年金生活者支援給付金, if you receive it, stop? The pages say it does on moving abroad.

**Tax adviser (税理士)**

- Are you resident or non-resident for Japanese tax when you keep a flat? Is a 納税管理人 (tax agent) needed? This was not verified on National Tax Agency pages.
- Rental or sale of the flat, property tax, local tax in the departure year, savings income, NISA, and inheritance with non-resident heirs. Also what Malaysia taxes: the MM2H site says foreign income is untaxed, but that is its own claim and was not checked with Malaysia’s tax authority.

**Malaysian side**

- Is insurance mandatory for the pass chosen, and what is the minimum? What are acceptance and pricing at 64 and 67, with pre-existing conditions, and what are the renewal limits at 70+?
- Is the RM 5,000 and RM 2,500 processing fee charged at first grant? Does the MM2H medical check-up fail on any condition? Can you apply without an agent?
