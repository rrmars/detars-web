You may have enough income to start thinking seriously about a home, yet still be short of the cash and certainty needed to buy one. **For this couple, keep renting and saving through 2027, then reassess once both permanent visas are actually granted.** Expected eligibility is not a grant date, and a lender accepting your visa does not mean the property is legal to buy.

At a constant **AUD $700,000** price and **80% loan-to-value ratio (LVR)**, buying a qualifying completed new house while both hold subclass **491** needs **AUD $239,065 upfront**. An eligible established first home after both PR grants needs **AUD $163,350** under the same current-rule assumptions. The **AUD $75,715** difference is an upfront cash comparison—not a loan approval, forecast or proof that waiting makes you wealthier.

This example follows **Wei Ling, 33, an accountant, and Daniel, 35, a mechanical engineer**, Malaysian citizens living in Perth on 491 visas. They have **AUD $185,000 combined gross income**, **AUD $95,000 savings**, and want a **three-bedroom house at AUD $650,000–750,000**. No nationality-based exemption is assumed. All amounts below are AUD; prices, rules and lender information are dated **7 November 2026** unless another effective date is stated. They need checking again before any commitment. The underlying source file is not published.

**There is a conditional alternative, not a comfortable shortcut:** an eligible off-the-plan home at hypothetical **95% LVR** could need **$84,065 before the actual lender risk fee**, leaving just **$10,935** before that fee and an emergency reserve. Keep that possibility in view, but do not confuse technical cash feasibility with affordability. This guide is not financial or legal advice.

## Start with permission, then tax, then lending

These are separate gates. Living, working and paying tax in Australia does not turn a temporary visa into a permanent one.

### An ordinary established home is not the 2027 buying route

The [Treasury residential-land rules](https://foreigninvestment.gov.au/guidance/types-investments/residential-land) and [Guidance Note 6, version 5, dated 1 July 2026](https://foreigninvestment.gov.au/sites/foreigninvestment.gov.au/files/2026-06/guidance-note-6-residential-land-v5.pdf) put the established-dwelling foreign-buyer ban at **1 April 2025–30 June 2029**. The former **31 March 2027** end date is not a buying deadline to plan around. Ordinary family-home ownership is not one of the redevelopment, commercial-scale or specified employee-housing exceptions.

Once both actually hold permanent visas, an ordinary personal residential purchase is exempt from foreign-investment approval, including an established home. **You do not then need to wait for the ban to expire.** But expected subclass **191** eligibility in **2028**, an application or bridging status is not a permanent-visa grant. The [491 page](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/skilled-work-regional-provisional-491), [191 page](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/skilled-regional-191) and [regional provisional stream page](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/Visa-subsite/Pages/work/191-regional-provisional-stream.aspx) were blocked when researched. Individual pathway eligibility, visa conditions, validity and regional postcode suitability remain unverified; owning a Perth home or having this income does not secure PR.

### A genuinely new home can be possible while on 491

Qualifying new dwellings and genuinely vacant residential land can generally be approved regardless of value. Obtain approval **before an unconditional acquisition**; a properly approval-conditional contract can be possible. Do not make an unconditional auction commitment without appropriate approval. A renovated resale or a replacement for a demolished dwelling is not automatically federally “new”; state grant or concession classification is a different test. Vacant land usually requires construction within **four years from the approval notice**.

A developer exemption certificate must actually cover the purchase. It is not automatically fee-free: per-sale fees can be passed on by agreement, and the certificate scheme is not assumed for this standalone-house budget. Foreign ownership registration and annual vacancy returns can still apply while you live there. Continuous occupation is the model's reason for assuming no vacancy fee, not an exemption from reporting.

The [federal fee schedule effective 1 July 2026](https://foreigninvestment.gov.au/sites/foreigninvestment.gov.au/files/2026-06/schedule-of-fees-v8.pdf) sets **$15,600** for the standard new-dwelling/residential-land application in this price band during **1 July 2026–30 June 2027**. Recheck for purchases from **1 July 2027**. The established-dwelling fee of **$46,800** does not purchase an exception to the ban. The [fees guidance](https://foreigninvestment.gov.au/guidance/general/fees), updated **18 September 2026**, also makes the proposal structure and correct payment important; the statutory **30-day** decision period starts only once the correct fee is paid, not a guaranteed settlement timetable.

## What changes in WA after PR?

| Rule | While both hold 491 | After both actual PR grants |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Foreign transfer duty | **7% of the full dutiable value**, on top of ordinary duty; charged once, not separately for each spouse | No foreign surcharge for this ordinary personal purchase if neither is foreign |
| First home owner rate of duty (FHOR) | Neither currently meets the citizenship/PR requirement; baseline pays general duty | Conditional first-home rate, for established as well as new homes |
| First home owner grant (FHOG) | No current entitlement while neither applicant is a citizen/PR | Up to **$10,000** for an eligible **new** home; **$0** for established homes |

The [foreign-duty fact sheet](https://www.wa.gov.au/system/files/2026-10/foreign-transfer-duty-fact-sheet.pdf), dated **22 October 2026**, records the surcharge applying from **1 January 2019**. One PR holder does not automatically remove foreign duty from the other purchaser's share. [PR before transfer](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/publications/change-foreign-status), in guidance updated **1 February 2024**, can support reassessment; PR granted after transfer is not a general refund entitlement. A longer settlement does not cure a federal purchase restriction.

For agreements from **7 May 2026**, the [first-home duty rate](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/publications/duties-fact-sheet-first-home-owner-rate) is **$0 up to $600,000**, then **$16.15 per $100 or part above $600,000 up to $800,000**, with general duty above **$800,000**. Contract date selects the rate. The [23 October 2026 housing taxation announcement](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/announcements/2026-27-housing-taxation-package) confirms the change; older metropolitan **$500,000/$700,000** thresholds applied **21 March 2025–6 May 2026**, not to these modelled contracts.

The [FHOG fact sheet as at 28 July 2026](https://www.wa.gov.au/system/files/2026-10/first-home-owner-grant.pdf) sets the Perth new-home value cap at **$800,000 from 7 May 2026**. At least one applicant must be a citizen/PR **at application**. Buying before PR is not necessarily permanent forfeiture: application is generally within **12 months of completion**, but future qualification or payment is not funding you can spend today. First-home duty reassessment also has deadlines, generally **12 months after title registration** for a home or a later applicable statutory deadline. Do not finance the purchase on an assumed refund.

Check both spouses' prior Australian residential ownership, occupation and grant/duty-benefit history. Malaysian ownership alone is not the stated Australian-property ownership test. Each applicant generally must live in the home for **six continuous months starting within 12 months of completion**. Their ages, jobs and income do not settle those tests. If first-home approval is not in place for settlement, [RevenueWA's assessment guidance](https://www.wa.gov.au/organisation/department-of-treasury-and-finance/transfer-duty-assessment), updated **30 July 2026**, requires general duty pending approval/reassessment. Budget for that timing rather than assuming a discount will appear automatically.

## The cash comparison: same price, different lawful purchase

The baseline compares a **completed new house bought on 491 in January–June 2027** with an **eligible established first home after both PR grants, hypothetically in 2028**. An established house while both remain on 491 is not a costed option because the ordinary route is unavailable.

Both scenarios assume one jointly notified single-title purchase; purchase price equals dutiable value and lender valuation; **20% equity deposit**; standard **80%-LVR** finance with **no LMI**; no off-the-plan concession; no rule or fee changes; and first-home eligibility after PR. **A lower lender valuation means more cash.** The no-LMI assumption is generic, not a Pepper-specific fee waiver.

### At $700,000

| Cash item | 491 · completed new house | PR · eligible established home |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Equity deposit | $140,000 | $140,000 |
| Ordinary transfer duty | $27,265 | $16,150 |
| Foreign transfer duty | $49,000 | $0 |
| Federal application fee | $15,600 | $0 |
| Other acquisition allowances | $7,200 | $7,200 |
| LMI / grant counted | $0 / $0 | $0 / $0 |
| Transaction cash excluding deposit | $99,065 | $23,350 |
| **Total cash required** | **$239,065** | **$163,350** |
| Current savings | $95,000 | $95,000 |
| **Additional cash needed** | **$144,065** | **$68,350** |
| Mortgage principal | $560,000 | $560,000 |

No emergency reserve is included. The **$75,715** difference is **$49,000 surcharge + $15,600 application fee + ($27,265 − $16,150) ordinary-duty difference**. Equal deposits and allowances cancel. The deposit is equity, not a transaction expense.

### Across the target range, at 80% LVR

| Price | General duty | PR FHOR | Foreign duty at 7% | 491 cash | PR cash | PR upfront advantage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $650,000 | $24,890 | $8,075 | $45,500 | $223,190 | $145,275 | $77,915 |
| $700,000 | $27,265 | $16,150 | $49,000 | $239,065 | $163,350 | $75,715 |
| $750,000 | $29,740.50 | $24,225 | $52,500 | $255,040.50 | $181,425 | $73,615.50 |

Each cash total includes the **$7,200** allowance, no grant and no retained reserve. General duty in this range is **$11,115 + $4.75 per $100 or part above $360,000** up to **$725,000**; above that, **$28,453 + $5.15 per $100 or part above $725,000**. At **$750,000**, that is **$28,453 + 250 × $5.15 = $29,740.50**—do not extend the lower band. At **$700,000**, general duty is **$11,115 + 3,400 × $4.75 = $27,265**, and FHOR is **1,000 × $16.15 = $16,150**. Round the statutory increments up, not down.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Conveyancing, legal work and searches | $2,200 | Contract, title and provider fees |
| Building and pest inspections | $700 | Actual scope and property |

The contract deposit paid earlier is part of the equity deposit, not another **10%** fee. New-home price is assumed to include applicable GST: no extra **10% GST** is added. Interest, ongoing rates, maintenance, strata levies and insurance renewals are separate ownership costs. Renovations, furniture and construction overruns sit outside this completed-house model.

## Smaller deposits help—but do not solve every gap

At **90% LVR** on **$700,000**, equity is **$70,000** and base debt **$630,000**. A lender and, where relevant, its mortgage insurer must accept both borrowers and the property.

| Scenario | Cash before LMI | With illustrative $15,000 cash-paid LMI | Gap against $95,000 savings |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 491 · completed new house | $169,065 | $184,065 | $89,065 |
| PR · eligible established home | $93,350 | $108,350 | $13,350 |

The PR case leaves only **$1,650 before LMI and reserves**. Treat actual LMI as unknown **L**: PR cash is **$93,350 + L**. If L were **$10,000 / $15,000 / $20,000**, cash would be **$103,350 / $108,350 / $113,350**. These are sensitivity allowances, **not actual premium estimates**. Capitalising **$15,000** makes debt **$645,000** and total LVR **92.14%**, not **90%**; lender/insurer permission and serviceability still matter. LMI protects the lender, not you.

### What if prices rise, or the first-home concession fails?

A purely illustrative **5%** rise takes **$700,000** to **$735,000**. PR FHOR becomes **$21,802.50**, equity **$147,000**, and the cash budget **$176,002.50**: **$12,652.50** above the flat-price PR case. It remains **$63,062.50** below the completed-new 491 baseline, but the loan becomes **$588,000**, **$28,000** larger. This is **not a total-wealth break-even**: rent while waiting, mortgage interest, ownership expenses, savings returns and market risk are excluded. No growth forecast is being made.

If first-home eligibility fails after PR, the **$700,000** established-home budget at **80% LVR** becomes **$174,465**: **$140,000 + $27,265 + $7,200**. Waiting still removes **$64,600** of surcharge and federal application fees versus the completed-new baseline. If you instead buy an eligible new home after PR, the same pre-grant budget is **$163,350**; a subsequently paid **$10,000** FHOG reduces net cash cost to **$153,350**, not automatically the cash needed at settlement.

## The off-the-plan exception is worth understanding

**Counterintuitive finding: temporary residents do not always pay the full combined WA duty.** The [off-the-plan concession](https://www.wa.gov.au/service/financial-management/taxation-and-duty/apply-the-plan-duty-concession), updated **29 July 2026**, can reduce ordinary and foreign transfer duty together. Eligible survey-strata freestanding homes are included for agreements **12 March 2026–30 June 2028**, as well as qualifying strata dwellings. This does not remove federal purchase restrictions, and no extension beyond **30 June 2028** is assumed.

At **$700,000**, combined duty is **$27,265 + $49,000 = $76,265**. For a qualifying purchase:

- Pre-construction: **100%** concession capped at **$50,000**, leaving **$26,265** duty.
- Under construction: **75% × $76,265 = $57,198.75**, also capped at **$50,000**, leaving **$26,265**.

| Conditional 491 off-the-plan scenario | Net acquisition cash | What it means against $95,000 savings |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 80% LVR | $189,065 | $94,065 short before reserve |
| 90% LVR | $119,065 + actual risk fee | With illustrative $15,000 cash-paid fee: $134,065, or $39,065 short |
| 95% LVR | $84,065 + actual LPF | $10,935 left before LPF and reserve |

At **95% LVR**, the sum is **$35,000 equity + $26,265 duty + $15,600 federal fee + $7,200 allowances = $84,065**, with **$665,000 base debt**. Add an illustrative **$20,000 reserve**, and the target is **$104,065 + LPF**, a **$9,065 + LPF** gap. LPF means Loan Protection Fee; its actual amount is unknown. Capitalising it may reduce immediate fee cash but increases debt and needs approval. For a Pepper loan, replace the generic fee sensitivity with the actual LPF and lender charges; do not automatically add LMI as well.

**This is net acquisition funding, not a promise that savings cover every payment date.** Confirm the contract deposit, duty-payment timetable and lender drawdown before assuming a high-LVR loan reduces pre-settlement cash. The completed-house baseline does not receive this concession. Verify title scheme, vendor, construction stage, federal “new” status and contract dates. A separate land-and-build package is not automatically eligible, and a state concession does not make a federally established replacement house “new”.

Keep rent during delays, progress interest, variations, landscaping and revaluation risk outside the tempting small-deposit headline. Completed turnkey, off-the-plan and land/build purchases need different budgets. PR before transfer may separately change duty: recalculate the concession and FHOR together rather than counting a full foreign-duty refund and a concession on duty no longer payable.

My read: revisit this route only for a genuinely suitable qualifying home, a lawful acquisition and verified finance—not merely because a brochure says “FIRB friendly”. It could be technically fundable without a reserve; it is not established affordable or approved.
