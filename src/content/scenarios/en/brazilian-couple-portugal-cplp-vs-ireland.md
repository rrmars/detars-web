You are two Brazilians with BRL 95,000 in savings and you are wondering whether Dublin, with an English course that lets you work part-time, is a better start than Portugal. **Short answer: start with Portugal's CPLP residence route and confirm it with the Portuguese consulate before you spend anything; keep Ireland as the fallback.** Ireland cannot take both of you on one student permit, and the version where both of you enrol does not fit your savings on the assumptions below.

The guide follows Beatriz and Lucas, a couple in Belo Horizonte. Lucas is an electrician; Beatriz works in administration. It was written on 8 November 2026 from public pages only; no forms, accounts, quotes or contact were made. All conversions use EUR 1 = BRL 6.1449 (the European Central Bank reference rate for 6 November 2026, read through the Frankfurter republisher; not checked against the ECB's own site, so treat it as unconfirmed). BRL 95,000 is about €15,460. **This is general information, not legal, tax or immigration advice.** The source materials are not published.

Tags used below: **PRIMARY** means an official body page; **UNCONFIRMED** means a school, listing, aggregator, news item or search summary, or anything that could not be read in full.

## The recommendation, with its conditions

**Portugal first, because it is the only one of the three options that covers both of you for more than about six months on this budget, on the assumptions below, but only if the CPLP visa turns out to be what the secondary sources describe.** The reasons:

- **Ireland cannot take both of you as a couple on one student permit.** Irish State guidance says spouses and partners of non-EEA students "cannot apply on the basis of their relationship to a non-EEA student". The accompanying spouse would have no residence basis and no right to work.
- **The only workable Irish version is that both of you enrol on separate courses.** This is not addressed on any page that could be read, so ask the Irish immigration service (ISD) before relying on it, and it roughly doubles the cost.
- **That version does not fit BRL 95,000.** Two tuition packages (the Atlas example, €6,415 each) plus two IRP fees come to €13,430 (R$82,526), which is 87% of your savings before any flight, rent or food. With the assumptions below, the one-off costs alone are €15,536 (R$95,467), above the €15,460 you have.
- **Ireland's route is time-limited and for low-skilled work only.** A language student gets at most 3 courses of 8 months (2 years in total) and may work 20 hours a week in term and 40 hours in the holiday windows, but only if the school is on the ILEP list (otherwise Stamp 2A, no work). It is not a work visa and does not lead anywhere by itself.
- **Portugal's CPLP route fits two Brazilians.** Each of you is a CPLP national, and the permit allows living and working (from a secondary source). That each spouse can apply separately is an inference, not something a page confirms. There is no tuition. The modelled first six months cost €13,905 (R$85,442), and BRL 95,000 lasts about 6.9 months after the one-off costs with no income.
- **The CPLP visa comes before you travel.** A CPLP residence permit now requires a residence visa first; entering as a tourist and regularising later no longer works.
- **Do not plan around the Portuguese job-seeker visa.** The old open job-seeker visa ended on 23 October 2025. Its replacement is limited to "highly qualified" work and, according to the VFS Brazil notice, could not be lodged until a ministerial portaria defines it. Today's status could not be confirmed on the official visa site.

**The big limit.** The Portuguese official pages (vistos.mne.gov.pt and AIMA) and the Irish ISD site refused automated reads, so several Portuguese figures (CPLP visa documents and fees) come from a secondary site. **If the CPLP visa needs a job contract or other documents you cannot get from Brazil, the ranking could change.** Your first step is to ask the Portuguese consulate in Belo Horizonte which documents the CPLP residence visa needs today, what it costs, and whether the qualified job-seeker visa is open.

## Can your spouse come too?

**Ireland: no, not on the other's student permit.** Citizens Information (the State information service, edited 16 April 2026) says non-EEA students "have no right to bring their family with them" and that spouses, partners and children "cannot apply on the basis of their relationship to a non-EEA student". Exceptions exist for PhD students, not for language students. So a spouse cannot get a dependant permit or a work right from the other's Stamp 2. A spouse needs their own basis: enrolling on their own eligible course (each with its own fees, funds and insurance; this exact scenario is not stated on the pages read, so ask ISD) or their own employment permit. The rule rests on Citizens Information only; the ISD page itself could not be read.

**Portugal: both of you are Brazilian, so each is a CPLP national and could in principle apply on your own.** That is an inference; no page read says so for couples. The alternative, family reunification, needs the sponsor to hold a residence permit valid for at least two years (art. 98), and the law allows 9 months to decide (art. 105). PRIMARY. The job-seeker visa has no family provision.

## Stamp 2 or Stamp 2A: does an English course allow work?

**Short answer: yes if the school is on the ILEP list; no if it is not, and then you get Stamp 2A.** Two sources seemed to conflict, and this is how it resolves:

- An English course on the ILEP list gives Stamp 2, with 20 hours a week in term and 40 hours in holidays. A course not on the list gives Stamp 2A, with no work. Citizens Information (PRIMARY) says the same.
- The wording from ISD, "Students with a valid Stamp 2 permission may work up to 20 hours a week while they are studying in Ireland" and "You can work in casual employment for a maximum of 20 hours per week during school term and 40 hours per week during holidays", was relayed from search-index text. **It is an indirect read:** the ISD pages themselves returned only their titles when fetched on 8 November 2026.
- A search snippet from settle.ie saying English students get Stamp 2A was not opened, contradicts the ISD text, and is treated as less reliable. UNCONFIRMED.
- A Citizens Information registration table says Stamp 2 is for a "course of at least one year", which sits oddly with the 25-week rule. It was not reconciled.

**This only works if the course and school are actually listed, so check the school against the ILEP or TrustEd list before paying anything.** The official ILEP list and the TrustEd list could not be read, so it is not confirmed that the two example schools below are currently listed.

## Portugal's job-seeker visa: what is known

- **Law:** article 57.º-A (Lei 61/2025, in force 23 October 2025) creates the "visto para procura de trabalho qualificado" for "atividade profissional altamente qualificada": 120 days plus 60, one entry. If you find no work you must leave and may re-apply after 1 year. The qualifying skills are to be defined by portaria. PRIMARY.
- **Return ticket:** article 52 n.º 2 requires one for these visas. PRIMARY.
- **Status:** VFS Brazil says the old job-seeker visa has not been processed since 23 October 2025 and that the new category opens only once regulated (UNCONFIRMED; the page was read to about 5,900 of 9,600 characters). Two secondary Brazilian sites said in August to September 2026 that it was still not operational. UNCONFIRMED. One of them mentions a "Lei 62/2026", which was not verified.
- **For you:** do not build the plan on it. Ask the consulate whether it is open.

## Lucas: electrician qualification

**Ireland.** Domestic electrical work by a company or individual must be registered with Safe Electric (RECI). The nominated Qualified Certifier needs a National Craft Certificate (Electrical) or equivalent at NFQ Level 6 or above, a Verification and Certification course certificate and a €75 application fee. Foreign qualifications "must be recognised" first by a panel of experts, and CRU says non-EEA applications "may encounter delays". PRIMARY. Whether an employee working under a registered contractor needs individual recognition was not found; recruiter blogs say yes, with delay, but are unverified. SOLAS and QQI recognition routes were not read. A Stamp 2 student is limited to 20 hours a week anyway.

**Portugal.** Acting as "técnico responsável" for electrical installations requires prior recognition by DGEG (Lei 14/2015): a request to the Director-Geral with proof of academic qualifications and ID and tax number. An installer company also needs IMPIC registration and €50,000 of civil-liability insurance. PRIMARY. DGEG's route for foreign (Brazilian) qualifications was not found. Working as an employee under a registered técnico is an inference, not confirmed. Beatriz's administrative role needs no recognition.

**Either way, recognition timelines are unknown and could delay Lucas's work in both options.**

| Health insurance, 2 × €30 (assumption) | €60 | R$369 |
| Monthly total | €1,690 | R$10,385 |

**First 6 months with no income: €3,765 + 6 × €1,690 = €13,905 (R$85,442).** Runway of BRL 95,000 (€15,460): (€15,460 − €3,765) ÷ €1,690 a month = 6.9 months after the one-off costs. This is sensitive to the assumptions: another €100 a month in costs cuts it to about 6.5 months.

With income, one person on the €920 minimum monthly wage (gross, before deductions) cuts the monthly gap to €770, so the runway after one-off costs becomes (€15,460 − €3,765) ÷ €770 = 15.2 months. Two minimum-wage earners (€1,840) exceed the modelled costs of €1,690.

## What is still open

- **The Portuguese CPLP visa itself:** the primary pages for visa fees, the document list, tourist entry rules, the AIMA backlog and the status of the qualified job-seeker visa were unreachable. The CPLP fees (€110 and ≈ €247.30), the €920 monthly means, the 4-month visa and the 3 to 6 month wait come from portugalimigrante.pt only, and AIMA's own fee table was not read. The Portugal recommendation is conditional on consulate confirmation.
- **Each spouse applying separately under CPLP, and the permit giving a right to work,** are an inference plus a secondary source.
- **Whether both spouses can enrol separately in Ireland** was not found on any page; ask ISD. ISD pages stayed unreadable, so work rights rest on Citizens Information plus relayed ISD text, and the spouse rule rests on Citizens Information only.
- **Whether the example schools are on the ILEP or TrustEd list** is unknown.
- **The assumptions drive the budgets:** flights, food, phone, Portugal health insurance, paperwork and deposits have no source. The sourced-only Ireland B items (€13,430, which is 87% of savings) support the conclusion that Ireland B is unaffordable without them, but the Portugal runway is sensitive.
- **Dublin rent for a couple** uses the national double-room figure (€806), likely too low for Dublin. Renting the Daft Q1 1-bed (€2,012) instead would add about €1,200 a month.
- **No dated individual listings** were obtained.
- **Income is modelled gross** (no tax, social charges or job-search gap); Portugal income uses only the €920 monthly minimum.
- **The exchange rate** comes from Frankfurter, which republishes ECB data; ecb.europa.eu was not opened.
- **Not researched:** food and GP costs from sources, tax and social charges, Portuguese electrician wages, Porto city rents, access to the Portuguese health service (SNS), other Dublin schools, and the exact tourist-stay length in Ireland. A WRC notice on Stamp 2 employment entitlements returned a 404.
- **Foreign electrician recognition timelines** in both countries are unknown.

## What to check next, without committing to anything

- Ask the Portuguese consulate in Belo Horizonte (or the Vistos MNE page) which documents the CPLP residence visa needs today, the visa fee, and whether the qualified job-seeker visa is open.
- Read the AIMA CPLP page and fee schedule in a normal browser for current waiting times.
- For Ireland, read ISD's student pages directly for work rights on an English course and whether spouses may each enrol; check any school against the ILEP or TrustEd list.
- Electricians: ask DGEG (Portugal) and CRU or Safe Electric (Ireland) how a Brazilian qualification is assessed.
