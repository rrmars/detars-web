Worried about losing invoices, missing a video call, or replacing a laptop that only needs a small repair? Back up first, then diagnose it. For this Leeds sole-trader example, aim to repair a sound, officially Windows-11-compatible machine for about £200 or less. Otherwise, compare a refurbished ThinkPad with the full cost of getting your work safely onto it—not just the price on the box.

This is desk research, not a hands-on test. The decision target is January 2027. The offers below carry a November 6, 2026 date-seen label; actual retrievals were on November 5, 2026, about 19:20–19:53 UTC, already November 6 in Singapore. That does not establish UK-local November 6 prices, today's availability or January prices. This page was adapted on November 6, 2026 without a research refresh. All prices are GBP except the explicitly labelled US dollar licensing reference.

## The short answer

**Do not order all three upgrades on spec.** Repair a sound, Windows-11-compatible machine for roughly £200 or less. Otherwise, my value pick is the **£331.99 refurbished ThinkPad T14 Gen 2** below [Back to the Office — T1A ThinkPad T14 Gen 2 L-T14G2-UK-P101](https://www.backtotheoffice.co.uk/products/t1a-lenovo-thinkpad-t14-gen-2-refurbished-14-laptop-l-t14g2-uk-p101-intel-core-i5-1145g7-16gb-ram-256gb-ssd-windows-11-pro)—but only after its battery, webcam and charger are confirmed. Reserve money for a better battery, moving your files and backup; do not inherit another weak battery.

Repair instead if it officially supports Windows 11, has a decent processor and sound hinges, and the **whole job is no more than about £200–£250**. That ceiling is my judgement, not a shop quote. An old hard drive is a good reason to fit an SSD. An existing SSD is a reason to diagnose the slowdown before buying anything.

If it cannot officially run Windows 11, I would not spend heavily on it as the main machine holding customer records. See the support catch below. A battery alone may still be worthwhile if everything else works.

## 1. Check the old machine before spending

1. **Save the exact model and processor.** Look in Settings → System → About and on the base label. Ask a shop to confirm Windows 11 eligibility with Microsoft PC Health Check. Six years old does not automatically mean incompatible. [Microsoft Support — Windows 11 system requirements](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/experience/compatibility/windows-11-system-requirements)
1. **Check the bottleneck.** Task Manager → Performance shows memory and disk type. If storage is full, the disk is failing, or startup software is the problem, buying RAM alone will not fix it. Ask for a health check, not a parts shopping list.
1. **Check what fits.** A 2.5-inch SATA drive and an M.2 NVMe drive are not interchangeable. RAM may be soldered; DDR4 and DDR5 are not substitutes. Use the model’s service manual. Price the exact battery part number, not just the laptop brand.
1. **Check battery condition.** A shop can compare full-charge and design capacity using `powercfg /batteryreport`. If the case is bulging, stop using and charging it and get professional help. Do not open or puncture the battery.

## 2. Windows 10 is already out of standard support

Standard Windows 10 support ended on **14 October 2025**. It still turns on. That is not the same as being supported: ordinary free security fixes and normal support have ended. For a business laptop used for banking and customer details, do not simply ignore this. [Microsoft Learn — commercial Windows 10 ESU](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/whats-new/extended-security-updates)

**The easy-to-miss catch:** The cited Microsoft UK consumer ESU page gives coverage to **12 October 2027**, but explicitly says the consumer programme cannot be used in commercial scenarios. Do not budget the free/consumer offer for this sole-trader work machine. [Microsoft UK — consumer ESU](https://www.microsoft.com/en-gb/windows/extended-security-updates)

Business ESU is a paid, limited security-update bridge, not a new lease of life. Microsoft lists US$61 for Year One, doubling each consecutive year, for up to three years. It is annual, not monthly; joining in Year Two also requires Year One. **That is not a UK price.** Obtain a UK commercial-licensing quote for the actual coverage needed before authorising a Windows-10-only repair. ESU is not included in the repair totals here. [Microsoft Learn — commercial Windows 10 ESU](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/whats-new/extended-security-updates)

A supported Windows 11 upgrade needs an approved CPU, TPM 2.0 and UEFI/Secure Boot capability, among other requirements. More RAM or a new SSD cannot make an unsupported processor eligible. Check PC Health Check and Windows Update first; do not build this business plan around bypassing the checks. [Microsoft Support — Windows 11 system requirements](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/experience/compatibility/windows-11-system-requirements)

## 3. Four replacements worth comparing

For this workload, I would prioritise **16GB RAM, 512GB SSD, a readable Full HD or better screen, and a decent webcam** over an “AI PC” badge. Integrated graphics are enough for ordinary social-post cropping and edits. A numerical keypad helps invoices; a smaller laptop is easier to carry.

## 5. Can the business claim it?

General information · UK sole traders

**Usually, the business-use part can be claimed.** Under cash basis, HMRC says computers kept for business use are allowable expenses. The cited HMRC guidance describes cash basis as the standard method for eligible sole traders and partnerships without corporate partners: record the expense when you pay. [HMRC — self-employed office, property and equipment expenses](https://www.gov.uk/expenses-if-youre-self-employed/office-property) [HMRC — cash basis overview](https://www.gov.uk/simpler-income-tax-cash-basis)

With traditional accounting, equipment is normally dealt with through capital allowances. Annual Investment Allowance can normally deduct qualifying equipment costs, within the £1 million annual limit. Private use must be excluded. For example, £600 × 80% business use = £480 potentially deductible; that does **not** mean HMRC refunds £480. Repairs and maintenance can also be allowable business expenses; a substantial upgrade may need different treatment. Keep receipts, payment dates and a sensible business-use record. Selling an asset previously claimed can have tax consequences. [HMRC — self-employed office, property and equipment expenses](https://www.gov.uk/expenses-if-youre-self-employed/office-property) [HMRC — Annual Investment Allowance](https://www.gov.uk/capital-allowances/annual-investment-allowance)

This is general information, not tax advice—your bookkeeper can kindly check the right treatment for your circumstances.

## 6. Keep the invoices. Lose the old data safely.

### Before a repair or a move

1. Copy invoices, receipts, customer records, photos, email archives and local bookkeeping databases. Export from the bookkeeping app too. Check what cloud storage actually contains; a sync icon is not proof of a complete backup.
1. Keep two copies: a secure cloud backup and an encrypted external drive, if available. Disconnect the drive when finished. Turn on two-step verification for the cloud account. Store encryption recovery keys somewhere safe, away from the laptop. [NCSC — backing up your data](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/small-organisations-guide-to-cyber-security/backing-up-your-data) [Microsoft Support — Reset your PC](https://support.microsoft.com/en-gb/windows/experience/backup-recovery/reset-your-pc)
1. Restore a sample invoice, a photo and the bookkeeping backup. Check recent transactions on the new machine before wiping anything. Save app licences and recovery codes; test email, printer/scanner and a video call. Keep the old machine securely stored until the move is confirmed.

### Before selling, donating or recycling

1. After the restored backup is checked, sign out of apps, deauthorise licences where needed and remove saved logins. Remove SD cards, USB receivers and any second storage drive.
1. **Do not just delete files or quick-format.** Windows “Reset this PC → Remove everything → Clean data” is Microsoft’s consumer selling option, but Microsoft says its erasure does not meet government/industry standards. Do not call it a guaranteed wipe for client records. [Microsoft Support — Reset your PC](https://support.microsoft.com/en-gb/windows/experience/backup-recovery/reset-your-pc)
1. **For this business machine, use a documented, verified sanitisation process appropriate to its drive.** NCSC guidance distinguishes encrypted-device manufacturer resets from unencrypted-media overwrite and verification. Ask a competent shop about manufacturer ATA/NVMe sanitisation tools, verification, and a written record of the drive serial number and result. SSD remapping makes casual overwriting an unsafe assumption. If a drive is faulty or cannot be verified, keep it securely or use a reputable destruction service; do not sell it with data still recoverable. [NCSC — secure sanitisation and disposal of storage media](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/guidance/secure-sanitisation-storage-media)
1. Use an authorised business-electronics recycling route and keep the handover/destruction record. **Leeds household recycling centres are not for waste from your own business.** Do not assume the local household tip will accept this laptop as business waste. [Leeds City Council — waste and recycling sites](https://www.leeds.gov.uk/bins-and-recycling/recycling-sites)

Paid sanitisation or destruction is extra; request a quote. Keep it within the £700 total by choosing below the ceiling. A removed old drive remains your data responsibility.
